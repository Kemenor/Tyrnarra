At a Glance

Etymology
An existing name, older than the record of its giving; scholars note the Old-Imperial word for whale and call it a mariner's joke about the island's humped profile. Demonym: Baleni.
Position
One of the hundreds of drifting isles of Shuun, and the most visited; a League island, on the Lists, its dues prompt and its Raft speeches long. Famously, one may also arrive across the water on foot.
The Island
An unusually well-kept drifter, low and green, crowned by a city built on a civic apparatus of weights, pulleys, and ritual-tuned counterbalances the Baleni treat as ordinary municipal engineering. Everything entering is weighed in; everything leaving is weighed out.

The Loud and the Yards

The first step off the boat is loud and greeting: the harbour front performs its welcome with the shining superficiality only tourist ports achieve, and performs it well. The Baleni call the harbour district the Landing on documents and the Loud everywhere else. But the city's grammar is depth. Off the Loud run the alleys, the threads, and they work on a visitor in stages: day one they read as off, unknown, faintly dangerous; day two they invite; day three you find yourself in a passage barely two shoulders wide that ends in a courtyard where the true welcome lives. There is laughter; a local turns and sees you; you brace for distance and are answered with warmth; and shortly you are at a table drinking what the family calls Ours, made to a recipe that is nobody's business, and in three hours, two courtyards on, you will taste a different Ours entirely.

A breeze that smells of the Midarra arrives and carries you to the next thread, the next yard, the next place that is home to people who extend it forward. The bottled stuff sold at the Loud is mirrorshine, and no local drinks it. Nothing true in Balaena is on the surface, and everything true is freely given once you have walked far enough in; the city keeps no secrets from anyone patient, the Baleni say, and believe it.

The Apothecary of Last Resort

The draughts are why the world comes. Balaena is the alchemical capital of the sweet sea: the product of an ever-floating island in a sea that carries most things, fed by the League lanes and by Balatur Erui's trade in the ocean's unclaimed goods, worked in a thousand family stillhouses whose craft runs back past counting. The sore that will not stop hurting, the sleep that will not come, the pain that shrugged off every other medicine: it ends here, and the continent knows it. Pilgrims of pain arrive with the tourists and stay past them, lodged in the deep yards, because the cure is never only the bottle; it is the courtyard, the table, the being-taken-in, and the yardholders have made convalescence a branch of hospitality.

The city's masterwork is sleep itself: no still-tradition on Talan matches Balaena's sedatives, soporifics, and dulling draughts, and the mark Balaena-assayed doubles a price in Merkavar. Trade peaks in the Glass, the calm-water season when the mirror mornings cluster and the tourist hulls arrive daily; prices double in the Glass, and the yards privately exhale when it ends.

The Four Wonders

What the visitors come for, the chronicles record as the city's signature marvels, each with its sensible civic explanation. Visitors find the weighing charming and the schedule strict; the Baleni find the visitors charming and the questions familiar.

The Bridge That Walks on Water

The footbridge to the neighboring stable islet sits five centimetres under the surface, the city having settled slightly as it grew, and pilgrims cross appearing to walk the sea. Painters love it. It has needed no structural repair in three generations.

The Blowhole Fountain

Twice daily the central fountain erupts in sheets and rainbows, fed by the weight-and-pulley works pressing a deep spring on schedule. The city gathers for it the way other towns gather for bells.

The Weight-Gates

Every arrival weighed in, every departure weighed out, a tally-token returned at the gate: folkloric civic pride in a city whose engineering runs on balance. No landings, no take-offs, and no teleportation in the city's airspace, by ordinance older than anyone asks about.

The Mirror Mornings

On calm dawns the sea stills to perfect glass and the city stands twinned in it: the most-reproduced image on the inner sea. Painters and pilgrims plan whole seasons around the forecast.

The Measured Service

Balaena is governed by its civil service, and the service is entered the same way everything in this city is entered: by degrees, tested at each door. Any family's child may sit the open examinations; those who pass join the Measured Service, and rise through it by the Measuring, the promotion rite the whole city turns out for. Each candidate, on rising, distills a measure, their finest and most exacting work, and pours it into the fountain at the rite's close: the officer's craft joining the city's works, the drop joining the whole. A Baleni who has stood the rite is one of the Measured for life. The Service's departments run the stills-excise, the assay-houses, the gate-tallies, the quays, and the fountain-works; its summit is the First Steward, who receives envoys, signs treaties, and instructs Balaena's Raft envoy, whose speeches for island sovereignty are the League's longest and most sincere. Beside and beneath the formal service stand the yardholders, the deep families whose welcome reaches furthest in: they legitimize, gossip, marry, and quietly make or break a First Steward.

The Known Flaw

A government selected by examination for competence and steadiness is a government of the even-tempered, allergic to its own reformers. Balaena promotes the agreeable, and its brilliant-and-restless take ship for Uravel and Merkavar young. The city considers this a fair price for streets that work, and says so, comfortably, at every table.

Growing Up Baleni

A Baleni childhood is a geography lesson in trust: which yards are yours, which will feed you anyway, which threads a child may run and which belong to the grown. The young rebel outward, as Isles young do, taking their crew-years on the League hulls, and Balaena's return-rate is the archipelago's quiet envy; the standing joke is that you can take the Baleni out of the yards, and that is the whole joke, because you cannot do the second half. Coming of age is domestic and exact: the day the family lets you run the still alone, start to finish, and pours what you made at the table without correcting it. Your first Ours. Nobody forgets theirs, and most are honestly bad, and are drunk anyway, ceremonially, by everyone who loves you.

The Live Tensions

The Glass money. The Loud creeps. Every season the tourist front wants one more street of the city behind it: outside money offers yard-families sums no still earns for house-room the yards were never selling, and a few have sold, and the sold yards die into lodging-houses within a generation. The yardholders now refuse as a bloc, the Service taxes conversions hard, and the question of whether Balaena can stay Balaena while the Glass pays for everything is argued at every deep table, right after the toast that spends the Glass's coin.

The Raft and the waterline. Balaena's sovereignty speeches are famous, long, and sincerely felt by the envoys who give them; the city bankrolls the sovereigntist argument against the Standing Watch and against every harmonized-law proposal the centralizers float. The chronicle-record notes only that a city this dependent on League lanes argues this hard against League reach, and that the Isles contain many mysteries smaller than that one.

The Baleni personify their island, universally and without a grain of belief in it: the island is the old girl, and everything is her mood. The bridge dips a knuckle deeper: the old girl's tired. The fountain throws high in a north wind: she's showing off for company. A still-batch fails across three yards the same week: she slept badly, leave her be. Sailors carry the habit across the Isles and are corrected by pedants that islands are not alive, and the Baleni agree pleasantly, the way you agree with a tourist, and go on saying it, because it is only a way of talking. Nobody has ever meant it.

⚿   There Is No Island

There is no island. The whole of Balaena, the ground underfoot, the low green hills, the soil the yards grow their herbs in, the shelf the harbour stands on, is one living creature: a young skywhale, half-submerged, and the city is the weight that keeps her down. Her hide is the bedrock, her back is the high ground, and the "deep spring" the fountain-works press is her blowhole. Her name is Maella, and she is held in the state the whole apparatus exists to maintain: an awareness kept sleeping, a dream kept waking; asleep enough not to rise, awake enough not to sink; balanced by measurement and methodology, to the gram and to the hour.

The four wonders are the apparatus showing: the fountain is her regulated breath, the bridge tracks her slow growth and settling, the weight-gates are her buoyancy ledger kept per-day, the mirror mornings are calm because nothing beneath the city thrashes. The vast majority of the Baleni do not know, and have never been given a reason to wonder; the city's whole public character, layered, patient, balanced, dosed, is the camouflage grown over the vigil, and grown so well that most of it is now genuinely, independently true.

⚿   The Dullest Body in the Service

The keepers sit in plain sight as the dullest body in the Measured Service: the Committee for Fountain and Foundation, which maintains the fountain-works and watches the city's foundations, both of which are the literal truth. Its founders' motives are a written past, greed and terrible curiosity generations dead; the present Committee is followers, common-good believers and quiet doubters together, every one a senior official, and none by chance. The temperament assay inside the Measuring is the Committee's sieve, run on every rising officer for cohesion to one unstated necessity: the survival of a city that cannot be carried into the sky. The steady are promoted; the restless are eased toward brilliant careers elsewhere; and for the rare officer who rises past their station with questions, blackmail has always been an option, and has been used.

Inside the Committee sits a smaller room: the Vigil, the few who hold what even the Committee's rolls omit, her name. Once a year the Vigil speaks Maella over the water, the only ceremony they keep and the only apology they make: a remembrance upheld as vigil. And every officer of Balaena, knowing none of this, takes the oath of the Measured at the fountain, over her breath.

⚿   Why the Draughts Are the Best

Balaena's alchemical supremacy has a cause the trade histories never found: for generations the city's stillcraft has been pulled forward by a single standing commission that cannot be refused, rushed, or revealed. Keeping an adult skywhale in the in-between takes sedation past any mortal pharmacopoeia, doses that would kill a ship's crew, delivered on schedule, forever; the Committee's formulary is the summit of the art, and the industry that towers around it began as its supply chain and became its camouflage. The measures poured at every Measuring are part of it: the offering-formula each candidate is assigned rotates with the Committee's needs, the fountain-works run both ways, and the rite that binds the Service to the city doses her in the same hour.

This is also why Balaena argues sovereignty at the Raft with such length and feeling: harmonized port-law, a pact census with teeth, or the Standing Watch's assessors would put outside eyes inside the works. And it is why the Committee's actuaries lose sleep over one sound no chart can price: whales sing before they rise. If the dosing ever slips, the first sign will be song, the whole archipelago has ears for whale-song now, and every League crew that passes knows what a crescendo means. (Beside Lomendi's entry on Balatur Erui, this is the pairing the canon built: one old whale that came down by choice and is kept in love; one young whale held down by choice and kept by method. The two stories have never met. They could.)
