The Whalehall
Valreka's Godshall · The Adventurers' Guild on Anzar
The only Godshall of the thirteen that travels. It rides a whale with a district and no house, at the leading edge of the herd, and the city has decided that this is fine.

At a Glance

What
Valreka's Godshall, the Adventurers' Guild's monumental rank, held in every divine city-state. The city says Whalehall; the paperwork says Valreka's Godshall; both are used daily.
Where
On Anzar, the Guild's own whale, riding apart at the herd's leading edge with her own Stokkul shoal. The first whale a stranger reaches and the last one the departing see.
Size
About four thousand people on Anzar's back, hall and district together.
Sovereign
Amestan Anzar, Ghoran, in the seat since 2489 MR. The clerks call him Amestanghar to his face.
Reach
Galdua Jendea, Hareaveldi, the River Duchies, and the Lost Isle. The perpetual standing warning over the Blackened Lands is signed from his desk.
Standing
A whale with no house. The Sovereign sits in no council of the houses, and no house crosses the whale. Three offers of a seat among the houses, three refusals.

A Whale With No House

Anzar was bonded wild by an outrider in the last decades of the Dark Era, and when the Guild came to Valreka in the reconstruction it had no whale and so no ground, while the rising house of Anzar had a half-empty back. The Guild took rooms as a paying tenant. It grew until the hall was the district.

In 2287 MR a bad crossing and a sand-fever took the Anzar guides inside a year, and the last of the blood was a child of six. The wardens' custom for an unguided whale is to take the people off and give her to the sand, since she drifts from the herd within a season. The Guild refused. For nine years its adventurers kept an unbonded whale with the herd by hand, riding her hide, turning her with Stokkul, feeding her at every halt, and forty-one of them died doing it. The child, Tasa ult Anzar, took the bond at fifteen and gave up her seat among the houses in the same week, declaring herself and her heirs the Guild's. Her blood still guides Anzar, as a whale requires, but as sworn Guild members on a Guild wage, the bond-line, holding no seat and sitting no council. The whale went on. The house stopped.

The Standing Nobody Names

Valreka says every whale has a house, and Anzar has none, and the city holds both facts at once without strain.

A house's child who takes the Guild oath forswears their claim, and the roll on Anzar's deck lists four such children today, among them the Eskua's younger brother, entered in 2470 MR as Massin Eramun Anzar. The crown has offered the Sovereign a seat among the houses three times, in 2301, 2398 and 2503 MR, and three times the seat was refused on the same grounds: a house owes the crown, and a Godshall bound to Valreka could hold relations with no Duchy and no one. The city treats the offer as a courtesy and the refusal as one. Outsiders find the arrangement odd. Valrekans find it odd that anyone asks.

"My grandmother's grandmother was ult Anzar. I am Anzar on a wage-slip, and the whale does not read wage-slips."

Tilelli Anzar, chief guide of the bond-line, asked by a Duchies chronicler whether she counted as noble

The Desk and the Standing Roll

A settled Godshall's board fills from the street. Valreka's fills in bursts, whenever the herd draws near enough to a settlement for a season's work to come aboard at once, and then goes quiet for weeks while the city crosses empty dune. What the desk keeps between the bursts is the Standing Roll, its register of parties who have declared themselves available on short notice: names, ranks, what they will take, and which whale a rider will find them on. A party on the Roll waits nowhere; it is somewhere out on the whales living its life, and a rider will come. A crew that keeps its entry current can be raised, briefed and put on the sand inside a day.

The work comes from everywhere. The Guild Bank never notices the whales, since the enchanted ledgers synchronise across distance, so a contract posted at a Kingshall four domains off reaches this counter the same day it reaches anyone's. The Post is the harder half: within the herd the hall runs its own riders off Anzar's shoal, and beyond it pays the Outrider Corps to carry the sacks to the River Duchies' edge and the scattered desert settlements and meet the settled routes there. A Roll party guards a dig in Galdua Jendea one week and walks into a Duchies toll-quarrel the next, and when a posting says it will not keep, the Whalehall sends whoever is ready ahead of whoever is near.

"I was Mez-Tafsut on Caldwel, I am Tafsut Caldwel Anzar on the Roll, and my daughter's crew call me Tafsutghar when they think I cannot hear. I can hear. Now: your names, your rank, and which whale I send the rider to."

Tafsut Caldwel Anzar, clerk of the Whalehall desk, entering a new party on the Standing Roll

Who Serves

The Sovereign

A Guild Sovereign holds the seat, by long custom a Ghoran elder, on the reasoning that an office whose whole weight is precedent belongs to someone who was present for the precedent. Amestan Anzar holds it now, born on Caldwel, Sovereign since 2489 MR, and the one who refused the third offer.

The Bond-Line

Tasa's blood, sworn Guild members on a Guild wage, who guide the whale from the head. Tilelli Anzar is chief of the line, the sixth to hold the guiding, and the only guiding blood in the herd with no particle before her whale.

The Desk

Tafsut Caldwel Anzar keeps the counter and the Roll. She enters a party's names exactly as spelled, twice, and has read available on short notice off the register so often that it is simply what such a party is called.

The Forsworn

Four houses' children stand on Anzar's roll today, their claims set down at the oath. Massin Eramun Anzar, the Eskua's younger brother, is the one everyone names; the other three the hall treats as ordinary members, which is the point.
