Ancestries
The Peoples of Talan & the Lineages Walking Among Them
A chronicler's account of the peoples of the continent: what each is like to meet, where each is at home, and which heritages reach which veins.
Peoples of Talan
The continent holds many peoples, distributed across the thirteen domains by ancient pattern of culture, climate, and kinship. The Dragons of Sumendar are an alien race whose mothership crash-landed in the Gods' Era; all other peoples are native.
A feeling, not a uniform
An ancestry is a temperament: the feel of a people, met anywhere on Talan. How that temperament lives is the business of each land; the same people keeps different customs in every region that holds it, and a region's culture is kept by everyone who lives there.
Heritages
Lineages that carry something other than ordinary ancestry: divine blood (god in the veins), elemental sparks from the Prelife layer, Life-Layer planar lines (Wood & Metal, from the Feyworld and Shadowplane), mortal-ancestry mixes, and a small handful of other unique lines.
Lifespan
No mortal of Talan is known to have lived past two and a half centuries. This shapes politics, warfare, and theology across the continent: dynasties move quickly, scholars work in lineages, and no one mortal sees a century of history through their own eyes alone. Gods and the undead step outside this limit.

The Peoples of Talan

Every people of the continent, in one roster, each led by the feeling a traveller meets before any flag or custom. Where a people keeps a heartland, the entry names it; where one region holds the people's fullest expression, the entry names that too. The customs themselves belong to the places, and the places are written on their own pages.

AnadiChaos · Nashavel · the Basogur Jungle
The patient lattice.
Eight-eyed spiderfolk, web-weavers of the Basogur's chaos-tangled interior, whose slow and deliberate craft strings a thread of quiet order through the churn of the Chaos domain. Talan's most famous Anadi is Matron Charna, proprietor of the Spider's Silk Inn at Crossroads.
Androidrare across Talan
The last of an old craft.
Synthetic people created by the Elden, and the only living link to Elden craftsmanship the world has been able to study at all. Their numbers were never large and have been declining for millennia; very few remain. The Golden Empire's attempt to recreate them produced the Automatons instead.
AthamaruWater · Floteyn · the Floating Isles & Hafra coast
The pod passing through.
Seal-folk, aquatic by anatomy and amphibious by habit, whose pods range the spray-zone islets of the Water domain and the saltwater shorelines of Hafra. They have shared the islet life with the Tripkee long enough to have settled the boundaries.
Automatonuncommon · common in Eldara (Fire)
Made, and still here.
The Golden Empire's would-be Androids: capable, formidable, and a people in their own right. In Eldara, Komo's forge-city, they are a common sight, maintained by dwarves and kobolds whose technology improves each generation; everywhere else they remain a curiosity.
Awakened Animalrare everywhere
Each one its own story.
Animals lifted into sapience by druidcraft, by the flame-touched lands of Sumendar, or by Feyworld leakage at the edges of Nashavel. No homeland, no shared past; the Adventurers' Guild treats any who join a party as full members.
AzarketiTime · Lioaru · the southern coast
The past carried as daily labour.
Water-adapted descendants of the civilisation mortal myth calls the Old Race. Their folklore still claims Elden blood, a long-distorted memory. In Valreka, Tani's whale-borne city, an Azarketi community serves as the water-bearers, finding and giving the water a desert city cannot live without; their people's old wrong runs behind them, and the life of the goddess's city now runs through their hands.
CatfolkKnowledge · Ezkudon
The keeping eye.
Curious and precise; Enki himself is depicted as a black-haired catfolk. The scholar-caste and archive-keepers of the Knowledge domain, the patient ones who say not yet and mean it as mercy. In Thekkavar they are the Wisdom pole of the city's long argument with its own hunger.
Centaur · the EizhalunDarkness · Myrkono · the Shadow Steppes
The night-hunt.
Once a Hunt-domain people, the longest-ranging of the Villtur tribes; in the Dark Era they followed the corruption-beasts into the dark and the hunt never ended. They are the Eizhalun now, the open-ground watch of the steppe, riding the night between the Wardstones under Araphel, for whom darkness is the quiet and the cover. Hunt-craft survived the crossing; the Hunt-god endures among them as memory and method, not worship.
ConrasuChaos · Nashavel
Chaos given a body, wearing restraint.
Beings of chaos given form, and the thread of caution in a domain short on it. In Nahaskel, Vesuna's ever-remade city, the Conrasu are the steadying counterweight to the gnomish appetite for trying it differently this week.
DragonetEarth · Brauogi · the deep substrate
The deep, remembered.
Indigenous wyrmkin, miners and metal-wrights whose lineages root in the old substrate where stone gives way to metal; the breadbasket above ploughs, reaps, shoes, and shears on their work. Their wyrm-blood remembers an age before the bread, and their tradition holds Zaharsuge as progenitor; many reject the authority claim of Dragon's Reach on theological grounds. The Dragons are no kin of theirs.
DragonsFire · Sumendar · Dragon's Reach
Seeking what was taken.
An alien race; their mothership crash-landed on Talan deep in the Gods' Era. Dragon doctrine holds that the gods of that era used them and, in the using, stripped them of their immortality; modern Dragons are mortal, remember being otherwise only as record, and every Dragon holds an opinion on what purity would mean and whether to seek it. The argument between purists and pragmatists is the shape of their civilisation.
DwarfFire · Sumendar
The steady hand at the engine.
Two cultures of one people: the New Age Dwarves of the Order of Steam, lawful and inventive builders of airships and arcane artillery, and the Sentinel Dwarves of the upper Darklands, the most reliable mortal early-warning system against what climbs up from below.
ElfLaw · Zuzental
The long view.
Patience as temperament: an Elf weighs a choice against decades, gives trust the way a bank gives credit, and is still watching a plan unfold when the mortals who started it beside her are grandparents. One of the two ruling bloodlines of the Thousand Kingdom, and the side of any Zuzental polity that remembers why the rule was made.
FetchlingDarkness · Myrkono
Refuge, found.
Remnants of a civilisation that sought shelter in the region of Darkness and found it; the lineage stayed, and Myrria grew around it. Many serve Araphel directly, whose doctrine of new faces and second chances is the welcome their ancestors received.
Fleshwarpthe Blackened Lands
The wound's children.
Born of the cursed ground where the land itself has never healed: mismatched limbs, fused organs, no two alike. A second, darker population is fabricated far away in the Red Empire's Menagerie; same end-product, completely different origin, and most Talanese cannot tell them apart on sight.
GhoranTime · Lioaru
Memory, rooted.
Plant-rooted and long-lived, the Guardians of Time: each elder a living archive of centuries the rest of the world holds only in fragments. They are the majority and the heart of Valreka, the whale-borne city whose whole work is to remember.
GnomeChaos · Nashavel
Let us try it differently this week.
Feyworld-native, long since migrated into the Material Plane, and now the civic temperament of the Chaos domain: in Nahaskel, a city that remakes itself overnight and decides what is shared by the toss of a coin, the Gnomes are the engine.
GoblinFire · Sumendar
Appetite as method.
Komo's alchemists: the first question a Goblin asks of anything is whether it can be consumed, and if it cannot, whether heat, distillation, or the right combination can make it so. Their craft is everything consumable, brews and tinctures, poisons and intoxicants, acids and alchemical fire, and the volatile charge a Kobold will happily build a shell around. The Kobold works in machine, the Goblin works in matter, and the two delight in finishing each other's devices.
GolomaHunt · Ehizahar · the Lands of Villtur
The watchers.
Secretive, many-eyed forest-folk: the watch-hunters of the Villtur, the ambushers and lookouts of the tribal mix where the Orcs charge and the Lizardfolk stalk. Their hidden communities sit at the quiet edges of the hunt-country, and a Goloma may set an eye to watch a trail and circle wide while it keeps seeing.
HalflingCommerce · Lautara · fullest in Atarialda
The road in the blood.
Wanderlust as temperament: the call of the next market, the horizon as a standing invitation. It holds even for the ones who never leave; a Halfling who stays has chosen the one place where the world keeps arriving, and a Halfling who settles comes home full. Atarialda is the feeling at its fullest, the road carried out and brought home to the hearth; the rail-Halflings of the HRA run the same feeling down the lines without ever sitting down.
HobgoblinLaw · Zuzental · the Order of Law
The given word.
For a Hobgoblin the sworn word is load-bearing: slow to promise, immovable once sworn, still keeping what was agreed long after everyone else has moved on. The constancy casts its own shadow, the grip that does not know when to let go, and Hobgoblin elders name it before any outsider can. The oath-minded gather thickest in the Order of Law, where the Hobgoblin is the dominant ancestry of the institution itself.
HumanLaw · Zuzental
The short fire.
Life is now, so the move is now: risk-takers and cycle-burners, the ones who force the question, take the loss, and start over twice in a working life. The other ruling bloodline of the Thousand Kingdom, and the side of any Zuzental polity that notices the rule has stopped working. The Fenurran tribes of Ehizahar are human-stock with a deep cultural register of their own.
JotunbornWind · Vindul · Baerfrost
The ones who stand in it.
Giant-blooded folk of the arctic north, the largest stable Jotunborn population on Talan. The storm arrives and they remain: stoic, slow, weather met as a fact to be respected and outlasted, anger as rare and as total as a winter front. Many read weather and ice-routes for the saltkeel crews of the northern Hafra arc.
KashrishiWind · Vindul · the Air Monastery
The ones who watch it.
The observer's stillness inside the moving air: quiet, precise, inward where others are loud. What a Kashrishi believes is their own; what a Kashrishi has measured is everyone's. The Air Monastery and its Wyndwalken cartographer-order are the public-civic crown of the inclination.
KholoEarth · Brauogi
The vigil kept.
Brauogi's herder-clans, matriarchal by disposition: the night-watch over the herd against wolf and weather, and the duty to herd and clan-line that no Kholo sets down lightly. Meat, milk, and leather to the Minotaurs' grain and stone; a clan is reckoned by its matrons and its beasts.
KitsuneCommerce · Lautara · fullest in Emarrea
The chosen reveal.
Known as the Divine Envoys; Fisaya herself is depicted as a kitsune, and the affinity goes back as far as the chronicle record reaches. Presence as craft: charm, performance, and discretion in one skin; what a kitsune shows you is exactly what she means to show you, and what she keeps is hers. Whether that becomes the Heartcourt's theatre, a quiet harbour bar, a market-stall, a veiled book of odds, or the waking-record of a dream-touched coast is the place's business; the populations diverged so long ago that one tradition reading another sometimes recognises only the foxfire.
KoboldFire · Sumendar
Invention, unchained.
Genius inventors with no fear in them, source of half the most exciting and most disastrous devices in the Order of Steam. Komo himself is depicted as a kobold; in the Order the dwarves harness him, and in Eldara he works co-equal and unchained. No relation to the Dragons, whatever the silhouette suggests.
LeshyLight · Egulon · fullest in Lua Lasai
The ones who stay.
Plant-spirits and a full people, born and raising children and holding lineages like any other folk. Rootedness is the temperament: to stay, to tend, to remember. In the longest-cultivated land on the continent the Leshy are the part that remembers, who planted this windbreak, what stood in this field before the vines, what the soil took badly the last time someone tried it; their lineages keep to the same grove and vine-stock across generations of the wine country's sun.
LizardfolkHunt · Ehizahar · the Lands of Villtur
Patience in pursuit.
The most strategic of the Villtur hunters: slower to start a hunt, more patient in pursuit, more deliberate at the kill. Their tribal cultures run parallel to the Orcs' and feud along recognisable patterns.
MerfolkWater · Floteyn & the Hafra coasts
The saltwater claim.
Aquatic folk of Floteyn's waters and the Hafra coasts, senior in their own reckoning wherever the water is salt. A small freshwater pod lives in Midarra and keeps an informal alliance with the Twin Cities pirate council: reconnaissance below for legal cover above.
MinotaurEarth · Brauogi
Stone, endurance, burden.
Sarrum himself is depicted as a Minotaur, and Talan's Minotaurs are his cultural cousins: immovable, patient, slow to act and slower to forgive. The grain-farmers, stonecutters, and wall-builders of the breadbasket, who carry the load others set down and build as if the work were meant to outlast them, which it is.
NagajiTime · Lioaru
Shed, and renewed.
Serpent-folk, fervent in faith to the point of zeal. A serpent sheds its skin and comes out whole and new, and the Nagaji read their own molting as a living liturgy of the goddess who died and returned: the worn self left behind like a cast skin, the next self emerging clean. Other folk sometimes find their certainty off-putting; the Nagaji answer that the proof is in the shedding.
OrcHunt · Ehizahar · the Lands of Villtur
First to charge.
Tribal and strong; Hinka is depicted as a red-headed orc, and the affinity runs deep. Within the Villtur mix the Orcs are the loudest, the first to charge, and the most consistently in feud with their counterparts.
Poppetrare everywhere
Small made souls.
Small constructed beings; rare everywhere, with no domain concentration and no shared origin-story beyond the hands that made each one.
RatfolkKnowledge · Ezkudon
Fearless in the deep.
Originally of the Darklands, and at home underground as few peoples are. In Ezkudon that fearlessness became a vocation: Enki's diggers, the tunnel-crews and shelf-divers who open the way down into the sealed lower stacks the Shisk hunt through, finding a route where the architecture insists there is none. Away from the heartland the same adaptability makes them the quickest and most resourceful folk of every city on Talan.
ShiskKnowledge · Ezkudon
The hunger to know.
Hunters of the kind of lore other scholars consider dangerous or forbidden; shisk-curiosity is a standing warning given to junior novices. In Thekkavar they are the Discovery pole and the city's reach into the deep: the divers who push past their access toward the sealed shelves, the city's only true way down and its standing temptation.
ShoonyLight · Egulon · fullest in Argia Esfera
The ones who keep faith.
All dog-coded peoples fall under the Shoony name: loyal as the dogs they take after and many as the breeds, a dozen dog-kinds under one name, almost all of them of Iro's faith. Their devotion wears three faces, the warm welcome, the bright zeal, and the healer's hope; a Shoony is the one still there, at the door, at the altar, at the sickbed, when everyone else has gone home. In Argia Esfera they tend a great flame that has not gone dark in living memory.
Skeletonthe Blackened Lands
The unquiet, returned.
Where the wound in the land pulls the dead back into motion, some return aware. Common in the Blackened Lands and nearly unknown elsewhere; émigrés carry the politics of that origin with them, many kingdoms refuse them entry, and the Adventurers' Guild does not. Spiritually tied to Betibizi, the Minor God of Undeath.
SpriteLight · Egulon · fullest in Harro Distiratsue
The ones who shine.
Two populations, one people. The Material-born Sprites of Egulon are settled and generational, a world away from their flighty Feyworld cousins: pride of making, delight turned craft, brightness as self-expression, glamour worn openly and well. Where a Sprite lives there is more light than the sun strictly provides. Their fey-and-chaos lineage still sits uneasily against Iro's zealous worship, a question Egulon has never settled. The Feyworld stock remains across the veil, and the few who cross stay rare and go where they go.
StrixWind · Vindul · Fellibylur
The ones who ride it.
Winged sky-people with weather in the blood: restless in still air, most alive when the sky is moving, reading a front the way other folk read a face. Flight is the disposition, whatever the trade, and their storm-attunement makes them the deepest keepers of storm-lore on Talan; the Stormriders and the Thunderhost moot are Fellibylur's institutions grown around the gift.
SurkiDarkness · Myrkono & the upper Darklands
The deep watch.
Insectoid sapients of the deep places. Myrkono is the one domain that takes them in: Araphel's darkness is the quiet and the cover, and Myrria's second-chances doctrine extends to the compound-eyed the welcome other cities refuse. On the Shadow Steppes they are the deep-ground half of one watch with the Eizhalun above, set hardest against the Vermin Queen's swarm, insectoid and utterly uncorrupted.
TenguWind · Vindul · Haizetsua
The ones who answer it.
Voice as temperament: expressive, social, song where another people keeps silence, shaped by leaving and shaped again by coming home. The wind speaks, and a Tengu speaks back. Their island holds the singing cities of the Skarvorn Republic, and the Vornsketta sends every young Tengu out into the world for years before the island calls them home.
TripkeeWater · Floteyn · the Floating Isles
The builders among the waves.
Amphibious islet-folk, the most settled of the three water-peoples: the Athamaru swim past, the Merfolk pass underneath, the Tripkee build.
VanaraChaos · Nashavel · the Basogur Jungle
At home between two domains.
Jungle-dwellers of the Basogur, which straddles Nashavel and Ehizahar; one of the few cultures comfortable across two domains, navigating the politics around the jungle rather than belonging to either.
VishkanyaCommerce · Lautara · fullest in Itsasalda
The shaping hand.
Community is a thing you make, and making is how you belong: what a Vishkanya touches takes a form that holds, a household, a ledger, a fair argument, a thing of beauty meant to outlast its maker, and she stays to keep what she shaped. Jianna herself is depicted as a vishkanya. In Itsasalda the feeling stands at its fullest as the steady watch of the Vordsbench; in Merkavar it is the fine-artisan guilds; in Azkataria, the form-masters of the Open Floor.

Heritages

Some mortals are born carrying something more than their parents' blood: a god's touch in the veins, a spark from one of the elemental currents of Prelife, a strain of the Life-Layer's twin sibling planes, a parentage of two mortal peoples whose lines mix, or rarer origins still. Each lineage is visible, often striking, and met with its own reception. Some are blessed, some are feared, most are simply known and lived around. The lines below are grouped by where the touch comes from. Nephilim is the universal divine-blood (and fiendish-blood) lineage; see the dedicated callout below the divine table.

Nephilim · the universal lineage
Nephilim on Talan carry both celestial and fiendish marks. Celestial marks can manifest in Nephilim descended from any god, most commonly Iro (Light) but any of the bound thirteen, and sometimes from gods now withdrawn beyond the veil. Fiendish marks manifest in Nephilim descended from any Vice Demon or Virtue Devil. Two Nephilim from different parent lineages may share nothing beyond the divine touch itself: one glows faintly, another smells of brimstone, another casts a shadow with three more limbs than the body has. Reading the parent line takes a practised eye, and is a service Enki's scholars sometimes sell.

These lines carry the spark of the Elemental Plane itself. The Primordial Six who shaped Prelife include the four bound elemental gods, so each Elemental-touched mortal has a doctrinal kinship with their corresponding god; the bloodline, though, runs to the plane rather than to the deity. Suli stand outside the four pure elements, mixed in their spark and at home in none.

How the planar lines run
The Feyworld and Shadowplane are the sibling planes of the Life Layer, standing without a god-claimant; all thirteen of the bound gods reside on the Material Plane. Ardande carry the Feyworld's living-growth element; their lineage runs to the Feyworld itself, alongside the bound thirteen rather than through them. Vesuna is god of Chaos on the Material, with her domain in Nashavel; Ardande gather by cultural gravity wherever their kin are welcomed. Talos carry the Shadowplane's eternal-structure element. The plane is order, and Talos express that order rather than the surface-verbal overlap with darkness. Their lineage runs to the Shadowplane itself, alongside the bound thirteen; Talos communities form around craft and structure, gathering by trade.

Sortalde · The Six Petals

Six ancestries native to Sortalde (Tao Hua Yuan internally), the petal-archipelago continent east across the Cloud Sea.

On Talan, all six are rare. The crossing is hard, and most arrivals come through the standing Sortalde embassies on the Emerald Isles' Bridgelands, the cloudship landing point for Sortalde traffic. A Sortalde-ancestry character on Talan is a story, not a demographic.

PeopleSortalde homelandSeen on Talan

TanukiXidao (Theatre Island), outer petalItinerant performer-merchants; the largest standing population on Talan is around the embassies.
SarangayNiudao (Ox Island), outer petalUsually attached to embassy security details. Their oaths survive reincarnation cycles.
WayangYingdao (Shadow Island), inner petalVanishingly rare; those who cross are typically working, embedded spies or freelance information brokers.
YakshaLingdao (Spirit Island), inner petalFunctionally never. Bound to specific places; a Talan-side Yaksha is doctrinally an exile whose bond was broken.
SamsaranLundao (Wheel Island), inner petalVanishingly rare. A Talan-side Samsaran is almost always a retired chancellor or a heretic.
