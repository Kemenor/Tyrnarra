A city that never sleeps in the same place twice, a Guild desk that keeps your name on a list, and an outrider who knows where on the herd to find you.

Where You Stand

You are based in Valreka, Tani's god-city, which is carried across the dune-sea of Galdua Jendea on the backs of nineteen titanic sand-whales and is never in the same place two seasons running. Each noble house guides one whale and lives on its back; the crown is whoever guides Lenama, the mother whale, oldest of the herd, who has carried the city since the goddess came back. When the herd draws close the contraption-bridges go up and the whole city is one place. When it spreads to travel, every whale is its own island, and you are home or you are a guest somewhere else for two days.

Your work comes from the Whalehall, the Adventurers' Guild's Godshall in Valreka and the only one of the thirteen that travels. It rides Anzar, the Guild's own whale, apart from the herd at its leading edge: the first whale a stranger reaches and the last one the departing see. Say Whalehall on the decks and Valreka's Godshall on the paperwork; both are correct and both get used daily.

The Whale You Ride

Anzar is the one whale in the herd with a district and no noble house. A rising house took the Guild aboard as a paying tenant in the years after the Dark Era; in 2287 MR the house's guiding blood failed to a single child of six, and where custom says an unguided whale is given to the sand, the Guild kept her with the herd by hand for nine years, at the cost of forty-one of its own, until the child was old enough to take the bond. She took it, and gave the house to the Guild in the same week. Her blood still guides Anzar, as sworn Guild members called the bond-line, and the house itself simply stopped.

So the city says every whale has a house, and Anzar has none, and nobody in Valreka finds that worth arguing. The crown has offered the Sovereign a seat among the houses three times and been refused three times, since a Godshall bound to a city could deal with no one else. Nobles' children take the Guild oath and give up their claim to do it; the Eskua's own younger brother is on Anzar's roll. The Sovereign is Amestan Anzar, a Ghoran elder who has held the seat since 2489 MR and refused the third offer himself. The clerks call him Amestanghar to his face.

Your Name on the Roll

A Valrekan name is a given name, then the whale you were born to, then, if you choose it, the whale you live on; the third name is a declaration of belonging, and people read it as one. Only the guiding blood put u (a son) or ult (a daughter) before their whale. Children carry Mez- in front of the given name until they come of age; elders acquire -ghar at the end of it when the young start using it for them, and eventually write it themselves. Taking the Guild oath adds Anzar to your name, always. Dropping your birth-whale is your own call. A party from elsewhere keeps whatever names it came with, and the desk will enter them exactly as spelled, twice, to be sure.

Reading These Pages

What is actually waiting at the far end of a contract stays where it belongs, which is at the table.

How the Work Reaches You

A settled Guild board fills from the street. Valreka's fills in bursts. When the herd draws near enough to a settlement, a season's worth of postings comes aboard at once; then the city crosses empty dune for weeks and the wall goes quiet. Waiting at the hall for something to appear is a way to waste a month.

So the desk keeps a register instead: the Standing Roll, the list of parties who have declared themselves available on short notice. An entry gives your names, your rank, what you will take, and where on the herd an outrider can find you. You do not sit in the hall. You are out on the whales living your life, and when something lands that will not keep, a rider comes across the sand for you. A crew that keeps its entry current can be raised, briefed, and put on the sand inside a day.

The clerks have read that phrase off the register so many times that it is simply what such a party is called. It is where your name came from, and the joke is entirely at the Guild's expense.

The work itself comes from everywhere. The Guild Bank's enchanted ledgers do not notice the whales at all, so a contract posted at a Kingshall four domains away reaches Valreka's counter the same day it reaches anyone's. The Guild Post is the slower half. Within the herd the hall runs its own riders off Anzar's Stokkul shoal; beyond it, a letter addressed to a roaming city goes to wherever the herd happens to be, which is a thing nobody outside Galdua Jendea knows, so the hall pays the Outrider Corps to run its sacks out to the River Duchies' edge and the scattered desert settlements and meet the settled routes there.

The upshot is that a Roll party guards a dig in the dune-sea one week and walks into a River Duchies toll-quarrel the next, and sometimes boards a ship for somewhere it has never heard of because the posting said it would not keep. The Whalehall sends whoever is ready ahead of whoever is near. It is the one Godshall on Talan where distance to the work is the second question the desk asks.
