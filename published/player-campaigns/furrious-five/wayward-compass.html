The Wayward Compass
Adventurers' Guild Office
A respectable face from the bridge; from the river, a building that wanted to be a boat and couldn't commit. The work hangs by the door.

The Quest Board

The board covers the whole right wall of reception, contracts pinned edge to edge, a few of them yellowed enough to have become part of the architecture. Mira Seld updates it every morning before she opens the door. Three postings are worth your time right now. Tap one to read the full notice and what the Compass can tell you across the desk.

The Posting · in the client's hand
"Missing: one consignment of goods, offloaded via private dock, bridge district, three nights past. Also missing: four-person retrieval team sent to locate same. Contract offers 250gp upon recovery of personnel, 100gp additional upon recovery of cargo. Enquire with Mira Seld for access details. Discretion absolute. — S.R."
What the Compass Will Tell You

Three nights ago a private cargo came ashore at one of the bridge district's unlisted docks, the kind of landing nobody asks about. It did not turn up where it was meant to. The client sent a four-person team after it: experienced people, known to the office, the sort who check in. They went down into the old passages under the Cape-side bank, and the office has not heard from them in two days.

The passages are old, older than Millhaven, and no one keeps a clean map of them. Mira holds the access route and a description of the missing team. The client wants the people back first and the cargo back second, and will not say what the cargo is. "Sensitive documents," is all you'll get, and then the subject changes.

Neutral charter applies."
What the Compass Will Tell You

You travel to Merkavar on the Guild's coin, board a sealed consignment, and ride it home through the hill country to deliver it whole. Simple work, except that the last two crews to try it were stopped and stripped on the same stretch of line, at a cutting the trainmen call the Arrol Cut. Whoever is hitting the shipments knows when they run and what they carry.

Aldric Fenn briefs it flat and asks nothing about the manifest, which is the whole reason the job came to a neutral-chartered office in a bridge-town instead of being hired in Merkavar. The Compass vouches that the contract is real and the coin is good. It does not vouch the cargo, and Fenn expects you not to ask about it either.

The Posting · Quest board
"Lord Caevan Veldtmark, merchant and landowner of the Cape district, has failed to respond to correspondence or attend any scheduled appointments for the past three weeks. His business partner, one Harlen Doss, requests a welfare check and, if the lord is well, collection of a debt of record. Inquire at the Wayward Compass for details. Discretion expected."
What the Compass Will Tell You

A Cape-side merchant lord has gone quiet: no answered letters, no kept appointments, three weeks of silence. His business partner Harlen Doss, who is owed a debt of record, wants someone to confirm the man is alive and, if he is, to collect. The pay is modest and the work reads as a knock-on-a-door errand. Kael posted it rated social, low risk.

Kael rated it before he thought about it too hard, and he has not changed the rating. He did mention to Mira that something about the posting sits wrong with him, and Mira did not argue. Neither of them said so on the notice. The estate is on the Cape side's northern edge, where the drift sits heavier in the air.

Take what suits the party. Mira logs who took what and when it is due; the office likes to know its people came back.

The Guildhall

The Wayward Compass did not start as a guildhall. It started as a storage shed a party of adventurers rented by the week, could not stop renting because it was convenient, and quietly bought when the owner died without heirs. It has grown the way the bridge district itself has grown: upward first, then outward, then down onto pilings that were only ever meant to hold a practice-post. The original shed is somewhere inside the walls of the second floor; nobody is entirely sure which walls.

From the bridge street it presents a respectable face: a wide door, the gold compass-rose sign gone slightly faded, Mira's desk visible through the front window. From the river it looks like something that decided it wanted to be a boat and could not commit. The sound of the Smáa is constant, louder the lower you go, and the regulars stopped hearing it years ago.

What Makes It Feel Like Home

Trophy cases line the stairs to the drinking hall: a basilisk scale, a piece of what might be Elden stonework, a handful of weapons of unusual make, each under a small plaque listing the one who brought it back and the year it was recovered. The plaques record when. They do not always record what became of the person who recovered it, and the regulars know which ones to avoid reading. It is, all the same, the warmest room on the bridge, and the place a new arrival is handed a pint they did not ask for and assessed over it.

The Staff

A small, settled cast you'll meet in a fixed order: the desk, then the bar, then whoever is running the floor that morning.

Guildmaster · Bridge Council Seat
Aldric Fenn
Human, late fifties · Millhaven-born

A bear of a man running a small-town office for the most powerful organisation in the known world, and acutely aware of both halves of that sentence. Direct, pragmatic, occasionally volcanic; he prefers to solve a problem with a handshake and keeps a shortsword on the desk for symbolic reasons. He treats the branch like family and tucks his guildmaster's medallion under his collar when he meets locals.

Branch Receptionist
Mira Seld
Vishkanya, mid-thirties

The first face every adventurer sees, and the last one they want to disappoint. She runs the front desk with terrifying competence and a slit-pupil smile that never quite reaches her eyes, and remembers every name, every face, and every outstanding fee. Ink-stained fingers, three quills behind one ear, fine scales beaded at the hairline and throat, a navy-and-gold uniform ironed that morning and to be ironed again tomorrow.

Quartermaster & Archivist
Velka Dorn
Shisk (lapsed cleric of Enki), forties

She manages the equipment locker and six centuries of contract archive with a precision that feels almost aggressive. Dry, with flashes of dark humour that arrive without warning; she will lend equipment to a party she trusts and will not lend her good ink to anyone. A silver-pale quill-ridge gone early, reading-lenses she refuses to admit she needs, a ring of keys too large for the building's known doors.

Field Trainer & Combat Coordinator
Kael Orvaine
Orc, late thirties

He runs orientation for new recruits, posts the difficulty ratings on the board, and is responsible for the sign that reads DEAD MEMBERS COMPLETE NO QUESTS. Measured and economical with words; he will give you exactly the advice you need to not die, once, and will pull a contract from a group he thinks is not ready and tell them precisely why. Open sparring at dawn, anyone welcome.

Guildhall Barkeeper
Dova Crestmark
Itsasaldan kitsune, ageless

She runs the bar down on the pilings with iron authority and an endless supply of something she calls the good stuff. Blunt, generous in unexpected moments, and possessed of an unsettling knack for knowing what you need before you say it. An anthro fox gone silver-frosted at the muzzle and ear-tips, burn-scarred forearms she does not explain, and a brown bottle of house brew under the bar for the people she is quietly worried about.

Server & Odd-Jobs
Pip Sallow
Halfling, early twenties · Millhaven foundling

The fastest-moving thing in the guildhall, somehow always where he is needed and almost never where he is not. Relentlessly cheerful, desperate to be an adventurer, and being gently protected from that ambition by everyone who likes him. He has memorised everyone's usual order, draws maps of the adventures he means to have someday, and is followed everywhere by the guildhall cats, all of them.

The regulars will tell you, after a pint or two, that the original shed is still in there, walled up somewhere inside the second floor, and that it still holds something the three adventurers who founded the hall brought back and never sold. The stories differ on what: a map to a place that is not on any other map, a weapon nobody living can lift, a sealed thing the founders agreed was better kept than carried. They agree on one point. If you go looking for it, Aldric will scowl, the conversation will be over, and somehow you will find yourself back out on the bridge with your pint finished and no memory of standing up.
