1 · The Morning After
Kesti DowndraftPell out of the Spar
Read Aloud · draft

The Jigger hangs warm and high, and nobody on either city seems to have slept. Kesti Downdraft counts the coin into your hands herself and tells you which airship leaves in three days.

Down on the Lower Spar, Pell is already telling it, and you hear your own names going round the room.

What happens

The party's pay is Kesti's: coin and passage off the Twin Cities. It carries no mark. The three days are the window for the rest. How the tellers tell the holds depends on where the sink clock stopped and who was saved at the hanging fleet.

2 · Nirfel's Night Whisper
Nirfel
Read Aloud · draft

It comes when you are nearly asleep, in a voice like coins sliding through fingers. You fought the dead for it. You saved their city. They will split it among people who never lifted a hand.

It is not anyone else's. It is yours by right. Go now, while the cabin is empty. Take what you earned.

What happens

A real choice at the table. Whoever goes and takes from the hoard by night carries the Greed-mark and has stolen from the crew that hired them, which follows them into the share-out.

3 · The Share-out
Merrin out of the Jigger
Read Aloud · draft

The keepers have made the Unspent safe and set out the hoard on her top deck: chests opened at last, gold and stranger things spilling into the lamplight. Merrin stands over it like a woman at her own wedding.

"Put it to the hands," she says, and the hands go up, and then she turns to you. "You fought for it. Take a share."

What happens

Every keeper who takes is marked. An Occultism or Religion check shows the mark for what it is, so sharp players choose with open eyes. The hoard is the temptation; the party's pay is already in their pockets.

4 · Ninehulls
NinehullsHalfpenny
Read Aloud · draft

Boots on the gangway. Ninehulls comes aboard with Halfpenny at his shoulder and a dozen of his crew behind, smiling the same wide smile he smiled at sea. "A dead Lord's prize," he says, "belongs to whoever can hold it. Treasure's mine, kids."

Then he looks at you, and the smile gets wider. "And you're the ones they're telling. Let's give them something to tell."

The fight

He claims the hoard by the custom the spires run on and calls the party out by name. Behind the screen: the tellers have been telling the party bigger than him, and a forgotten Lord stops being one.

The arena: the ballast tilts the deck (Interact + Athletics; everyone standing makes Reflex or Acrobatics or slides); surviving swivel guns can be re-aimed; the kill-gangway, if still rigged; cutting a line tilts the whole ship. What the party wrecked or left when they first boarded is what they have now.

The Man Who Cannot Die: a reaction the first time he reaches 0 HP: he stays up and heals substantially, once per fight.

If he wins: he takes the hoard and leaves them alive. No told name; he laughs.

5 · The Last Hands
HalfpennyPell out of the Spar
Read Aloud · draft

Ninehulls lies on the deck where he fell, and his crew stand over him with their hands at their sides. Nobody looks at you.

Then the hands go up, all of them. "Deposition," says Halfpenny. Down below, a teller is already climbing the stack.

What happens

Always deposition: the fight was fair and seen. It sets the party free. Pell tells the fight to the room; the table picks the party's told name from the deed the room loved most.

Coda · they leave
6 · The Round
Ninehulls
Read Aloud · draft

Three nights later, the night before you sail, the tavern goes quiet. Ninehulls walks in, a little pale and entirely alive, and leans on the bar.

"A round," he says, "for the crew that killed me fair." He raises his cup to you. Up on the stack, his flag is flying again.

What happens

The being lives by the story, and the story now says this crew beat him fair; so he honours it. Anyone carrying the mark has a thread: the Guild knows a ritual that burns it off. The party leaves on Kesti's airship.
