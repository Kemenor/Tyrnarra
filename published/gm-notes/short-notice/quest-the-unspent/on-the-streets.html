On the Streets
What the party runs into between places, in Freeboard and on Skysail.
Freeboard · d12Skysail · d10
Draft · Claude

Roll when the party moves between places or lets an hour go by: about one roll per hour on the town, or whenever the table needs a push. Cross an entry off once it has happened and reroll a repeat. Most entries are scenes to play, a few carry a check. Skysail's table only opens after the Mooring.

d12 · the promenades
Freeboard
1The leaking roofa deck votes
Read Aloud · draft

The promenade ahead is jammed. Forty-odd people stand shoulder to shoulder under a sagging stretch of deck, water dripping on them from above, while a man in a good coat on an upturned bucket explains why the roof has not been fixed. Nobody is listening to him.

"Hands," somebody shouts, "for putting him out." Hands go up all down the promenade, more than you can count at a glance.

Running it: a deck putting out its captain, the way the articles work. Outsiders have no hand to raise, and the crew say so cheerfully. The old captain buys the deck a drink, the new one goes to look at the roof.

If the party asks: "Deposition or murder?" someone jokes, and the whole deck laughs.

2The spire darehalfers
Read Aloud · draft

A knot of youngsters is daring each other at the foot of a ladder that climbs and climbs toward the very top of the stack. One of them, a gap-toothed girl of maybe twelve, spots you. "Bet you can't touch a spire. Bet you'd fall off."

Check: Athletics DC 25 to climb the outside of the stack and slap a spire before its crew chase you off; Acrobatics DC 25 to get back down on the run. On a success the halfers tell it all over the Spar by evening; on a critical success, it was Ninehulls' spire and his crew laugh rather than chase.

On a failure: the spire crew catch them halfway up and send them down with a cuff and a warning, to the halfers' delight.

3Your own story, told wrongNinehulls' crew
Read Aloud · draft

Outside a tavern, three of Ninehulls' crew are telling the capture to anyone who will stand still. In this telling, the Goodmeasure's guards fainted when the broadside hit.

"Dropped like sacks," says the loudest. "Ninehulls had to step over them."

Check: Diplomacy, Intimidation or Performance DC 25 to set the story straight. On a success the tellers add the party, grudgingly. On a critical success the party end up the best part of it, and it is that version the Spar repeats. On a critical failure the story gets worse.

4Light fingersa pickpocket
Read Aloud · draft

In the crush of the Lower Spar a boy brushes past you, says sorry very nicely, and is gone into the crowd.

Check: each player's Perception DC 23 (the boy's Thievery). On a failure that player's purse is lighter by 20 gp. Whoever succeeds catches his wrist: Bertil out of the Kittiwake, eleven, freckled, unrepentant. Let go, he offers to show them the quick ways round the stack, and he means it; he knows every ladder that used to be a mast.

5The bilge hatchMerfolk trade
Read Aloud · draft

Down on one of the lowest promenades, a square hatch stands open in the floor on black water. A cook is lowering a basket of bread into it on a rope. The basket comes back up full of silver fish, and a sleek, dark head rises after it to look at you.

Running it: the Merfolk pods trade with the city through hatches like this, fish up, everything else down. The young one at the hatch wants something from the dry world, anything at all, and will trade a story of the drowned decks for it.

What she tells: the oldest hulls are under the water now, and the pods live in them; a hull the dry folk give up to the sea becomes theirs.

6A bought namea teller and a captain
Read Aloud · draft

A big man in a red-and-gold coat is pressing coins into a teller's hand on the corner, loudly enough for the whole promenade. "Gale," he keeps saying. "Captain Gale. Tell it properly this time."

The teller catches your eye over his shoulder and rolls hers.

Running it: a captain buying his own name, the thing the Spar despises most. He wants the party to vouch for a raid they never saw. If they do, the Spar hears that too, and it costs them with Pell.

Why it matters: it is Redsail all over again, in front of them, and the players will recognise it.

7The city movesthe engines
Read Aloud · draft

Somewhere deep in the stack, something enormous starts up with a groan you feel in your teeth. The deck under you shifts, then shifts again, and every lantern on the promenade swings the same way. A pile of melons rolls past you, chased by its owner.

Check: Acrobatics or Reflex DC 20 or fall prone among the melons. The engines are turning the city a few degrees onto a new heading; it lasts a minute.

If they ask: the Lord of Freeboard sets the course, and the stack goes where she says, at a crawl.

8The Lord of FreeboardOdile Brasshand
Read Aloud · draft

A square, weathered woman with iron-grey hair cropped close comes down the promenade with two clerks trotting behind her. People make way for her without being asked. Her left hand is brass, and it clicks when she points it at you.

"You were on the Goodmeasure. Whose colours was she flying?" She does not wait. "Nobody's. Then she was nobody's, and now she's a deck."

Running it: Odile Brasshand, Lord of Freeboard, on her rounds. She wants one pass that every Lord honours, so ships like the Goodmeasure would sail safe, and the Lords will not have it. She will talk colours with anyone who listens, briefly, and then she is off.

9Hot nutsgossip
Read Aloud · draft

At a corner of the Spar a woman roasts nuts on a brazier made from a ship's bell turned upside down. She hands you a paper twist of them before you have asked, and holds out her other hand for the coin.

Running it: the best gossip on the deck. She knows the sky is due home any day (the Mooring), which Lord's crew is drinking where, and that the keepers on the Jigger have a new captain who has been asking about old work.

10Hands wanteda recruiter
Read Aloud · draft

A woman with a ledger and a pistol in her sash stops you. "You've got the look. Hands wanted for a run north. A full share each for anyone who's held a blade before, half for the rest. Sign here."

Running it: a captain's quartermaster hiring for a raid. She will not take no for an answer the first time, and takes it fine the second. If they ask what the run is, she smiles and says "Profitable."

11Deposition or murdera brawl
Read Aloud · draft

A tavern door bursts open and two crews come out of it in a tangle, still arguing. "Deposition!" bellows one side. "Murder!" howls the other. At the centre of it, as far as you can tell, is a dented hat.

Running it: a fight for fun, fists only. Anyone who wades in and holds their own is invited, by both sides, to the Wash. Intimidation DC 23 or Diplomacy DC 25 to stop it; on a critical success both crews buy the party a drink.

12A toast for the deada wake
Read Aloud · draft

A long table has been dragged out across the promenade, and everyone at it is standing with a cup raised. "To the Saltjaw," says an old woman at the head, "forty years dead, and still told." Then she sees you. "Well? Get a cup."

Running it: a toast to a dead Lord whose story is still told, an ordinary evening on the Spar. They tell the party the Saltjaw's story for the price of one of their own.

d10 · the islets, once moored
Skysail
1A coat for the JiggerKesti Downdraft
Read Aloud · draft

A small skiff swings in over the edge of the square, still moving, and a lean grey Tengu drops off it before it has stopped, landing light among the tables. She looks you over once, quickly, the way a captain looks at weather.

"You're the guards off Ninehulls' prize." She holds out a fleece-lined coat she was carrying. "If you're going near the Jigger, you'll want one of these. The Jigger's the cold one." Then she is off across the square.

Running it: Kesti Downdraft, Lord of Skysail and a Pirate Lord besides, met before Merrin ever names her. She hands over the useful thing first and asks nothing. If they follow, she answers one question, briskly, and goes back to her work.

2Jumping the gaphalfers
Read Aloud · draft

Where two islets swing close, a crowd of youngsters is lined up at the edge, taking a run at the gap between them. One makes it. The next one jumps short.

Check: Reflex or Athletics DC 25 to catch the falling halfer by the wrist. On a failure a skiff swoops under and catches him anyway, and the crowd boos the skiff for spoiling it. The halfers adore whoever made the catch for the rest of their stay.

3Back from the cloudsa sky crew
Read Aloud · draft

An airship comes in low and fast out of a bank of cloud, every hand on her deck soaked to the skin and laughing. Water beads on her rails and drips off her lines onto the tables below, and nobody at the tables minds.

Running it: sky crews dive their ships into clouds for the feel of it. They will take anyone brave enough along on the next dive, for the price of a round.

4The keeper on the linesa keeper
Read Aloud · draft

Under the edge of the Jigger, a young man in patched canvas is splicing a line, working with his back to a dark airship that hangs close by on lines of her own. He does not turn round once.

Running it: one of Merrin's keepers. He mends the Unspent's lines and will not look at her. Asked why: "Rule's the rule. Mend her, don't board her." If they mention Merrin, he goes quiet, then: "She'll open her. She promised."

5A change of windthe city hums
Read Aloud · draft

The wind turns, and the whole city changes its note. The hum that has been all around you drops, wavers and settles into a new chord, and all along the square people get up and move their tables, as if a clock had struck.

Running it: Skysail keeps the sun's hours and moves with the wind; the open windows sing it. The Tuning House on the Fore knows every chord, and a new wind brings new trade.

6An apple from the skya fruit seller
Read Aloud · draft

A girl with a basket of red apples on her hip is working the tables. "Grown up top," she says, holding one out. "There's folk below who'd give a week's wage for one of these."

Running it: Skysail grows fruit and greens on its islets; an apple from the sky is a gift in Freeboard. Two apples bought here make a good gift for anyone the party meets below.

7Cold under the Jiggergrumbling
Read Aloud · draft

Two sailors in thick coats are arguing at a table, breath steaming. "It's not the height. The Main's as high and it's warm as toast." "Then it's the Jigger. It's always the Jigger."

Running it: everyone on Skysail knows the Jigger is the cold one and carries the most airships on its lines. Nobody can say why. A hint the players can carry into Part III.

8Four notesOld Hobb
Read Aloud · draft

In a passage cut into the rock, an old grey Tengu is touching a row of brass plates set into the wall, three fingers on each, and humming four notes to every one. He does not stop when you come by.

Running it: Old Hobb out of the Jigger, tending the old work the way his mother showed him, before the party need him. Asked what the plates are for: "Keeping her up." Asked how: "Like this." And he hums.

9The skiff racea race
Read Aloud · draft

A horn blows, and half a dozen skiffs leap off the edge of the Main at once and go racing round the islets, banking hard under the Fore. The crowd along the edge is shouting odds.

Running it: bet if they like, at even money; a party member with Sailing Lore (sky) can pick the winner on a DC 23 check. A pilot short of crew may offer someone a seat for the next race (Acrobatics DC 25 to stay aboard).

10A hammock over the dropa sleeper
Read Aloud · draft

Out over the edge, a man is asleep in a hammock slung between two posts, nothing under him but air. One of the ropes holding it has frayed almost through.

Check: Athletics DC 23 to haul the hammock in before the rope parts, or a quick idea. On a failure he wakes as it goes, and grabs a post. Either way he is not grateful: "I was sleeping."
