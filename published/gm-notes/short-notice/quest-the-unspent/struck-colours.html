Calm, then fight · the opening
1 · The Goodmeasure
Ship: the Goodmeasure, a small luxury stillship of the Solkai crossingMaster Ozhrathe crewRedsail
Beat 1 · Where you are, and why
Read Aloud

The posting on the Guild board in Merkavar had been boring. Boring but profitable. A merchant, some precious cargo and a need for some weapons, ideally attached to people that knew how to use them.

A simple guarding job: make sure that the cargo reaches Solkai, preferably in full.

You took it, the clerk stamped it and half a day later you were on a ship. Drifting across the lake at Merkavar watching Helgafjall shrink behind you. Not literally, or so you hoped.

Down the river you went and then the ship was dumped into the Midarra.

And you'd been here ever since, moving for nine days, still Midarra. The most exciting things were other ships that were drifting by close enough that the next round of I spy at least had something to go on other than blue.

Maybe it was time again. The time of day to open the crate for the 17th time and check that indeed yes, the Lautaran silk had stayed Lautaran silk.

Maybe it was time to get Master Ozhra to open one of his precious bottles of Catjomin sake, 4pm was a reasonable time after all.

Maybe it was finally time to break into the locked cabin and see the dark and twisted objects of secrecy Master Ozhra had kept for himself. Because surely he must be a cult worshiper and hidden behind there must be a corrupted crystal turning the minds of mortals into mush. Surely he hired you so you could serve as his next victims.

After all the alternative was a worse fate. You were just normal guards stuck on a merchant ship.

Why they are aboard

The party are the ship's hired muscle, taken on through the Guild at Merkavar on short notice. A crew of their level is not cheap; the cargo is worth it. Ozhra carries luxury goods north to Solkai: Eldaran almosts, Emarrean sake, Lautaran silk.

He bought no Lord's colours for the crossing, on purpose. Colours are worth only as much as a Lord's name, and the raid the tellers love best is the one that breaks another Lord's colours; so on this run Ozhra trusts steel over pennants. He is in the middle of the crossing, in water the Lords share out, flying no one's colours, and that is why the ship is fair prize.

The advance

The Guild contract pays the party 2,000 gp for the crossing, half in advance at the Guild in Merkavar and half on delivery in Solkai. They start the quest with Ozhra's advance, 1,000 gp in one purse, stamped and receipted by the Guild clerk. That is a little under a quarter of a level-11 party's currency for the level (Party Treasure by Level: 4,200 gp for six), and it is what they have to spend in Freeboard.

The other half was for delivery, and the cargo never reaches Solkai, so it is never owed. The advance is all they get from the job.

An eight-stripe Catjomin Sake (Uraharagiri, Mist of the Hidden Meadow): a Hidden Treasure from a kitsune sacred vault, opened only by ancestral rite. It should never have left Emarrea. Anyone who knows Catjomin recognises the tier at a glance, and the stripes are never spoken aloud.

Temple-stamped dream-blends from the Dreaming Cape: genuine tier-four brews, the Twin Lantern's mark on every flask, made to promote vivid restful dreaming. A stamped blend is only to be drunk under a chartered inn's roof; carrying it away breaks the temple's licence. Ozhra bought a case from an inn-keeper who said yes to an outside merchant, the Cape's own sore point, and Solkai's rich will pay well for a real Cape dream at home. A Religion or Society check (or anyone from Lautara) knows the flasks should never have left the inn.

Either one would ruin Ozhra if found. They are also exactly the kind of prize a Pirate Lord's crew would love to tell about.

Beat 3 · Ozhra's question
Read Aloud

While you talk, Master Ozhra walks the deck: up to the bow, back to the wheel, up to the bow again, looking at the horizon and not at the sails. At last he drifts over to you, too casual.

"You'd know a Lord's ship, would you? At a distance, I mean. Say, a mile off. Before it was close enough to matter."

If they ask why: "No reason. Good eyes are good to have. That's all."

Then the thanks, too warm and too long: he takes the nearest hand in both of his and holds on to it. "Thank you. Truly. I chose well with you, I knew it at the Guild, and I'll tell the clerk so myself when we're home. Have you eaten? Hangas will feed you. Go on, go on."

Then he is off again, bow to wheel. His claws click on the planks, quick and even. At the bow he stops, ears forward, and stares out over the water far longer than there is anything to see. On the way back he looks at the locked cabin door and away again. At the wheel he turns his back on the sea, and his hand finds the key on its chain at his throat.

≤22 Critical Failure: You are sure you have it: he is testing you, checking that the guards he paid the Guild for are worth the money, and he seems pleased with the answer. (False: he is afraid, and hiding why.)

Beat 4 · The lookout calls
Read Aloud

"Sail!" Patala's voice comes down from the nest, high and certain. "Sail off the port bow!"

A lean two-master is coming out of the glare, flying a trader's pennant, the kind half the ships on the crossing fly. You hear a breath escape Ozhra and see his shoulders drop, before they seem to pull up again. Then the pennant comes down, hand over hand, and a flag runs up in its place: a black skull on blood red. The two-master leans into the wind and comes straight for you, and as she closes you can see her deck: crowded rail to rail, far more hands than a ship that size needs to sail her.

"Pirates," says Tarrek, quite calmly, and starts herding the crew toward the hatch. "Below. All of you. That's what we pay them for." Ozhra goes down the hatch last of all, and on the top rung he stops and looks aft, at the locked cabin door, before he ducks below. On the other ship, you see someone swinging a grapnel, then a shot whisks past your face.

The fight

Cold start into a hot opening. Redsail's crew is unflagged and poaching; the Goodmeasure flies no colours, so she is anyone's prize. The crew shelter below; the party fights alone, which is what they were hired for. Run the fight until the party is clearly winning, then cut to the broadside. Keep Redsail alive for it.

Running the fight

Round 1: grapnels and a boarding plank at the port rail. The two gun crews stay on their own deck and open with Pistol Salvo across the gap, one after the other; both boarder troops and the mate come over the plank, and the mate howls first. The knife comes over the bow on a line and goes for whoever the boarders have knocked prone or flanked.

Then: the boarders Rock the Boat to put the party on the deck and use Repel Boarders! to shove them toward the rail; anyone who goes over is in the Midarra, and Redsail answers with Sink or Swim. Redsail stays on his own quarterdeck and casts: blade barrier across the rail, bane, downpour.

The pressure: four troops are more bodies than the Goodmeasure's deck can hold. Fill the port rail and the main deck with them, keep the gun crews firing from the other ship once their salvos are spent (they board after), and let the party see Redsail's ship still has more on it than they have.

The cut: after two or three rounds, or as soon as the party is clearly holding (a troop broken, a strong one down, or Redsail himself in danger), Ninehulls' broadside lands. If it goes badly for the party, it lands sooner. The troops' weakness to area damage is what makes cannon shred them; Redsail survives it, for Ninehulls.

The bought name: Redsail calls his own name like a battle-cry; his crew do not echo it. A Society check (DC 25, the Twin Cities' tellers' trade) or anyone who has heard Freeboard's taverns knows nobody tells of Redsail.

Social · the Pirate Lord
2 · Ninehulls' Broadside
NinehullsHalfpennyRedsail
Read Aloud

Something cracks the sky open, so loud you feel it in your teeth. For a heartbeat the world is only ringing.

Then the thunder comes again, and again, and again: a rolling line of cracks, one on the heels of the next, and you feel every one of them in the wood under your feet. In the next breath the poachers' rail has stopped existing, along with some of its crew.

Out of the haze to starboard come two black-hulled ships, flying the same flag: black, with a red nine painted on it, a skull grinning out of the loop of the nine and bleeding down into red water.

Then a third comes out of the smoke behind them, and the other two look like her boats. She is three-masted and broad as a warehouse, black canvas stacked up into the sky, two decks of gunports open down her side with the smoke still pouring out of them. Her hull is a patchwork: planks of other ships worked into the black, a strip of green paint here, a run of gilt scrollwork there, half of a merchant's painted name upside down along her waterline. From her mainmast flies the nine, so big the wind can barely lift it. Her rails and her rigging are packed with crew, and they are cheering.

Before the smoke clears a figure the size of a wardrobe swings across on a line and lands on your deck like a dropped anchor, and the planks groan under him. He straightens up, a head above anyone aboard, and looks the poachers over with real disappointment. "Now what," he asks, "are you doing in my waters?"

Redsail answers for them. "Redsail!" he roars, and charges, rapier out. "You've heard of Redsail!"

The big man lets the point skid off the front of his coat. One hand closes on Redsail's throat and lifts him off the deck; the other brings the cutlass up under his ribs and out between his shoulders. Redsail kicks, a child struggling against a mountain. A gurgle, his clothes darken to the colour of his flag. Ninehulls holds him up until he stops, then lets him fall in a heap at his boots and wipes the blade on his sleeve. "Red-What?"

What happens

Over in a breath: a nobody against a legend. The rest of the scene is the surrender, in three beats.

Beat 2 · Weapons down
Read Aloud

For a moment nobody on deck moves. Then a cutlass hits the planks, and another, and the poachers' weapons go down one after another until the deck rings with them. Somebody's crossbow goes off into the sail as it lands. Nobody laughs.

The big man looks at the pile, then at you, and his whole face changes: the eyes crinkle, and the smile comes out with every tooth in it.

"My apologies. Truly." His voice sounds sincere. "You were having a nice quiet crossing, and this lot came and spoiled it. In MY waters, no less. Which makes me responsible for it, or something close to it." He claps his hands once. "Ninehulls. You'll have heard."

Beat 3 · Redsail's ship goes down

Read once the players have had their moment with Ninehulls, before it drags on.

Read Aloud

The high-pitched groan of wood cuts off every word. Ropes snap, one after another, and the sound pulls every eye toward it. The mainmast of Redsail's Reckoning rips down, taking more of the ship with it, or what was left of her after Ninehulls' salvo hit.

There is a hole in her flank you could drive a cart through, and the sea pours in at it. She leans toward you, and her people come over your rail any way they can: down the grapnel lines, across the boarding planks, a few in the water, swimming hard, two of them dragging a sea-chest between them.

Behind the last of them she rolls onto her side, shows her keel once, green with weed, and goes under with a long belch of air. The red flag goes last.

Beat 4 · Your turn
Read Aloud

Your gaze is dragged back as more of Ninehulls' crew come spilling down the lines, as numerous as the dead, landing with the grace of cats and armed to gut a royal fleet. Behind them, the long guns of the Ninefold track your every breath.

Ninehulls turns back to you, his smile still wide and honest, in contrast to the blood on his coat. "Now. Be dears and put those down too, will ya?" He gestures at your weapons. "Nobody's in any trouble. I'd just hate for anyone to get excited and start something I have to end."

Running it

His tone is the whole threat. He never raises his voice, and he does not need to: three ships and a full crew answer for him. If the players fight anyway, his crews board from all three ships at once and the scene ends badly for everyone; let them see that before they decide.

He waits until every weapon is dropped or stowed: sheathed, slung or set down will do. Nobody takes them yet. Then he nods, and turns to his own people.

Beat 5 · Everyone on deck
Read Aloud

Nobody slips away. Ninehulls' people go down the hatch in twos and come back up with the Goodmeasure's crew: Master Ozhra between two of them, a hand on each arm, his feet hunting for the rungs; Tarrek, twisting his horns sideways to fit them up through the hatch; Shab with the ledger clutched to her chest; Hangas still holding his cleaver until someone gently takes it off him. Patala is talked down out of the nest last.

Redsail's survivors are herded in from the rail, wet and bleeding, and the pirates make a ring of them around their captain's body. Nobody touches him. Ninehulls stands back with his arms folded, and waits.

Running it

Everyone aboard is brought to the body so the whole deck witnesses the Last Hands, the party included. The Goodmeasure's crew stand to one side, Redsail's in the ring.

3 · The Last Hands, at Sea
NinehullsTeofoga out of HringseyjaHalfpennyFirstropeMaster Ozhra
Beat 1 · The vote
Read Aloud · draft

Redsail's crew stand over their captain's body with their hands at their sides. Then an old lizardfolk with one milky eye steps into the ring, and the others give him room.

"The Last Hands, for Redsail," he says, loud enough for the black ships to hear. "He drew on a Lord and the Lord put him down. Hands for deposition."

The hands come up one by one, slow at first, then all together, until every survivor's hand is in the air.

"Hands for murder." Nobody moves. He waits the full count anyway, the way it is done. "Deposition, then." He turns to the man with the black flag. "We'll sail under you, if you'll have us."

Ninehulls claps once. "Course I will." He turns and bellows across the water. "Halfpenny! Come and count my new hands. Firstrope, open a barrel for them, they've had a long day!"

A tall elf with ink-black fingertips and a brass half-penny at her throat drops onto your deck with a wax tablet already open, and starts at the nearest of Redsail's people, writing before her boots are dry. Over on the black ship, a lean catfolk in a striped sash knocks the head off a barrel with one blow of a belaying pin, and a cheer goes up.

What happens

A killed captain gets the Last Hands: deposition frees the killer to stand for the seat, murder sees them marooned. The read-aloud lets the players see the ritual and hear its two words, deposition and murder, without explaining them, so it is familiar when it comes back at the end. Teofoga, Redsail's quartermaster, calls the vote.

Beat 2 · The master of the ship
Read Aloud

Ninehulls turns to look at Ozhra, the warmth still on his face. He bends down and holds out his hand. "Master of the ship? A pleasure. A real pleasure. She's a lovely thing, I have to admit. Kept well."

"Now. I could set you adrift in a jolly boat, but this far out?" Something like worry crosses his face. "Well, let's just say I'm not an animal. I'll carry you to Freeboard, every one of you, fed and dry." His voice drops to a rumble you feel through the deck. "But the ship? Is mine. A deck in Freeboard needs a hull, and this one fits just right." A deep chuckle goes through him.

Ozhra looks up at the giant, then at you, then his gaze drags over the pirates around his crew, still ready, the guns still trained on them. He sighs. His hand goes to his throat and touches something under his clothes. Then he shakes Ninehulls' hand.

"Good man." Ninehulls' hand slaps down on Ozhra's shoulder, and the merchant stumbles. Then the Pirate Lord turns to you. "What about you lot?" He smiles. "Fancy a fight, a jolly boat, or a ride to Freeboard?"

Running it

The question is real; let the players answer it.

  • A ride to Freeboard: the way the chapter runs. He claps once and gets on with it (Beat 3).
  • A jolly boat: he gives them one, with water and a compass, and wishes them luck; it is days to any coast. Lost at sea, they are picked up by the ring of ships round Freeboard a few days later, thirsty, with the story already told by someone else.
  • A fight: his crews board from all three ships at once, and the Ninefold's guns stay on the Goodmeasure. He takes no pleasure in it and stops it the moment they yield.

The something under Ozhra's clothes is the key on its chain. Shab looks at the locked cabin door once and then very hard at the deck. The locked cabin is Ninehulls' business now.

Beat 3 · All hands
Read Aloud

Ninehulls straightens up and fills his chest, and when he shouts, the black ships hear it. "Halfpenny! She's yours to Freeboard. Take twenty of ours. Redsail's lot, half aboard her and half with me; mix them up, I want them making friends. Get that rag off her mast. Let's go home!"

The deck comes apart and puts itself back together around you. Strangers run up the shrouds, a line of hands passes Redsail's sea-chest over the rail, and Halfpenny walks the deck behind them, counting, her stylus never stopping. The trader's pennant comes down, and the red nine goes up in its place. Before the smoke has quite cleared, the Goodmeasure is under sail again, the black ships around her like an escort.

Beat 4 · Names
Read Aloud

When the deck has settled, Halfpenny finds you. She is tall and spare, silver-grey hair cropped short against the wind, and up close her fingertips are black with ink that will never wash out. She does not look at your faces. She looks at your hands, and at what is in them.

"Names," she says, stylus ready. "And out of where, if you don't mind." She glances at your weapons. "Keep them. You're passengers, not prisoners, and I'd rather you had them if anything else comes over that rail." She touches the brass half-penny at her throat. "But I'd like to know whose is whose now."

Running it

The party's first real talk with Halfpenny. "Out of where" is how the cities ask where someone is from: a newcomer is out of a port, the city-born out of a deck. She writes down whatever they give her, true or not, and asks nothing more. The weapons stay with the party.

The way home: time to talk

A day and a night at sea, among the people who just tried to kill them, with Halfpenny captaining. Let the players wander and ask. The Goodmeasure's own crew are kept below by day and let up at dusk.

Exploration · the stack

Beat 1 · The stack
Read Aloud

On the second morning the lookout calls, and you go up to see. Master Ozhra stays where he is, at the stern, with his back to the bow.

At first it looks like an island with a storm sitting on it. Then it comes closer, and the storm is masts: hundreds of them, standing out at every angle from a mountain of ships. Hulls stacked on hulls, black and brown and every colour paint has ever been, piled so high the top of it catches the sun while the water around its foot is still in shadow. Gangways and rope-bridges run between them like cobwebs. Smoke climbs from a dozen chimneys that you'd wager were once portholes.

Around its foot sails a ring of ships under canvas, keeping pace with the mountain, and it is only when you see them that you understand the mountain is moving. The noise reaches you before the smell does: bells, hammers, a hundred voices shouting about prices, somebody singing badly. Then the smell: tar, lamp oil, wet wood and, surprisingly mixed into it, the smell of fresh bread.

On the very top stand five ships stood on end like spires, each one flying its own flag, and one of them is a red nine on black. A sixth flies a flag faded almost to nothing.

Heads come up out of the water as you close with the ring, sleek and dark, and watch you pass: Merfolk, keeping pace with the hull without seeming to swim.

Beat 2 · The landing
Read Aloud

The Goodmeasure comes in under the stack's shadow and makes fast at a landing of old hulls lashed flat, where the boats tie up three deep. Halfpenny nods to each of you as you pass, and makes a mark on her tablet.

You are hardly off the gangway when the chains come down. Two great booms built from old masts swing out from the stack above you, the chains bite under the Goodmeasure's hull, and with a shout and a groan of winches she lifts out of the water. Sea water pours off her keel in sheets. She turns slowly in the air as she rises, past the first deck, the second, the third, toward a gap in the mountain that is exactly her size.

Ninehulls stands beside you watching her go, his hands on his hips, like a man showing off his house. "She'll make a lovely deck," he says.

Read Aloud

You hear Master Ozhra let out a long breath. Then he walks off along the landing without a word, his crew behind him, his eyes already going from mast to mast at the boats tied up three deep. Ankhes is the last to go, and looks back at you once before the crowd swallows them.

Welcome to Freeboard. Your employer is looking for a ship home, and you are on your own in a city built of ships. What do you do?

What happens

Ozhra, his crew and the party go free on the landing. Ozhra goes looking for a ship home without a word to anyone; his crew go after him, Ankhes last and looking back. The party are on their own, with their weapons and their purses, in the city that asks no questions.

Beat 3 · Pell
Read Aloud

You notice the crowd on the landing part. All because of a halfling, freckled, grey at the temples, in a long coat sewn all over with coins that chime as she walks.

"You're the Goodmeasure's guards." It's not posed as a question. "Off Ninehulls' prize. Don't look like that, the whole Spar had it before your boots were dry." She looks you over, one at a time, like a merchant pricing a cargo. "Pell, out of the Spar. I tell for a living. Names, please, and out of where. And then a tale told tall."

"Anyone can tell a capture. I want the words. What did he say when he came over the rail? Exactly. And what did you do?" She smiles. "First drink's mine, at the Fairturn. Second's yours, if the room asks for it."

Running it

Pell trades: a drink and directions for the tale. Whatever they tell her, she tells tonight at the Fairturn, bigger and better, and the party are in it by name. If the players keep it to themselves, Ninehulls tells it anyway, in his own version where he saved them from the poachers, and by the next watch half the Spar has heard.

Their tale spreads from here on, wherever they go. Strangers know them by the capture. That is how Merrin finds them.

Set-piece · the sky comes home
5 · The Mooring
Kesti DowndraftOdile Brasshand
Read Aloud

It starts as a shout somewhere high up, and it comes down the stack deck by deck, gangway by gangway, until the whole city is shouting it: the sky! The sky's coming home! Doors bang open. The promenades empty upward. Everyone climbs: up ladders that used to be masts, up rope-lifts packed to the rails, up the outsides of hulls hand over hand. Children are already on the spires, and the spire crews are not bothering to chase them off.

You come out onto a high deck into wind and daylight, and see them.

Out in the west, low over the water, four islands are coming closer. Dark rock underneath, bearded with roots that trail in the wind, and along the top of each the edge of a city glinting in the sun. Each with a cluster of airships riding above it on long lines drawn tight as bowstrings.

They come on slowly, growing as they come, until their shadows reach the ring of ships at the stack's foot and begin to climb toward you. The air turns cold. A sound arrives with them, low and many-voiced: the wind singing through a whole city of open windows. By the time they are overhead, you can make out faces along the edges, looking down at you.

Then they stop, and hang there over Freeboard. As they settle, you feel a cold, fine rain falling out of the sky onto every upturned face on the stack. The whole city cheers it.

Then the lines come down. Baskets drop from the islets on ropes, gangways swing out and bang down onto the highest decks, and people come pouring across in both directions. You reckon that by midnight the two will be one market, one feast, one argument, one city.

What happens

Skysail moors over Freeboard; the two Lords and the Pirate Lords sit together, and the party can see Kesti Downdraft and Odile Brasshand before they need either of them. The baskets carry anyone up who pays the basket-crew.

Places on Skysail

Up by basket once the cities are moored.

At a glance
  • The Edge: cheap, very good food and drink at the drop; Kesti Downdraft's table; talk of winds, crews and the Jigger's cold.
  • The Ropewalk: rope, rigging and airship gear made to measure, in a cored-out airship; Merrin and her keepers at the far end of the walk, with the Unspent in view.
  • The Tuning House: wind-bells, chimes and signal whistles; Ilta hears everything on the Fore, and says the Jigger's note has always been wrong.

Skysail is for meeting people more than for spending: Kesti at the Edge, Merrin at the Ropewalk, and the first sight of the Unspent.

A young woman in a coat two sizes too grand for her comes straight to your table, with the flat, hungry look of someone who has just been voted into a job.

What happens

The keepers are the grandchildren of the crews of Goldwake's other two airships. They are broke and voted Merrin in on the promise to open the Unspent; Nirfel has been whispering to them. None of them will board her. Her offer: passage to any coast on a keepers' airship, and a share of the sale of what is inside.

Up by basket to the Unspent, hanging under the Jigger.

Merrin's offer

The keepers are poor, and Merrin has nothing to pay with until the cabin is sold. She offers what they do have.

  • Passage to any coast they name, on one of the keepers' two airships, the day the work is done. She knows this is what they need most, and leads with it. "You want off the Midarra. We can fly."
  • A keeper's share each, of whatever the Unspent's cabin sells for. Thirty-four keepers hold a share apiece, so with six of them the party holds 6 of 40, about a seventh of the sale. The share is fixed; nobody gets more than a keeper. "Same as us. One each. Nobody gets more than a keeper."
  • Nothing up front. Board and lodging on the Jigger while they work, and her word. "We've been broke for three generations. A few more weeks won't kill us."

What the share really is

The takers of Goldwake's last cache are Merrin and her keepers: they open the cabin by their right and sell what is inside, and the Greed-mark falls on them. The party are paid out of the sale and take nothing, so their share carries no mark. Only if one of them takes from the hoard for themselves, the way Nirfel's night whisper urges, do they become a taker too. Kesti's pay, later, is theirs as well.

The sale price is set with the hoard's contents in Part IV.
