Beat 1 · Where you are, and why
Read Aloud · draft

The posting on the Guild board in Rika Tikur had been three lines long: Guards wanted, experienced. Stillship Goodmeasure, crossing to Solkai with valuable cargo. Sails at dawn. The ink was still wet when you signed.

That was nine days ago. Since then the Midarra has been flat and bright and empty in every direction. Below decks the Goodmeasure carries crates of Eldaran almosts packed in straw, cases of Emarrean sake, bolts of Lautaran silk, and a locked cabin aft that the master keeps the only key to. The work so far has been walking the deck, eating the cook's porridge, and watching the horizon not change.

Why they are aboard

The party are the ship's hired muscle, taken on through the Guild at Rika Tikur on short notice. A crew of their level is not cheap; the cargo is worth it. Ozhra carries luxury goods north to Solkai: Eldaran almosts, Emarrean sake, Lautaran silk.

He bought no Lord's colours for the crossing, on purpose. Colours are worth only as much as a Lord's name, and the raid the tellers love best is the one that breaks another Lord's colours; so on this run Ozhra trusts steel over pennants. He is in the middle of the crossing, in water the Lords share out, flying no one's colours, and that is why the ship is fair prize.

An eight-stripe Catjomin Sake (Uraharagiri, Mist of the Hidden Meadow): a Hidden Treasure from a kitsune sacred vault, opened only by ancestral rite. It should never have left Emarrea. Anyone who knows Catjomin recognises the tier at a glance, and the stripes are never spoken aloud.

Temple-stamped dream-blends from the Dreaming Cape: genuine tier-four brews, the Twin Lantern's mark on every flask, made to promote vivid restful dreaming. A stamped blend is only to be drunk under a chartered inn's roof; carrying it away breaks the temple's licence. Ozhra bought a case from an inn-keeper who said yes to an outside merchant, the Cape's own sore point, and Solkai's rich will pay well for a real Cape dream at home. A Religion or Society check (or anyone from Lautara) knows the flasks should never have left the inn.

Either one would ruin Ozhra if found. They are also exactly the kind of prize a Pirate Lord's crew would love to tell about.

Beat 2 · A moment to breathe
Run it

Let the players introduce their characters to each other and to the crew: a meal on deck, a game of dice on a grain sack, the evening watch. The crew run the ship; the party only guard it, and the crew are curious about them. Give each player a crew member to talk to.

Beat 3 · Ozhra's question
Run it

While they talk, Master Ozhra walks the deck: up to the bow, back to the wheel, up to the bow again, looking at the horizon and not at the sails. At last he drifts over to the party, too casual.

"You'd know a Lord's ship, would you? At a distance, I mean. Say, a mile off. Before it was close enough to matter." If they ask why: "No reason. Good eyes are good to have. That's all." He thanks them, too warmly, and goes back to pacing.

Critical Success (≥42): He bought no Lord's colours for this crossing, by choice, and he is carrying something in the locked cabin he must not be caught with. He is afraid of a Lord's ship more than of pirates.
Success (≥32): He is hiding something about flags or colours. His question was about a Lord's ship specifically, and pirates in general do not worry him half as much.
Failure (<32): A careful master with a valuable cargo on a long crossing. Nothing more.
Critical Failure (≤22): He convinces them all is in order, and on the way back to the wheel he pats the pennant locker as if colours were in it.

Beat 4 · The lookout calls
Read Aloud · draft

"Sail!" Patala's voice comes down from the nest, high and certain. "Sail off the port bow!"

A lean two-master is coming out of the glare, flying a trader's pennant, the kind half the ships on the crossing fly. Ozhra lets out a breath you didn't know he was holding. Then the pennant comes down, hand over hand, and a red flag runs up in its place: no Lord's mark on it, nothing on it at all but red. The two-master leans into the wind and comes straight for you, and as she closes you can see her deck: crowded rail to rail, far more hands than a ship that size needs to sail her.

"Poachers," says Tarrek, quite calmly, and starts herding the crew toward the hatch. "Below. All of you. That's what we pay them for." Ozhra is already halfway down the ladder to the locked cabin. On the other ship, someone is swinging a grapnel.

The fight

Cold start into a hot opening. Redsail's crew is unflagged and poaching; the Goodmeasure flies no colours, so she is anyone's prize. The crew shelter below; the party fights alone, which is what they were hired for. Run the fight until the party is clearly winning, then cut to the broadside. Keep Redsail alive for it.

Who's here · Redsail's ship

Redsail's two-master carries far more crew than the troops on the map: the rest work the sails and the guns and wait their turn at the rail. Ninehulls' broadside kills many of them. The survivors are the ones who hold the Last Hands over Redsail and sign under the black flag, and the ones the party can question afterwards.

  • Teofoga out of Hringseyja · quartermaster: never draws, calls the Last Hands over Redsail
  • Togafiti out of Rika Tikur · the mate (stat block above)
  • Naama out of Solkai · the knife (stat block above)
Running the fight

Round 1: grapnels and a boarding plank at the port rail. The three gun crews stay on their own deck and open with Pistol Salvo across the gap, one after another; all three boarder troops and the mate come over the plank, and the mate howls first. The knife comes over the bow on a line and goes for whoever the boarders have knocked prone or flanked.

Then: the boarders Rock the Boat to put the party on the deck and use Repel Boarders! to shove them toward the rail; anyone who goes over is in the Midarra, and Redsail answers with Sink or Swim. Redsail stays on his own quarterdeck and casts: blade barrier across the rail, bane, downpour.

The pressure: six troops are more bodies than the Goodmeasure's deck can hold. Fill the port rail and the main deck with them, keep the gun crews firing from the other ship once their salvos are spent (they board after), and let the party see Redsail's ship still has more on it than they have.

The cut: after two or three rounds, or as soon as the party is clearly holding (a troop broken, a strong one down, or Redsail himself in danger), Ninehulls' broadside lands. If it goes badly for the party, it lands sooner. The troops' weakness to area damage is what makes cannon shred them; Redsail survives it, for Ninehulls.

The bought name: Redsail calls his own name like a battle-cry; his crew do not echo it. A Society check (DC 25, the Twin Cities' tellers' trade) or anyone who has heard Freeboard's taverns knows nobody tells of Redsail.

    2 · Ninehulls' Broadside
    NinehullsHalfpennyRedsail
    Read Aloud · draft

    The first gun is so loud you feel it in your teeth. The poacher's rail simply stops existing. Out of the haze to starboard comes a black-hulled ship, then a second, then a third, every one of them flying the same flag: black, with a red nine painted on it, a skull grinning out of the loop of the nine and bleeding down into red water.

    Before the smoke clears a figure the size of a wardrobe swings across on a line and lands on your deck like a dropped anchor; the planks groan, and the whole ship dips toward him. He straightens up, a head above anyone aboard, and looks the poachers over with real disappointment. "Now what," he asks, "are you doing in my waters?"

    What happens

    Redsail goes for him; Ninehulls kills him without effort. Then he claps his hands and apologises to the party for the whole mess, with a wide smile.

    His terms: he will not set them adrift in the jolly boat, he is not an animal; he will carry them to Freeboard. But the ship is his. A deck in Freeboard needs a hull, and this one fits.

    The fleet's size does the arguing. If the players fight him anyway, his crews board from three ships at once.

    Redsail's ship goes down. The broadside dismasts and holes the two-master; she settles within the hour. Her survivors, Teofoga among them, come across to the Goodmeasure with what they can carry.

    Ritual · the players see it early
    3 · The Last Hands, at Sea
    NinehullsRedsail's crew
    Read Aloud · draft

    Redsail's crew stand over their captain's body with their hands at their sides. Nobody tells them what to do. One by one the hands go up.

    "Deposition," says the oldest of them, and then, louder, to the man with the black flag: "We'll sail under you, if you'll have us." Ninehulls claps once. "Course I will. Somebody get them a drink." Then he turns the smile on you.

    What happens

    A killed captain gets the Last Hands: deposition frees the killer to stand for the seat, murder sees them marooned. Here it is quick and clear, so the players know the ritual when it comes back at the end. Teofoga, Redsail's quartermaster, calls the vote.

    The way home. Ninehulls puts Halfpenny aboard the Goodmeasure as her captain for the run to Freeboard, with Redsail's survivors as her new hands and Ozhra's crew kept below; his fleet keeps company. Ozhra's locked cabin is Ninehulls' business now. A day or two at sea, among the people who just tried to kill them: the time to question Teofoga, and Togafiti and Naama if they lived.

    Exploration · the stack
    4 · Freeboard
    NinehullsPell out of the SparOdile Brasshand
    Read Aloud · draft

    On the second morning the lookout calls, and you see what Master Ozhra will not look at: an island with a storm sitting on it, except the storm is masts, hundreds of them, standing out of a mountain of stacked hulls at every angle. Ships ring its foot under sail, keeping pace with it.

    By evening the Goodmeasure is on chains, and you watch her rise, dripping, onto the stack. Ninehulls stands at the rail beside you like a man showing off his house. "She'll make a lovely deck," he says.

    What happens

    Ozhra, his crew and the party go free. The party walks the promenades under the lanterns: no sky inside, each deck keeping its own watch. In a tavern on the Lower Spar, Pell out of the Spar is already telling the capture, in a version where Ninehulls saved them.

    Set-piece · the sky comes home
    5 · The Mooring
    Kesti DowndraftOdile Brasshand
    Read Aloud · draft

    The shout comes down the gangways and the whole city climbs. Out in the west, low over the water, four islands are coming home: dark rock bearded with roots, a city glinting along their tops, airships hanging beneath them on lines drawn tight as bowstrings.

    As they settle over the stack they shed their clouds, and a cold, fine rain falls out of a clear sky onto every upturned face. By midnight the baskets are down and two markets are shouting at each other about the price of everything.

    What happens

    Skysail moors over Freeboard; the two Lords and the Pirate Lords sit together. A good scene for the party to see Kesti Downdraft before they need her.

    Social · the keepers' captain
    6 · The Hire
    Merrin out of the Jigger
    Read Aloud · draft

    She finds you, which tells you something: a young woman in a coat two sizes too grand for her, with the flat, hungry look of someone who has just been voted into a job.

    "Merrin, out of the Jigger. They're saying you're Guild, off Ninehulls' prize, and that you know old work." She does not wait for an answer. "There's a ship under the Jigger nobody has boarded in a hundred and forty years. She's ours by right. I need someone to open her."

    What happens

    The keepers are the grandchildren of the crews of Goldwake's other two airships. They are broke and voted Merrin in on the promise to open the Unspent; Nirfel has been whispering to them. None of them will board her. Pay is on sale of what is inside.

    Up by basket to the Unspent, hanging under the Jigger.
