Recall Knowledge · common on the Midarra
The Twin Cities

Anyone who has worked the Midarra has heard of them. Easy for a sailor, harder for a landsman.

◈ The Twin Cities
Society DC 20 · Sailing Lore, Pirate Lore or Midarra Lore DC 15
≥30 + Critical Success: You also know the boasting that comes with them. Both cities call themselves the truly free city, and both sneer at Frae City across the water, which sneers right back. And you know the story every Freeboarder tells sooner or later: two generations ago Legea sent a whole fleet to hunt them down, and while it searched the open Midarra, Freeboard came over the horizon behind it, shelled the Legean half of Hringseyja's harbour, and sailed off before the fleet got home. Legean wax ships have paid for colours ever since.
≥20 Success: You have heard dockhands on every quay of the Midarra talk about them. Two pirate cities that never stay in one place: Freeboard sits on the water, Skysail hangs in the air, and when they can, they moor together, the sky city over the sea. Some seventy-five thousand people live aboard, mostly along the Solkai crossing, and every one of them has a vote. Each deck is run like a ship, its crew raising hands to choose a captain and a quartermaster, and each city elects a Lord: Odile Brasshand holds Freeboard, Kesti Downdraft holds Skysail. Anything that needs selling without questions ends up in Freeboard, and prize cargo goes cheap there.
<20 Failure: Pirate cities, somewhere out on the Midarra. That is as far as it goes.
≤10 Critical Failure: You are fairly sure the Twin Cities belong to Askamira and are counted under Cronus's domain, like the island itself. Mention the island, and the pirates will treat you as neighbours. (False: a Freeboarder takes it as an insult.)
Recall Knowledge · the sea city
Freeboard

The stack, the decks, and how a Freeboarder lives.

◈ Freeboard
Society DC 22 · Pirate Lore, Sailing Lore or Freeboard Lore DC 17 · Mercantile Lore DC 20 for its market
≥32 + Critical Success: You know how the money and the stories move, too. The Lord of Freeboard lives on the Tenth, one share in every ten of every prize sold aboard. The city's great vice is its tellers, who tell the Lords' deeds in the taverns for coin; a captain caught paying to have their own deeds told has a bought name, and nobody lets them forget it. When a captain is killed, the crew holds the Last Hands over the body and votes: deposition, and the killer walks free and may stand for the seat; murder, and the killer is marooned. And one of the spires is flying a dead Lord's flag this season. Her crew still holds it, with fewer hands every time the cities moor.
≥22 Success: Everyone who has sailed the Midarra has a Freeboard story. It is a mountain of ships piled on ships, ringed by working ships under sail, and it moves: old engines deep in the stack push it along at a crawl, and the Lord of Freeboard sets the course. Every deck is named for the prize it used to be, and every walkway for a piece of rigging; the one strangers end up on first is the Lower Spar. Inside, the sky is gone. Each deck keeps its own watch, so the city never sleeps, and if you ask a Freeboarder the time, they ask you which watch. The Merfolk live in the drowned hulls at the bottom and trade up through hatches. On the very top stand the spires: each one the ship that made a Pirate Lord, stood on end and flying the Lord's flag.
<22 Failure: A pirate town that floats. That is all.
≤12 Critical Failure: Freeboard belongs to Ninehulls, and his word is law on every deck. (False: the Lord of Freeboard is Odile Brasshand, and a Pirate Lord commands no deck.)
Recall Knowledge · the sky city
Skysail

Four islets in the air, and the old work that holds them up.

◈ Skysail
Society DC 24 · Sailing Lore (sky) or Pirate Lore DC 19
≥34 + Critical Success: You know where it came from. About 2270 MR a handful of airship crews moored to a single stone already hanging low over the Midarra, the one they now call the Main, and later found and woke the other three. What holds the islets up is old work set deep in the rock. The tenders keep it running the way their grandparents showed them, and the scholars who have seen drawings of it call it Elden. The Jigger is the cold islet, and it carries more airships on its lines than any of the others. Under it hangs the Unspent, Goldwake's old flagship, and nobody boards her.
≥24 Success: Skysail is the airship pirates' city: four islets of rock hanging in the air, named for a ship's masts, the Fore, the Main, the Mizzen and the Jigger. Under each one hang airships on long lines that are always taut. Up top the city is all open air: wide stone squares, a table in front of every door, hammocks slung over the edge, and walls that are mostly window, so the whole place hums whenever the wind blows. It keeps the sun's hours, works in the morning and spends the long afternoon at the tables, and skiffs carry people between the islets. Its Lord is Kesti Downdraft, a Tengu and a Pirate Lord besides; more Tengu live up there than anywhere outside Vindul.
<24 Failure: A city on flying rocks. That is all.
≤14 Critical Failure: The islets hang from the airships underneath them; cut enough of the lines and an islet drops. (False: the airships carry only what the old work cannot.)
◈ The old work (only once they have seen it, or ask what holds the islets up)
Arcana or Crafting DC 32 · Engineering Lore DC 27
≥42 + Critical Success: And it was built to lift something far smaller than an island. It is straining.
≥32 Success: It is engineering, very old, and outside every school of magic you know. It runs on habit: the tenders keep it going without understanding what it does.
<32 Failure: Stone and old brass. It means nothing to you.
≤22 Critical Failure: It is a shrine to Fisaya, and the islets fly by her favour. (False.)
Recall Knowledge · the names the taverns tell
The Pirate Lords

What a Pirate Lord is, and who counts.

◈ The Pirate Lords
Society DC 24 · Pirate Lore or Midarra Lore DC 19
≥34 + Critical Success: You also know why the cities put up with them. On the Midarra belief is power, and a name enough people believe in carries real weight. Colours are honoured only by the captains of the Lord who sold them. Odile Brasshand wants a single pass that every Lord honours, and the Lords refuse, because the raid the tellers love best is the one that breaks another Lord's colours. Of the Lords who are gone, the one still talked about is Goldwake, a sky Lord who died at anchor about 2390 MR.
≥24 Success: A Pirate Lord holds no office and wins no vote. A Lord is a pirate whose name every tavern on the Midarra tells, and captains sail under a Lord's flag for the sake of that name. About seven count right now, and people argue over the edges of the list. A Lord's word binds every captain who flies the Lord's flag. The Lords share out the waters between them and sell colours, passes across their own water, through factors in Solkai and Rika Tikur. A Lord who stops raiding stops being told about, and before long stops being a Lord. The names you hear most are Ninehulls, Sunfall, who strikes out of the sun, and Kesti Downdraft.
<24 Failure: Famous pirates. That is all.
≤14 Critical Failure: The Pirate Lords are elected by the hands, like everybody else on the cities. (False: nobody votes on a Lord.)
Recall Knowledge · the most told name on the Midarra
Ninehulls

Everyone has a version.

◈ Ninehulls
Society DC 25 · Pirate Lore, Sailing Lore or Midarra Lore DC 20 · a failure still knows the name and the flag
≥35 + Critical Success: You know where the scar comes from, and what came after it. Years into his raiding he took a cut across the throat that should have killed him; he lived, and he wears his shirt open so everyone can see the scar. Then he was killed for real, from ambush, in Freeboard, and nobody saw who did it. The Last Hands were held over his body with no killer to free or maroon. Within days he was back, and the man the taverns had named for the ambush was dead, struck down by Ninehulls himself. That man's name is forgotten now. The Voroir Daua keep Ninehulls on their list.
25–34 + Success: He took nine ships in one night and became a Lord for it. He is a Human, two metres thirty and built like a wardrobe, and the tellers say he grows a little with every tale; old hands will swear he was a finger's width shorter last season. The taverns tell him as the man who cannot die, and his spire flies on top of Freeboard.
16–24 Failure: Ninehulls is a Pirate Lord, one of the names every tavern on the Midarra tells, and that flag is his: a red nine on black, a skull grinning out of the loop of the nine and bleeding down into red water.
≤15 Critical Failure: Ninehulls is a title, handed from captain to captain along with the flagship; the one sailing now is the fourth. (False.)
◈ A legend that walks (only if they wonder how he came back, or what he is)
Religion or Occultism DC 30
≥40 + Critical Success: Such a being knows itself only as its story tells it, and enough belief can bring it back after it is killed. What is forgotten stays gone.
≥30 Success: A legend believed by enough people can begin to exist, built out of that belief: a belief being, a soul born of belief and no undead thing. It lasts as long as it is remembered.
<30 Failure: Nothing comes to mind.
≤20 Critical Failure: He is undead, held back by a pact; destroy his phylactery and he stays down. (False.)
