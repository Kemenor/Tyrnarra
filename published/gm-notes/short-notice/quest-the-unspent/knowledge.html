Recall Knowledge · common on the Midarra
The Twin Cities

Anyone who has worked the Midarra has heard of them. Easy for a sailor, harder for a landsman.

◈ The Twin Cities
Society DC 20 · Sailing Lore, Pirate Lore or Midarra Lore DC 15
Critical Success (≥30): All of the Success, and: both cities call themselves the truly free city and sneer at Frae City, which sneers back. About 2460 MR Legea sent a fleet after them; Freeboard came over the horizon behind it, shelled the Legean half of Hringseyja's harbour and was gone before the fleet came home. Legean wax ships have bought colours ever since.
Success (≥20): Two pirate cities that move: Freeboard on the water, Skysail in the air, some 75,000 souls, keeping mostly to the central Midarra on the Solkai crossing. When they can, they moor together, sky over sea. They run on the articles: every deck is a ship, everyone living aboard is its crew, and the crew elects its captain and quartermaster by show of hands. Each city elects a Lord: Odile Brasshand holds Freeboard, Kesti Downdraft holds Skysail. Freeboard is the market that asks no questions: prize cargo sells cheap there, without its history.
Failure (<20): Pirate cities, somewhere on the Midarra. Nothing useful.
Critical Failure (≤10): (false) The cities are Askamira's, counted under Cronus's domain; name the island and the pirates will take you for neighbours. A Freeboarder takes that as an insult.
Recall Knowledge · the sea city
Freeboard

The stack, the decks, and how a Freeboarder lives.

◈ Freeboard
Society DC 22 · Pirate Lore, Sailing Lore or Freeboard Lore DC 17 · Mercantile Lore DC 20 for its market
Critical Success (≥32): All of the Success, and: the Lord lives on the Tenth, one share in ten of every prize sold aboard. Freeboard's vice is its tellers, who tell the Lords' deeds in the taverns for coin; a captain caught paying for their own telling has a bought name, and it is an insult. A killed captain gets the Last Hands: the crew votes deposition (the killer goes free and may stand for the seat) or murder (the killer is marooned). One spire on top flies a dead Lord's flag; her crew still holds it, and fewer every season.
Success (≥22): A mountain of ships stacked on ships, ringed by working ships under sail; its old engines drive it at a crawl and the Lord of Freeboard sets the course. Decks are named for the prizes they were, promenades for rigging (the Lower Spar). Inside there is no sky and no common hour: each deck keeps its own watch, so you ask a Freeboarder which watch. The Merfolk hold the drowned hulls at the bottom and trade through hatches. On top stand the spires, each the prize that made a Pirate Lord, flying the Lord's flag.
Failure (<22): A floating pirate town. Nothing useful.
Critical Failure (≤12): (false) Freeboard belongs to Ninehulls; his word is law on every deck. The Lord of Freeboard is Odile Brasshand, and a Pirate Lord commands no deck.
Recall Knowledge · the sky city
Skysail

Four islets in the air, and the old work that holds them up.

◈ Skysail
Society DC 24 · Sailing Lore (sky) or Pirate Lore DC 19
Critical Success (≥34): All of the Success, and: the first crews moored to one stone already hanging low over the Midarra, about 2270 MR, the one now called the Main, and later found and woke the other three. What holds the islets up is old work in the deep rock that the tenders keep by rote; scholars who have seen drawings of it call it Elden. The Jigger is the cold islet and carries the most airships on its lines, and under it hangs the Unspent, Goldwake's flagship, which nobody boards.
Success (≥24): Four islets in the air, named for a ship's masts: the Fore, the Main, the Mizzen and the Jigger. Airships hang beneath them on long lines, always taut. The city is all open: wide stone squares, a table in front of every door, hammocks slung over the edge, walls that are mostly window, so the whole city hums in the wind. It keeps the sun's hours, and skiffs carry folk between the islets. Kesti Downdraft, a Tengu, is its Lord and a Pirate Lord besides. More Tengu live there than anywhere outside Vindul.
Failure (<24): A city on flying rocks. Nothing useful.
Critical Failure (≤14): (false) The islets hang from the airships beneath them; cut enough lines and an islet drops. The airships carry only what the old work cannot.
◈ The old work (only once they have seen it, or ask what holds the islets up)
Arcana or Crafting DC 32 · Engineering Lore DC 27
Critical Success (≥42): All of the Success, and: it is lift-work of a kind that was built for something much smaller than an island, and it is straining.
Success (≥32): No school of magic they know. Engineering, very old, and running by habit: the tenders keep it going without understanding it.
Failure (<32): Stone and old brass. Nothing useful.
Critical Failure (≤22): (false) It is a shrine to Fisaya, and the islets fly by her favour.
Recall Knowledge · the names the taverns tell
The Pirate Lords

What a Pirate Lord is, and who counts.

◈ The Pirate Lords
Society DC 24 · Pirate Lore or Midarra Lore DC 19
Critical Success (≥34): All of the Success, and: on the Midarra belief is power, and a name believed by enough of it carries real weight; that is why the cities sit with the Lords. Colours are honoured only by the selling Lord's captains. Odile Brasshand wants one pass that every Lord honours, and the Lords refuse: the raid the tellers love best is the one that breaks another Lord's colours. Of the dead, Goldwake, a sky Lord, died at anchor about 2390 MR.
Success (≥24): A Pirate Lord holds no office: a Lord is a pirate whose name every tavern on the Midarra tells, followed by captains who sail under the Lord's flag for the name. About seven count now; who counts is argued at the edges. A Lord's word binds every captain under that flag. The Lords share out the waters and sell colours, passes across their water, through factors in Solkai and Rika Tikur. A Lord who stops raiding stops being told of, and in time stops being a Lord. Named: Ninehulls, Sunfall (who strikes out of the sun), Kesti Downdraft.
Failure (<24): Famous pirates. Nothing useful.
Critical Failure (≤14): (false) The Pirate Lords are elected by the hands, like everyone else on the cities. Nobody votes on a Lord.
Recall Knowledge · the most told name on the Midarra
Ninehulls

Everyone has a version.

◈ Ninehulls
Society DC 20 · Pirate Lore or Midarra Lore DC 15
Critical Success (≥30): All of the Success, and: he was killed from ambush in Freeboard, and no one saw who did it. The Last Hands were held over him with no killer to free or maroon. Within days the man the taverns named for it was dead, struck down by Ninehulls himself. No one remembers that man's given name. The Voroir Daua carry Ninehulls on their list.
Success (≥20): Took nine ships in one night and became a Lord. His flag is a red nine on black, a skull grinning out of the loop of the nine and bleeding into red water. A Human, two metres thirty and built like a wardrobe; the tellers say he grows a little with every tale, and old hands swear he was a finger's width shorter last season. Years later he took a wound that should have killed him and lived, and the taverns have told him since as the man who cannot die. His spire flies on Freeboard.
Failure (<20): A big pirate with a famous flag.
Critical Failure (≤10): (false) Ninehulls is a title, handed captain to captain with the flagship; this one is the fourth.
◈ A legend that walks (only if they wonder how he came back, or what he is)
Religion or Occultism DC 30
Critical Success (≥40): All of the Success, and: such a being knows itself as its story tells it, and enough belief can bring it back after it is killed. What is forgotten stays gone.
Success (≥30): A legend believed by enough people can begin to exist, built from the belief: a belief being, a soul born of belief and no undead thing. It lasts while it is remembered.
Failure (<30): Nothing useful.
Critical Failure (≤20): (false) He is undead, held back by a pact; destroy his phylactery and he stays down.
