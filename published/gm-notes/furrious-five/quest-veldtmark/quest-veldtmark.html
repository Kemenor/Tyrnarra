The Contract

The contract on the board is unremarkable at first glance: a welfare check on a local wealthy resident who has failed to attend several scheduled appointments. It was posted by his business partner, who is owed a significant sum and is beginning to worry. The expected difficulty is listed by Kael as "social, low risk."

Kael rated it before he thought about it too hard. He has not changed the rating. He has, however, mentioned to Mira Seld that something about the posting feels off to him. She agreed. Neither of them has said this to the party.

The sum is the part that should have caught someone's eye. Fifty gold for the welfare check, three hundred more on recovery of the debt of record: three hundred and fifty gold, posted against a knock-on-a-door errand two miles up the Cape road. Nobody pays that to have a door knocked on. Harlen Doss pays it because he is frightened, and because the coin is borrowed, and both of those facts are legible in the figure before the party has met the man.

Read Aloud · Quest Board
"Lord Caevan Veldtmark, merchant and landowner of the Cape district, has failed to respond to correspondence or attend any scheduled appointments for the past three weeks. His business partner, one Harlen Doss, requests a welfare check and, if the lord is well, collection of a debt of record. Fifty gold offered on confirmation of the lord's health; three hundred further on recovery of the debt. Inquire at the Wayward Compass for details. Discretion expected."
GM Note · What Harlen Doss Actually Knows
Harlen Doss is genuinely worried about his money but also genuinely worried about Veldtmark. The last time they spoke, three weeks ago, Veldtmark was distracted and strange, kept referring to "the great work" and "the invitation being answered." Harlen assumed he was drunk. He has been assuming that ever since, because the alternative is worse. If the party presses him, he will admit this. He will not volunteer it. What he volunteers readily, and at length, is the business: the Emarrea order, the rail allocation he bought, the bolts sitting in a Stillside warehouse going nowhere for three weeks. He talks about the cloth because talking about the cloth is easier than the other thing.
Background · Lord Caevan Veldtmark

Caevan Veldtmark is thirty-five, and he is one of the best logistics men on the Midarra's southern shore. He is one of many; Millhaven keeps a dozen freight houses and the Stillside counts more. He is simply the one whose consignments arrive. He inherited the Cape estate from his father at twenty-six and inherited nothing else worth the word: the freight house is his own, built over nine years out of a clear head for routing and an unfashionable insistence on paying dockhands properly and knowing their names.

Millhaven has no railhead, so the work is the leg the rail cannot reach. Veldtmark moves goods off the Smáa-mouth, down the coast and up the river-craft chain to Merkavar, and holds standing allocations on the magirail out of the lake-city. A bolt of cloth that leaves his warehouse on a Tuesday is on the northern network inside the month. That is the thing he sells, and until eight months ago he sold more of it every season.

He built the small church on the grounds about eight months ago, ostensibly as a private chapel to Bikiargi of the twin moons, which fit the Cape's dreaming character and raised no suspicion. His staff read it as grief arriving late. His competitors read it as a rich man finding religion on schedule. The Merchant Council noticed only that he had stopped chasing new contracts, and assumed he was consolidating. Harlen Doss's allocation was among the last agreements he signed.

The truth is that Veldtmark has been a member of a secretive philosophical society for years, one obsessed with what lies beneath the Cape's drift, what the disturbance on the Cape actually touches, and whether something on the other side of it can be contacted. Six months ago they made contact. Four months ago something answered. Three months ago Veldtmark stopped attending social functions. The church is not dedicated to Bikiargi.

The Thing That Answered
It has no name that mortals have recorded. It exists in the space the drift brushes against, not the past or the future, but the gap between moments where time does not flow. It is not a demon. It is not a vampire. It is something that was old before either category existed. It experiences hunger the way time experiences forward motion: constantly, inevitably, without cruelty but also without mercy. Veldtmark offered it worship, feeding, and a vessel. It accepted. It is currently contained in the basement because containment was part of the original bargain, until it is strong enough not to need the bargain anymore.
Getting There
Read Aloud · The Road to Veldtmark Estate
The estate sits on the Cape side's northern edge, where the salt-crusted streets thin out and the drift sits heavier in the air. You have walked this road before. You know you have not, this is your first time here, but the certainty keeps surfacing, quiet and wrong, like a word you cannot stop almost remembering. The iron gate is closed but not locked. The grounds beyond it are well-kept. Too well-kept, perhaps, for a man who has not been seen in three weeks. The gravel path to the front door is raked perfectly smooth. There are no footprints in it. Not even the gardener's.
GM Note · The Drift
From the moment the party enters the gate, light drift effects apply: persistent déjà vu, a sense that they have done each action seconds before doing it, and occasionally a fractional delay, a sound that arrives slightly before or after its source. These are disorienting but not mechanically harmful. Increase the intensity as they approach the basement. By the time they reach the ritual chamber, the drift is strong enough that they may see brief ghost-images of past events in the estate, fleeting, silent, gone before they can examine them. Do not explain this. Let them ask questions.
The Butler
Read Aloud · The Front Door
The door opens before you knock. The man standing in it is tall, precisely dressed, and the colour of old bone. Not pale, bone. His eyes are the grey of something that has stopped having preferences. He inclines his head at exactly the angle required by courtesy and no further. "The lord is not receiving visitors," he says. His voice is correct in every technical respect. "If you have business with the estate, I am empowered to take messages." He has not blinked since the door opened.
GM Note · Aldous, the Butler
Aldous is not dead. He is not undead. He has simply been in proximity to the Thing long enough that something has been smoothed away. He was a perfectly normal man eight months ago. He will answer questions politely and deflect them precisely. He will not lie; he will use technically true statements to mislead. He knows the party is here because of the debt. He will offer to write a bank draft. If the party insists on seeing Veldtmark personally, he will "consult the lord's schedule" and return to say the lord can see them tomorrow. He is trying to make them leave. He is not threatening. He is deeply, quietly wrong, and players who succeed on Perception or Insight checks will feel it even if they cannot name it.
GM Note · Getting Inside
Social entry works if the party is persistent or clever. Aldous will eventually allow them to "wait in the sitting room" while he checks on the lord, which gives them access to the ground floor. They can also find their way in through the church on the grounds (unlocked, "open to worshippers"), through a window, or by following a staff member running an errand outside. The estate does not want to seem like it is hiding anything. That is more important to Aldous than keeping them out.

Read Aloud
The ballroom is impeccable. Blue silk on the walls, candelabras burning low, chairs arranged as if for an event that ended recently or is about to begin. The flowers in the arrangements are fresh. Someone tended them this morning. There is a single wineglass on a side table, half full, the wine inside perfectly still.
GM Notes
The glass has been half full for six days. The wine does not spoil. This is a minor effect of the Thing's proximity: time moves strangely near objects the cult has touched. A Nature or Arcana check notes that the wine should be vinegar by now.
Encounter · ⚔ Moderate 105
7× the Guests (Creature 4, 15 XP each). They are the party this room keeps almost having: the murmur of a gathering that never resolves into words. They do not attack on sight. They drift toward warmth, and the fight begins the moment a PC realises the sound has been getting closer.

Run it as a pressure, not a charge. Their Aura of Sobs (10 ft, Will DC 19 or stupefied 1) stacks as they close, so a party that lets them converge fights stupefied. Steal Voice on a Bony Hand hit costs a caster their verbal components for a minute, which is the real threat here: separate the casters early or the Guests will do it for you.
What's Here
  • A framed portrait of Veldtmark near the fireplace, painted six months ago; he looks healthy. The eyes follow nobody. This is not supernatural. It just feels that way.
  • Three invitations on the mantelpiece, unopened, dated three weeks ago: social events he missed.
  • A locked writing desk holds correspondence about "the Society" in veiled language. The cipher requires an hour and a Cryptography check.
II
The Dining Room
Left Center · Long Red Table
▾
Read Aloud
A table set for fourteen. Fourteen chairs, fourteen place settings, fourteen glasses, all clean, all waiting. The room smells of beeswax and something faintly metallic underneath. The candles here burn red-orange rather than white. They have always burned that colour, the butler will say, if asked. The butler is lying.
GM Notes
The table is set for cult meetings; fourteen is the full membership of Veldtmark's philosophical society, though only six are still active. The red-orange candles are treated with a compound that mildly suppresses Perception: anyone sitting at this table for more than ten minutes takes a -1 circumstance penalty to Perception until they leave and rest. Veldtmark uses these meetings to make the cult members easier to manage.
Encounter · ⚔ Moderate 120
6× the Seated (Elite Creature 5, 20 XP each). These are the six still active. They are sitting the dinner they sit every night, and they will go on sitting it while the party searches the room; the fight starts when someone reads the names under the table aloud, takes a journal off the sideboard, or sits down in the seventh chair.

The hardest fight above ground, and it plays well above its 120. Funereal Dirge (Will DC 21, frightened, 50 ft, recharge 1d4) opens the encounter as the grace before the meal, and a frightened party in a room where every hit carries Drain Life (Fort DC 18 or drained 1) loses ground fast. Do not forget Final Spite: each one gets a Strike as it drops, so the last round is more dangerous than the first. Anyone killed here rises at the table in 1d4 rounds, which is the point: this is how the fourteen were made.
What's Here
  • Under the table, carved into the wood: fourteen names in a spiral. One of them is a sitting member of the Millhaven Merchant Council.
  • The metallic smell is dried blood, faint enough that only high Perception or a Scent ability identifies it.
  • A sideboard holds fourteen identical small journals, cult members' devotional logs. Most are banal. Two are not.
III
The Organ Room
Top Left · Entry Point II
▾
Read Aloud
The organ fills the entire north wall, pipes running floor to ceiling. It is playing. Softly, slowly, a melody that has no beginning you can identify; it feels as though it has always been playing and you have only just walked into earshot. There is no one at the keys.
GM Notes
The organ is playing itself, a minor enchantment Veldtmark commissioned two months ago. The melody is the cipher sequence for Entry II played on loop. A Performance or Arcana check reveals the enchantment; a second check identifies the sequence. The room feels like standing inside a held breath.
Encounter · ⚠ Haunt 8
The Meditation (simple haunt, Hazard 7, Stealth +20). The haunt is the piece on the stand. When someone reads the sheet music, plays the organ, or stays long enough for the loop to come around, Mental Bind triggers: every creature within 30 ft is wrapped in spectral chains forged out of its own regrets (Will DC 25).

Disable with Religion DC 26 (expert) to disrupt it through prayer, or Deception DC 27 (expert) to openly defy your regrets. A character with nothing to regret, or who has already made amends, is simply unaffected, which is worth knowing before you roll: this haunt reads the party, and what it catches tells them something about themselves. Trivial as a budget line (8 XP); it earns its place as the first thing in the house that is openly hostile.
What's Here
  • Sheet music on the stand: "Meditation on the Spaces Between," not a real composition, the entry cipher.
  • A locked music cabinet holds compositions in a notation system nobody uses anymore. Pre-Lost Era, a History check confirms.
  • One organ pipe has been sealed; something was stored inside it. The seal is recent wax, stamped with the cult's mark.

The Kitchen & Storage
Top Center · Entry Point IV
▾
Read Aloud
The kitchen is working. Pots on the fire, bread in the oven, two kitchen staff moving with the mechanical precision of people who have stopped thinking about what they are doing. They look up when you enter. They smile. The smiles arrive a half-second after the looking, as if loaded separately.
GM Notes
The kitchen staff, two humans, middle-aged, have been in proximity to the Thing long enough to develop mild drift effects. They are not hostile, not thralled, just blurred at the edges. They answer questions with cheerful irrelevance. The bread in the oven has been baking for four days and is not burned. The locked storage door is behind the largest rack of shelves.
Encounter · ⚔ Low–Moderate 80
The Provisioner (Elite Creature 9, 80 XP) works the larder. He is the one who signs for "the requirement," and he is the only thing the party fights in this house that is entirely mortal and entirely willing. He knows exactly what the deliveries are. He has known for four months.

He opens with conversation, not a weapon. He will chat, offer the party bread, and put himself between them and the storage door without ever saying that is what he is doing. He fights when they reach for the lock. Running Poison Strike (3/day: Stride + Strike, +2d8 poison) means he opens at range and closes on his own terms, and he will use the shelving to break line of sight rather than stand and trade. If he is losing, he runs for the basement stair to raise the alarm, which is a worse outcome for the party than letting him go.
What's Here
  • The staff cannot tell you what day it is. They will tell you with complete confidence, and they will be wrong.
  • A ledger on the kitchen table logs deliveries, including a weekly delivery of something listed only as "the requirement," signed for by Aldous.
  • The locked storage door: Aldous has the key, or DC 18 Thievery to open.

The Conservatory / Garden Room
Center · Glass and Plants · First Veldtmark Encounter
▾
Read Aloud
The conservatory is humid and green and smells of living things. Plants crowd every surface, not wildly, but insistently, with the confidence of things that have been growing unopposed for some time. In the centre, on a white iron bench, Lord Caevan Veldtmark sits reading a book. He looks up when you enter and smiles with the warmth of a man who has been expecting you for some time. "Ah," he says. "The guild sends representatives. How perfectly timed."
GM Notes
The party's first encounter with Veldtmark. He is not hostile. He is gracious, articulate, and wrong in ways that take time to notice. He knows who sent them (Harlen Doss) and finds it gently amusing. He will offer tea. He will pay the debt immediately, in full, from a purse he produces without counting it. He is trying to make this easy. He wants them to leave. He answers questions with partial truths. He will not discuss the church or the basement. If pushed, he becomes vague rather than defensive: "private spiritual matters, you understand." He is not afraid of the party. He should be, but he is too far gone to know it.
Encounter · ◈ Social ⚔ Trivial 10
Lord Caevan Veldtmark (Noble, Creature 3, 10 XP) — and he stays Creature 3 all the way to the basement. He is deliberately not a fight. A party that decides to cut him down in his own conservatory can do it in a round and a half, and that is the correct outcome of that decision: he is a logistics man who has been hollowing himself out for eight months, not a boss.

What killing him costs comes later, not here. He is the only person alive who understands the terms of the bargain, so his death closes the Save Veldtmark ending, removes the one lever that can call off the finale, and leaves the party holding a dead lord, a Council member's name carved under a table, and no one to explain any of it to the Guild. Let them do it. Let the bill arrive in a later session.
What's Here
  • His eyes have a faint luminescence in low light, visible on a high Perception check.
  • The book he is reading has no title on the cover. Its pages appear blank from a distance. Up close, text appears in peripheral vision only.
  • Plants nearest him thrive abnormally. Plants nearest the door wilt slightly, as if the room's energy flows toward him.

The Lower Bedroom
Bottom Right · the room he left
▾
Read Aloud
This was the master bedroom once; the proportions say so, and so does the view. It is barely used now. The bed is made with the precision of habit rather than rest, and the linen has the flat, pressed smell of cloth that has been folded a long time. A reading chair faces a cold hearth. On the side table, a book lies open and face-down at page two hundred and eleven, holding a place for somebody who stopped coming back to it.
GM Notes
Veldtmark moved upstairs about five months ago and told the staff it was the light. It was not the light. The upper floor is closer to nothing in particular; he moved because the room he sleeps in now is directly above the ritual hall, and he wanted to be able to hear it. The staff still turn this bed down every evening out of habit, which is why it looks tended and feels abandoned at the same time.

A party that searches carefully finds the room is a record of the man before: a ledger-hand's bedroom, orderly, unmysterious, with nothing hidden in it at all. That absence is the clue. Everything worth finding moved up with him.
What's Here
  • The book is a dry treatise on river-freight tariff law, annotated in a careful hand as far as page two hundred and eleven and untouched after it. The annotations stop mid-sentence.
  • The hearth has not been lit in months, but it has been swept. Every hearth in the house has been swept.
  • A wardrobe of good, plain, well-maintained clothes, all of them at least a year old. Nothing has been bought since the church was built.
  • The door to the upper stair is at the end of the corridor outside, and it is the only door on the ground floor that Aldous will physically place himself in front of.
VII
The Church
Right Side Building · Entry Point I
▾
Read Aloud
The church is small, stone-built, with genuine craft in its construction. Pews of dark wood, candles on the altar, a stained-glass window at the far end depicting a figure standing between two moons, one bright and one dark, over a field of still water: a common Bikiargi motif. The air here is different. Quieter than quiet, as if sound is being absorbed rather than reflected. Your footsteps do not echo.
GM Notes
The church looks like a Bikiargi chapel. The stained glass is a Bikiargi motif. The hymnal in the pews is genuine Bikiargi liturgy, except for the last section, which contains the cipher. The altar's prayer rail slides aside when pressed in sequence (Religion DC 16 to identify the sequence from the cipher). Below the altar, carved into the stone floor, is the cult's true symbol: not the twin moons, but something older, asymmetric, that makes the eye want to slide away from it.
Encounter · ⚔ Moderate 120
4× the Answerers (Creature 6, 30 XP each). The first four things that came through when the society made contact: small, incomplete, and left here because the church is where the door is thinnest. They are why the room has no echo.

They wait in the rafters and the window-glass and drop on whoever reaches for the altar rail. Burning Cold (+17, 2d8 cold + 1d8 persistent cold) outranges most of the party's openers, and Curse of Darkness (Fort DC 23) is the fight's signature: it drains the colour out of a character, permanently until removed, and gives them Light Blindness. A PC who walks out of this church grey is carrying a visible mark of the thing downstairs, and every NPC in Millhaven will notice. Their own Light Blindness cuts both ways: a bright light in the nave is the single best tactical answer the party has.
What's Here
  • The absence of echo is unnatural, a consecration effect for something that is not Bikiargi.
  • Six offering bowls on the altar, five holding dried flowers, one holding a dark dried substance that is not flowers.
  • The cipher in the hymnal: last 12 pages, Bikiargi's lunar symbols used as numbers corresponding to organ keys.

The Rotunda
Bottom Centre · the round room
▾
Read Aloud
The round room is the hinge of the house: every corridor on this floor arrives here eventually. The floor is one enormous burgundy medallion, worked in a pattern that resolves differently depending on where you stand on it. Chairs are set around the curve of the wall, facing inward, spaced with the evenness of something measured rather than arranged. There is no table. There is nothing to sit around.
GM Notes
The room the party will cross most often, and the room they should grow least comfortable in. Nothing happens here on the first pass. On the second, one of the chairs has moved; on the third, a chair is facing the wrong way. Do not explain it and do not attach a mechanic to it. It is the house keeping count.

The medallion pattern is a spiral of fourteen arms, which anyone who has read the names under the dining table will recognise. A character who stands at the centre and looks down has the brief, strong impression of being looked at from directly below, which is correct: the ritual chamber is under this floor, and the coffin sits at the spiral's centre one storey down.
What's Here
  • Standing at the medallion's centre, Perception DC 20: something breathing, faintly, underneath the floor. It is not synchronised with anyone in the room.
  • The chairs are numbered underneath, one to fourteen, in the same hand that carved the dining table.
  • Nothing in this room can be searched to any useful result, and Aldous will let the party search it for as long as they like.

GM Notes
Aldous's post, and where the party meets him (his full read-aloud and behaviour are under The Approach). He returns here between errands, always, and a party that circles the grounds will find him back at the door no matter how they came around. He does not appear to travel between the two points.

Encounter · ⚔ Low–Moderate 80 ◈ Only if forced
Aldous (Elite Creature 9, 80 XP) — if and only if the party attacks him, or tries to force the upper stair. Those are the two triggers. He does not initiate, he does not pursue beyond the threshold, and he stops the moment they do.

He Floats, so difficult terrain and the porch steps mean nothing to him, and he opens with Synaptic Overload (30-ft cone, 4d12 mental, Will DC 25 elite) because it is the most efficient thing available and he is nothing if not efficient. Involuntary Reaction (Reflex DC 28 elite on a Nerve Ending hit) shuts off reactions, which hurts the Guardian more than anyone. He has weakness slashing 5 and no ranged option beyond the cone.

He does not gloat, threaten, or explain. He fights with the same courtesy he answers the door with, and if the party disengages he stops immediately, straightens his coat, and resumes taking messages. Nothing in his manner will ever acknowledge that a fight occurred.
The Top Floor · Map

Veldtmark moved up here five months ago and told the staff it was the light. The upper floor is where he actually lives now, and where almost everything the party needs in order to understand him has ended up: the bed, the mirror, the desk, and the hall he built the circle in.

There is no fight on this floor. That is deliberate. The party arrives here off the back of the dining room, which is the hardest thing above ground, and the basement is waiting below; this storey is the module's breathing room and its investigation payoff at once. If they never come upstairs, they can still finish the quest, and they will finish it understanding roughly a third of what happened.

The church is roofed on this floor — there is no upper storey over it.

This room is lived in, and it is the first room in the house that has been. The bed is unmade. There is a cup on the floor beside it. What's Here
  • Under the bed, a locked iron strongbox (Thievery DC 22): 200 gp, a deed to a property up the coast in the Muino-saila ports, and a letter addressed to "whoever finds this first, if I cannot finish it."

  • The chest at the foot of the bed holds bedding and nothing else. The lock hangs open because he stopped seeing the point of locking it.
  • He has not slept in this bed in some time either. He lies on it. There is a difference, and the impression in the linen shows it: one shape, on top of the covers, always the same way round.

Read Aloud
A washroom in pale tile, scrupulously clean. A bath, a basin, a small table with a razor laid square to its edge. Against the long wall stands a tall mirror in a heavy carved frame, tilted slightly back on its feet. It reflects the room correctly. It reflects you correctly. A slip of paper has been stuck to the bottom corner of the frame, and there is handwriting on it.
GM Notes
The mirror reflects the room accurately in every respect except one: Veldtmark does not appear in it. The note taped to the frame reads "normal, do not worry" and it is in his own handwriting. He wrote it to himself.

Play this straight and do not make it a puzzle. If a PC stands where he would stand, they appear normally. If the party later brings him up here, or fights him in this room, the absence is the fastest way to show a player what has been done to him, and it lands harder than any amount of exposition. Save it for a moment when he is present if you can.
What's Here
  • The razor is sharp, clean, and has not been used in months. He has not needed to shave since roughly the time the church was built, and a Medicine check notices he has no stubble at all.
  • Behind the mirror, wedged between frame and backing: a second slip of paper in the same hand, older, reading only "it started on the eleventh." No year.
  • Every surface has been wiped down. Aldous cleans this room daily, including the mirror, which he also does not appear in.

A long gallery runs the width of the house, windows on one side looking down into the courtyard, portraits on the other. The portraits are all of the same family and they are hung in order. The last four frames on the wall are empty.

The Ritual Hall
West Wing · directly above the dining room
The circle
▾
Read Aloud
The long hall above the dining room has been stripped of everything a hall like this is usually for. A great circle has been laid into the floor in white chalk and salt and something powdered and grey, and it has been laid many times: the current circle is crisp, and beneath it you can see the ghosts of older ones that were swept and redrawn. At the head of the hall and at its foot, mounted high, two long animal skulls face each other down the length of the room. The chalk is fresh. Somebody drew this within the last day.
GM Notes
This is where the fourteen actually meet. The dining room downstairs is the social face of the thing; this is the thing. The room is directly above the dining table, and directly above that spiral of names, and the geometry is not a coincidence: the circle up here, the table below it, and the coffin below that are on one vertical line through the house.

The chalk being fresh is the clock. It tells the party the completion is close without anybody having to say so. If you want to apply pressure to a slow-moving group, have them find this on their way through and let them draw the conclusion themselves.

The powdered grey component in the chalk is bone, and Medicine or Crafting identifies it as such. It is not human. Do not volunteer what it is; the party will assume the worst, and the truth (it came off the skulls, which are ordinary cattle) is a small mercy they have not earned yet.
What's Here
  • The circle is a working, not a summoning: Arcana or Occultism DC 24 reads it as a maintenance diagram, something for keeping an existing arrangement stable rather than opening a new one. That is the bargain being held to its terms.
  • Scuffing or breaking the circle does nothing immediately and is noticed by Veldtmark within the hour, wherever he is.
  • Fourteen positions are marked around the circumference. Six are worn down to the boards. Eight are not.
  • The skulls are cattle, mounted for effect, and were bought from a Millhaven butcher eight months ago. Veldtmark paid far too much for them and the butcher still tells the story.

What Is In This House

Every creature in the estate is a reskin. The numbers are stock Pathfinder blocks and can be run straight off the compendium; the names, the traits and the reasons are the estate's. Nothing in this house is undead, whatever the source book calls it. What Is Actually Happening

Eight months ago, Lord Veldtmark made contact, through rituals his society had refined for years, with something that exists in the space the Cape's drift brushes against. Not a spirit. Not a god. Something that experiences time differently than anything else alive, because it exists between moments rather than within them.

The contact was intoxicating. The thing communicated through sensation rather than words: a feeling of total significance, of being seen across all of time at once. Veldtmark interpreted this as communion. It was closer to the attention of something very large noticing something very small.

The bargain was Veldtmark's idea. He offered the Thing a vessel, worship, and regular feeding, mortal vitality drawn through the cult rituals, in exchange for what he called "the gift of true sight." He wanted to see across time the way it does. Instead he got something closer to standing too close to a furnace: he can sense echoes now, see ghost-images of past moments, feel the future as a pressure behind his eyes. It is destroying him slowly. He believes it is transcendence. He is incorrect.

The Thing in the coffin is not imprisoned. The chains are ceremonial. It remains in the basement because it is not yet strong enough to move freely in mortal time; the feeding has been irregular, the cult is small, and the bargain includes a clause Veldtmark does not fully understand: it cannot act until invited above the threshold by the person who made the original pact. Veldtmark has not yet done this. He is saving it for what he calls "the completion."

What "The Completion" Means
Veldtmark believes that when the Thing is strong enough, he will formally invite it above the threshold, it will grant him true sight, and they will exist in a partnership of mutual elevation. What will actually happen: the Thing will step above the threshold, the bargain will be technically fulfilled, and it will no longer be bound by any of its terms. It will feed freely, move freely, and Veldtmark will be its first unstructured meal. It has no malice toward him. It simply will not need the arrangement anymore.
How Strong Is It Currently?
About 60 to 70% of what it needs to operate freely above the threshold. At current feeding rates it would reach completion in six to eight weeks. The party has arrived at a point where the situation is still containable, barely. If they do nothing and leave, they will be back within two months for something considerably worse.
How This Can End

There is no single correct resolution. The party's choices determine the outcome. Each ending has consequences that can ripple into future sessions.

Destroy the Vessel
Combat Resolution · High Risk

Break the chains, force a confrontation, destroy or drive out the Thing before Veldtmark can complete the ritual. Breaking the chains voids the Thing's obligations. It will not immediately attack; it will assess, then act. Combat at 60–70% strength is survivable for a level 7 party but dangerous. Destroying the vessel does not destroy the Thing; it disperses it back to the between-moments. It will be significantly set back. It may return. Veldtmark, if alive, will be devastated; this was his life's work. The estate's temporal effects fade over weeks, Aldous and the staff slowly recover, the cult disbands or goes underground. The party are heroes, quietly, in a way nobody will officially acknowledge.

Expose Veldtmark
Social / Investigation Resolution

Gather enough evidence to take to the authorities and let institutions handle it. Complicated by the fact that a member of the Merchant Council is in Veldtmark's cult (their name is on the dining-room table). The Stillside Watch has no jurisdiction on this part of the Cape. The Guild can act, but Aldric will need compelling evidence and will be deeply uncomfortable about the Council member. Slower, and creates political fallout. The Thing remains in the basement during the process. Every week of delay is a week closer to completion. Veldtmark is arrested or flees, the cult is exposed, the Council member is a problem, and someone has to actually deal with what is in the basement, which turns out to be the party again.

Save Veldtmark
Difficult Social Resolution · Requires Genuine Effort

Convince Veldtmark of what is actually happening to him before it is too late. This requires the party to dismantle his internal framework carefully, showing him evidence, using his own documentation against his beliefs, possibly using the scrying pool to show him something true. High Diplomacy or Deception checks, and the letter under the bed is the key: if they find it and give it back to him, it cracks something open. A saved Veldtmark becomes a complicated ally: he knows more about the Thing than anyone, he is deeply ashamed, and he is dying slowly from the exposure. He can help them deal with what is in the basement but cannot undo what was done to Aldous, the captives, or himself. Most merciful, most complicated.

Contain and Report
Neutral Resolution · Kicks the Problem Upward

Secure the estate, free the captives, and report everything to the Guild for Head Office to handle. Aldric will accept the report and forward it. Head Office will respond. They will respond slowly. The Thing will be three to four weeks closer to completion by the time anyone arrives. The estate will need watching in the meantime, which is either the party's job or a problem. Head Office's response, when it comes, will involve someone with a perfect smile and cold eyes, the same type of person Aldric warned about. They will handle the Thing efficiently and ask questions about what the party saw that are slightly too specific.

Make a Deal
Chaotic Resolution · High Consequence

Bargain with the Thing directly. It will deal. Any bargain it makes is precisely honoured. Precisely. It understands exchange, not intention. If the party asks for something, they receive exactly that thing with none of the context they assumed. Ask for the captives to be freed and the captives are freed, from the cell, from the estate, from the Thing's influence, and also from about three weeks of continuous memory. Ask the Thing to leave and it will, to the scrying pool, then the organ room, then the dining room. It is very literal about directions. No deal will contain it permanently. Every deal will be honoured. Every deal will have a consequence the party did not account for. This resolution is for the party that has decided they know what they are doing. They do not know what they are doing.

The Letter · Found on Harlen Doss
GM Note

Veldtmark wrote this about four weeks ago, in a morning, while he still had enough of himself left to write coherently. He never sent it. It sat on the desk in his sitting room beside the other letter he could not make himself deliver, and nine days ago Aldous found it, saw a written and sealed letter, and carried it to Harlen Doss's counter, because walking a written letter to the name on it is the correct sequence of actions. He handed it across, said good morning, and left. Doss has not opened it.

Two things keep it sealed, and the party needs both before Doss makes sense. The second: money never occurs to him. The letter offers him one outcome he can imagine and cannot face, and no upside he can see.

Veldtmark Estate · Cape District · written 4 weeks prior · never sent · walked over by Aldous 9 days ago

Harlen,

I am writing this in the morning, which is when I am most myself. I have noticed that I am less myself as the day progresses. I thought you should know that I have noticed this, because it means part of me is still paying attention, and I find that reassuring even if I suspect I should not.

I want to tell you that the work is going well, because it is, by every measure I have established for it. The contact is stronger. The communion deepens every week. What I have described to you as the great work is proceeding exactly as I hoped when I began it. I am not writing to tell you it has gone wrong.

I am writing because I had a dream last night in which I was myself at twenty-three, sitting in the study with my father, and my father asked me what I wanted from my life, and I said I wanted to understand things. He said that was a fine ambition. In the dream I knew he was going to die in three years and I did not tell him. I woke up and I could not remember the last time I thought about my father.

I could not remember the last time I thought about anything that was not the work.

The debt I owe you, the allocation you bought from me last autumn and which I have not filled, I am enclosing a draft for the full amount. You should have it regardless of how things proceed. You have been a good partner and a good friend, and I am aware that I have not been available to you in recent months, and I am sorry for that in a way I am finding it difficult to fully articulate.

I believe what I am doing is important. I believe it is the most important thing anyone has done in this city in a very long time, possibly ever. I believe I am on the threshold of something that will change what it means to understand time and existence and the space between moments. I believe all of this completely.

I am also, Harlen, and I am asking you to keep this between us, as I have asked nothing else of you, I am also afraid. I do not know what I am afraid of exactly. It is not failure. I think it might be success. I think it might be that I will get exactly what I asked for and discover that what I asked for and what I wanted are not the same thing, and that by the time I know the difference it will be too late to say so.

I am most myself in the mornings. I am writing this down so I do not forget that I knew it.

Give my regards to your wife. Come to dinner when this is over; I will have Aldous prepare something good. I owe you a proper meal and a proper conversation, the kind we used to have before I became so thoroughly occupied with becoming something other than what I am.

Your friend,
Caevan

P.S. The draft is enclosed. If you do not hear from me for more than a month, please do not come yourself. Send someone. Send several someones. I am telling you this as a precaution and I am nearly certain it is unnecessary.

GM Notes · Using This Letter

Three moments in this letter do the most work at the table. The first: "I am most myself in the mornings." If the party reads this aloud to Veldtmark, watch his face. He wrote it. He remembers writing it. He has been telling himself for a month that he was being dramatic.

The second: the postscript. He knew. Four weeks ago he knew well enough to write a warning into a letter he told himself was a precaution, and the month he allowed himself in it runs out this week. The party can point out that the precaution was correct, that it reached the one person he told not to come himself, and that it reached him at all only because a butler with nothing left inside him walked it over. Save the best of it for last: the letter told Doss to send someone rather than come himself, and Doss sent five, having obeyed to the letter an instruction he has never read.

The third, and this one is for the party, not Veldtmark: "I think it might be that I will get exactly what I asked for and discover that what I asked for and what I wanted are not the same thing." He diagnosed himself perfectly and proceeded anyway. That is the tragedy, and that is what makes the Save Veldtmark ending feel earned rather than easy. He is not a fool who did not see it coming. He is a man who saw it coming and walked toward it, and the question the party has to answer is whether that makes him more or less worth saving.

A fourth beat belongs to Harlen, and it lands after the party has been to the estate and back. The draft is in the envelope. He has carried the settlement of his own debt against his chest for nine days and spent three hundred and fifty borrowed gold to avoid opening it. If the party is the one to put that in his hands, play it quietly and let him sit down. The contract he posted is answered by the letter he was too frightened to read. Award the fee anyway; he offered it in good faith and would be insulted to have it refused.
