Aldric Fenn
Branch Guildmaster · Bridge Council · Millhaven-born
A bear of a man running a small-town office for the most powerful organisation in the known world, acutely aware of both halves of that sentence. He will buy you a drink, turn you down, and have you thanking him on the way out.

The Man

The Read: big, warm, and immovable; he treats his branch like family and the rest of the world like a problem to be solved with a handshake. You like him at once, and only later notice he gave you nothing he did not mean to.

Physical
Big and weathered, heavy through the shoulders and a little slower than he was, still the largest thing in most rooms. A salt-and-pepper beard kept slightly unkempt, the kind of scarring that tells stories, and the weather-creased face of a man who grew up where the river wind comes off the Smáa. The guildmaster's medallion is real and lives tucked under his collar, so that on first meeting a local sees only the man.
Clothing & Dress
A practical worn guildmaster's coat and boots resoled more times than replaced; he could afford better and sees no reason to own it. He tucks the medallion away by reflex when he meets locals, because he does not want the people he grew up beside afraid of what it represents, and unfolds it only when he needs the organisation in the room instead of the man.
Voice & Mannerisms
A low unhurried rumble, warmest in the half-second before he disappoints you. He keeps a shortsword on the desk and has never once drawn it; he rubs the bridge of his nose when tired and pinches it when he is about to say no; he calls Head Office "the suits" with real feeling. Occasionally volcanic, but the eruption is always in service of a person, never of himself.
He Might Say
"I'm not going to do that. Sit down, have a drink, and let me tell you why you'll thank me later."
What Drives Him
Disposition
Warm and immovable, and both are real. He gives his attention freely and his position almost never.
Background
Born on the Millhaven bridges, on ground that belongs to neither bank, which is exactly the man he turned out to be. He came up through the branch, served his years as a field agent, and took the guildmaster's chair and the bridge council seat without ever once leaving the town he was born in. He runs the most powerful organisation in the known world out of a converted storage shed and works hard to keep the town from fully noticing. The work he brings the party himself is the quiet, off-the-books kind: not illegal, just not for the council's ears.
Goal
Keep the branch standing, the bridge ungoverned, and his people coming home. He votes the Guild's chartered neutrality and turns both banks down with equal warmth, because a Compass that belongs to either bank is a Compass that has stopped being his.
Fear
That the thing he has given his life to is rotten at the top, and that one day the Guild will ask him to choose between the Charter and the people the Charter is supposed to protect. He suspects he already knows which way he would choose, and that it would cost him everything, and he does not look at it directly.

Aldric was offered a Regional Director position three times and turned it down each time. Head Office sent an auditor to find out why. He bought them a beer, explained nothing, and the auditor left confused but oddly at peace.

The real reason is two-fold, and he has never said either half aloud. He knows what Regional Directors are actually asked to do, because the deep records under Velka's hand describe operations the Guild should not have survived, and some of it keeps him up at night. And he is Millhaven-born: the town is the thing he is protecting, and Head Office has never once understood that a man might simply be from somewhere and refuse to leave it. A director travels. Aldric does not. That is the whole answer, and he will let them call it a lack of ambition forever rather than give them the true one.

At the Table · Numbers

Skills, Will, and Perception key off his Non-Combat Level 8 (a veteran administrator who reads people for a living); Combat Level 7 sets AC, HP, Fortitude and Reflex if the bear is ever cornered. He has not been in a real fight in years and is still not someone you want to start one with.

Faith
Casual, as most folk are: a nod to Jianna at the Held Stone like any Millhaven man of business, a Cape-bred glance at Shuun for the river he grew up over, and a private, near-devotional respect for the Charter itself, which is the closest thing he keeps to a creed.
How to Play Him

Warmth first, every time. He is genuinely glad to see almost anyone, pours the drink before the conversation, and makes the person across the desk feel looked after. The warmth is not a tactic; it is just that the immovability underneath it never wavers, so the kinder he is, the more final the answer usually turns out to be.

He solves problems with a handshake and means it. His first instinct is always the deal that lets everyone walk away whole. The shortsword on the desk is symbolic; he would far rather never need it, and on the rare day he raises his voice, it is because someone he is responsible for is at risk, never because his pride is.

He will bend a rule and will not break the Charter, and he knows the difference exactly. Play that line as bright and bone-deep. He will quietly waive a registration fee, lose a piece of paperwork, send a party out the back door; he will not falsify a contract or hand the branch to a faction. When the party pushes on that seam, let them feel the steel under the warmth.

The work he brings is off the books, never off his conscience. When Aldric hires the party himself, it is the quiet kind: not for the council's ears, not against the Charter, the sort of job a man does because the official channels would only make it worse.

The Two Halves He Holds Apart
The man is from here: he drinks at the Mast, he knows your mother's name, the medallion is under his collar. The office is the most powerful organisation in the world, and he unfolds it only when he has to. A party earns the man first. They meet the office on the day they make him reach for it, and that day always means something.
Read Aloud · Turning Down Both Banks
He sets two cups down without asking and takes the third himself. "Bram wants the Compass behind the grain. Dara wants us behind the fleet. And here you both are, certain I've simply not understood the offer." He smiles, and it is warm, and it is the end of the conversation. "I understood it. The answer's the same one it was last year. Drink up, the pair of you; it's a good barrel and the council's already cost me an afternoon."
Read Aloud · When It Stops Being Warm
He does not stand. He pinches the bridge of his nose, once, and when he looks up the warmth has gone somewhere you cannot follow. "Let me be plain, because I'm only going to be plain the once. These are my people. I have buried more of them than you've met. You will not spend one of them to settle something of yours, and if you try, you will find out why Head Office stopped sending people to ask me questions." Then he pours you another, and that is somehow worse.
Who He Holds, Who Holds Him

His visible power is the council seat. His real power is the branch and the six people who run it, and the fact that the town does not quite realise how much weight he carries. For the office itself, see the Wayward Compass dossier ↗.

The Bridge Bloc
Three seats, with Sable Rei (Low Span) and Tomas Hewer (Exchange). Cape and Stillside sit locked at eight apiece, so any motion needs ten of nineteen, so it needs two of the three bridge seats. Aldric votes the Guild's chartered neutrality, which in practice means he votes to keep the bridge ungoverned by either bank, and he is the steady centre the other two orbit.
Sable Rei · the accommodation
They see each other clearly and leave each other alone. Fifteen years ago Aldric scratched the guild's compass-rose mark on the Low Span door and never mentioned it; Sable noticed within a week and never mentioned it either. Neither has called in the favour the silence represents. Aldric knows more or less what Sable is and has decided the town is better off with him in it. (Cross-ref the Deed Question on the Low Span dossier, and Sable's read of the same silence on the Sable Rei dossier.)
Tomas Hewer · the third seat
The scrupulously legitimate Exchange man whose vote makes the bloc function and who drinks more than he used to. Aldric respects him precisely because he is the uncomfortable honest one in a bloc of a guildmaster and a probable pirate, and quietly works to keep the pressure on him survivable.
The Branch · his people
Mira Seld, Velka Dorn, Kael Orvaine, Dova Crestmark, Pip Sallow. He treats them as family, which means he worries constantly and says so never. Mira is the only one who knows what is in the chest under his desk. Velka holds the records that taught him what the Guild is capable of. Kael is the one relationship with a fault line running through it (see Threads). (Full staff on the Wayward Compass dossier.)
Head Office · "the suits"
They have offered him Regional Director three times and been refused three times. They do not understand him and have stopped sending people to find out why. The relationship is functional, dutiful, and quietly braced: Aldric will serve the Guild to the letter of the Charter and trusts the people above that Charter not at all.
Bram Aldover & Dara Wynn · the two banks
The Stillside grain-master and the Cape's former-captain speaker court him constantly and he refuses them with a warmth that infuriates both equally. He likes them both. He will not be owned by either, and the missing-ship crisis is currently testing how long that neutrality can hold.
Watch-Captain Holt Drevyn · the decent man
The overworked Stillside watch-captain with technical jurisdiction over the bridges and no real reach onto them. He and Aldric are two fundamentally decent men who have each privately decided the other is one of the few he can mostly trust, and neither has said so.
Live Threads

Seeds, not fixed canon. Each is a hook the campaign can pull when it wants Aldric's history, his loyalties, or his line to matter.

On the staff-quarters floor, the fourth room stands clean, made up, the window always slightly open. Aldric keeps it that way and does not explain.

Whose room it is, is open: a partner, a child, a field agent who did not come home, the version of himself who took the director's chair. The window left open is the tell. Pull this when the party earns enough trust to be told, or when someone they care about goes missing and they watch Aldric quietly have the room made up again.

On a shelf in his office: a folded map, a small portrait, and a sealed letter addressed to someone he has never named. Under the desk: a locked chest only Mira Seld knows the contents of.

The map, the face in the portrait, and the letter's recipient are three threads of one story you can claim when you need it. The chest is the harder seal: Aldric trusts exactly one person with it, and the day its contents become the party's problem is the day they learn what a Millhaven-born guildmaster keeps where even Head Office cannot reach.

Kael Orvaine lost a full party on a Head Office mission sold to him as low-risk, and he has the real orders. He does not know what he will do if he learns Aldric knew it was compromised.

Whether Aldric knew is the single most dangerous question in the branch, and it is deliberately left open. If he did, he has carried it in silence to protect the one thing he has left, the branch as family, and the cost of that silence is Kael's trust on a fuse. If he did not, then someone above him spent Kael's party and let Aldric take the unspoken blame. Either answer detonates differently. (Cross-ref Kael's secret on the Wayward Compass dossier.)

Head Office has always asked Aldric to take the promotion. The thread is the day they stop asking and start ordering, or stop bothering with him entirely.

His refusals have been tolerated as eccentricity. Pull this when the campaign wants the Guild's upper reaches to lean on Millhaven directly: a director installed over his head, a mission routed around him, an instruction that puts the Charter and his people on opposite sides. This is the scenario his Fear is built around, and the one the party may have to stand in with him.
