Sable Rei
Merchant-Lord · Bridge Council · ex-pirate captain
An ex-pirate captain who got out clean and rebuilt as a merchant-lord. The town is certain he is still a pirate; that misread is the best cover he has.

The Man

The Read: charming, theatrical, introduces himself differently every time; you like him immediately, and that is the point.

Physical
The face Sable wears most is a slim Kitsune man in a well-kept mid-thirties the eyes do not support: silver-black fur, dark through the coat with a cold silver wash at the temples and along the tail, one fox ear out and the other tucked under his hair as if he forgot to finish the disguise. The amber eyes catalogue a room and settle on nothing in it. A hand, a tail, sometimes the line of the jaw flickers to fox for half a sentence and back, never the full change in company, just enough to remind you he could. The smile runs a half-count too many teeth. Under all of it, when he forgets to manage it, sits the stillness of someone who has spent a lot of his life deciding very fast whether a stranger is a problem.
Clothing & Dress
Immaculate, and never quite formal. A high-collared coat cut for movement rather than show, boots that are genuinely excellent and have genuinely run, and exactly one flamboyant note per outfit that turns over by the day: a cravat the colour of a signal flag, a heavy ring on the thumb, a brooch shaped like a ship that is on none of his manifests. Nothing carries a maker's mark you could trace to a city, and every layer has a pocket. He dresses a half-step richer than a bridge merchant and a half-step poorer than he could afford, so you read him as successful rather than dangerous. That is the whole intention.
Voice & Mannerisms
Warm, quick, performative; he shifts register to fit whoever he is working, and the charm thins out of it the moment something matters. He flicks a hand or tail to fox mid-sentence for emphasis and never sits with his back to a door. The tell: the hands stay steady and the tail does not when he is rattled.
He Might Say
"Oh, I have several names. Use whichever one buys you the most trust today. I certainly do."
What Drives Him
Disposition
Friendly, and entirely self-interested. Both are real.
Background
A pirate captain who got out clean and rebuilt as a Millhaven merchant-lord. The fleet registered across six ports is legitimate freight now, and also the laundered paper of a former life. He runs on a captain's code: the crew relies on the captain and the captain relies on the crew, which is why "Rei always comes looking" for his people, every time. The town assumes he is still a pirate lord; that misread is useful cover for the truth, which is that he got out, and the one thing that can drag him back is the name he sailed under.
Goal
Stay out. Keep the papers clean, the bridge bloc ungoverned, and build an information position so complete that no one can ever set a hook in him again.
Fear
Being named, and losing people under his flag a second time. One ledger, one sealed file, one face off a Rika Tikur dock, and the merchant comes apart.

"Sable Rei" is a chosen name. The captain's name is buried in a sealed guild personnel file, and at least one person in Rika Tikur still knows it.

He is genuinely out. The fleet is real freight now; the six-port registration is the laundered paper of a former life. The town's certainty that he is still a pirate lord is the best cover he has, because the truth is more fragile than the rumour: he left, it cost him, and the only thing that can drag him back is the name. He got out clean, and the paper is the only place that stays clean.

How to Play Him

Lead with the warmth; it is genuine. He is fun to be around, quick, generous with attention, and he makes whoever he is talking to feel like the most interesting person in the room. The danger lives underneath and only surfaces when something he cares about is on the line. Play ninety percent charm so the ten percent lands.

The loyalty is the spine. The captain's code is the one fixed thing in him: his people come home. If the party ever become his people, he means it, completely. If they are the reason one of his people does not come home, that is the single thing that switches the charm off.

He is always a step ahead, and it is not luck. He bets on whoever is more interesting and always wins; he was in conversation with someone useful between every bout; he noticed the thing you thought you hid. Let him know one fact he should not, occasionally, and never explain how. A light foxfire touch sits behind it if you want a reason.

He hands out names like calling cards. "Call me Rei." "Sable, please." "Use the full name if you like formality." He never volunteers the real one, and a player who notices he has given three names and confirmed none is reading him correctly.

The Trust Ladder
Where he meets you tracks exactly how far he trusts you. The ground-floor rail of the Low Span: anyone who paid to get in. The second-floor table: you are worth talking business to. The River Room: he trusts you, or he needs you, which for him are adjacent. Moving a party up a rung should always feel earned.
Read Aloud · The Charm
He finds you before you find him. A cup arrives at your elbow that you did not order and is, somehow, exactly what you would have. "You're the contract the Compass has been so quiet about. No, don't tell me, I like the not-knowing. Tell me one true thing and I'll tell you two, and only one of mine will be a lie. Better odds than you'll get anywhere else on this bridge."
Read Aloud · When It Goes Cold
The warmth does not leave so much as switch off, like a lamp. The tail goes still. "Let me be clear, because I am only going to be clear once. The people who work for me come home. That is the entire arrangement. If you are the reason one of them does not, there is no dock in the three seas far enough." Then the smile comes back, and that is somehow worse.
Who He Holds, Who Holds Him

His council seat is the visible power. His real position is the Low Span's lower floors and the information that moves through them. For the establishment itself, see the Low Span dossier ↗.

The Bridge Bloc
Three seats, with Aldric Fenn (Guild) and Tomas Hewer (Exchange). Any motion needs ten of nineteen votes, so it needs two of the three bridge seats; one of them can always defect, and all three know it. Sable votes open water and light oversight, always with a reasonable justification ready.
Aldric Fenn · the accommodation
They see each other clearly and leave each other alone. Aldric scratched the guild's compass-rose mark on the Low Span door fifteen years ago and never mentioned it; Sable noticed within a week and never mentioned it either. Neither has called in the favour the silence represents. (Cross-ref: the Deed Question on the Low Span dossier, where the guild quietly co-owns the place.)
Tomas Hewer · useful, and a little fun
The stressed, scrupulously legitimate third seat whose vote makes the bloc function. Sable finds him genuinely useful and enjoys his discomfort a fraction more than is kind.
Watch-Captain Holt Drevyn · the watcher
Knows exactly what Sable is and can prove none of it. The relationship is cordial, wary, and entirely about who blinks. Sable keeps it that way on purpose.
The Sable Woman · the operative
His headline fighter at the Low Span and a keyholder to the River Room. He always bets on her; she has never spoken to him in public. Run her as his hand at one remove. (Full card on the Low Span dossier.)
The Old Crew · Davo Kenn and the rest
The people who sailed with him now crew the freight. Loyal both ways; Davo has stayed six years on the strength of "Rei always comes looking." They are the living proof of the captain's code, and the soft spot an enemy would aim for.
Rika Tikur · the past
Where his old life lives, and where someone still knows the name. Any thread that reaches the plutocracy island risks reaching him.
Live Threads

Seeds, not fixed canon. Each is a hook the campaign can pull when it wants Sable's past or his reach to matter.

There is a guild personnel file, sealed with a Head Office mark, that holds the captain's name.

Who sealed it, and why the guild protects a former pirate, is open. Pull this when you want the party to hold leverage over Sable they may not understand, or when someone else goes looking for it first. The line he leaves them with: "There's a name you won't recognise on the third page. Don't look it up in the guild records. I'm serious." The name is in the records.

A piece of dangerous correspondence names a bridge councillor by title, not name.

Three seats: Aldric, Sable, Tomas. It reads like Tomas, the obvious quiet collaborator; it could as easily be Sable, using the party to retrieve incriminating paper before anyone else finds it. Leave it unresolved and let the table argue.

He offers exceptional pay, full discretion, and a destination he calls "scenic."

The job is real and the pay is real. The part he does not mention is that the destination has a complicated history with Rika Tikur, and someone there knows his actual name. The job is the trap and the confession at once.

The moment the cavern under the Quiet Docks is cleared, he sees what it is worth.

A cleared, defensible space beneath Millhaven is a far better operation than the Quiet Docks. Whether he asks the party's leave, the guild's, or no one's is a question of how the venom-queen job ends. (See Below the Quiet Docks.)
