The Establishment

The Low Span started as a fighting ring in the southernmost bridge's undercroft about forty years ago, two dockworkers settling a disagreement in front of an audience that was supposed to be three people and became thirty. Someone started taking bets. Someone else started charging admission. By the time anyone thought to ask who owned it, ownership had become a complicated question answered by three different names on three different documents, none of which are Sable Rei.

What it is now: three levels of descending legitimacy beneath the bridge's public face. The ring on the ground floor is completely above board, agreed rules, consenting fighters, betting conducted openly and taxed, theoretically, by the bridge district's self-appointed revenue collector. Below that, through a trapdoor behind the bar, a second floor where the rules are fewer and the stakes are higher. Below that, a third level that is not on any document and where the word "below" starts to feel more like a moral description than a physical one.

Sable Rei does not own it. Sable Rei is simply always there when something important happens, which is a different kind of ownership entirely.

Read Aloud · Arriving
First Visit
You smell it before you find it: sawdust and river damp and the particular kind of noise that only happens when a crowd is watching something they have put money on. The entrance is a door set into the bridge's south face, unmarked except for a small brass compass rose scratched into the wood at eye level. Inside, the ceiling is the underside of the bridge itself, raw timber and iron bolts, and the floor drops away in tiers toward a sand circle thirty feet across, lit by oil lamps on chains that sway with the river movement. The lamps flicker in sequence. Nobody has explained why.
The Crowd
The crowd is three deep at the rail on the best tiers. Cape dock workers, bridge merchants, two people who are clearly neither and trying to look like both. A halfling woman is taking bets from a leather satchel, moving through the crowd with the efficiency of someone who has memorised everyone's face and their credit. Nobody looks at you when you walk in. In a place like this, not being looked at is a form of welcome.
GM Note · The Compass Rose
The compass rose scratched into the door is not the Low Span's mark; it is the Wayward Compass guild symbol, very small, done with a knife rather than a tool. Aldric Fenn put it there about fifteen years ago and has never mentioned it to anyone. Sable Rei noticed it within a week and has never mentioned it either. It means the guild knows this place exists, has always known, and has chosen not to ask questions. A mutual arrangement. It has served everyone adequately.
Three Floors Down

Each level is accessible from the one above. The transitions are not secret, exactly, but they are not advertised. First-timers stay on the ground floor. Return visitors find the trapdoor. What is below that finds you, when it wants to.

01
The Ground Floor · The Ring
Public · Legitimate · Taxed (theoretically)
Above Board
▾

The main floor is exactly what it looks like: a fighting ring, tiered standing room, a bar running the north wall, lamp chains swaying overhead. The ceiling is raw bridge timber. The crowd is mixed and loud. Betting is conducted openly by Marta, the halfling bookkeeper, who has a memory for faces and figures that makes most guild archivists look careless.

  • The Sand Ring · thirty-foot circle, fresh sand before each bout, blood raked under not out
  • The Bar · north wall, three staff, stocks two things the menu does not mention if you know to ask
  • The Tiers · standing room in concentric steps to ring level, best rail is second tier east
  • Marta's Corner · the bookkeeper works from a stool near the entrance, a satchel of coin, a ledger of every bet placed in the last four years
  • The Entrance Door · the compass-rose scratch. Aldric put it there. Sable noticed.
02
The Second Floor · The Real Room
Semi-Private · Grey Market · Card Tables & Fewer Rules
Grey Market
▾

The trapdoor behind the bar opens onto a ladder. The second floor is longer and lower than the ground floor, running beneath the bridge's structural span. Card tables, a second ring with no posted rules, a bar stocked differently. The clientele here knows each other, at least by reputation. New faces are noticed and assessed within thirty seconds of descending the ladder. Sable Rei has a table. It is never reserved. It is always available.

  • The Card Tables · four tables, ongoing games, information exchanged in the language of raised bets
  • The Lower Ring · no posted rules, smaller, the sand darker and not always raked between bouts
  • Sable's Table · southeast corner, sight lines to both the ladder and the south door, always clear
  • The South Door · leads to the River Room, requires a key, the key does not officially exist
  • The Second Bar · stocks what the upstairs bar does not, including two substances of uncertain legal status and one bottle that predates the current Millhaven charter
03
The River Room · The Platform
Invitation Only · Over the Smáa · No Document Exists
Restricted
▾

Not a room. A platform of treated timber cantilevered over the Smáa itself, close enough to the water that river spray reaches it in high current. Sound is impossible to carry here; the current takes it. Six people can stand comfortably. A mooring point for a small vessel. A waterproof document locker built into the piling. This is where things are decided that cannot be decided anywhere else. The party will not see this on a first visit. When they are invited, it will matter.

  • The Platform · treated timber, river spray at high current, six-person capacity, no walls
  • The Mooring Point · iron ring, chain, accepts a vessel up to twenty feet
  • The Document Locker · built into the east piling, waterproof, combination known to three people maximum at any time
  • The Current · makes conversation above a whisper inaudible beyond the platform's edge. Deliberately.
Tonight's Card

The ring runs three to five bouts a night, dusk to midnight, with a late card on Fridays that goes until the lamps run low. The fighters are independent: they contract per bout, take a cut of the betting pool, and are responsible for their own medical arrangements. The Low Span provides the sand, the lamps, and the crowd. It does not provide opinions on outcomes.

Sable Rei always bets on whoever is more interesting, regardless of odds. This is known, frequently imitated, and almost as frequently regretted by imitators who lack Sable Rei's specific judgment about what interesting means in a fight.

GM Note · The Fights as Scene Texture
The ring is not a combat encounter; it is atmosphere and timing. Use the fight in the background of the Sable Rei conversation to control pacing: a good hit and the crowd's reaction gives Sable a moment to think before answering a hard question; the end of a bout is a natural scene break. If the party wants to bet, let them; the odds above are the current card, outcomes are GM's discretion. Sable always wins their bet. This is not luck.
Regulars & Staff

Marta
Bookkeeper · Ground Floor
Tap ▾
A halfling woman in her 50s who has run the Low Span's books since before Sable Rei was involved. Precise, warm, and in possession of a four-year ledger of every bet placed here.
She does not share the ledger. She will tell you the odds on anything, including things not currently on the card, if you know to ask. She has a very clear sense of who in Millhaven has a gambling problem and quietly adjusts their credit limits without being asked by anyone. A party she has watched across a couple of visits may find a small folded note pressed on them: a correct tip on the next bout, source unexplained. She does this exactly twice; the third note is not about a fight.
⚔️
The Sable Woman
Fighter · Headliner Regular · no name given
Tap ▾
She fights under no name; the card just says "The Sable Woman" and has for three years. Exceptional, efficient, and wins in a way that suggests she could end bouts earlier than she does and chooses not to.
Sable Rei always bets on her. She has never spoken to Sable Rei in public. She has a key to the south door on the second floor. These facts are not connected, officially. A PC who watches her fight closely (a high Perception check) sees her holding back in a very specific way — like someone who learned where the stakes were lethal, and has decided this ring is exactly as much exposure as she wants.
🍺
Cord
Second Floor Barkeeper
Tap ▾
A large, quiet human man who has tended the second-floor bar for eight years and in that time has heard approximately everything decided in Millhaven's grey economy. He has never repeated any of it.
He is not loyal to Sable Rei, or anyone else, in any way that could be demonstrated. He is loyal to the bar, which is a distinction that has served him well. He serves anyone who comes down the ladder without comment, and will not serve anyone twice who has caused a problem. Once, for a party he has decided is neither stupid nor dangerous in the wrong direction, he will answer a single direct question about what he has heard: one accurate sentence, no elaboration.
🦊
Sable Rei
Bridge Council · Merchant Lord · always at the rail
Tap ▾
Present most nights, standing at the second-tier rail of the ground floor like anyone else who paid to get in. Different face on different nights. Same coat. Always the same coat.
Bets on whoever is more interesting regardless of odds, collects their winnings without counting them, and is in conversation with someone between every bout if you watch closely enough to notice. The silver-streaked face, a younger darker one, occasionally something that splits the difference in a way that suggests they stopped being careful. On a third or later visit, if the party has handled itself well, Rei drifts to the rail beside them between bouts and, without looking over: "You keep coming back. That means you like it here, or you're learning something. Either way, come find me downstairs after the headliner." The south door will be unlocked.
What the Low Span Actually Is

The fighting ring is real. The gambling is real. Everything below the second floor is also real, and considerably more interesting, and the party will only access it if they are invited or very clever or both.

Through the trapdoor behind the bar, down a ladder that was not built for people in a hurry, a second room runs the length of the bridge underbelly.

Card tables, a second ring with no rules written anywhere, and a bar that stocks things the upstairs bar does not acknowledge carrying. The second floor is where bridge-district business actually happens: contracts discussed at card tables, information exchanged in the language of raised bets and folded hands. Sable Rei has a standing table here that is never officially reserved and always available.

The third level is not a room. It is a platform over the Smáa itself, accessible only through a door in the second floor's south wall that requires a key nobody admits to having.

The platform has a mooring point, a waterproof document locker built into the piling, and enough space for six people to have a conversation the river current ensures no one above can hear. This is where Sable Rei conducts business that cannot be discussed anywhere else. The party will not access this level on a first visit. They will, eventually, be invited here, and the invitation will mean something significant has changed in how Sable Rei regards them.

The Low Span's deed is held in three names, and the Wayward Compass is quietly one of them.

A retired Cape dockmaster named Holt who died eight years ago and whose name was never removed; a woman named Cress who operates a candle shop on the Stillside and has never visited the Low Span in her life and receives a small quarterly payment she has never questioned; and a guild holding entity registered under the Wayward Compass that Aldric Fenn signed into existence fifteen years ago and has not mentioned to anyone, including his own staff. The guild technically co-owns the Low Span. This has never come up. It will, eventually.

Information, primarily. The Low Span is the best place in Millhaven to learn something.

Not because anyone tells you things, but because everyone in the bridge district passes through eventually and people talk when they are watching a fight. Vara Mink sends someone on Tuesdays to sit at the second-floor bar and listen. Dova Crestmark has never visited but receives a written summary every two weeks from a source she has never named to anyone. Kael Orvaine comes once a month to watch the fights, does not speak to anyone, and leaves before the headliner. Nobody has asked him about this.
