Below the Quiet Docks
The cargo went down. The crew went looking. Nobody came back up.
Party Level 53–5 SessionsBeneath Millhaven · Cavern SystemDungeon Crawl + DiscoveryContract: Sable Rei
The Contract

The contract appears on the quest board one morning, posted by hand, no guild intermediary, no formal submission. Mira Seld received it directly from a Kitsune in a very good coat who set it on her desk, smiled with too many teeth showing, and said "the usual terms" as if there were usual terms. There are now.

The pay is exceptional. The listed objective is simple: locate a shipment of unspecified cargo offloaded via the Quiet Docks three nights ago and not collected. The retrieval team Sable Rei sent to find it has also not returned. The contract requests "recovery of assets and personnel, in that order of priority." The last line is crossed out and rewritten: "personnel first. assets if convenient."

Read Aloud · Quest Board
"Missing: one consignment of goods, offloaded via private dock, bridge district, three nights past. Also missing: four-person retrieval team sent to locate same. Contract offers 800gp upon recovery of personnel, 400gp additional upon recovery of cargo. Enquire with Mira Seld for access details. Discretion absolute. S.R."
GM Note · What Sable Rei Actually Knows
The cargo was a crate of documents: correspondence between a Rika Tikur merchant house and a member of the Legea Empire's trade council, the kind of letters worth significant money to the right buyer and significant danger to the wrong one. Sable Rei does not know what is down there. They know the retrieval team is good and has not checked in for two nights. The fox is genuinely worried, which they express by posting an extremely professional contract and not sleeping. If the party talks to them before going in, Sable Rei provides the access route to the Quiet Docks and a detailed description of the retrieval team. They will not mention what the cargo contains; they answer "sensitive documents" if pressed and change the subject.
GM Note · Aldric's Position
Aldric will approve this contract with the expression of a man biting into something he expected to be sweet. He will remind the party that the guild takes no position on the legality of the cargo and will not be liable for what they find down there. He will also, quietly, tell them to be careful, not because of Sable Rei, but because four experienced people went into a hole under his town and have not come out. That bothers him more than the politics.
What Is Actually Down There

The cavern system beneath the Smáa's eastern bank has existed for centuries, old enough to predate Millhaven, old enough that parts of it show stonework from an era nobody has cleanly identified. Smugglers found it about two years ago via a drainage collapse near the Quiet Docks and have been using its upper chambers as a staging area ever since. The operation was modest, functional, and entirely unaware that the deeper caverns were already occupied.

The Venom Queen, the party's name for her is as good as any, she has no name she offers, is a Giant Spider of unusual age and unusual awareness. She did not choose the upper caverns as her territory because they were useful; she chose them because the bioluminescent spore colonies on the ceiling are her eggs, her larder, and her early warning system. The smugglers moved into her antechamber. She noticed immediately. She waited.

Three nights ago, a cargo drop disturbed a web sensor in the upper warehouse. The Queen sent three of her brood. The smugglers panicked, ran deeper into the cavern trying to find another exit, and found only more of her territory. She has been feeding since. The retrieval team arrived the following night, descended the Quiet Dock access, and has not been seen since.

The Queen's Intelligence
She is not sapient. She does not speak, plan in the abstract, or have goals beyond territory, feeding, and the protection of her eggs. What she has is awareness: she knows how many creatures are in her cavern at any moment, she remembers what hurt her and what did not, she adapts her approach between encounters, and she uses her brood as extensions of her senses. By the time the party reaches her chamber, she knows exactly how many of them there are, which direction they came from, and roughly how dangerous they are. She has been preparing since they entered. She is not angry. She is efficient.

Getting Down
Read Aloud · The Quiet Docks, Access Hatch
The hatch is iron, set into the dock's lowest platform, above the waterline by a handspan. It opens onto a ladder descending into dark and the smell of cold stone and river damp. Someone has been here recently: the hatch's locking bar is drawn back and there are scuff marks on the rungs. At the bottom of the ladder, the light from above reaches just far enough to show you a passage heading east into the rock. Then it stops reaching.
GM Note · The Access Ladder
The ladder descends 40 feet into the first passage. It is in reasonable condition; the smugglers maintained it. A DC 14 Perception check on the way down notices a thin thread of silk crossing the passage at chest height, about 20 feet below the hatch. It is not a tripwire in a mechanical sense. It is a web sensor; disturbing it alerts the Queen to a new entry. The party almost certainly triggers it. That is fine. She already knows they are coming from the moment they opened the hatch; the vibrations in the dock structure carry to her web network. The sensor just confirms numbers.
GM Note · Light and Sound
The upper passages are dark except for faint bioluminescent spore-glow filtering from the main cavern ahead. The party will need light unless they have darkvision. Sound carries unusually well in the stone passages; a dropped object in the upper warehouse can be heard in the deep cavern as a faint tap. The Queen uses this. If the party is loud, encounters in the next chamber will have her brood pre-positioned. If they are quiet, they buy a round of surprise. Tell them this through the environment: their footsteps sound too loud to them. They are right.
Upper Cavern · Smuggler Territory

The upper sections were in active use until three nights ago. The smugglers were competent people running a modest operation, and the remnants of their work are still here, along with evidence of what happened to them.

Read Aloud
The room smells of sawdust and river-damp and something underneath both that you would rather not identify. Crates and barrels line three walls, some opened, some stacked in the organised fashion of people who expected to come back. A torch has burned itself down to the last finger of wax on the far wall. GM Notes
The smugglers' primary staging area. The torch has been burning for three days; a long-burn alchemical variety, not magical. The handprint is dried blood (DC 12 Medicine confirms human). A DC 16 Survival check reads the scene: multiple people ran east in a hurry, one fell, got up, kept running. The cargo crate is here, buried under a collapsed shelf on the north wall (DC 16 Perception). The Queen did not take it. She does not care about crates.
What's Here
  • The cargo crate: buried under collapsed shelving, DC 16 Perception, contains the documents
  • A smuggler's ledger on a barrel top: three weeks of entries, last entry three nights ago, reads only "something in the walls"
  • The eastern passage has a fresh web membrane across it, thin enough to walk through, leaves a silk residue. The Queen knows they found it.
  • One barrel holds not smuggled goods but someone's personal effects: a portrait, letters, a Guild badge from a branch in the River Duchies

Read Aloud
The left wall of the passage opens onto the underground canal, a channel of dark river water running south, fast enough to hear but not fast enough to see move. Planks have been laid across it at intervals, some still in place, one broken and trailing in the current. On the far bank, half-visible in the dark: a shape that might be a person, seated, not moving.
GM Notes
The canal connects to the Smáa above and eventually to the coast, a smuggler's emergency exit never fully developed. The shape on the far bank is one of the smugglers, cocooned in webbing and still alive (DC 14 Medicine confirms pulse). Reaching them requires crossing the planks (DC 12 Acrobatics) or swimming (DC 16 Athletics against the cold current). The brood uses the canal passage to move between the upper and lower cavern; a DC 18 Perception check notices fresh silk markings at the waterline showing their route.
What's Here
  • The cocooned smuggler is Petra Voss, alive, unconscious, been here two days
  • The broken plank was broken by something heavy moving across it quickly, spider-sized
  • Following the canal south leads to a grate above the Smáa, escapable, but the party would emerge in the river, in the dark, possibly mid-combat
  • Silk route markers at the waterline: DC 18 Perception reveals how many brood passed through in the last 24 hours

Read Aloud
The cavern opens above you like a held breath releasing. The ceiling. The ceiling moves. Slowly, with the rhythm of something sleeping, thousands of pale violet lights pulse across every surface above you. Egg clusters. Hundreds of them. The floor below is open stone littered with the remnants of the smuggler operation: broken crates, a dropped lantern, a boot with a foot still in it. Against the far wall, three shapes hang wrapped in silk at head height, turning slowly in no discernible breeze.
GM Notes
Three brood spiders (Creature 3, use Giant Spider stats at -1 level) are here, two visible on the ceiling edge, one hidden behind a ceiling cluster (DC 19 Perception). They will not attack immediately if the party moves slowly and does not approach the cocoons. If the party approaches the cocoons or makes a sudden movement, they drop. Two cocooned figures are dead smugglers, two days gone. The third is alive: Davo Kenn, one of Sable Rei's retrieval team, weak but conscious.
What's Here
  • Davo Kenn: alive, third cocoon from left, knows the other three retrieval team members went deeper
  • The dropped lantern is still functional; someone dropped it running, not falling
  • A smuggler's crossbow, loaded, on the floor; the owner did not make it to use it
  • The egg clusters: destroying one triggers the Queen's awareness and her emotional response. The party should understand this before they are in her chamber.

Read Aloud
Someone built something here with real intent: work tables, a ladder descending through a hole in the floor, barrels of materials organised by type. The work surface holds tools, half-finished mechanisms, and a journal open to a page that has been read so many times the ink is worn faint. Whatever this place was for, it was a going concern three days ago.
GM Notes
The smugglers' working area; they were modifying the cargo before handoff, more sophisticated than simple moving. The journal belongs to a tinker named Moss, who will not be found alive. His notes describe the operation professionally and then, in the last six entries, describe something moving in the lower passages. His final entry: "it's not rats. the webs are fresh every morning. someone is tending them." The ladder descends to the deep cavern. It is intact. A web spans the hole about 15 feet down, visible from the top with a light source.
What's Here
  • Moss's journal: six entries of professional notes, six of increasing dread, final entry the discovery of fresh webs
  • The ladder descends 20 feet; the web below is a sensor, not a trap, but breaking it alerts the Queen
  • One barrel holds a contact-numbing agent used in document preservation that is also mildly effective against spider venom (DC 16 Arcana or Nature to identify; +1 item bonus to Fortitude saves against venom for 1 hour)
  • The work table has a hidden drawer (DC 18 Perception) with Moss's personal correspondence, including a letter to someone in Rika Tikur he meant to mail "when this job is done"
Deep Cavern · The Queen's Territory

Past the main cavern ceiling the environment changes. The bioluminescent ceiling thickens to a carpet of glowing spores, the Queen's egg clusters, slowly pulsing. The stonework here is older, pre-smuggler, and carved rather than natural. Something was here before her. She moved into an existing structure.

Read Aloud
The passage opens into a series of chambers where the floor has given way to water, shallow in some places, deep in others, glowing with the same blue-green as the ceiling above. Stone columns rise from the water, carved with a repeating motif you do not have a name for. Between two columns: a backpack, half-submerged, its contents spilling into the water. Someone moved through here in a hurry.
GM Notes
The water glows because of a bioluminescent organism, harmless, beautiful, and useful for visibility in this section. Depth varies: knee-deep near the columns, over-head deep in the centre pool (DC 14 Athletics to cross without going under). The backpack belongs to one of the retrieval team, Lena Marsh, who made it through this section. Her trail continues east toward the statue room. She is not found alive. The column motif matches the stonework in the statue room: same era, same unknown culture. A DC 22 History check begins to connect this to something but cannot place it definitively.
What's Here
  • Lena Marsh's backpack: her supplies, a coded note in Sable Rei's hand, and a partial map of the upper cavern she drew on the move. Lena is not found, only the backpack.
  • The centre pool: deep, cold, and something moves in it, not hostile, a blind cave-fish species, but in the dark it feels significant
  • Column carvings: a spiral terminating in a shape that resembles an open hand, repeated twelve times around each column
  • The glowing water: a small vial provides 10 minutes of dim light, useful, memorable, and Dova will want to know where they got it

Read Aloud
Eight figures stand in two rows facing each other across a central aisle, carved in stone darker than the surrounding rock as if brought in from elsewhere. They are not human. Not quite. The proportions are close enough to be unsettling rather than alien: taller, narrower, with expressions that are either serene or empty depending on where the light hits. At the far end of the aisle, a natural stone dock and a small boat, wood-and-hide, intact, with two sets of footprints in the silt beside it.
GM Notes
The statues are old; History DC 22 estimates pre-Golden Empire, possibly pre-Lost Era. The figures match no known cultural tradition in the guild's records. Two of the eight have had their faces deliberately damaged recently, chisel marks fresh enough to show clean stone beneath. Someone who came through recently did not like being watched. The boat is functional and connects to an underground water route that eventually surfaces near the river mouth, a secondary exit the smugglers did not know about but the retrieval team found. Two sets of footprints lead to the boat. Only one set leads away, toward the Queen's chamber.
What's Here
  • The defaced statues: fresh chisel damage, not spider-related; someone came here deliberately to do this
  • The boat: functional, two-person capacity, route to the river mouth takes about 30 minutes in the dark
  • One set of footprints to the boat and back: someone considered leaving, did not
  • Behind the rearmost statue: a small carved alcove with an object wrapped in oilskin, here decades at minimum, unrelated to the current smugglers (a sealed letter in a language no one in the party can immediately read)

⚠ Hazard⚔ Combat
Read Aloud
The passage narrows and the walls become silk. Not draped in it: silk from floor to ceiling, woven so densely that the stone beneath is invisible. The floor is tacky underfoot. The light from the egg clusters above is filtered through layers of web into something softer and more diffuse, like standing in fog that glows. There is no sound except what you make. Something is very aware of what you are making.
GM Notes
Heavily webbed, difficult terrain throughout. Every 10 feet of movement requires a DC 14 Acrobatics check or the character is Grabbed (DC 18 Athletics to Escape). Two Brood Spiders (Creature 3) are embedded in the wall silk (DC 22 Perception to spot before they move). They will not attack until the party is at least halfway through, then drop to create maximum entanglement. A designed killing ground. The Queen has used it before.
What's Here
  • The silk walls can be burned; fire clears 10ft squares and reveals stone, but the smoke is acrid and causes Sickened 1 for 1 round in anyone who breathes it
  • Embedded in the silk wall halfway through: one of the retrieval team, Lena Marsh, alive, badly dehydrated, here 36 hours
  • The passage ends at an arch with the symbol from the upper statues carved into the keystone; the Queen did not carve it, it was here when she moved in
  • Sound from the Queen's chamber is audible here, not sound exactly, a vibration in the web the party can feel through their feet

The Venom Queen
Read Aloud · First Sight
The ceiling of the final chamber is entirely alive. Every surface above you moves with the slow pulse of something breathing: egg clusters, hundreds of them, glowing violet-white in a light that makes everything below look like it is underwater. At the centre of the chamber, suspended in a web that spans wall to wall like the floor of a second room above yours, she hangs. Eight eyes. Each one separately focused. She has been watching you since you entered the cavern. She makes no move toward you. She is simply letting you understand the situation before it begins.

  • Egg Cluster Destruction Each egg cluster destroyed (AC 14, HP 12, immune to piercing) causes the Queen to take 2d6 psychic damage and lose her reaction until the start of her next turn. She immediately prioritises the creature that destroyed it. This is not rational. It is the one thing that overrides her tactical awareness.
  • Web Platform The Queen's web spans the upper half of the chamber. Creatures on the web treat it as difficult terrain. The Queen ignores this. Webs can be cut (DC 20 Athletics) or burned (fire drops sections). A fully burning web section forces the Queen to relocate and deals 3d6 fire damage to her.
GM Note · Running the Queen
She opens by throwing web at whoever looks most dangerous: the heaviest armour, the biggest weapon. She will not drop from the ceiling immediately; she observes for one round if the party hesitates, positioning for a Ceiling Drop on whoever moves first. She uses Brood Call only when taking significant damage, not as an opener; she conserves it. She targets isolated characters preferentially. If a character destroys an egg cluster, all of that tactical patience evaporates and she pursues that character single-mindedly for 1d3 rounds before her awareness reasserts. This is the lever the party can pull if they are struggling.
GM Note · Retreat and Pursuit
The Queen will not leave her egg chamber voluntarily. If the party retreats, she lets them go; they are no longer in her territory. She will, however, completely reseal the passage with fresh webs within the hour. A retreating party can rest outside the cavern system and return; the encounter resets her HP but not her Adaptive Memory. She remembers what worked on her. She positions differently the second time.

What Happens After

The cavern system does not stop being a problem when the Queen is dead. It stops being an immediate problem. Everything else remains.

If the Queen Is Killed
The egg clusters survive her death and will hatch in about three weeks without her to regulate them. The juvenile spiders will be individually manageable but collectively a significant infestation problem beneath Millhaven. Someone needs to deal with the eggs: either a follow-up contract or a problem Aldric quietly asks the party to handle "since you were already down there." The cavern is now a known, cleared space, and Sable Rei will immediately recognise its potential as a far better base than the Quiet Docks. Whether the Guild knows about this use is a question for later.
If the Queen Is Driven Off (Retreated Below)
The deeper passages continue below the egg chamber; the map shows only the upper system. She retreats into whatever is further down, which is uncharted. The upper cavern is safe. The question of what is below it remains open. A hook for a later, deeper delve at higher level.
The Documents
The cargo crate is in the warehouse room, buried under collapsed shelving from the initial panic. The documents inside are water-damaged but legible. They are exactly as politically dangerous as Sable Rei implied: correspondence implicating a Legea Empire trade official in smuggling that technically constitutes economic warfare against Merkavar's markets. Sable Rei pays in full and asks no questions about whether the party read them. The party almost certainly read them. What they do with that knowledge is their business. It is, however, notable that one of the letters references a contact in Millhaven by title rather than name: "our friend on the bridge council."
The Pre-Smuggler Stonework
The carved stone in the deep cavern predates the smuggler operation by centuries, possibly by much more. A History check in the statue room puts it contemporaneous with the late Golden Empire or earlier. The statues depict figures that do not match any known culture's aesthetic. Professor Odo Mast, if the party rescued him from the Veldtmark estate, will be intensely interested in rubbings of the stonework and will connect them, eventually, to something he found in a very old text. That connection is a later thread. Plant it now.
The Bridge Council Contact
The letter references "our friend on the bridge council." There are three bridge council members: Aldric Fenn, Sable Rei, and Tomas Hewer. Sable Rei posted the contract and therefore either does not know about this letter or wants the party to find it for reasons of their own. Aldric is Aldric. That leaves Tomas Hewer, the legitimately stressed legitimate businessman, or it is a misdirect and it is Sable Rei after all, using the guild to retrieve incriminating evidence before anyone else finds it. Leave this unresolved. Let the party argue about it.
