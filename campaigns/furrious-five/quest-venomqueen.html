Below the Quiet Docks
The cargo went down. The crew went looking. Nobody came back up.

The Contract
Read Aloud · Quest Board, the Wayward Compass
"Missing: one consignment of goods, offloaded via private dock, bridge district, three nights past. Also missing: four-person retrieval team sent to locate same. Contract offers 800gp upon recovery of personnel, 400gp additional upon recovery of cargo. Enquire with Mira Seld for access details. Discretion absolute. S.R."
GM Note · What Sable Rei Knows
The cargo is a crate of documents: correspondence between a Rika Tikur merchant house and a member of the Legea Empire's trade council, worth significant money to the right buyer and significant danger to the wrong one. Sable Rei does not know what is down there. They know the retrieval team is good and has not checked in for two days. The fox is genuinely worried and expresses it by posting a flawless contract and not sleeping. They provide the Quiet Dock access route and a description of the four-person team; they will not say what the cargo contains ("sensitive documents") and change the subject.
GM Note · Aldric's Position
Aldric Fenn approves it with the face of a man biting something he expected to be sweet. The Guild takes no position on the cargo's legality and will not be liable for what they find. Quietly, he tells them to be careful: four experienced people went into a hole under his town and have not come out. That bothers him more than the politics.
What Is Actually Down There

The cavern beneath the Smáa's eastern bank is older than Millhaven, old enough that parts of it show worked stone from a people nobody has cleanly named: an Old Crypt of five effigy-tombs and a sealed Old Prison whose only record rots in a forgotten tongue. Smugglers found the upper passages two years ago through a drainage collapse and ran a modest operation out of them, never knowing what shared the dark.

Three powers hold the deep now, and they do not love each other. The Venom Queen, a giant spider of unusual age and awareness, nests in the great chamber and her brood hunt the halls. The water that feeds the cavern through old sewer pipes runs thick with brine-shark elementals, and a single life-spring is warded by them. And in a side-cave, fat on fresh kills, a Behir sleeps. The smugglers' cargo drop three nights ago woke the spiders; the retrieval team that followed is scattered through the caves, the lucky ones hiding, the rest feeding the dark.

And in the Old Crypt, a fourth thing has woken: the smugglers' tinker, Moss, robbed an effigy-tomb on his way through, and the Tomb-Keeper has risen to answer the theft.

The Queen's Intelligence
She is not sapient: no speech, no abstract plans, only territory, feeding, and her eggs. What she has is awareness. She knows how many creatures are in her chamber, remembers what hurt her, adapts between rounds, and uses her brood as extra senses. By the time the party reaches her, she knows their number, their direction, and roughly their threat. She is not angry. She is efficient. Her one blind spot is her eggs (see The Queen).

The Venom Queen · Room 13
Read Aloud · First Sight
The chamber's ceiling is alive. Every surface above you moves with the slow pulse of something breathing — egg clusters, hundreds of them, glowing violet-white in a light that makes everything below look drowned. At the centre, suspended in a web that spans wall to wall like the floor of a second room above yours, she hangs. Eight eyes, each separately focused. She has been watching you since you entered the cavern. She makes no move. She is letting you understand the situation before it begins.

The Encounter · escalating set-piece
Start at Moderate on the field: the Queen (80 XP) + 4 Hunting Spiders already in the chamber (40) = 120. Then Brood Call adds 2 Hunting Spiders per recharge, climbing through Severe (150) and toward Extreme (200) the longer the fight runs. The math: budgets for 5×L5 are Severe 150 / Extreme 200; the Queen is 80, so every two called spiders add ~20. Do not dump twelve spiders on the table at once — let the clusters feed them in, and let the party choke the supply by destroying egg clusters (Egg-Rage), which both hurts her and breaks her tactics. Oryn is cocooned on the web platform here (NPCs), alive; the Queen positions near him when the party reaches for him, using him as deterrence without understanding that she is.
GM Note · Running Her
She opens with a Web Throw at the most dangerous-looking PC, then waits one round if the party hesitates, positioning for a Ceiling Drop on whoever moves first. She conserves Brood Call for when she is pressed. She targets isolated characters. The instant a cluster dies, her patience evaporates and she pursues that PC for 1d3 rounds — the lever a struggling party pulls. She will not leave the chamber; a retreating party she lets go, then reseals the breach with fresh web within the hour (HP resets, Adaptive Memory does not).
The Old Crypt · Room 3 · the Keeper's Grudge
Read Aloud
Five stone effigies lie on five carved biers, hands folded, faces worn smooth by time and not by weather. They are of no people you know, and they were here long before the smugglers, long before the town. Above the centre effigy's head, a space in the air is empty in a way that feels recent — a small orbit with nothing in it. Then the cold arrives, and the centre effigy turns its smooth stone face toward you, and a voice that has not used breath in an age says one word. Thief.
GM Note · What Happened Here
Moss broke in first, disturbed the tombs, and stole the relic that orbited the central effigy (the Aeon Stone (Crescent), now on his corpse in the Abandoned Room). The theft woke the Tomb-Keeper and its four interred guards. The Keeper meets every intruder as the thief returning. The empty orbit over the central bier is the tell.
◈ Settling the Grudge — a multi-stage challenge
DCs anchored on the Keeper (a Creature-9 undead), eased to the table per the cheatsheet
1 · Read the room — Recall Knowledge (Religion) DC 18. Success: these are ancient keepers, risen and wronged; the relic is gone; the Keeper is Site Bound and Rejuvenates — you cannot end it with steel. Crit: you grasp that it wants the thief answered for.
2 · Be heard, not struck — Diplomacy (or Deception) DC 18. Open parley before violence. Failure: the four Skeleton Guards animate and the Keeper attacks.
3 · Settle it — Diplomacy or Society, DC 18 with Moss's journal in hand; DC 28 (effectively impossible) without it. Present the journal — Moss's theft, his confession, his death. Returning the stolen relic itself is an automatic success.
Settled: justice is done (the thief is dead, the record set straight). The Keeper lays its guards down, ceases to Rejuvenate, and lifts the curse on the Crescent, granting it freely with the one-time Tomb-Keeper's Blessing (before leaving the cavern, one PC may reroll a failed save).
Fought instead: the skeletons shatter, but the Ghost reforms in 2d4 days — force never ends it — and the Crescent stays cursed until the grudge is laid. Three reasons not to fight: it is deadly, it cannot be killed, and the prize is poisoned.

The Fight, If They Insist · Extreme
The Tomb-Keeper (Creature 9, 160 XP / PL+4) plus the four interred rising as Skeleton Guards (C−1, extra bodies and actions) reads as an Extreme encounter and is meant to. The Keeper hits for ~24 a touch, Moans the party into fleeing, and cannot be permanently destroyed. This is the design saying, plainly: do not fight this. Settle it. If you want it merely Severe, drop the Keeper to Creature 8 (PL+3, 120 XP).

The documents
objective
The contract cargo: Rika Tikur / Legea Empire correspondence, water-damaged but legible. The cargo crate, Smuggler Storage. 400 gp on delivery; politically radioactive if read.

Taken from Moss without settling the Tomb-Keeper, the aeon stone carries the Keeper's wrath on stolen grave-goods. While cursed it orbits but will not be stowed or removed (invested, stuck), gives none of its benefits (no light, no Moonbeam, no forbidding ward), and its bearer suffers the Keeper's regard: −1 status penalty to all saves, no benefit from rest while in the cavern (fatigued), and undead in the dark are drawn to them.

Settling the grudge (The Crypt) lifts the curse instantly: the stone wakes to full power, and the bearer gains the one-time Tomb-Keeper's Blessing — before leaving the cavern, one PC may reroll a failed save. The party can recover the stone any time; making it theirs requires laying the grudge.

What Happens After
The Documents
Water-damaged but legible: correspondence implicating a Legea Empire trade official in smuggling that amounts to economic warfare against Merkavar's markets. Sable Rei pays in full and asks nothing about whether the party read them. They did. One letter references a Millhaven contact by title, not name: "our friend on the bridge council." Three sit that council — Aldric Fenn, Sable Rei, and Tomas Hewer. Leave it unresolved; let the party argue.
If the Queen Is Killed
The egg clusters outlive her and hatch in about three weeks without her to regulate them — a brood infestation under Millhaven that becomes a follow-up contract or something Aldric quietly asks the party to handle. The cleared cavern is, to Sable Rei, a far better base than the Quiet Docks; whether the Guild ever learns of that use is a later question.
If the Queen Is Driven Off
She reseals the breach and retreats into the dark below the egg chamber, uncharted. The upper caves are safe; what lies further down is a hook for a deeper delve at higher level.
The Tomb-Keeper
If settled, it rests and the Crypt goes quiet. If only fought, it reforms in 2d4 days and the curse rides out with the stone — the ghost will reach for its bearer in dreams (Lena Marsh is the warning) until the grudge is finally laid, which may take a return trip. The forgotten-language thread (Old Prison) and the effigy-people tie to the same pre-Golden-Empire culture the Cape's secrets circle; bring rubbings to Professor Odo Mast for a later arc.
