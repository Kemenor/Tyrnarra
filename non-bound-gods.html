Non-Bound Gods & Beings
Layer 1 Primordials · Layer 2 Sibling-Planes · Layer 3 Residents
The thirteen Bound Gods reside on the Material Plane under the Gods' Law. Beyond them, divinity continues: Layer-1 primordials tied to the substrate of Prelife; the sibling-plane primordials of Feyworld and Shadowplane; and the Layer-3 gods whose portfolios reach Talan from across the veil. None are bound by the Compact. The infernal orders of Abyss have their own home: Bolverk.
Non-Bound Gods
Belief-Shaped · Sancta-less on Talan

Layer 1 (Prelife) and Layer 3 (Postlife) hold many gods and beings beyond the Thirteen, unbound by the Gods' Law. They are belief-shaped: created and sustained by mortal prayer, rite, and the steady weight of folk practice. None maintain a sanctum on Talan; faith reaches them across the veil, and they answer through grants and dreams.

Prelife · Layer 1 · PrimordialPlane-Bound Embodiments · The Seven of Prelife · Predate Mortal Belief

Layer 1 (Prelife) hosts primordial beings whose portfolios are tied to the substrate itself rather than to personal worship. They are belief-shaped, but by a belief older and more diffuse: mortals will always hold reverence for the elemental forces themselves, regardless of which named god they invoke. A sailor may pray to Shuun and still fear the storm and the water in their own right. A smith may pray to Komo and still know that fire is fire before any god named it. That older, undirected awe (for the wind, the stone, the flame, the void) is what created them and is what sustains them. Their relationship to the Primordial Six (the six Grand Gods who shaped Prelife) is unknown.

Each primordial grants its element's cleric domain to faithful who reach the substance directly (Suzar → Fire, Urzar → Water, Lurzahar → Earth, Haizar → Air, Indazar → Healing, Hutzar → Nothingness, Iturima → Soul). The primordials do not judge: they impose no moral anathema beyond what the element itself imposes. Think a fanatic of fire: the worshipper who taps Suzar gets fire, regardless of what they intend to burn.

Seven of the nine primordials reside here on Layer 1. Two reside on Layer 2 instead (Zurzar in Feyworld for Wood, Burdinzar in Shadowplane for Metal); their cards sit in the Layer 2 group below.

Life · Layer 2 · The Living LayerMaterial Plane (the Bound 13) · Feyworld · Shadowplane

Layer 2 (the Life Layer) hosts three planes: the Material Plane (where mortals live and the Bound Thirteen reside under the Compact), Feyworld (the Chaos plane), and Shadowplane (the Order plane). The Bound Thirteen are catalogued in full on the Grand Gods page. Two Layer-1-tier primordials reside on the sibling planes rather than on Layer 1: Zurzar in Feyworld (Wood) and Burdinzar in Shadowplane (Metal). Both are bound by the Compact in proportion to power while on the Life Layer: weakly on Feyworld and Shadowplane, fully on the Material Plane.

Postlife · Layer 3 · Unbound GodsBeyond the Compact · Prayed-to · Belief-Shaped

Layer 3 (Postlife) is home to a vast and varied population of gods unbound by the Gods' Law. Major and minor deities, ancestral spirits, judges of souls, self-ascended powers, dynasty-spirits, place-gods, deep cults, and patrons whose names are spoken in only one valley. The Postlife holds them all. They are created and sustained by belief: a god remembered survives, a god forgotten dissolves back into the Wellspring. Mortal cults, churches, household shrines, and the steady weight of folk practice are what keep them shaped and present.

None maintain a sanctum on Talan; the Gods' Law keeps them on their side of the veil. Worshippers pray, the prayer crosses, and the answer comes back through grants, omens, and dreams. The figures below are the Layer-3 residents whose portfolios appear most often in clerical practice on Talan, a small selection from a much larger field.

Elysium · Layer 3 · GoodThe radiant plane of mercy, blessing, and benevolent patrons · the Kyūbi-no-Den and the wanderer-god Uthra

The Kyūbi-no-Den · The Nine-Tailed Hall. The kitsune ancestral pantheon of Emarrea. Three named gods sit the Hall, all Minor and all Elysium-resident; the kitsune say the other six seats wait on ancestral spirits, place-spirits, or stories not yet long enough to fill them. The number nine echoes the kitsune nine-tail mastery ladder and the Heartcourt's nine pillars. Elysium residency reflects the pantheon's devotional centre of gravity: love freely given, communal feast, the kindness-trickster ethic. None of the three appear in person on the Material Plane; the Compact closes that road to all Layer-3 residents.

Uthra · the Out-Longing. Sharing the radiant plane but none of the Hall is Uthra, the belief-formed god of wanderlust and the open road, who keeps no kingdom in Elysium and no temple anywhere. Where the Kyūbi-no-Den anchor the hearth, Uthra is the god of leaving it: adventurers and mendicants are his, and his worship is the wayside cairn, never a shrine.
