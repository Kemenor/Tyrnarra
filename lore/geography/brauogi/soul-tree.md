## The seed (locked)

**The dying outrank the living.** Standing on the island is trust, trust runs inward toward the Tree, and the deepest trust belongs to the dying. A living king stops at the registration ring with everyone else; a dying beggar is carried past him by litter toward the hospice-ground, because the closer you die to the door, the shorter the crossing. The island is the terminus where the dying are the nobility; Tvisol across the water is the warm kingdom that hosts death as a festival season. The bearer-work (carrying the dying inward at dusk) is the island youth's coming-of-age labour.

## The access tiers (locked structure; names TBD)

Geography measured in permission, not miles. Islanders ask "how far in have you been?"; the answer is the local measure of a life. The rings are ancient; the *guarding* is newly hard (see tensions).

1. **The port** — commonly accessible, mixed heritage, everyone lands here.
2. **The common viewing platform** — open to everyone.
3. **The registration ring** — entry recorded in the island's book.
4. **Special permission** — case-by-case grant.
5. **Order-only ground** — Voroir Daua alone (hospice-ground exception: the dying are carried here).
6. **The Tree itself** — no one touches it. No exceptions.

The one exception that crosses every ring: a death in progress.

## Feeling and texture (locked)

- Gradient: warmth at the rim, otherness at the core. The port is warm, welcoming, gentle; a community where everyone feels found, even if the place found for you is to die there. No one on the island says "goodbye."
- The Tree at moon-scale: sheer size boggles the mind; eternal pale glow like standing moonlight (the island has never known a dark night); leaves winking out. Folk-belief: one leaf's wink per soul passing.
- Otherness nearer the core: a slight chill that catches you off guard, a whisper at the edge of hearing, a strange calm about what dying means.
- The crossing is ordinarily invisible. At the thin times (Guestide, full moon-channels) it shows imperfectly: a silhouette, a hint; vanishingly rarely, a face.

## The port (locked direction)

- Small town, a few thousand, swelling to multiples of itself every Guestide.
- Economy: almost entirely grief-trade; the welcome, the doctors, the ease, the rituals, the priests. Everyone can find their death here. Plus fishers and agriculture.
- **A fermented food and a fermented drink unique to the island** (names + specifics TBD): things ferment strangely under the Tree's grave-light; the pair exists nowhere else for the same reason Erfivin exists nowhere else.

## The Returnee (locked; name TBD)

A legend that is a truth. No one is allowed through the Soul Tree, for it breaks the cycle; but Epairima's court holds one verdict that is hers alone: **the reversal**, sending a soul back to the layer above for a second pass at the life they failed. Exceedingly rare; one in living memory. The Returnee died on the island (in the order's own hospice-rooms), stood judgment at Dauria, and walked back out of the Tree carrying the rarest verdict the Warden of the Cycle gives. The order did nothing about them: their own goddess sent them; the cycle was never broken, because the cycle itself issued the return. Open: who they are now, what the island does with someone who has already been all the way in, spent the island's highest standing, and came back to being ordinary.

## The live tension (locked, twofold)

1. **The order gets stricter.** The rings were gently kept for centuries; since the door-seekers came (~2524 MR), the guarding hardened. Every pretender caught makes the order look at everyone colder.
2. **Grief-trade curdles into grief economy.** The good death used to be a promise: everyone finds their own. Now parts of the port sell it. Mainland brokers work the quays at Solkai and Merkavar, selling "the good death, arranged" to the wealthy dying, feeding a few island houses that took the coin; most old hosting-families refuse and are slowly outbid for the very rooms they used to give freely.
3. The tensions feed each other: every gilded deathbed package makes it harder to tell a rich pilgrim from a rich pretender.

## Government direction (picked, Phase 3.5; names TBD)

**The Registry, wrapped loosely in an old three-seal concordat.**

- **Dual polity baseline:** the Voroir Daua charter is sovereign over the rings; the port governs itself; an old agreement joins them.
- **The Registry** (rule by the book of the queue): the port is governed by the civil registry that keeps the island's book: arrivals, registrations, hospice beds, litter order, burial-slots. Head + clerks (titles TBD). Fitness: allocation under scarcity, practiced continuously through every Guestide surge, is statecraft. Accepted because of the founding story: when the dying came in floods, the promise "everyone finds their own death" survived only because someone kept a fair queue; the book is the promise, kept in ink. Founding-flood candidates: the 2524 MR wave (recent) or a Dark-Era origin (old); pick at Phase 5. Honest cost: a clerisy; the queue is power; mercy quantified. The commercialization tension lives inside the constitution: what brokers buy is places in the queue, so corrupting the grief-trade and corrupting the government are the same crime, fought at the registry counter.
- **The three-seal concordat** (frame): charter (the rings) + the book (the shore) + Tvisol across the water (the ferries and the peace, guaranteeing the island against foreign muscle). Anything touching the whole island needs all three seals. Cost: slow, which the commercializers exploit.
- Distinctness noted vs. Lurrath's Bearers (rank persons by burden; the Registry ranks no one, keeps one book), vs. Itsasalda's Vordsbench, vs. Baratalda's Housen.
