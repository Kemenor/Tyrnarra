# The Soul Tree: the Island of the Door (Brauogi · Midarra)

**Etymology:** *Soul Tree* is the modern English name, and the island has never needed a grander one; the thing itself outranks any word for it. The port town is **Hinstavor** (Icelandic *hinsta* "last, final" + *vör* "small boat-landing," accent lost in drift): the Last Landing.

**Position:** a small rocky island in its own grey water off the Twin Suns coast (see [Tvisol](tvisol.md)), close enough to see from any sunward roof. One town, one tree, and the tree is visible from every window of the town. The ferries run from **Solkai**; at Guestide, some passengers are booked one way.

---

The dying outrank the living here. That is the island's whole constitution, and everything else is administration. Standing on the Soul Tree island is trust, trust runs inward toward the Tree, and the deepest trust belongs to those closest to death: a living king stops at the order's gate with everyone else, and a dying beggar is carried past him at dusk, because the closer you die to the door, the shorter the crossing. Ask an islander about anyone and the answer measures the same way. *How far in have you been?* is what a life amounts to on this island.

## The Tree and the rings

The Tree stands at moon-scale, pale at all hours, and the island has never known a dark night; things and people live differently under a light that never quite goes out. The crossing itself is ordinarily invisible. At the thin times (Guestide, the full moon-channels) it shows imperfectly: a silhouette, a hint; vanishingly rarely, a face.

Around the Tree the island is arranged in rings of permission, and geography here is measured in trust rather than miles: **the port**, where everyone lands; **the common viewing platform**, open to all; **the order's gate**, where entry is written into the charter's book; the ground beyond, by grant alone; **the hospice-ground**, the order's own; and **the Tree**, which no one touches. No one. The one exception that crosses every ring is a death in progress. The rings are ancient; the hard guarding of them is new (see the pressure below).

## Hinstavor, the Last Landing

A town of a few thousand that swells to several times itself every Guestide, when the ferries from Solkai run heavy. It is the warmest town on the Midarra and knows exactly why: everyone who lands here feels found, even when the place found for them is to die well. No one in Hinstavor says goodbye.

The trade is the good death entire: the welcome, the doctors, the priests of a dozen faiths keeping death-rooms so that every pilgrim can end under their own god's rites, the litters, the burials, the letters home. Fishers work the grey water, and the island's two tables travel:

- **Grafisk** (Icelandic *gröf* "grave" + *fiskur* "fish"): the grave-fish, cured buried beneath the standing glow, which ferments like nothing cured on any mainland shore.
- **Folmjod** (Icelandic *fölur* "pale" + *mjöður* "mead"): the pale mead, because bees that have never known a true night make a honey that never quite sleeps either.

Both taste, islanders say cheerfully, like here and nowhere else.

## The Winter Roll

The town governs itself by the simplest constitution on Talan. Every spring **the Wintered**, those who kept the island through the empty season, elect a **Harbourward** to run the small things: the quays, the wells, the landing-dues, the watch. The franchise is the winter itself. Guestide fills every bed with strangers and coin, and the cold empties them again, and whoever remains when the last ferry has gone home is the town. A broker can rent the whole quay for a season and it buys him nothing in spring; wintering cannot be purchased, only lived. Foreign powers learn to write two letters: to the Harbourward for anything on the water and the quays, and to the charter for anything past the gate.

## The Filgja

The island's coming-of-age is the carrying. At dusk the dying are borne inward by litter from the hospice-houses toward the order's ground, and the bearers are Hinstavor's young, serving their **Filgja** years (Icelandic *fylgja*, "to accompany"; the old word for the attendant spirit that walks beside a soul). A Filgjur walks beside the dying as their brief attendant spirit, learns their names, carries them through the gate the bearer cannot yet pass, and hands them on. Islanders reckon adulthood from the first carry, and no one who has done the work ever quite manages to fear the Tree again, which is, the old Filgjur say, the point.

## The charter

The **Voroir Daua** hold everything from the gate inward, under the oldest charter their order keeps (see [`../../factions.md`](../../factions.md)); church, hall, and barracks in one, sovereign on its own ground, answering to no one off the island. The order keeps the gate-book of everyone written past the threshold, keeps the hospice-ground where the carried spend their last days, and keeps the watch. Between the charter's book at the gate and the town's Winter Roll at the quay, the island's whole government fits in two ledgers, and the islanders consider this exactly enough.

## The Again

Once in living memory, the door worked backwards. A pilgrim died in the order's own hospice-rooms, was carried through, stood judgment at Dauria, and walked back out of the Tree alive, carrying the rarest verdict the Warden of the Cycle gives: the reversal, a second pass at the life they failed (see [`../../gods.md`](../../gods.md), *Epairima*). The order did nothing, because there was nothing to do; their own goddess had sent them, and the cycle cannot be broken by the cycle's own hand. Hinstavor calls them **the Again**, and does not press. What the island does with a person who has spent its highest standing and come back to being ordinary is a question the town is still, gently, working out. *(The Again's identity, ancestry, and present life are open canon; see `docs/open-threads.md`.)*

## Faith

Epairima's funerary folk-cult runs deep here, as across the strait; Sarrum's chapels keep the town's records and Araphel's keep the unasked, in the Tvisol way. And every church on Talan keeps at least a room in Hinstavor, because *everyone finds their own death here* is a promise about rites as much as beds. The order attends none of it and all of it, in the way of the sovereign next door.

## The pressure of the open era

Two pressures, and they feed each other. Since the door-seekers began arriving under Guestide's cover (~2524 MR; see [Tvisol](tvisol.md)), the order's gentle old guarding of the rings has hardened, and every pretender caught makes the gate colder for every honest pilgrim. And the grief-trade has begun to curdle into a grief economy: mainland brokers work the quays at Solkai and Merkavar selling *the good death, arranged*, a few island houses have taken the coin, and the old hosting-families who gave freely are being outbid for the very rooms they used to give. The two knots tie: every gilded deathbed makes a rich pilgrim harder to tell from a rich pretender. In Hinstavor the fight is now electoral, fought openly every spring over a few hundred wintered votes, broker-funded candidates against the old families, money courting the roll it can never join.

**◈ Popular Belief: the leaves.** Every islander will tell you the Tree's pale leaves wink out, one per soul that passes, and half of them will tell you which leaf was whose. Widows pick one out the first Guestide and greet it. And the bee-keepers will tell you, entirely unbothered, that their bees visit the Tree itself, that nobody stops them because nobody stops anything on its way to that tree, and that this is what Folmjod actually is: the one drink on Talan with a little of the door in it. The order has never confirmed a word of this, which the keepers point out is not a denial.

## ⚿ GM Secret: what the watch is for

The full truth of the charter lives with the door's own secret in [Tvisol](tvisol.md), *⚿ the door's light*: the Soul Tree is a standing passage to the Postlife, the veil around it thins on Dauria's moon-cycle channels (Dausol is that light), and the Duskmire beyond is one of the two veils where shards of divinity wash up. The order's oldest charter has never primarily been about pilgrims. It is the watch on the one door through which a sufficiently learned mortal could walk toward the Mortal Ascent Ladder's raw material and come back, and the door-seekers arriving since 2524 MR suggest someone has worked that out again. The hardening of the rings is the mortal-visible surface of that watch, and neither the Harbourward, the Wintered, nor most of the order's own Greenears know it.
