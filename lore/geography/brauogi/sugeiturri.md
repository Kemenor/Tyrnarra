# Sugeiturri: the Source-Country (Earth · Sarrum)

**Etymology:** Basque *suge* (serpent) + *iturri* (source, spring) → **Sugeiturri**, "the serpent-source." Deliberately undrifted, and that is the characterisation: in a land whose whole identity is unbroken continuity, whose Houses keep names that never change, and whose people keep the one thing that never stops, this is the single place-name in Brauogi that never eroded. Everywhere else the old tongue drifted because the world moved on; here the word stands as first spoken, because the thing it names never changed. The name also says the faith aloud: the source is the serpent's, and the rivers are the serpent's body.

**Position:** the green, well-watered northern corridor of Brauogi, running west to east from the **Hafra coast**, past the south-western mountain gap that is the **gateway into Ilun Tasun** (Myrkono), along the spine to the **Iturmen passes** (which open north into **Fellibylur**, Vindul), and up to the **source-mountains** in the east where the rivers and **Iturburu**, the mother-lake, rise. The **magirail to Lurrath's Gate** runs through it; Sugeiturri is the rail-approach to Lurrath. South lies the **Earth Realm**; **Azkamour** sits to its north-west.

**The two halves.** The **east is the source**: the Iturmen mountains, the springs, Iturburu, the River Houses, the faith, the power. The **west is the world**: the Hafra ports, the Ilun Tasun gateway, the pass-towns, the rail, the busy trade-corridor through good green country, where most of the people and the money are. The Houses keep the holy water in the east while the world crosses the region in the west. The power is old and eastern; the wealth and the crowds are worldly and western.

## Every town drinks their water

Nearly every river in Brauogi rises in Sugeiturri (Twin Suns the main exception), and the water runs out from here to the whole domain. Azkamour's water, the Hafra-coast towns', the breadbasket's, the cities': almost none of it begins anywhere but the eastern sources. This is the region's true power, and it is bigger than leverage, because it is never wielded. The old blood keeps; it does not grasp. The quiet, total fact under everything is that no one in Brauogi drinks water that did not begin in the Houses' keeping. The breadbasket grows on it, the cities drink it, and the Houses simply hold it flowing.

## The people: Keepers of the Current

Sugeiturri is the Dragonet old-blood heartland, and here the old blood is a **vocation**, not a disposition carried while living some other life. For a Lurrath or Earth Realm Dragonet the old blood is the colour under a craft; for a source Dragonet it is the craft. The ancient thing is not behind them as memory. It runs through their hands this morning. They do not merely rest on what persists; they keep it persisting, tending the spring so the current never stops. Their continuity is active and present-tense, never contemplative: they keep the **current**, the living now, not a record of the past.

The place turns the feeling outward into the body. A source Dragonet **reads water the way the wind-folk read wind**: the wyrm-undertone every Dragonet feels in the throat here hums with the flow, so they know a spring's mood and hear a river's health. And their belonging has an address. Not "something older than every map," but *my river, my House, my stretch of the god.* The tell that gives them away abroad: a source Dragonet cannot rest where there is no running water. They stop at every brook, read its flow at a glance, and sleep badly in a dry place, because the sound of a current is the sound of the god still going.

## The river-tree is the House-tree (politics)

**House and river are one life.** Every river is bound to a House that keeps it, its springs and its run, and the two share one fate. A river runs dry, and the House has run dry.

**The river-tree is the House-tree.** A trunk-river is a Great House; its tributaries are the cadet branches; where two rivers meet, two Houses are kin; where a river forks, a House has branched. The whole region is one watershed-family-tree rooted at Iturburu. You do not found a House, because you cannot found a river. You branch. Every House traces up the water to an older House, and up to the source.

**River Blood.** The aristocracy is a living current, not a remembered pedigree: you are noble because you are a branch of the one current still running. Rank is closeness to the source, read up the water as the number of forks between a House and Iturburu, so a trunk-House runs high and a feeder-stream cadet runs lesser. Geography, genealogy, and rank are one gradient. Below the River Blood are the **commoners** who live along the rivers and do the daily keeping under a House's name, carrying no current of their own.

**Each House owns its own source**, and the cardinal crime follows: to seize, dam, or foul the spring a House holds is the one unforgivable act, the murder of a House and of a current that ran since before the bread. The rare exception is a House whose source has fallen partly into another's hands through some old bargain (springs merged by marriage, a source held in trust after a defeat, water-rights paid against a debt); such a House lives with a knife at its throat.

**The crown and its prerogative.** Iturburu is a true crown, not a first-among-equals, and the royal prerogative is the gift of current. The crown is supreme in bestowal and bound in seizure: it alone makes and remakes Houses by bestowing water, and it cannot break or take a current that still runs, for that is the cardinal crime whoever commits it, and the crown bends to the source like every House below it. Only the water unmakes a House. The crown has two kinds of water to give, both windfalls: a **new spring**, the rarest sacred event, risen where no current ran; and a **living current come free**, when a House ends not by drought but by blood, its line failing with no heir while its river runs on. A dry House prays for the first and schemes for the second.

**Unrivered Houses.** A House whose river runs dry is not erased. The current breaks but the blood persists, so the line becomes River Blood with no river: above the commoners, because the blood is real, and powerless, because no current runs. It holds only its name and waits, each one a name walking a riverbed. When the crown has water to give it most often re-waters such a House: a new spring sets a dry line on a wholly new current (never its old one, because the broken thing stays broken), and a freed living current lifts a dry House onto another's still-running river, the richest restoration the region knows.

**The circling.** A House ends two ways, and both draw the others in. Its river can fail, by drought or by a source slowly thinning, and it dies with the current and leaves a grave. Or its line can fail while the water still runs, and a living river is about to come free. Either way a weakening House is the thing that moves the high country, because what it holds will be given again. The dry Houses court the crown, rivals press a faltering line toward its end, and the cardinal crime hangs over all of it, so the work is done at one remove, in marriages and debts and patience, and now and then in a drought no one can prove was made.

## The crown: the current, the still, and the rising

The crown is styled in water, and the styles make a succession institution out of one image.

- **The First Current** is the reigning ruler (man or woman), the original current from which every River House branches, the most direct expression of the god's living body.
- **The Still Water** is the abdicated ruler. The honoured, ideal transition is to pass the living current to the heir *while alive* and go still: the current moves to the next channel, it does not stop. The distinction is sacred and exact. **Houses run dry; rulers go still.** One is a grave, the other a retirement, and the words are never confused. A Still Water rules nothing but is far from powerless: still waters run deep, and they become the realm's deep counsel and living memory, the pool the First Current comes to. The realm can carry several at once.
- **The Rising Water** is the named heir, the current gathering in the next channel. The First Current names the Rising Water the same way the crown grants a river, and may re-choose at will until death or retirement, because the Rising Water holds only the *promise* of the flow, not a current, and a promise is revocable where a flowing current never is. At the First Current's death or going-still, whoever holds the title at that instant receives the flow.

*The Still Water was, the First Current is, the Rising Water will be.* The one cardinal sin of the crown is to refuse to go still in time, clinging to the flow into stagnation and holding the succession hostage; it is the one ruler in Sugeiturri fighting the current instead of riding it.

**The living court.** The seat is **Amategi** (Basque *ama* "mother" + *-tegi* "place, abode," the Mother-Hall), standing on an island in Iturburu, the crown sitting at the still centre of the mother-lake, the one still water in a realm of moving currents. There **Naroa Amasuge, the First Current** (*Naroa*, "abundant"), reigns and grants the waters; she took the flow when **Erro Amasuge, the Still Water** (*Erro*, "root, origin"), went still in good time, and she still comes to him for counsel, the root the present current grew from. She has named **Ibon Amasuge, the Rising Water** (*Ibon*, off *ibai*, "river"), her heir, but keeps **Naia Amasuge** (*Naia*, "wave") a genuine close second, a cousin-branch backed by **House Ibaizar**, so Ibon's title is never safe and Ibaizar's pressure never lifts. The court reads the gap between Ibon and Naia the way it reads a river's level.

**The Houses.** Beneath royal **House Amasuge** (Basque *ama* + *suge*, "the mother-serpent," which holds Iturburu, the mother-coil): **House Ibaizar** (*ibai* + *zahar*, "the Old River"), eldest and greatest of the trunk-Houses, ranking just under the crown, proud and secure, and the power-bloc behind Naia; and **House Urbeltz** (*ur* + *beltz*, "the Black Water"), on a deep river running out through the shadowed gorges toward the Ilun Tasun side, the storied compromised House whose source fell half into another's hands generations ago and which schemes endlessly to make its spring whole again, the cautionary tale every River House tells its children.

## Faith: Zaharsuge, the living current

The region keeps its own faith, and it is the Dragonet faith at full expression: **Zaharsuge, the wyrm, read here as the living current.** The spring is the deep-earth serpent surfacing into the light, the river is its body in motion, and Iturburu the mother-lake is the great coil where it gathers. The god has no spoken voice, so it speaks in the motion of the water, read and never heard, and the whole high country hums with the bone-deep pulse of the springs.

Because the rivers are the god's body, the politics and the faith are one thing. The watershed-family-tree *is* the god, branching. To hold a river is a priestly act before it is a noble one, so **the River Houses are the priesthood**, every noble a priest of their own water, with the royal House the high keepers of the mother-coil. There is no separate clergy. The cardinal crime is deicide in miniature, the wounding of the god in that stretch. And the daily keeping is the worship: every day begins at the water, where a keeper clears the channel and the springs and *reads* the current's strength and mood, chore and prayer in one act with no seam between them. The reading is a daily divination read like a pulse, a strong clear current meaning the god runs well and the House is sound, a thinning or muddying or cooling meaning dread, and a change in the current travels House to House up and down the watershed within days.

## The route, and the cold-broken-by-warmth

The Earth Realm feeds the domain, so Sugeiturri is not a food region; it is **the route**. The corridor is the road merchants choose, and its signature is that every stop keeps its own hot springs, so crossing the north is a chain of warm waters, each town resting weary bones in its own pools. *Take the northern road and you'll never sleep cold.* The best-loved route on the continent runs straight through the source of its water. The body of the region is working **river-towns** strung down the runs (every town belonging to the river it sits on, and so to that river's House), **pass-towns** living off the crossings, **Hafra ports** in the west, and **hot-spring towns** as the warm waystations on all of it. The east is Dragonet-dense and holy at the sources; the west is cosmopolitan, every people the traffic brings, all of them drinking the same eastern water.

## Founding: the rivers taken from the gods

Sugeiturri is one of the oldest kingdoms on Talan, and its claim to age is the rare true one. The *people, the faith, and the keeping* are Gods'-Era ancient: the old blood were at the sources before the bread. But in the Gods' Era the rivers were the *gods'*, and the old blood kept a source they did not own. The Week of Crimson Rain was the founding act. As the gods died and the divine hand was torn off the world, the mortals of the sources **took the rivers from the gods**, seized the water no dying god could hold any longer, and never gave it back. That birth of self-rule lands at the 0 GR / 0 MR hinge, the same span Frae City rose as the seat of mortal resistance: Sugeiturri took its water the week Cronus's people took their freedom. Through the Lost Era the old blood kept on, holding the rivers through the lost, unrecorded centuries while the river-tree of Houses and the crown at Iturburu took their shape. It was still lost in the Lost Era, fragmentary and unwritten, but always itself, because the source-keeping never changed across the fall. Its older-claim is not "we kept a king since the Gods' Era" (no one did), but "we took our water from the gods when the gods fell, and we have never once let it go."

#### ⚿ GM Secret: Sugeiturri Made Its Own God

Zaharsuge was born here. The first century of the Lost Era was the great Wellspring leak, the belief-easy years when concentrated faith crystallised into actual gods, and Sugeiturri held the most concentrated river-serpent faith on Talan, sitting on the source of all the water, in exactly those years. The Dragonets who had just taken the rivers from the dead gods worshipped the current as the great wyrm, and that belief, accumulated at the wellhead of the domain through the leak, brought **Zaharsuge into being in Diyu.** The relationship is inverted from what the faithful believe: the source-country did not descend from the serpent-god; the serpent-god rose from the source-country's faith. This is the Dragonet heartland's true origin, and it sits over the existing Zaharsuge belief-formed canon ([`../../gods.md`](../../gods.md)); the older deep-substrate / Wyrmkin-enslavement origin is superseded for this line. Zaharsuge is real and answers prayer, and does not know its own beginning, because the god believes its own faithful and the faithful do not know.
