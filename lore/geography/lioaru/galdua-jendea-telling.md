# The Fall at Tamalut: a telling of the Kageroza

**In-world document of Galdua Jendea** (region canon: [`galdua-jendea.md`](galdua-jendea.md)). Shaped together by the GM and Claude (2026-10-04): the GM set the arc, the two voices and the ending; Claude wrote the text; accepted by the GM. The traveller's image for Galdua Jendea (sub-region-workflow Phase 3): an Emarrean troupe's telling of the herd coming to a rock, the noon flight out to it, and the watering. Published whole as a log card, with excerpts at the section heads.

**Voice block.** **Takane no Hana, of one** · a kitsune player of the Kageroza, an Emarrean troupe touring the rock cities by sandsail; three tails; counted at Tamalut's watering. Afraid of heights and a hopeless romantic: falls a little in love on every rock, dramatic about every sight, drawn forward with her eyes shining and her tails flicking while the rest of her holds on to the wall. **Aksil n Tamalut, of sixteen** · a Desert Palm Ghoran adventurer of Tamalut, forty years on the sand and over it, on other people's errands and the Guild's; comes home for every watering. Unhurried and dry, sees people the way a plant sees light; his flower opens in the heat. **The chorus** · the troupe, who found them.

---

*A telling, as the troupe has played it on the rocks of Galdua Jendea and the stages of Emarrea since 2531 MR. Two players speak; the troupe plays the rest.*

*(The stage dark. A single lantern, high.)*

**THE CHORUS:** Every word of this is true, except the parts that are better.

**TAKANE:** We had played Tamalut three nights, in a hall cut into the cliff, where the stone sweats cold all day and the audience sits in the dark like roots. I was in love with the hall. I was a little in love with the lamp-keeper's daughter. I was not in love with the ledges, because the ledges hang over nothing, and I keep my feet where the ground is.

On the fourth morning the bells went, and the whole rock ran for the lookouts. So I ran too, because a kitsune who hears a crowd running does not ask where.

*(She runs to the lip of the stage and stops dead.)*

**TAKANE:** There was dust on the horizon. Then backs under the dust. Then roofs on the backs. Nineteen, the lamp-keeper's daughter said, counting on her fingers, and the great one in the middle carrying a palace. A city, coming across the sand like weather. I have built storms on a stage out of foxfire and silk. I have never built *that*.

*(The lantern swings away from her. A Ghoran player steps out of the troupe: pale, unhurried, a white flower closed at his collar. He speaks to the audience as if she cannot hear him.)*

**AKSIL:** I had come home for the watering. I always come home for the watering. I have walked the sand and flown over it for forty years, on other people's errands and the Guild's, and whatever I am doing, when the word comes that the herd is turning toward Tamalut, I put it down and come home.

So I was on the lookout, and the whole rock was looking at the herd, and I was looking at her.

She was leaning out over a drop she plainly hated. One hand was white on the rail. The other was already reaching, as if she could take the herd off the horizon and keep it. Her eyes had all nineteen whales in them. Her tails had opinions, three of them, and none of the opinions agreed with the hand on the rail.

I have seen many people want a thing. I had never seen anyone want it with half of herself while the other half held on to the wall.

*(The lantern finds them both.)*

**TAKANE:** By noon the herd had stopped a few hours out and lain down in the sand to wait out the sun, as Valreka does. And by noon every child on the rock had a wing on their back. They run at the edge of the high ledge and drop off it. I watched the first one go. She fell out of the shade into the white light, and fell, and fell, and then the heat caught her and threw her upward like a leaf over a fire, and she laughed all the way up.

I was holding the wall. I would like that remembered.

**AKSIL:** She was holding the wall. And leaning toward the edge from it, the way a plant leans toward the window.

So I went to her and said, "You want to go."

**TAKANE:** I said I wanted to live.

**AKSIL:** I said, "Both, then." And I held out a wing made for two.

*(The troupe lifts them both. The lantern swings wide.)*

**TAKANE:** I will tell you what it is to fall into the sun. The shade lets go of you all at once. The air goes white. The rock drops past you, and you drop with it, and there is nothing in the world but the drop.

**AKSIL:** She screamed. Everyone screams the first time. She screamed in verse.

**TAKANE:** I did not.

**AKSIL:** Something about the sun being a cruel lover.

**TAKANE:** And then the heat hits from below, like a hand. The cloth goes tight. The falling stops being falling. You are higher than the rock, and the whole sand lies white and silent under you, and the only sounds are the wind in the cloth and a man laughing behind you.

**AKSIL:** My flower opened. It does that in the heat. She saw it and laughed at me, with her eyes still shut.

**TAKANE:** They were open by then.

**AKSIL:** They were shut until I told her the whale was under us. After that she would not close them, and I had to tell her when to lean.

**TAKANE:** We came down on the first whale, the Guild's, at the head of the herd, and the Strays came leaping up the dunes on their sand-beasts to see who had flown in.

**AKSIL:** The Strays always come. Anything that flies is a dare to them.

**TAKANE:** I was in love with all of them. And with the whale. And a little with the man who had dropped me off a cliff.

**AKSIL:** A little, she says.

*(Dusk. The lantern lowers and reddens.)*

**TAKANE:** In the evening the herd rose and came on, and we rode it in. The whales lay down along the foot of the rock, one after another, the great one in the middle, and nineteen districts of people stood on nineteen backs and looked up. The whole city went quiet. Up in the cliff, in the dark of the spring-hall, the keepers opened the stone.

The water came down the face of the rock in one long white fall, out of the shade into the last red light, down to the troughs where the water-bearers stood waiting with their lines. Every soul on nineteen whales stood and listened to it. A whole desert of silence, and then that.

**AKSIL:** From the herd it is a fall of water. From the rock it is a door. The spring-hall stays shut and dark from one watering to the next, years sometimes, and the keepers sit with the spring all that while. The oldest keeper on Tamalut has opened that door for two hundred years. She remembers every watering: which whale came in first, who was sick, who was born on the crossing, what the herd brought and what it took. Valreka carries its memory on its back. Ours stays in the stone, and the keepers stay with it.

The Valrekans call us the lost ones, because we stayed when the goddess's city left. We call it staying. Then we open the door, and they drink.

**TAKANE:** He told me that at the foot of the fall, with spray in his flower.

**AKSIL:** She cried. She will say she didn't.

**TAKANE:** I sat down because my knees had given out.

**AKSIL:** I sat down because she did.

*(The troupe rises and comes down the stage in a body, lanterns lit.)*

**THE CHORUS:** And where did we find her, when the troupe came down off the rock at dusk to look for its lost lead? At the foot of the fall. Sitting. Holding a Ghoran's hand.

Is any of it true?

**TAKANE:** Ask him.

**AKSIL:** Ask her.

*(The two players join hands and bow, glancing at each other. The lantern goes out.)*
