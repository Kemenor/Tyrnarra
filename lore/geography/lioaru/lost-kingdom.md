# The Lost Kingdom (Lioaru)

*Written under the [`region-prose.md`](../../../docs/region-prose.md) contract (2026-10-05). Parent domain: [`../lioaru.md`](../lioaru.md). The record quoted throughout is [`lost-kingdom-lantern.md`](lost-kingdom-lantern.md). Its one land neighbour: [`galdua-jendea.md`](galdua-jendea.md). The killers' full history: [`../../timeline.md`](../../timeline.md), *⚿ GM Secret: The Storveldi Denbora: The Real History*.*

**Etymology:** plain Talanese, the name the rest of Talan gives it. Folk names: **the Blackened Lands**, and *the black spot on the map*. Its own people speak **Hizva**, the oldest of the south-western tongues, and name their places in it: **Ida**, **Vardana**, **the Dakhma**. The ring of stones around the killing ground carries a Court Tongue name, **the Mugarri**. (Derivations in the glossary.)

**Position:** the south-west coast of Lioaru, on the Hafra. Its one land neighbour is Galdua Jendea, beyond a dark ridge along the north-east edge; past the ridge lies Galdua's pale band. Everywhere else is sea.

**Terrain:** about 57,900 square miles of black ground under a standing haze of black sand; clear days come a few times a year. The soil takes nothing. The ruins of a great city stand in the west of the interior, ringed by standing stones, and a walled town on the south coast.

**Character:** the returned dead, ruled by the strongest of them, in bones that remember the empire that killed a god.

**Peoples:** about 30,000, half of them in Ida. **The risen** (Skeletons) are about half; **the warped** (Fleshwarps) about a quarter; **the kept**, the living who came to the edge and stayed, about a quarter. Skeletons and Fleshwarps are at their fullest here, and Duskwalkers are born here more than anywhere on Talan.

**Tongue:** Hizva, which comes back to its speakers through their memories; Talanese with an old shape to it, for everything else.

**Faith:** Tani, whose divine caster sits the city's seat; Betibizi, whose priests hold their stretches of the wall.

**Rule:** **the Daraya**, the one who has done the greatest holding against what comes out of the ground. **Rauca Urvan** holds the seat; she sealed the Dakhma's gate in 2524. The **pathra** hold the wall under her; a **steward** runs the rest.

**Capital:** **Ida**, on the south coast, about 15,000.

**Founded:** the land at the Week of Crimson Rain; Ida in the Dark Era; the present Daraya in 2524.

---

## What the land is

The Lost Kingdom is the ground where a Grand God died. The gods' retaliation for Tani's killing fell on this one coast for the better part of the Week of Crimson Rain, and the land has carried the blow for some two and a half thousand years.

**The haze.** Black sand hangs in the air over the whole country, fine as flour, and the sun stands in it like a copper coin behind a cloth. A few days a year the wind clears it; on the rest, a traveller sees ten paces by lantern and the wardens on the ridge see the edge and nothing past it.

**Signs of the curse.** The soil is black all the way down, and it takes no crop, no grass and no lichen. Moments repeat in pockets, a gust that blows twice, footsteps that land before the foot; other moments pool and hold a traveller in one second for what feels like an hour. The stone of the ruins reads newer than it should, clean-edged, as if it were cut last year. A constant pressure lies on the spell-sense of anyone trained to feel it; necromancers describe crossing the ridge as the background hum of the dead turned up. The living who stay too long come back changed in body. And the dead get up: the ground raises its own, with no hand needed.

**The Mugarri.** Around the killing ground stands a ring of great stones, the Mugarri, set by the gods in the Week of Crimson Rain. Inside it lie the ruined city and the worst of everything above: the time-warps thickest, the raising strongest. The Adventurers' Guild counts every party that passes the Mugarri as lost from the moment it crosses.

**The ridge.** Along the north-east border runs a dark ridge, and beyond it Galdua Jendea's pale band. Ghoran wardens keep watch along the ridge and post the warning-cairns travellers read; most outsiders come no farther.

## Why the land is this way

The chronicles agree on the public part of the story. Tani, the Goddess of Time, was struck down here in her own domain, the first Grand God ever slain, and her death began the Week of Crimson Rain. The people who held this coast were annihilated by the gods in answer, and their great city lies in the west of the interior. The people of Ida call it **Vardana**, the city; its own name is lost.

Who the killers were is where the chronicles thin. Most call them the Old Race who killed Tani; some make them the Elden, some a mortal people of the Gods' Era who lived among the Elden's ruins and claimed their blood, some sorcerer-kings who pried open a secret meant for gods. The surviving record confirms only that their homeland was destroyed here, by the gods themselves.

#### ⚿ GM Secret: the Storveldi Denbora

The killers were the **Storveldi Denbora**, a mortal people of the Gods' Era who built their capital on Elden ruins and claimed the Elden's blood, a lie they made themselves. Their ruling class lifted itself to Demi-God by belief and divine engineering, and they developed **the integration procedure**, which turns a found shard of divinity into ascension; two of their sovereigns rose to Minor God by it. The two, with the Demi-God class beneath them, killed Tani. The gods' retaliation annihilated Vardana and wounded the land past healing.

One of the ruling class survived, used the procedure on himself, and fled to Abyss as **Betibizi**, Minor God of Undeath; the ground's raising traces to him. The experimented-upon commoners who were away from the capital survive as the Azarketi, who still repeat the Elden-blood lie. Full account: [`../../timeline.md`](../../timeline.md), *⚿ GM Secret: The Storveldi Denbora: The Real History*.

## Ida: the risen, the warped, the kept

> *"Who are you?"*
> *"We live here."*
> A Guild party and a Skeleton of Ida, in the square of a ruined street, as the vault lantern on Anzar keeps it (2531 MR)

The ground has raised its dead since the Rain, and for most of that time the aware ones rose and drifted away into a world that hunted them. In the Dark Era, when the world hunted everything strange, they began to come back, and on the south coast, outside the Mugarri, where the sea air thins the haze and the curse runs weakest, they built **Ida**.

Three kinds of people live in it. **The risen** are Skeletons, old bones that got up with someone new inside; they are half the city. **The warped** are Fleshwarps, born living and reshaped by the ground, each the only one of their kind. **The kept** are the living who came to the edge and stayed: Duskwalkers born in the cursed land, exiles, pilgrims who never went home. About 15,000 live in Ida and as many again in holds among the coastal ruins, inside the wards or close to them; the rest of the country holds no one.

The ground feeds no one, and the sea feeds the city. The kept and the warped fish the Hafra at first light and gather kelp and shellfish along the shore; the risen do not eat. The water comes from the old cisterns under the town, sunk deep. Ida is walled on the landward side, and along the wall run the wards.

**The flashes.** Everyone in Ida, living or risen, has flashes of the kingdom that stood here: a street, a smell of roasting meat, a procession, sometimes a face. In Hizva a flash is a **marthra**, and the first thing anyone asks after one is *whose was it?* Each comes from one place: from a soul's own past, from the bones' own life, or from the ground, which keeps its moments. A flash comes from only one of the three, and whose a given flash was is a guess the whole city argues over. The old tongue comes back the same way, a word at a time, and Ida speaks it.

**The seeing-to.** Every body that falls in or near the cursed ground is broken and buried before the ground can raise it: an enemy's, a friend's, an outsider's, one of the risen whose soul has gone. Nothing is left whole for what comes out of the Dakhma. A body's own people see to it where they can, and anyone does where they cannot, with a few words to Tani.

## The one who holds

> *"Ask the wall who rules. It has been asked before, and it answered."*
> Rauca Urvan, the Daraya, to a Guild envoy at the landward gate, 2530 MR

Ida is ruled by whoever has done the greatest **holding**: held the wall, burned a captain of the dead, sealed a gate. That person is **the Daraya**. Rule passes when someone holds against something greater, in the open, before witnesses, and the city acclaims it; or when the Daraya dies or fails at the wall.

**Rauca Urvan** has been Daraya since 2524. She is risen, a divine caster of Tani, and when the Dakhma broke open that year she sealed its gate, and the city has acclaimed no greater holding since. Under her the **pathra** hold the wall, each a stretch of it and the wards along it, and a warden's stretch is their standing in the city. The strong come from every kind of casting: arcane, primal, occult and divine all count, as long as they hold. At dusk each of the pathra lights their stretch, and the city glances up to count the lights.

The Daraya calls anyone to the wall, commands the pathra and the wards, deals with the outsiders who come to the landward gate, and names a **steward**. The steward, **Utana Zam**, one of the kept, runs the fishing, the stores, the cisterns and the trade at the cairns. The Daraya may not keep the seat once a greater holding has been acclaimed. What counts as greater, and how the city acclaims it, is written nowhere.

The strongest arcane caster in Ida is **Vindafarna Ast Arshama**, a risen of the old way: the way the bones remember, of study and working, and of what the strong of Vardana once made of themselves. He holds the longest stretch of the wall, and in 2531 he came out of the haze with Hutaosa Zam and the Fleshwarp **Taxma Ast** and burned a captain of the legions to save a Guild party. Betibizi's priests hold their stretches too, and say the people of Ida are their god's, since his resonance raised them.

The Daraya means to kill the Rot-Tyrant herself. Whoever does it does the greatest holding there is, and rules after; she cannot do it alone, and every helper is a possible successor.

#### ◈ Popular Belief: the Daraya's marthras are the goddess's own

In Ida the faithful say that Rauca Urvan's flashes come from Tani herself, the last things the goddess saw on the day she fell here, and that this is why the gate held under the Daraya's hand in 2524. The Daraya has never said what her marthras show.

#### ⚿ GM Secret: Vindafarna's bones

Vindafarna's bones were one of the Storveldi ruling class: a Demi-God who died at Vardana in the retaliation. His marthras carry true pieces of **the integration procedure**, which canon says survives on Talan only in fragments; one of the fragments walks Ida's wall. That is his might, and why the ascension-cultists court the cairns, and why the old way in Ida is more than a memory. The bound thirteen suppress integration work wherever they find it, and would care very much who rules Ida if they knew.

## The Dakhma and the legions

> *It is not a drum. It is feet. A great many, in step, slow, iron on bone and bone on stone, from somewhere inside the haze.*
> The vault lantern on Anzar, as transcribed in 2531 MR

Under Vardana the old kingdom laid its dead in great bone-halls, the **Dakhma**. In 2524, when the Nine Dungeons broke open across Talan, the **Rot-Tyrant**, the corpse-warlord in rusted armour, came up beneath them, and raised the dead of the Dakhma as its legions. Rauca Urvan sealed the main gate that year. The legions still come up through the lesser ways under the ruins, captains in rusted armour ahead of them, and range out across the pale band into Galdua Jendea, where they hold the lost rock of Tazrut.

The legions march in step. The risen walk out of step, each as the person it is, and that is how Ida tells its own from the enemy in the haze. The legions' bones are the same old bones the risen got up in: one people's dead, on both sides of the wall.

**Who kills the Rot-Tyrant.** The Daraya means to, and needs help to do it. Vindafarna Ast Arshama holds the longest stretch of the wall and has burned a captain already. Guild parties out of Tassast and the Whalehall come for the second General ever felled, and if one of them lands the blow, Ida has no rule for who holds the seat after. Betibizi's priests hold their stretches and wait. Across the ridge, Tassast wants Tazrut back.

## The ancestries of the cursed ground

**Fleshwarp.** The ground twists living flesh into irregular bodies: the children of survivors, of trespassers who lingered, and of the kept, worked on slowly by the land. Each is unique, and the Lost Kingdom is the one place on Talan where a Fleshwarp is ordinary. Elsewhere a Fleshwarp of the Blackened Lands is rare and pitied, and few can tell one from the Red Empire's made fleshwarps on sight.

**Skeleton.** The wound pulls the dead back into motion with no necromancer's hand. Some rise with nothing in them; those are the dead the city sees to. The aware ones are the risen, and most of them live in Ida. Elsewhere on Talan a Skeleton is almost always a Blackened Lands émigré, refused entry by many kingdoms and watched, in some charters hunted, by the Voroir Daua.

**Duskwalkers.** The Lost Kingdom is the largest source of the Duskwalker heritage on Talan. A Duskwalker manifests as a mortal child where death runs close to the surface, and here it runs closest; the same ground that raises the dead seeds the children around whom the dead lie down. In Ida they are among the kept, and the city needs no word for what they are.

## The cairns, and who goes there

Few outsiders cross the ridge. Most of the dealing between Ida and the world is done at **the cairns**: the people of Ida leave what the ruins give on a warden's cairn and take what was left in exchange, so no one has to stand face to face. The Ghoran wardens see it and let it be. What goes out is bronze and worked glass of the old kingdom's make, tools of a dead people, and rarest, an object that holds a moment, sold carefully and refused to the ascension-cultists unless someone is desperate. What comes in is cloth and metal for the living, gloves, masks and hoods for the leaving, books, and grain in a lean year. Most of it goes on through **Tassast**, Galdua Jendea's frontier rock, and some reaches the Brokers' Walk on Anzar at one remove.

**The Guild's standing warning.** The Adventurers' Guild posts a permanent warning over the Blackened Lands, the only one of its kind on Talan, signed from the desk of Valreka's Guild Sovereign. A standing Guildhall instruction keeps every party below Platinum away from the cursed ground, and any party that passes the Mugarri is entered as missing, presumed lost; no recovery is sent. The Guild publishes no map of the interior.

**Who comes.** Tani-mourning sects come on pilgrimage to the edge and leave offerings at the Mugarri; the city leaves the offerings where they lie. Necromancers come for the resonance, and ascension-cultists for whatever the ruins might still hold of the old road. Guild parties come for recoveries, for the watch-works at Tassast, and since 2524 for the war.

**The leaving.** Few of Ida's people leave, and those who do go hooded and masked, with papers and a borrowed name: over the ridge to Tassast and its sandsails, which take in anyone before the sun, or by night in a smuggler's boat from the little harbour below the town. The Voroir Daua watch for them.

#### ◈ Popular Belief: the dead eat the living

The oldest horror told of the Old Race is that they ate the flesh of mortals as everyday food, meat on a stick from the street vendors; mothers frighten children with it, and sermons against the killers open with it. To most of Talan the dead are the dead, and over the centuries the tale has moved to whoever lives in the killers' ground: travellers say the dead of the Lost Kingdom eat the living. A party back from the edge will swear the wind off certain ruins smells of woodsmoke and roasting meat.

## Daily life

Under the haze, day and night barely differ, and Ida keeps time by the watch on the wall. The kept and the warped fish at first light and work the cisterns and the stores; the risen work the wall, the salvage and the long hours. There is black sand in everything, in the bread of the living and the joints of the risen, and the sea is the one clean smell. Some nights the legions can be heard walking somewhere out in the haze, and the whole city goes quiet to hear which way.

Ida speaks Talanese with an old shape to it, and Hizva coming back through the marthras. To an outsider who asks what they are, the people of Ida say *we live here*, and say no more.

**Going in.** Some go inside the Mugarri, nearer Vardana, to have more marthras: to live a few hours in the old kingdom their bones remember. They come back quieter, and some come back wanting the old way.

#### ◈ Popular Belief: the marthra of the killing

Everyone in Ida knows of one flash and fears it: the day the goddess fell, seen from the ground where it happened. Folk say those who come back from going in having had it are quieter than the rest; some never go in again, and some go in every month after. Whose memory it is, bones, soul or ground, is the oldest argument in the city.

**Out into the haze.** The people of Ida go out to the ruins in **salvage parties**, always with a caster, roped together so no one is lost in the haze, a ward-light carried at the head.

**Visitors.** A visitor sees the dead and draws steel. A visitor cannot tell a citizen from a legion soldier in the haze, and a native can, by the step. A visitor takes the masks for shame; at home in Ida a face of bone is a face, and masks are for going out.

**The young.** The risen have no childhood, but every one was new once. A living or warped child of Ida comes of age at the **first watch on the wall**; a risen at the **first marthra they can read**, when they can say *whose* and be believed, and that is when a risen takes a name. The young walk up the ridge to look at the living world from the cairns and come back before they are missed, and the pathra pretend not to know until someone does not come back.

**Faith.** Tani is Ida's goddess, and the elder face is the one the city keeps, the one who remembers. The seeing-to is done in her name, and the pathra light the wards each dusk with a word to her. Betibizi's priests keep their own rites and their stretches of the wall.

## What the people of Ida are called

A name in Ida is a given name and the source of the first marthra its bearer could read: **Ast** for the bones, **Urvan** for the soul, **Zam** for the ground. It is taken when its bearer can first say *whose* and be believed, living or risen, and it records the guess the city accepted: **Rauca Urvan**, **Hutaosa Zam**. Many add a **remembered name**, a name out of the marthras that they feel bound to, never certain whether it is theirs: **Vindafarna Ast Arshama**. Outsiders call the people of Ida *the cursed-born*.

**Sample names.** Given: Rauca, Utana, Hutaosa, Vindafarna, Taxma, Vahauka, Artavardiya, Gaubaruva, Hardanya, Irtashduna, Vidarna, Bagapana. Remembered: Arshama, Ariyaramna, Vishtaspa, Humaya. Whole: *Rauca Urvan* · *Utana Zam* · *Taxma Ast* · *Vindafarna Ast Arshama*.
