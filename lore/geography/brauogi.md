# Brauogi: Earth · Sarrum

**Borders:** Vindul, Ehizahar, Myrkono

**Seas:** Hafra (western coast) and Midarra (eastern and south-eastern coasts; Twin Suns; Soul Tree island offshore).

**Etymology:** Icelandic *brauð* (bread) + Basque *ogi* (bread) → fused to *Brauogi*. Both roots mean bread; the breadbasket named twice over in two ancient tongues.

**Position:** Northwest-central Talan, directly south of Vindul. Neighbours: Vindul (north), Ehizahar (east), Midarra (east/southeast), Floteyn (southeast corner), Myrkono (south); Hafra coast to the west. Twin Suns and Soul Tree sit on the Midarra-touching corner.

**Terrain:** Rolling fertile lowlands cut by dense river networks; most of the domain's rivers spring from **Iturmen**, the central east-west mountain spine, with their headwaters in **Iturburu**, the great mountain-lake under Iturmen's tallest peak. **Eraztumen**, the ring of peaks, encloses Lurrath and is the city-state's own territory and natural wall. **Mugamen**, the southern range, walls Brauogi off from Myrkono's deep forests. Western dark-mountains shelter the domain from weather rolling in off the Hafra coast. Agricultural heartland in earth-tones throughout: deep greens, brown river-soil, grey-and-rust mountain-stone, the gold of late-summer fields.

**Cultural character:** Earth-domain, Sarrum-the-Steadfast at its centre. Old noble houses control river trade and the granaries; the three-way ancestry politics (Minotaur / Kholo / Dragonet) is more cooperative than feud-prone because the rivers and roads have always been worth more to share than to fight over. The breadbasket feeds Vindul, Ehizahar, Myrkono, and Floteyn through nearby supply lines; the south-toward-Lautara grain trade runs across Midarra. The domain reads as the steady counterweight to its neighbours' more dramatic registers: Vindul's storms, Myrkono's shadow, Ehizahar's wild, Floteyn's water.

**Peoples:** Three dominant ancestries are anchored here: **Minotaur**, **Kholo**, and **Dragonet** (detailed below). Sarrum himself is depicted as a Minotaur, and Brauogi's Minotaurs are his cultural cousins. The three-way of Earth-domain politics is cooperative rather than feud-prone; grain, herd, and ore each need the other two, and they trade rather than fight.

**God's city:** Lurrath, The Steadfast City; stands inside the **Eraztumen** ring of peaks, which is the city-state's own territory (see *Lurrath* below). *(The former sub-region name Gotorlekua did not survive the Lautara/Brauogi border redraw; Lurrath's territory is simply the interior of the ring.)*

**Sub-regions:**
- **The Earth Realm**: the agricultural heart of Brauogi, the southern deep basin between Sugeiturri's source-uplands (north) and Mugamen (south); the broad fertile plains of the breadbasket. Fully golden plains: grain, livestock, market-towns at every road-fork, the daily-bread of the northern domains. Hirubaso, the sacred druid grove, sits as an enclave in its south-centre but is its own independent sub-region (see below).
- **Sugeiturri** *(the source-country)*: the high northern source-corridor and the Dragonet old-blood heartland, carved from the domain's rougher northern uplands and the source-mountains. Runs west to east from the Hafra coast past the Ilun Tasun gateway and the Iturmen passes (north into Fellibylur) to Iturburu and the source-peaks; the magirail to Lurrath's Gate runs through it. Nearly every river in Brauogi rises here, so every town in the domain drinks its water. An ancient blood-aristocracy of **River Houses** keeps the springs under the crown at Iturburu, worshipping Zaharsuge as the living current; one of the oldest kingdoms on Talan, founded when mortals took the rivers from the gods at the Crimson Rain. Full canon: [Sugeiturri](brauogi/sugeiturri.md).
- **Hirubaso**: an independent sub-region in the south-centre of Brauogi, the sacred druid grove of Sarrum where three rivers converge in the otherwise open plains. *(Basque hiru "three" + baso "forest", drifted from hiru-ibai-baso "three-river-forest" via loss of medial ibai.)* Tended by an order of Sarrum's faithful, Hirubaso is the still point of the breadbasket: the wild left untouched at the heart of the cultivated world. Governed by the druids' order itself; independent of the surrounding Earth Realm. **The only routine Primotech site on the continent**, where Brauogi's legendary mineral-Magitech tradition (see `../cosmology.md`) finds its grown-not-built source; the rumoured Brauogi heart-stone is grown here.
- **Baratalda**: the eastern arm of Brauogi at the Ehizahar border. *(Basque baratze "garden, orchard" + aldea "region" → drift drops the medial z to give Baratalda.)* Mixed golden plain and green orchard country; the orchard-and-fruit-cellar of Brauogi where the harvest-fairs draw Ehizahar hunters down from the Lands of Villtur to trade game and furs for wine, apple-brandy, and stone-fruit. The frontier-trade kingdom of the bread-domain.
- **Azkamour**: the north-western march of Brauogi, now **fully Brauogi** (formerly a Vindul/Brauogi shared border with its capital on the Vindul side, redrawn to the Brauogi side; its inland shoulder went to Sugeiturri). *(Basque azkar "swift" + muga "border": "the Swift March.")* Golden grain territory running up to the Vindul/Fellibylur frontier, with a stretch of Hafra coast where the storm-fishing routes meet the grain-roads; the fast riders carry both ways. See also [Vindul](vindul.md).
- **Twin Suns**: coastal sub-region on the Midarra-touching corner, the Brauogi-side half of the **Twin Suns / Bikitsa** border kingdom shared with Myrkono. Golden, food-rich coast where the two-sun phenomenon is observed at certain seasons; Soul Tree island lies offshore. The Myrkono-side counterpart (**Bikitsa**, Basque *biki-itzal* "twin-shadow", see [Myrkono](myrkono.md)) sees the same phenomenon from its shadow-domain side; the two halves are jointly governed under a single structure (form canon-pending). Modern English name; origin of the "twin suns" framing canon-pending (see `../../docs/open-threads.md`).
- **Soul Tree**: a small rocky island in Midarra, just off the coast of Twin Suns. Named for the single massive pale tree at its centre, an actual tree, through which souls may wander on to the Postlife. One of the most cosmologically significant sites on Talan: a natural passage point between the Life Layer and the Postlife that does not require death in the conventional sense. **Voroir Daua oldest charter**: the cycle-warden order maintains a permanent presence directly on the island, and this charter is their *oldest* (predates the Lograth / Thousand-Kingdom / Myrkono-edge presence noted in `../factions.md`). The site's natural-soul-passage role is exactly the cosmological function the order exists to watch, so charter primacy follows. The thematic match between a great rooted tree and Sarrum-the-Steadfast's Earth-domain affiliation is the cosmological resonance behind the sub-region's Brauogi attribution. Modern English name. The island's nearness to a true soul-passage makes it a known source of **Duskwalkers**, the psychopomp-touched children who manifest where the ties to death run close (see [`../ancestries.md`](../ancestries.md), Duskwalker).

**Note on Iturmen + Iturburu**: the central spine (Iturmen) and the source-lake under its tallest peak (Iturburu) belong to **Sugeiturri**, the source-country; they are the sacred heart of the Dragonet heartland, the wellhead where nearly every river in Brauogi rises and the seat of the crown at Iturburu. (Formerly framed as unclaimed druid-tended territory; the Sugeiturri build supersedes that, and the druids now keep Hirubaso alone.)

## Grain, Herd, and Ore

Brauogi is the cooperative domain the chronicles name because grain, herd, and ore each need the other two; three peoples share that triangle, each leaning into it by temperament. The domain-level read is a feeling of each people, never a practice.

**The Minotaurs outlast.** Nothing about a Minotaur moves quickly: anger arrives slowly and forgiveness slower, trust is laid course by course, and a task once shouldered is carried to the end, because setting it down half-done never occurs to them. They build as if the work were meant to outlast the builder, and they are right; a Minotaur's grudge and a Minotaur's friendship are made the same way, slowly, and to last. Sarrum is depicted as a Minotaur, and his cousins wear the resemblance without ceremony: they are the grain-farmers and stonecutters, the wall-builders and road-layers of the breadbasket, and their patience reads to outsiders as the confidence of the whole domain made flesh.

**The Kholo keep their own.** The first fact about any Kholo is who is hers: family by blood or family by decision, the circle she has taken responsibility for, and toward that circle a Kholo is never off duty. Matriarchal by disposition, they reckon a life by what it kept safe through the lean seasons; ask a Kholo who she is, and she will answer with who she keeps. In Brauogi they are the herder-clans of the breadbasket, driving the flocks across the river-margins and the upland grass, meat and milk and leather to the Minotaurs' bread and stone, and the night-watch over the herd is simply the temperament given work.

**The Dragonets are the old blood.** Their wyrm-line remembers an age before the bread, before the kingdoms, before the Dragons fell from another sky, and the remembering is in the blood rather than in any book: a Dragonet is born already belonging to something older than every map of Talan. It makes them unhurried and very hard to impress; a thousand-year ruin called ancient strikes them as a charming use of the word. Where the alien Dragons mourn what they lost, the Dragonets rest on what persists, and most feel a natural affinity toward Zaharsuge, whom their tradition holds as progenitor. The old blood wears a different life in each place it roots. At full expression in **Sugeiturri**, the source-country, they are the Keepers of the Current: the river-blood Houses who keep the springs all Brauogi drinks (see [Sugeiturri](brauogi/sugeiturri.md)). Elsewhere in the domain the same memory is the colour under a craft, often the deep-work and the metal-craft where stone gives way to metal, on which the breadbasket ploughs, reaps, shoes, and shears. The breadbasket eats on Minotaur grain, and the old blood is the colour under whatever patience it turns to.

**Cooperative by need.** None of the three is whole alone. The Minotaurs' fields want the Dragonets' iron and the Kholo's beasts; the Kholo's clans want grain through the lean months and metal for their gear; the Dragonets' deep-work wants the bread and the leather and the labour the other two trade down. So the domain that might have been three rival peoples is instead the steadiest on the continent, because Sarrum's earth gives each of them a third of what a living needs and none of them all of it. The rivers were always worth more shared than fought over.

## Lurrath, the Steadfast City (god's city-state)

Lurrath is the bedrock, and the name is the fact: *lur* and *harri*, earth and stone, the ground that holds. It stands inside Eraztumen, the ring of peaks, and the ring is its wall and its whole territory, one city in a basin of mountains that has never once been taken. Through the Crimson Rain, through the Lost Era and the Dark Era, through every spawn-wave and every war that broke the kingdoms around it, Lurrath held, and it is the continuity Talan reaches for when it has lost everything else: the city that does not fall. People come to live here for the plainest reason a city can offer. It is the one place that has never let its people down. The walls have never been breached, the harvest comes in, the wealth is safe, the contracts hold, and a family can build a life here and know to a certainty it will still be standing for their great-grandchildren, which everywhere else on Talan is a thing you can only hope.

Endurance here is not stillness, it is foundation. Lurrath grows and changes on the surface like any living city, new building, new generations, its own forward craft; what does not change is the bedrock it all stands on. The error a visitor makes is to expect a museum and find instead a confident, prosperous, deep-rooted place that simply does not worry, because the ground under it has never given way. Sarrum's own teaching runs against the museum: tradition is worth keeping only for what it carries, and the question his Keepers ask of every old thing is what it is holding and whether the weight is worth the bearing. Lurrath is not the city where nothing moves. It is the city you can build on.

**You are borne in.** Eraztumen has one gate, **Harrate**, the stone-gate, and the fast world reaches it and stops. The magirail runs a spur as far toward Lurrath as mountain country cheaply allows, to a railhead at Harrate, and ends there; airships moor at the gate-towers on the ring-crest. Nothing fast crosses the threshold. At the gate, everything (cargo, traveller, treaty, coffin) is taken up by Lurrath's great stone-oxen and its porters and carried the last miles in, slow, over roads cut to outlast the kingdoms that use them. To enter Lurrath is to be borne, to become for a few miles one of the burdens the city carries, and a traveller feels the city's pace take hold at the threshold, the rail-noise falling away behind and the slow creak of the dray ahead. Lurrath was never the northern rail-hub for no grander reason than cost: driving rail through a mountain range is ruinous, so the network's traffic gathers at flatter, cheaper cities, and one of those became the hub by simple trade-gravity. The mountains that keep Lurrath unfallen are the same mountains that keep it dear to reach, and that is the price of the safest walls on Talan, paid in trade.

**The ring feeds itself.** Inside Eraztumen, between Harrate and the city, lie the fields, because the basin has conditions found nowhere else: a high sheltered ring, a specific cold and light, soil rich off the same stone the city's craft draws on. There grows **Iraungar**, the lasting grain, a hard storing grain that keeps for decades without spoiling and grows in no other ground on Talan. It is Harvest and Endurance in one plant, and it makes Lurrath the continent's hedge against hunger: kingdoms buy Iraungar and stockpile it against the bad year, and when the bad year comes, Lurrath's granaries feed Talan. From the same grain comes **Iraungardo**, the enduring-beer, a low-alcohol beer that will not spoil either, the humble indestructible drink that travels anywhere and keeps for years, the everyday cup of Lurrath and a steady export. Where other god-cities prize a drink for its rarity and its aging, Lurrath's signature cup is prized for the opposite, for keeping, the beer that outlasts the famine and the voyage.

**What Lurrath builds.** The city's other great trade is permanence itself. Lurrath builds the things that hold: its Minotaur masons and Dragonet metal-wrights raise the roads, walls, bridges, and foundations of half the continent, stone laid to outlast the realm that commissioned it, and a Lurrathi road still carries traffic three dynasties after the dynasty that paid for it has fallen. The continent comes to Lurrath for the grain that outlasts the famine and the stonework that outlasts the kingdom, and the city is rich on the strength of building what lasts.

**How Lurrath moves, and what powers it.** Inside the walls you travel the worn way, the **graven ways**, streets of stone cut and worn into routes by forty generations of the same wheels and feet, so the traffic runs in the grooves the dead left and the depth of a rut tells a native how much a road has mattered and for how long. The city is a city of ramps, not stairs, because everything here is carried and a burden cannot climb a step. Drays and stone-oxen run the graven ways, and so do Lurrath's own machines, for Lurrath is not a backward city: it has its own earth-craft, **divinotech**, older than the magirail and entirely its own. Divine magic channels the god, who channels the Wellspring, and Lurrath industrialised exactly that. Its haulers and its works run on Sarrum's own power, drawn through the steadfast faith of his people, and divine magic is the most reliable there is, so the machines of the most reliable city on Talan essentially never fail. The belief holds them, and the belief is in steadfastness. A machine tended as a sacred duty across generations runs best of all, because the channel stays open through faithful keeping, so in Lurrath the oldest engines are the finest, the reverse of the Order of Steam's hunger for the new. It cannot be carried off, which is why it never spread and why the rail and the city never competed: divinotech needs Sarrum's presence and the faith-community both, and away from his reach and his people the channel fails. The continent runs on portable, godless, scalable arcanotech; Lurrath runs on the god who holds it up, through people who will not stop holding. The quiet truth beneath the safest city on Talan is that its foundation is not stone but faith, the one ground that is not mineral, and Lurrath has never once stopped believing.

**The Naming.** Each dawn before light, the Keepers read the city's dead. It is not one voice but hundreds, every Keeper at a scroll, a chord of overlapping names that runs from the dark to the first sun, a liturgy in which no single name can quite be picked out of the tide. The folk call it the reading of the city's dead, and that is true, but the Keepers, Sarrum's clergy and the keepers of the roll, hold the deeper shape of it: the Naming reaches every soul that still lingers in the Postlife, for a soul does not end at death but passes on, lingering there as itself until it is drawn at last through the Duskmire into the Wellspring, shedding all it was. That passage, not death, is the true ending, and a name falls from the roll only the dawn its soul finally goes into the source. To be read at Lurrath is to still be, somewhere. To fall from the list is the only death that counts, and Lurrath is the last thing on Talan that knows the difference between dead and gone. The roll turns over as the truly-gone drop away, so it never grows past bearing.

**The day, and the unchanging way.** The city wakes to the Naming, the whole of Lurrath rising in the dark to the chord of names, and then the ordinary day takes over: the masons to the stone, the metal-wrights to the deep, the granaries and the deep-cellars worked through the cool hours, the Iraungardo poured at evening in halls that have stood for forty generations. And here Lurrath turns the usual traveller's trouble on its head, for in Lurrath you do not get lost. It is the one city on Talan a stranger can cross unaided: the graven way they walked ten years ago is exactly where they left it, the same stones and the same turns, and a grandparent's directions still run true. Everywhere else a god-city shifts or drifts or reshuffles and the visitor is helpless; here the visitor walks as surely as a native and mistakes that ease for knowing the place. They do not. The native reads what the visitor walks straight past: the grooves that tell how much a road has mattered and for how long, the name-stones, the walls that carry the dead, the slowness that is care rather than torpor, the weight that is the whole point. The stranger has the map; the Lurrathi has the meaning. A visitor finds Lurrath simple, and a little dull, and the Lurrathi, who has stood at the crest and seen how the rest of the world will not hold still, knows that the simplicity is the rarest thing there is.

**Government: the Bearers.** Lurrath is run by those who carry it. There is no vote, no lot, no assembly: authority is responsibility made literal, and the only way up is to start at the bottom carrying small duties and climb by bearing more, year on year, never dropping a load. The civil service of the **Bearers** is the most senior the most burdened, each carrying their own portfolio of the city's duties, and the **First Bearer** at the top is simply whoever carries most. It is an administration, not a debating-house, and the weight is its own check: a tyrant cannot seize it, because nothing in Lurrath shortcuts forty years of proven bearing, and anyone who drops a duty is finished, not voted out but quietly no longer trusted with the load. The most powerful Lurrathi is the most bound, office being a weight one is trusted with rather than a prize one wins, and the city runs on reliability the way other cities run on ambition.

**The peoples, and the young.** Lurrath is the three peoples of Brauogi at their work: the **Minotaurs** who cut and lay, the stonemasons and road-builders and wall-keepers whose patience is the city made flesh and who outlast everything they set their shoulder to; the **Kholo** who keep their own, the drovers and the breeders of the great stone-oxen, the herder-clans who reckon a life by what it kept safe; and the **Dragonets** of the old blood, the miners and metal-wrights whose lineages root where stone gives way to metal and whose memory is older than every map. The young pull two ways at once. The coming-of-age is the climb: a young Lurrathi ascends the inner faces of Eraztumen to the ring-crest and stands there between two views, the protected basin that raised them on one side and the wide precarious world on the other, and chooses. Most come down and take up their place; some go over the crest and into the world to trade and wander, and the Minotaur trade-houses scattered across Talan are the ones who chose out, each with a home that keeps a place for their return. And against the sanctioned climb runs its shadow, the forbidden descent: under the founded city lie the sealed deep, the old mine-workings and shut lower levels the city dug and then built over across the ages, and the young break into them by night on a dare and a lantern, down into the dark the city closed, to walk under the whole weight of the place and feel whether the ground truly holds. It is the one doubt the safest city raises its children never to feel, taken in the one form it can be taken, and every Lurrathi adult carries a story of the night they went down and what they thought they heard. In the city of permanent stone even a child's scratched name outlives empires, so a Lurrathi knows their grandmother's mark waits in some sealed shaft, cut the night she went down.

**Faith: the one already standing.** Sarrum is present in **Zutarri**, the standing-stone, his sanctum and the pillar at the city's heart, the foundation the divinotech draws through and the temple-archive where the Keepers hold the roll. He does not walk his city the way the lighter gods walk theirs. You never catch Sarrum arriving or leaving; he is simply already there, vast and still, ore-light faint beneath granite, as though he has stood in that place since before you were born and will stand there long after, the god of the unmoved being the one who was always already holding. Each dawn he takes his place among the Keepers at the Naming, his own scroll in his hands, and reads names off it, no louder than anyone, one more steadfast voice in the chord, a god doing the liturgy rather than presiding over it.

**◈ Popular Belief: he will read you.** They say the god takes the hardest scroll of all, the **scroll of the unremembered**: the names with no family left to speak them, the unmourned, the forgotten, the ones whose every living memory has died. No one in Lurrath slides off the roll for want of a voice, because when the last person who knew you is gone, your name passes to the god's own scroll, and Sarrum reads it himself, one more steadfast voice, for as long as you linger anywhere in the world. So the comfort offered to the truly alone at a Lurrathi deathbed is three words, and they are not small ones: *he will read you.* You may be forgotten by every soul who ever knew your face, and still, every dawn, a god will say your name into the light.

**⚿ GM Secret: the names of power.** The Naming reads every mortal-origin soul still lingering in the Postlife, and that is a larger company than the city dreams. Among the thousands spoken each dawn, drowned in the chord and known to no one, are the true mortal names of the seat-holders of Bolverk: the Vice Demons and the Virtue Devils who were once mortal, whose original names are the names of power, the names that crack them. They are said aloud at Lurrath every dawn, unattributed, the one place on Talan where every demon lord's true name still sounds, and the Keepers have no idea which names in the tide are the load-bearing ones. The demon lords can do nothing about it: the Naming reads what cosmologically still is, and the Keepers have neither the power nor the will to omit a soul. The deeper turn is in the god's own scroll. Those demon lords whose mortal selves are utterly lost, scoured from every record and mourned by no one, fall to the **scroll of the unremembered**, so Sarrum himself reads the true names of the forgotten seats of Bolverk each dawn, in his steadfast voice, among ten thousand other forgotten souls, and whether the god knows which of his forgotten names could unmake a throne of the Abyss is the question under the question. The lone exception proves the rule: the fallen Sun-Champion, **Drambur** of the Pride seat, is *remembered*, his name held in Iro's own memory across the continent (see [`../gods.md`](../gods.md), *Iro → GM Secret: the wound from Drambur*, and the Ljosarn build in [`egulon.md`](egulon.md)), so his name is read at Lurrath in the general flood by an ordinary mortal voice and not on the god's scroll, the one place outside Iro's mind where the Sun-Champion's name is still spoken aloud, by people who will never know they hold it. And one name the liturgy can never reach at all: **Lethar** of the Sloth seat came from outside Tyrnarra, never Wellspring-born and on no roll the Wellspring keeps, the single seat of Bolverk whose name Lurrath cannot say.
