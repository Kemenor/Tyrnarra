# Ehizahar: Hunt · Hinka

**Borders:** Nashavel, Vindul, Brauogi

**Seas:** Hafra (northern arctic coast; Ardo Beroa ice-isle archipelago offshore).

**Etymology:** Basque *ehiza* (hunt) + *zahar* (ancient, old) → *ehiza-zahar* → naturally eroded to *Ehizahar*. The ancient hunt. The oldest name on Talan, for the oldest land.

**Position:** Northeast Talan: the largest domain, spanning arctic north to jungle south

**Terrain:** The most varied domain. Arctic/icy tundra in the far north, with a small ice-isle archipelago off the northern coast. The highest mountain range on the continent and the oldest trees (ancient dark conifers, northwest corner). Open grassland through the center. Deep jungle-like old-growth forest in the south and southeast. Rivers throughout. The domain that feels most primordially "of the world itself." A meteor struck deep antiquity, leaving a vast crater that still bleeds slow volcanism into the surrounding land: the **Fenurra** sub-region, the only significant volcanic activity outside Sumendar.

**God's city:** Veidrath, The Hunting City (see *Veidrath* below)

**Peoples:** Three dominant ancestries are anchored here: **Orc**, **Lizardfolk**, and **Goloma**, the three phases of one hunt; beside them stand the **Fenurran tribes** of the crater-country, several peoples united by one wyrm-blood (see *Fenurra* below). The domain-level read is a feeling of each people, never a practice; the cultures live with the sub-regions, and Hinka's faces sit behind the three as inspiration: Predation, Patience, Survival. Her Cruelty is the face nature itself claims.

- **The Orcs close.** Predation as temperament: the world met head-on, now, holding back the one unbearable thing. Loudest, first in, most consistently in feud; Hinka is depicted as a red-headed orc, and the affinity runs deep. The hunt ends with them.
- **The Lizardfolk never stop coming.** Patience as temperament: slow to start and impossible to shake. A Lizardfolk decision takes its time arriving, and once made it is inevitable; the people of the long stalk, deliberate at the kill, unhurried in a way that frightens prey more than speed would.
- **The Goloma see it first.** Survival as temperament: the hunt known from the prey's side. Many-eyed, secretive, patient at the quiet edges, a Goloma is the one who noticed before anyone else and said nothing yet. A people shaped by being hunted, at home among the hunters, because nobody reads a predator better than the watched.

The Goloma see it, the Lizardfolk run it down, the Orcs bring it low: one hunt in three peoples, and the Villtur feuds are what happens when the three phases of a hunt share one land and each believes its phase is the essential one. **Cruelty** is the face nature itself claims: not the cruelty of machinations but the cruelty of survival, the lean winter, the kill that feeds, some dying so that others may live. No hunter needs to be cruel for the hunt to have cruelty in it; the land holds that face, and the tribes leave it there.

**The roaming and the rooted.** The present age runs one tension through every clan of all three peoples: most still ride the hunt-circuits as they always have, but a growing number have begun to settle, villages at the river-fords, towns where the rail touches the domain's edges. The question every settling clan answers differently is whether the hunt survives an address: a settled Goloma watches a town the way her grandmother watched a trail, a settled Orc finds the crush of market-day carries something of a well-led charge, and the roaming clans have not decided whether their settled cousins are scouts in new country or prey that built its own pen.

**Sub-regions:**
- **Lands of Villtur**: the umbrella tribal territory covering most of Ehizahar. The domain is overwhelmingly tribal: no unified kingdoms, no formal borders. The many tribes that live across the arctic tundra, grasslands, and ancient forests all fall under this collective name. Veidrath sits within this expanse. *(Icelandic villtur = wild, lost, strayed; old name, fits the ancient naming rule.)*
- **Fenurra**: a sub-region of Ehizahar in its own right, bordering the Lands of Villtur but distinct from them. Defined by an ancient meteor crater (the **Scar of Aeris**, capital city **Aeris** carved into its inner walls) and the volcanic activity that still surrounds it: sulfurous fumes, basalt fields, slow lava flows, and strange star-metals seeded into the ground by the impact. Harsh and resource-poor in conventional terms, but rich in materials no other place on Talan produces (Blackglass, Velthite, Ghost Willow, Obsidian Silk). The Fenurran tribes who live here are hardened, pragmatic, and politically distinct from the wider Villtur tribal sprawl. The tribes are united by the **Dragonblood** heritage rather than by ancestry: each tribe its own major people, one wyrm-blood through all four (Draconis Dwarves · Vexiren Goloma · Brakkaun Orcs · Seravain Hobgoblins). The only significant volcanic activity outside Sumendar. **Full canon: [`ehizahar/fenurra.md`](ehizahar/fenurra.md)** (four tribes Draconis · Vexiren · Brakkaun · Seravain, Speaker's Mantle politics, Bone Gong + War Council, materials, warfare doctrine, Notable Weapons table, the schism with the Divine Faith).
- **Basogur Jungle**: the deep jungle territory in the south and southeast, shared with Nashavel. See also [Nashavel](nashavel.md). *(Continental rail-blocker; see the Nashavel entry above for the full breakdown.)*
- **Ardo Beroa**: the small ice-isle archipelago off Ehizahar's northern arctic coast. *(Basque ardo = wine + beroa = hot, warm → "warm wine" or "hot wine." Old name, etymology partially obscured by drift; the ironic mismatch between the literal meaning and the arctic character is part of the local lore.)*

## Veidrath, the Hunting City (god's city-state)

Veidrath is the hunting-stead, Hinka's seat, and it is the one city on Talan you can stand in and watch becoming something else. It is the Hunt's gathering-place caught in the middle of settling down, and the settling is not a question it might face one day but a thing happening to it now, year by year, in stone you can put your hand on. Veidrath is the crucible itself, the place where the roaming life and the rooted life meet at Hinka's own door and have not yet decided what they make together; here the question stays open and lived, argued anew each season in the city's own shifting stone, an answer still being made rather than a settlement already reached.

**Three strata, and a frontier that moves.** At the heart is the ancient Core, the permanent sacred ground around **Igarbe**, Hinka's sanctum, where the great hunts are blessed out and the dying are crossed over, the one part of Veidrath that has always stayed. Around it has grown a belt of permanent districts, the clans that committed: houses, walls, year-round trades, and the two flashpoints that mark how far the settling has come, the district that raised an airship-docking tower to pull sky-freight down out of the Hafra winds, and the district courting the magirail with a bid to drag a railhead up to the city's edge. And around those still lie the seasonal tent-rings, bare frames and old fire-scars most of the year, blooming overnight with hide-tents and smoke and picketed beasts when a clan rides in. The frontier between stone and tent moves outward season by season: the roamers return to find more stone where their grandparents pitched camp, the tower a little taller against the sky, the rail a little nearer, and the city no longer empties the way it did in living memory.

**The clans are varied peoples, and the divide is a spectrum that has always existed.** Veidrath's clans are semi-nomadic in the steppe way, and the everyday of them is far more than the hunt: herding above all, for the beasts are the wealth; seasonal cultivation, a clan reaching a region to raise a camp and sow and harvest a season's growing before the migration moves on; craft in felt and leather and dairy and metal; and trade. The hunt runs through all of it as the sacred thread, Hinka's own thing and the oldest prestige, but it was never the whole of how a clan eats. Each clan is its own mix, one heavy with horse-herds, one known for a craft, one that cultivates more than it hunts. And the settle-roam divide is not two camps but a continuum with every gradation living on it, a continuum the domain has held forever (Fenurra has been settled crater-country for ages; the deep-Villtur clans have ridden the same loops just as long). What is happening now is the whole spectrum sliding a notch rooted-ward each generation, and the new infrastructure giving the settled end a reach it never had. So the friction is spread all along the gradient, not at one line: a clan that shortened its circuit looks roaming to the settled district and settled to the deep-Villtur clan, and takes it from both sides. The question *is a hunter who stays still a hunter* is not a wall between two peoples; it is a question every clan and half the families inside each clan answer a little differently, and where a person sits on it shifts under them across a life.

**The city breathes irregularly, in many tempos at once.** It is not one pulse of full-then-empty. Different clans hold Veidrath completely differently: some winter here in full and ride out only when the grass returns, some keep a foot in a settled district year-round and send the rest of the clan on the circuit, some pass through twice a season to trade and move on, some come only for the peak convergences. There are peaks, the great seasonal markets, Hinka's winter when goddess and clans alike come home, the big communal hunts that need a dozen clans to bring down what one cannot, and at those the city swells hardest; but between them Veidrath is never simply empty, only a constant changing population, this clan arriving as that one leaves. And status rides the same spectrum: the kill is the oldest and most sacred measure of a clan's standing, Hinka's own coin, but a settling clan's wealth in herds and holdings and secured trade-routes has begun to compete with it, so the drift rooted-ward is also a quiet argument about what counts, the beast you brought down or the beasts you keep, the hunt you led or the route you hold.

**Movement is the city's split made visible.** The old circulation reaches outward: the roaming clans have no addresses, they have circuits, seasonal migration-loops that run from Veidrath into Villtur for hundreds of miles, following pasture and water and game and growing-ground, the same loops their ancestors rode. To find a clan that is out you do not go to a place, you learn its circuit and ride to intercept it where the season says it will be. They move mounted on the open grass and run with the bond-beasts each hunter raises and hunts beside, and the circuits turn all year like a great slow wheel with the city at the axle. The new circulation reaches inward: the airship tower and the courted rail pull the wider continent toward the city, the goods and the traders and the news, so the settled no longer ride anywhere to touch the world but wait for it to arrive at the dock. And Veidrath is the one square on Talan where the two meet, where a clan rides in off a three-hundred-mile circuit with a season's kill lashed to the beasts and pickets its mounts in the shadow of an airship mast while a sky-freighter unloads milled grain at the dock beside them. The roamers see the towers and read precisely what they are: the world reaching in to a place that used to reach out.

**The great exchange, and who earns the worth of the wild.** Veidrath exists because it is the one market where the wild interior trades with the settled world. Villtur is the most varied land on Talan, ice to jungle, and the clans bring the produce of all of it: the widest range of furs and pelts the continent can buy, hides and leather and felt, livestock and horse-herds, horn and bone, the hunt's harvest and its prestige trophies, the seasonal crops, the clan-crafts. Against that the settled world sends in what a herding people does not make: worked metal, milled grain, woven cloth, manufactured goods. Three exports are Veidrath's own and no one else's: the furs, because no other domain spans arctic white to jungle spot; the **bond-beasts**, the finest hunting and riding animals on Talan, which the continent's great houses and cavalries come here to buy; and the people themselves, for a Villtur-raised tracker, scout, guide, or beast-handler is the premier of that craft anywhere, hired across the continent the way Fellibylur hires out its Stormriders. Increasingly the great hunt is an export too, the continent's nobles riding the airship in to hunt the deep wild with a clan guide. And the settle-roam tension runs straight through the money, because the value captures at the settled end: the roaming clan that took the white-fox in a northern blizzard sells it raw to a settled fur-house that finishes and brokers it to the airship-trade for many times what the hunter saw. The settled say they built the market that makes the fur worth anything; the roamer says they are growing rich off a kill they would never have dared. Who earns the worth of the wild is the tension at its hardest and least romantic, and it sharpens every year the towers grow.

**No single government, by no accident.** Veidrath rules itself in two layers that do not mesh, and the lack of a bridge between them is the crucible, not a flaw waiting to be fixed. The old layer is Hinka's clergy, the **Stillhands**, as the neutral host of the gathering. They command no clan; each clan runs its own affairs its own way, and the Stillhands host and arbitrate the shared thing, keeping the calendar, assigning the gathering-grounds (which is real power, since who camps where and every tideline dispute routes through them), mediating the feuds that cannot be settled clan to clan, and above all guaranteeing the **Gathering-Peace**, the truce that lets clans who would kill each other on the circuit trade safely in the same market. They are trusted with this for a reason that is not political: they are Hinka's, they take no clan's side, and they are the ones who cross your dying over, so the hands that hold your death are trusted to hold your disputes. Breaking the Gathering-Peace is the one offense roaming and settled treat alike as unforgivable. The new layer is the settled districts growing the town-institutions the old custom never covered, a market-court here, a dock-authority there, a committee for the rail, the unglamorous bureaucracy of a town becoming a town, and the roaming clans have no part in it and recognise none of it. Nothing governs both. The Stillhands will not claim the settled districts' new business, which is neither the gathering nor sacred nor theirs; the settled institutions hold no standing over the roaming clans, who never agreed to them. To resolve the government would be to resolve the city, and the city has not.

**The young, and the rite the settling cannot shed.** The coming-of-age is the first hunt, and it is the one thing the whole spectrum still shares: however settled a family has become, however many generations deep in the tower district, the child goes out to the wild and makes a kill to cross into adulthood in Hinka's eyes. The most settled district on the continent cannot raise an adult without Villtur, and it knows it, for the wild reclaims even the most rooted child for that one season. The rite is itself under the settling pressure, and the argument over it is sharp: the rooted families want it softened, a nearer hunt, a guided party, a kill made easy for a child who has never slept a night off a street, and the deep-Villtur clans despise the soft version and say a kill handed to you crosses nothing, that the city is raising children who wear the rite without paying it. And the rebellion is the engine of the whole thing, because the young rebel by crossing the spectrum away from their parents: the roaming-clan child who comes to the gathering and will not leave, who takes work on the airship mast against a father who rides out without them, and the settled-district child who makes a first kill and keeps going, rides off down a clan's circuit and sends word they are not coming back. Both happen, and Veidrath sings about both, but not in equal numbers: more roaming-young want to stay than settled-young want to ride, every generation, because the towers are bright and the sleet is cold and the world is arriving here now. So the settling of Veidrath is no policy and no faction's victory; it is the sum of each generation's young drifting a notch rooted-ward, voting with their feet toward the dock. The romantic counter-current, the townie who rode off and became a legend on the circuits, is real and beloved and the minority, which is exactly why they sing it: the song honours the rarer choice, and the drift the other way needs no song because you can watch it happen, one young hunter at a time deciding the gathering is enough hunt for a lifetime.

**Faith: the goddess who is settling with her people.** Hinka hunted as her people hunt. Through the hunting-seasons she was out, in Villtur with the clans, and her sanctum at Igarbe stood as empty as the tent-rings around it; you could petition her only in winter, when goddess and clans alike came home, or you might cross her by pure chance far out on the circuits, a red-headed orc-hunter who falls in beside your hunt, reads the trail you missed, takes the one clean shot, and is gone over the ridge before you know who shared your fire. A wild, elusive, mostly-absent goddess, found and never summoned. And she is settling, because her people are. Her hunts have shortened; she stays in the sanctum longer; she comes back earlier and dips into the city mid-season now, where a generation ago she was gone until winter. Her clergy, the Stillhands, have always served her at both ends of the one vocation, the field-priests who bless the hunt and bring the still clean hand to the kill, and the death-priests who sit with the dying and bring the same still hand to the bedside, and the balance between those two is tipping in living memory: as her settling people die settled deaths, old age in a bed, sickness in a town, rather than the clean death of the hunt, the death-crossing swells and the hunt-blessing thins. Her theology holds through it unchanged, for death was always *completion, a threshold crossed with dignity*; what changes is its reach, the goddess of the hunting-death broadening into the Tender of every threshold, the one who sees you across however you go. This is no diminishment and no danger to her, for gods do not die, they transform with the belief that shapes them, exactly as Araphel the god of Darkness became the god of second chances when Myrria grew into the city of new beginnings. Veidrath is only the rare place to watch it happen in real time, one settled clan at a time. The argument over it runs through the temple as it runs through every clan: the field-priests read the broadening as the Hunt being watered into a hearth-comfort and resist it, keeping to the old wild rites; the swelling death-priests embody the other reading, that this is no loss but a deepening, the goddess of the kill growing into the keeper of every ending. Neither can prove it, because both Hinkas are fully her.

**◈ Popular Belief: the two thresholds.** Veidrath meets its goddess at the two ends of her work. The hunters say she still comes to the wild kill, the red-headed huntress who shares your fire and your last clean shot and is gone over the ridge, met one of the last places the wild Hinka is still met at all. The settled say she comes to the bedside, a peaceful presence at the crossing, and that a death she attends is a good death, unhurried and unafraid. And everyone has noticed she is *around* more than she used to be, present in the city where her grandmother's grandmother saw her once in a lifetime and far away. Most take it for a comfort, the goddess staying with her people now. The old hunters take it more quietly, because they can feel the wild one receding, and they have no word for what they are losing that does not sound like ingratitude.

**⚿ GM Secret: there is no first Hinka to keep.** The field-priests and the deep-Villtur clans fight to preserve the *true* wild Huntress against the settling, certain they hold the line for the real and timeless goddess against a late dilution. The hidden truth is that the wild Huntress is herself a late form. Hinka has transformed in every age, always, because belief has always shaped her, and the fierce red-headed Orc drawing the bow of horn is simply the shape the last long age of wild faith gave her, no more original than the Tender she is becoming now. There is no first Hinka beneath the transformations to recover; every Hinka was the belief-shape of her era, and the conservatives are straining to freeze a single frame of a goddess who has never once stopped becoming, venerating as eternal the most recent thing belief made of her. The deepest turn sits on the far side of it: the Tender they are resisting will herself, in some age to come, be the *old wild Hinka* that a future clergy fights to preserve against whatever belief makes of her next. The Hunt they think they are defending was always already a transformation, and so is everything they will lose it to.
