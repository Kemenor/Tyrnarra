# Nashavel: Chaos · Vesuna

**Borders:** Zuzental, Ehizahar

**Seas:** Hafra (eastern coast).

**Etymology:** Basque *nahas* (chaos, disorder, to mix) → rearranged and drifted to *Nashavel*. The chaos of the domain is in the name itself: the root scrambled on its way to the present.

**Position:** Northeast Talan, east of Ehizahar and Midarra, north of Zuzental and Egulon, coastal on Hafra

**Terrain:** Overwhelmingly dense old-growth forest, far more tree coverage than any other domain. Open grassland only in the south. A lake to the west, river in the south, mountains to the east. Wild magic woven into the trees themselves; paths don't stay fixed.

**God's city:** Nahaskel, The Unmapped City

**Peoples:** Four dominant ancestries are anchored here: **Gnome**, **Vanara** (Basogur), **Conrasu**, and **Anadi** (Basogur). The domain-level read is a feeling of each people, never a practice; the cultures live with the sub-regions, and the four feelings are four stances toward the domain's one weather, change. Vesuna's faces sit behind them as inspiration: Spontaneity, Change, Creativity, Madness; her Luck stays in the coin, which belongs to the goddess and no people.

- **The Gnomes spin it.** Spontaneity as temperament: the idea arrives and the hands are already moving. *Let us try it differently this week* is the resting state, and boredom is the only suffering a Gnome takes seriously. They do not endure the churn; they generate it.
- **The Vanara ride it.** Change is the element they are native to, the way the Strix are native to moving air. Neither seeking the current nor resisting it, sure-footed on ground that will not hold still; where others need the world fixed or need it changing, a Vanara needs only to know which way it is moving today.
- **The Anadi re-weave it.** Making as devotion. An Anadi's order is not a wall against the churn but a daily practice inside it: the web tears, and the Anadi is unbothered, because the web was never the point, the weaving is. Creation re-done every morning, serene about destruction in a way that unnerves tidier peoples.
- **The Conrasu know what is underneath.** A Conrasu is chaos given a body: they have been formlessness, and the chosen shape, kept deliberately, is sanity practiced as a daily act. Their carefulness is the discipline of someone who knows exactly what is at the bottom, because they came from it. The thread of caution in any room is the voice of the only people who do not have to imagine what unravelling means.

Spontaneity spins, Change rides, Creativity re-weaves, and Madness, known from the inside, chooses a shape and keeps it.

**Sub-regions:**
- **Vernua Dominion**: the sub-region housing Nahaskel, Vesuna's city-state. The city sits within the Dominion but resists being governed by it; the relationship between mortal authority and the god's presence here is perpetually contested. *(Vernua likely drifted from Vesuna's own name; the Dominion named in her shadow.)*
- **Kaosadaemi Principality**: *"the chaos district."* Basque *kaosa* (chaos) + Icelandic *dæmi* (district, domain). Old compound name, fits Nashavel's chaos character.
- **Basogur Jungle**: dense jungle sub-region shared with Ehizahar. *(Basque baso = forest/wood → drifted to Basogur. An old name for an old place.)* The deep interior holds the **Anadi web-holds**, the thickest clustering of the re-weavers on Talan, and the Vanara live throughout, navigating the politics of the two domains around the jungle rather than belonging to either. See also [Ehizahar](ehizahar.md). **The continental rail-blocker:** Basogur bisects the east-central waist of Talan and is the reason there is no through-rail between the northern and southern Magitrain networks (see `../cosmology.md`, *Technology: Magitech and Infrastructure → The Continental Rail Network*). Arcanotech engineering attempts founder on the jungle's Vesuna-touched chaos-magic interference (rails warp, runes fail); indigenous Vanara/Anadi/leshy/beast-spirits/druid resistance dismantles construction as it happens; and the Nashavel/Ehizahar political deadlock prevents any consortium from securing the right of way. **North-south travel routes around Basogur by stillship across Midarra, by airship over the jungle (Arcanotech airships suffer turbulence and instrument drift; Occultech airships fly clean), or by the long overland bypass.**

#### Nahaskel, The Unmapped City (god's city-state)

The cartographers gave up centuries ago; the locals never bothered. Nahaskel is the city its own people will not stop remaking. **What is yours, you remake freely:** your home, your shop, your craft, the trade you ply, all of it changed overnight on nothing but a notion, no permission asked and no authority to ask. The baker who was here yesterday is a nail-shop this morning because the baker simply decided, and no register tracks it because none could. **What is shared answers to the coin.** A bridge, a public square, a rule that would bind a neighbour, the very government of a quarter: those go up as a proposal, and the proposal is settled by a throw. The city erupts a thousand small changes a day and a few enormous ones a week, and the one thing that never changes is that the people do not mind.

**The coin and the Casting.** Vesuna's worship keeps no fixed liturgy anywhere on Talan, and in her own city the coin that unifies all her rites is also the machinery of state. When a shared matter comes due, the quarter gathers and the coin is thrown in the open. This is **the Casting**: governance and worship in a single act, and the one event a city that schedules nothing reliably turns out for. A throw is sacred and final. You do not appeal a cast; you propose again next time, and the city has reinvented itself out from under your grievance before the bitterness can set. It is the deepest reason no tyrant has ever held Nahaskel: you cannot keep an office the coin reassigns, nor rig a throw the whole quarter is watching. Below the civic scale, the faith lives in the **personal coin** most Nahaskeli carry, spent on the small private ties of a day. *Heads I take the river path, tails I try the new baker.* You let the coin pick to keep yourself limber, to practise letting go of the wheel where it costs nothing, so that when it costs something you already know how.

**Getting about, or not.** Nahaskel has no map and wants none, and where Haizava hires a wind-reader to read its skies, Nahaskel keeps no one whose trade is knowing where things are, because no one fully does. A native is not oriented; a native is at peace with being a little lost. There are no fixed addresses. You go toward a thing and ask the last few yards (*past where the glassblower was last week, ask again there*), and asking a stranger the way is constant, universal, and carries no shame whatever. What floors a visitor is that there is nothing to master: they keep trying to learn the city the way you learn any other, and it will not hold still long enough, and the local finds their suffering gently funny. How people move is its own churn. One quarter is deep in a one-wheel craze while the next has gone back to horses; a man rides a friendly summoned thing named **Grumble** that bites only when its stomach growls; somebody walks because why not. Every so often a fad blooms across the whole city the way starlings turn together, three weeks of everyone on scooters that nobody decreed, then it shatters and scatters back into the jumble. The conveyances pay the **Chaos tax** too: Grumble is the charming end of a spectrum whose far end is the contraption that throws its rider and the summons that did not come out friendly. The one transport that keeps pace with the city is the **web-walks**: the Anadi weavers' silken walkways strung between rooftops and across the gaps the rebuilds open, broad enough and tough enough for anyone to walk, re-strung weekly and sometimes overnight, so that when a quarter remakes itself in the dark the ways across it are already hanging by morning. The web-walks are the nearest thing Nahaskel has to infrastructure, and they last precisely because their makers never mind taking them down: the weavers re-string the city the way the city remakes itself, and neither would dream of asking the other to stop.

**An ordinary day.** A Nahaskeli wakes not quite knowing what changed in the night, and the first hour is re-learning the city, neighbours calling across the street what moved and what is gone and what somebody built where the well used to be. It is not a burden; it is the day's first small pleasure, weather you can talk to. Work is fluid, trades picked up and set down, and the texture of the crowd is every ancestry that thrives on change: **Gnomes** above all, the chaos-feyfolk who hold the city's temperament and treat *let us try it differently* as the resting state; **Ganzi** with the planes in their blood; **Conrasu**, chaos given a body and a careful disposition, who tend to be the ones keeping a thread of caution in a city short on it; and the **Anadi** weavers of the web-walks, whose re-strung walkways are the one thing in Nahaskel that adapts as fast as Nahaskel does.

**The young.** Where reinvention is the establishment, rebellion has to invert, and it does, two ways. The **thrill-crews** chase the Chaos tax their elders will not: summon the beast that might not stay friendly, ride the rebuild that might fall, stake casts no sensible adult would touch, for the thrill and the standing of having ridden the thing and lived. They are where the tax takes its casualties. The quieter rebels do the genuinely shocking thing, which in Nahaskel is to *stay the same*: pick one trade, one face, one shopfront, and refuse to change it out of sheer principle while their parents fret that they have gone rigid. It is the only city on Talan where *you never change* is the daring thing to say. The famous one is **Dossa**, a Conrasu (chaos given a body, choosing to hold still) who has kept the same tea-stall on the same corner for two scandalous years and will not alter so much as the sign. Her neighbours fret; lost visitors adore her, because Dossa's stall is the one place in Nahaskel you can find *twice*, which has made a permanence-rebel into the most reliable direction-giver in a city that gives directions by asking. A Nahaskeli **comes of age** with their own coin, the one they will carry for life, given or struck or won, and stakes their first binding cast: the first throw on something real and shared that they must then live by, un-taken-back. A child has others throw for them. You are an adult the day you let the coin decide a thing you cannot undo.

**What the city makes and cannot keep.** Nahaskel exports novelty itself, in every form: an invention, a spell, a new working of magic, an item, a technique, a recipe no stable workshop would have risked. It cannot make the same thing twice (no process survives the week), so it ships the spark and never the product line. Eldara's devotional crafters come not to copy but to be *inspired*, to carry a new idea home into their singular wonders; the actual mass-production happens far downstream, in the Order of Steam's works and Lautara's markets. **Prospectors** from all of them haunt Nahaskel as a perpetual idea-fair, racing to be first to the week's inventions and first to sign the mind behind one before it burns out or wanders off. That is the quiet drain on the city: a Nahaskeli with an idea worth keeping often leaves to go *build* it somewhere it will survive. The standing example is **Pim**, a Gnome who one night cast a small clockwork into being on the Casting-floor and, finding no way to make a second, carried it to the Order of Steam, where it became a thing now sold in half the kitchens on Talan as a *pim*. He lives rich and far away now, but he comes back every so often, throws in on a rebuild for a few weeks for the sheer joy of it, and leaves again. In Nahaskel the night he first cast it is forgotten; nobody quite connects the cheerful Gnome lending a hand on the scaffolding to the *pim* in their own kitchen, the eponym outliving its origin everywhere except the city that birthed it. What the city cannot do is hold still, so it sends its permanence outward: its banking to Merkavar, its binding law to Lograth, its manufacture to the Order of Steam. And it imports what must be reliable, food and durable staples off the Vernua Dominion countryside, because no dependable chain runs from farm to market inside the churn.

**The Tellers.** Vesuna's clergy keep no creed to preach, so **the Tellers** do other work: they call the coin at a Casting and witness the throw, they read it in divination for those who ask, and they keep the telling, the running memory of what the city was last week and the week before, the nearest thing a place with no archive has to one. The eldest of them is **Old Tally**, a Gnome who has kept the telling longer than anyone alive and can name three vanished versions of any street you point to, the city's living memory in a body that has outlasted half the streets it remembers. She tends Vesuna's Sanctum, which the city believes drifts and reshapes like everything else.

#### ◈ Popular Belief: the New Face

Vesuna wears no fixed form, no gender, no ancestry, so in her own city she is **the New Face**, the face nobody knows. The newcomer at the Casting with the mad proposal; the unfamiliar voice in the crowd that says *what if we tried it the other way* and watches it catch. The Nahaskeli instinct to hear out the stranger with the impossible notion is older than the city's manners and deeper than its patience: in a place where everyone is a little lost and any new face might be the goddess, no one is turned away unheard. The one time you waved off the stranger, the chronicles like to say, that was Her.

#### ⚿ GM Secret: the coin is honest, except at the two edges

By every count Nahaskel should have torn itself apart a thousand times over and never quite has. No tyrant has *ever* entrenched here, and the Chaos tax has never once tipped from an abomination you learn to live with into the one that ends the city. The people credit fair luck and their own resilience, and ninety-nine throws in a hundred they are exactly right: the daily churn is genuinely theirs and genuinely chance, which is what keeps Nahaskel itself and not a second Haizava (the people remake the city; the wind does not do it to them). Vesuna's hand rests on the coin at two thresholds and nowhere else. **The floor:** when a throw would let someone entrench for good, seize a quarter, make an office stick, the coin comes up against him, and the safeguard the city calls structural fairness is Her, on purpose. **The ceiling:** when the tax would go lethal, a summons that would not stay bound, a rebuild that would collapse the last coherence the city has, the coin turns against that too. *Luck is a coin; it has two sides; she is both,* and she only ever turns it at the brink.

**The Unthrown.** At the innermost point of Nahaskel is a room whose door never opens onto twice (the way in shifts like all else), but the room itself never changes, the one fixed thing in a city that fixes nothing. A single chamber: a table, a coin atop it, two chairs. One is beautiful, carved, a seat that would shame any mortal throne. The other is plain bare wood, uncomfortable, splinter-ready. The coin between them is the only gamble Vesuna has never finished, never thrown to learn heads or tails. The wager is hers and unspoken, and the city is free for exactly as long as it stays in the air: the goddess of chaos, in the one city she could simply rule, sitting before a throne and a pauper's chair and a coin she has not cast on which of them she will take. The Tellers tend a Sanctum they believe wanders. None of them has ever found this room twice, and only a few suspect it is always there.
