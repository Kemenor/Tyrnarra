# Letters from Biozuri

The Emarrea-specific section of Golivander's periodical ***The Travelling Chronicle***. Sixteen years of running coverage of the Heartcourt and its surrounding kingdom-life, circulated to the literate classes of Lautara, Frae City, and Sumendar. This file holds the canonical text of the present dispatches, quoted in-world on the Heartcourt page (`/setting/talan/domains/lautara/emarrea/heartcourt.html`, Columns 0-10) and on the Emarrea page (`/setting/talan/domains/lautara/emarrea/emarrea.html`, Columns 11 on, the kingdom beyond the court).

Golivander writes in the *Chronicle* register of [`../../voices.md`](../../voices.md) (rewritten in his reworked voice, 2026-10-12 to 10-15): witty, warm, deeply familiar with everyone at court, never spiteful and never blandly neutral. He reports what the kingdom is saying, names what can be named, and leaves unresolved what cannot. Some Hearts cultivate him; some pointedly do not.

The columns are dated by the year (MR). The present year is 2532 MR.

See also: `../../glossary.md` (*Golivander*, *The Travelling Chronicle*, *Letters from Biozuri*); `emarrea.md` (*The Heartcourt → Per-Heart vignettes*) for the canonical events these columns reference.

## Column 0 · Editor's Preface to Letters from Biozuri

*Dear reader,*

*My publisher, who has views on everything and opinions on the rest, asked me sixteen years ago to write a few columns about Emarrea while I was passing through. I am still passing through. I come to Biozuri whenever the road allows, which over sixteen years has been more often than my other subjects would like. In that time I have been fed at the House of a Thousand Flavors more often than is good for a chronicler or his waistcoat, lodged by Hearts who knew perfectly well I would write about them, and corrected, once, by one of them, who read a column before it went to press and suggested, kindly, that I had given a remark to the wrong Heart. I revised it. She was right. She is usually right, which the kingdom finds restful and visiting envoys do not.*

*A word on manners, for readers elsewhere. In most kingdoms, gossip about the court is something done behind the court's back. In Emarrea it is done in front of the court, with the court's help, and frequently at the court's table. The kitsune do not consider gossip beneath the dignity of governance; they consider it the texture of governance, and they would think a ruler nobody talked about had failed at something. The Hearts rule in public, love in public and argue in public, and once in a while, as readers of this column will discover, do rather more than argue in public. My task is to write down what the whole of Biozuri already knows, and to know when to stop.*

*I do know when to stop. I have stopped, in sixteen years, more often than my readers would credit and less often than the Hearts would like. Where a column ends a sentence early, the reader may assume the sentence was finished in Biozuri, by someone else, over tea.*

*What follows are dispatches from a court that wants to be written about. May the kingdom of Emarrea remain as marvellously itself as it has always been, and may the rest of Talan go on under-estimating it at its own expense.*

– *G.*

## Column 1 · On the Heart Who Says Less and Knows More
*(Saemi, Azure)*

*The Azure Heart, Saemi, has been a Heart for twenty-two years and a diplomat for all of them. She spends half the year on the road to Frae City and the trade-courts of Lautara, so the kingdom knows she travels well. What the kingdom under-estimates is how much she listens.*

*I offer the trade-talks at Frae City five years ago as evidence. A Cronus-clergy envoy sat across the table from her for three days of sessions. She let her clerk read the Emarrean terms, poured the envoy's water, nodded at his points and said nothing herself beyond courtesies; once she passed him the salt before he had asked for it, which he later told me was the most unnerving moment of the week. On the fourth morning he broke, addressed her directly and asked the question she had been waiting for, and the answer was already drafted, in her hand, in the folder under her elbow. Emarrea came away with a trade preference it still enjoys. The envoy came away with a great deal of respect and, I believe, a mild fear of condiments.*

*She was raised to inherit the Saiken-Veksir spice fortune, three generations of Lautaran trade. When the Azure Tail was offered, she signed the inheritance over to her younger brother, who still sends her a statement of accounts every quarter. She does not read them. I asked whether this was a principle. She said it was a filing system.*

*She has lately developed a fondness for walking in the Heartplaza gardens at sunrise. She is never alone. I shall not name the Heart who keeps the early hours with her; you all know which Heart it is, and the discretion is the point.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2531 MR*

## Column 2 · On the Heart Whose Generosity Is Also a Wager
*(Houreni, Golden)*

*The Golden Heart, Houreni, gives the best receptions in the kingdom, and knows it. An invitation to one is the most coveted object in Biozuri, narrowly ahead of a seat at the Spectacle and well ahead of this column. Her generosity is real, and it is also a wager.*

*Some autumns ago, three chefs of the Cook's Circle threatened to walk out of the House of a Thousand Flavors over a dispute about where the mushrooms came from. Houreni underwrote three seasons of their specialty ingredients from her own purse, the chefs stayed, and the Nine Tails of Autumn menu is still served every autumn, with her seal-cipher worked into the dessert course. I have eaten it, and searched it for the seal as I went, and found it on the last spoonful.*

*Lately she sent the Rose Heart a lantern. It was made by a Honokage-cult artisan who has, I am reliably told, refused commissions from three Hearts in the past decade because the work would not be sufficiently meant. This one was meant. It took six weeks. The Rose Heart's unpublished verses are woven into the paper so that they emerge only at certain angles as the lantern turns. He received it at a Heartplaza banquet so public that the kingdom now calls it the Lantern Banquet, and he wept, openly. The Golden Heart watched him weep with the air of a woman who has won by gift what others compete for by courtship.*

*The Violet Heart was also present. The Violet Heart smiled.*

*The Ivory Heart, who considers the Golden Heart's style vulgar, was not present, and has mentioned this to me twice.*

*No one in this kingdom does anything by halves, and I find it exhausting and enlivening in equal measure.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR*

## Column 3 · On the Heart Who Loves the Orchards
*(Shionori, Emerald)*

*The Emerald Heart, Shionori, is one hundred and forty-two years old and has eight tails. There are perhaps three living kitsune of whom this is true, and Shionori is the only one I have ever seen forget they have them. They walk the orchards barefoot, speak slowly enough that visitors finish their sentences for them and are wrong, and are, by the agreement of the other six Hearts, the one each of them goes to first when something has gone badly.*

*Twenty years ago, when the last polycule they belonged to lost one of its four to age, Shionori stepped out of it and did not seek another. The two who remain still visit them at the Birch Grove twice a year, and the visits, I am told, are cheerful.*

*They dine every week with the Golden Heart, a pairing the court found surprising for about a fortnight and has found obvious ever since. The Golden Heart asks after the orchards. Shionori asks whom the Golden Heart most wants to impress this season. Both, I gather, find the answers more useful than either expected.*

*I asked them once whether they meant to keep the seat into a ninth tail. They considered the question for long enough that I finished my tea and began to wonder whether I had asked it aloud. Then they said: "I do not think the orchards would forgive me."*

*I have turned that answer over for some years now and am no nearer a reply. I print it as it stands.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2530 MR*

## Column 4 · On the Heart Who Is Still Astonished to Find Himself Here
*(Norikaze, Ivory)*

*The Ivory Heart, Norikaze, was thirty years old when he was elevated, and until that morning had been a junior professor of jurisprudence at Lograth, where Forseti's clergy taught him law as a structure of weighed evidence and ratified precedent. He told the Hearts he was not ready. They elevated him anyway, which is the most Emarrean thing I have seen a government do, and I have seen this one settle a tariff at a banquet.*

*His doctoral thesis argued that Forseti's law could admit the kitsune way of choosing rulers by consent without any violence to doctrine. It is still cited at Lograth. He has not been back to hear it cited.*

*He is a precise jurist. Six months into his seat he heard a household dispute the court expected to be over by lunch: three lovers had held a vineyard together, and one of them had walked out with the year's harvest in a sealed wagon. Norikaze took four hours. He asked about the vines, the contract, the wagon and the seal, and he drafted a ruling the court has cited eleven times since. The lover returned the wagon. I am told the wine was good.*

*I have watched his hairline retreat half an inch in the eighteen months he has worn the white-and-black Tail. He is also, as the kingdom knows, the subject of the most-discussed unfinished sentence in recent memory. I shall not finish it for him. He cannot finish it for himself. Whether anyone will is, at present, the kingdom's favourite open question.*

*Norikaze, my publisher sends his sympathies and his subscription rates.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR*

## Column 5 · On the Heart Who Is Currently Two Hearts
*(Yorume, Violet + Onyx Tail)*

*The Violet Heart, Yorume, has carried the Onyx Tail for nine months, since the Onyx seat fell vacant and the Spectacle of Selection began. She is therefore two Hearts and does the work of both. By every report she sleeps four hours a night, which she denies convincingly, being the finest illusionist in the kingdom.*

*Her tea deserves a sentence of its own. I have been served it on three visits. It is brewed to a strength I cannot reproduce at home, though I have tried, and it is right.*

*By morning she is at the foxfire-masters' atelier with Kasumi, whom she chose at sixteen, when Kasumi's second tail had already come, a decade before most kitsune grow one. She is also somehow at court. She is also somehow at the Lantern Banquet, smiling. She was also, on the afternoon the kingdom will be discussing for a generation, on a balcony above Heartplaza, watching, and has said nothing about it since. She is also, on certain nights I shall not specify, somewhere quieter than any of the above. (I shall not say where. The Heart who keeps that quiet knows already.)*

*I asked her what she teaches Kasumi. She said: "Illusion is mercy. Secrecy is mercy. The trick that ends in laughter is mercy. Choose mercy. The Onyx Heart decides when truth would harm, and when it would not."*

*Yorume, the kingdom would like you to sleep. The kingdom would also like the Spectacle to conclude. The kingdom would, on balance, like the Spectacle to conclude first.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR*

## Column 6 · On the Heart Who Was Always Going to Be Written About
*(Reien, Rose)*

*The Rose Heart, Reien, was twenty-six when he stood on the Spectacle stage and performed a festival cycle of his own composition in seven acts, the Cycle of the First Lantern. The kingdom voted by acclaim before the seventh act ended, so he finished his own audition as a Heart. He wept. The kingdom wept. The Violet Heart lit the air gold with foxfire, and I wept too, which my publisher asked me to leave out and which I have left in.*

*He has been a Heart for two years. In that time he has hosted a festival for nearly every reason the calendar offers and two it does not, been loved by two Hearts I can name and possibly by one I will not, and published, in the last fortnight, a slim octavo titled Of Lanterns and the Lighting of Them, which the literate classes of Lautara are already pretending not to read aloud at parties.*

*Two of the verses, transcribed with his publisher's grumbling permission:*

> *Two lanterns make a path home.*
> *A third lantern is for the friend*
> *who hasn't decided yet whether to come.*
> *Light it anyway. Light it kindly.*

> *You say I am shallow because I dance.*
> *I say you are deep because you do not.*
> *Neither of us has bothered to ask*
> *what the river thinks of either.*

*The Ivory Heart has not, by any account I trust, read the volume. I find this very hard to believe.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR*

## Column 7 · On the Heart Who Holds Every Other Heart's Names
*(Shirae, Sapphire + Crimson Tail)*

*The Sapphire Heart, Shirae, keeps the House of Names, an archive begun four hundred and twelve years ago of every kitsune death the kingdom remembers, each entry annotated with the stories told at the funeral. Some fifty kitsune die in Emarrea on an ordinary day, and their stories come in from the district copy-houses, so the youngest entries are always about five days old. She reads in it every day, which is either the gloomiest habit in Biozuri or the most cheerful, depending on the stories, and she will tell you it is the second.*

*I have read in it. I will not tell you what I read. She once told me, kindly and firmly, that "a chronicler who quotes from the House of Names without leaving an offering at the lantern is a chronicler I do not next welcome". I have left an offering on every visit since, and been welcomed on every visit since, and drawn the obvious conclusion.*

*Since the Crimson seat fell empty she has also carried its Tail, for the plain reason that she is the only Heart who can read the old strategic treatises in the Sapphire archives. She has read all of them. I asked her what the kingdom should build with what she had learned. She said: "A library, Golivander. Build the library. The army can wait for a reason to exist."*

*The court reads her coolness toward the Azure Heart as a feud. It is two people who would both rather be reading.*

*She keeps her evenings to herself, and I keep them out of this column. Shirae, this is the closest the column will come.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR*

## Column 8 · On the Spectacle the Kingdom Is Watching Instead of Working
*(The Onyx Spectacle)*

*The Onyx Spectacle has produced two candidates, two schools of secrecy, and an unprecedented run on the box-stands of Heartplaza, which now sell numbered seats for the trial-days at prices I would call extortionate if I had not paid them. It is drawing more spectators than the last harvest festival.*

*I attended the third trial. I sat in row C. I have an opinion, and I shall keep it, since both candidates are trained in the art of finding out what people think.*

*Kasumi, mentored by the Violet Heart, is twenty-five years old and has two tails, the second grown at sixteen. In three trials she has made the audience laugh, made the audience gasp, made the audience unsure whether the trick had happened yet, and on one occasion made the audience cry. Her school is secrets-as-illusion: the foxfire that misdirects, the kindness that hides what should not be seen. Her foxfire is, to my eye (and I have seen foxfire in seven kingdoms), the cleanest I have witnessed outside her mentor's own.*

*Murasaki, mentored by the Azure Heart, is forty-one years old, has four tails, and spent the past decade learning her trade in the trade-courts of Lautara. In three trials she has said almost nothing, won twice, and lost the third in a manner widely judged afterwards to have been on purpose. Her school is secrets-as-information: the well-placed ear, the quiet record, the network of trusted strangers. She is the candidate the merchants prefer. She is the candidate the rest of the kingdom finds faintly unsettling, which is, I suspect, also a vote in her favour.*

*The seventh and final trial is set before the next Festival of the Fox Lanterns. Place no wagers. The Heartcourt has formally disapproved of public betting on a Spectacle, the kingdom has formally ignored the disapproval, and I am formally not advising anyone.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR*

## Column 9 · On a Single Public Second
*(The Kiss · the long dispatch)*

*Reader, I have written this column for sixteen years. I have reported elevations, retirements, marriages, three deaths, one war-vote and sixteen Festivals of the Fox Lanterns, and in all that time I have never written a dispatch I expected the kingdom to learn by heart. I expect it of this one, and I would like it noted that I tried to make it dull.*

*Three weeks ago, on the eighteenth day of the second month of autumn, at the open afternoon session in Heartplaza, the Ivory Heart and the Rose Heart were arguing about the trial-rules of the Onyx Spectacle, as they argue about something every fortnight. The Ivory Heart held the stricter view and the Rose Heart the freer. Between them they had exhausted every arguable position some months earlier and were arguing now chiefly for the pleasure of it, and the kingdom, which turns out for these sessions the way other kingdoms turn out for a hanging, approved.*

*Voices rose. The crowd thickened, as crowds do. At a moment the chronicle cannot fix precisely (I have spoken with eleven witnesses, who agree on the fact and on nothing else, including where they themselves were standing), the Ivory Heart and the Rose Heart **kissed, in full view of the assembled people of Biozuri,** for one long second. Then they separated, looked at each other for another second, and walked off in opposite directions, which in a square of that size takes some time, and was watched for all of it.*

*Both have kept silent since. Their next public meeting was so formal that a visiting envoy asked whether they had been introduced.*

*I have asked. I have asked carefully, and indirectly, and once, I regret to say, at dinner. The Ivory Heart's answer, given in court on each of the three occasions I raised the matter, has been that "the question is not properly framed", which from a professor of jurisprudence is either a dismissal or an invitation to frame it better, and I have not yet worked out which. The Rose Heart's answer, given at a banquet, was to recite a verse he had not previously published, which I include for completeness:*

> *Some questions are mine.*
> *Some questions are not mine.*
> *The kingdom does not always get to know which is which.*

*The kingdom finds this answer wanting. The kingdom finds it, in fact, more wanting than any answer in the present generation. It has been three weeks.*

*What the kingdom is split on is this: was it a single eruption, never to be repeated, or the first crack in something longer, unspoken, running underneath? Each reading has its partisans, and the argument is held at every tea-house, every Catjomin tasting and every workshop in the potters' quarter. I have heard sixty-year-old kitsune put down their cups to argue it. I have heard the lantern-makers' apprentices argue it. I once watched a Komo-clergy visitor from Eldara stop on the path to Heartplaza and ask a passing kitsune to explain "the entire situation, please, from the beginning, slowly." The kitsune did. It took an hour. The visitor missed her appointment and did not mind.*

*The Violet Heart, who watched the whole of it from a balcony and is known to be the Rose Heart's other public love, has said nothing whatever, and from the most theatrical Heart in the kingdom, nothing whatever has been noticed by everyone.*

*I have my own view. I shall keep it. This is a chronicle, reader, and not a verdict. The verdict is yours.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR (rushed edition, second printing)*

## Column 10 · On an Afternoon at Kawaakari with the Retired Crimson Heart
*(Yorimichi's Catjomin tasting · Kawaakari supplement)*

*Reader, indulge me. I am not the kingdom's tasting-critic; I am its chronicler, and my publisher's note at the head of this supplement reminds me of the difference. I have ignored my publisher, who is paid either way.*

*Yorimichi, formerly the Crimson Heart and presently the most sought-after Catjomin brewer working at Kawaakari, has seven tails and some hundred and forty years, and looks, in afternoon light through the steam of the bath-house pavilion, perhaps sixty. On the seventh day of the eighth month she held a tasting for ten. I had the eighth seat. I was, I will confess, honoured, and I wore the waistcoat.*

*She poured five. I shall not number them in order; she would not approve.*

*The **Shirohana** came first, young and pale and bright as the river below the pavilion: her apprentices' work, poured first because "the palate should remember it is still capable of being surprised by a small thing." I was surprised.*

*The **Hanatsuyu** opened on stone-fruit and closed on cold cedar, and I held my second cup for some minutes, because the closing note did not seem to want to be hurried.*

*The **Togetsu** I will not describe. It was, quite simply, the best sake I have ever drunk. I am told that to be a connoisseur of Catjomin is to learn never to say this aloud. I have said it in print, which is worse. Take it as the chronicle's confession.*

*The **Kōmyōzen**, her usual, brewed in the still that has stood in her brewery for the ten years of her retirement, drank like a memory. I cannot put it more precisely, and I have tried for a week. The other guests put down their cups and said nothing for some time. Yorimichi watched us say nothing and was satisfied.*

*The **Kinkanrou** she opened only because, she said, "the chronicler should know what the noble courts of Lautara fight over before he writes about it." Reader, the noble courts of Lautara are right to fight. I will not describe this either. Some things deserve to stay in the bottle they were brewed for.*

*Between the pours she told us of the Crimson seat. She was offered it in 2487, accepted, and served thirty-five years, and retired on a morning when she woke and could not, for the first time in thirty-five years, see a reason to put the Tail on. She walked it to the next session of the Heartcourt and laid it on the table. Her Growing Circle heir had been seated as the Onyx Heart three years before she laid her Tail down; he died on the dais last year, of a heart-stoppage in his sleep, "a kind death," she said, and she said it in a tone that closed the subject. Of the Crimson seat, empty since, she said: "Let it stay empty until someone the kingdom would follow into a fight stands up of their own accord. The kingdom has yet to produce that kitsune. Until one stands up, the seat does its work by being visible."*

*The pavilion's foxfire-lanterns came on as we finished the fifth pour, and the steam caught the gold. I have written this column three times trying to find a closing that does not over-state, and have failed three times, so I shall only write what I thought as I left:*

*Some afternoons are not, in fact, for the column.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri (Kawaakari supplement), 2532 MR*

## Column 11 · On the Feast That Is Always Brewing Somewhere
*(the circles of importance · a village feast in the orchard country; quoted on the Emarrea page)*

*I had meant to spend the evening writing. I had a room above a tea-house in a village in the orchard country west of Biozuri, a lamp, a column two days late, and no intention whatever of going out, and I record these facts so that the reader understands the scale of what follows.*

*At sunset the tea-house keeper's cousin arrived. I had not been introduced to the cousin, and the cousin did not consider this an obstacle. There was a feast, the cousin explained, at the far end of the village, for the year the village well was dug. The tea-house keeper was going, so the tea-house keeper's guest was going, because a guest under your roof is inside your circle for as long as he sleeps there, and one does not leave one's circle sitting alone with a lamp. I said I had a column to write. The cousin said the column could come too.*

*The kitsune keep their obligations in circles. At the centre sits the family, and the family is more than blood: a sworn sibling counts, a lifelong friend counts, a lover counts, and a call from any of them is never refused. Around the family stand the neighbourhood, the village, the region and the kingdom, and every circle keeps its own festivals. A kitsune owes her neighbourhood's feasts her presence, her village's feasts her presence in most years, and her region's feasts a journey when she can make one; she owes the kingdom's great festivals nothing at all, which is why going to them is a point of pride. The result is a calendar in which, on any night of the year, there is a feast brewing somewhere and a cousin ready to drag you to it. I had heard the saying before. I had not, until that evening, been the one dragged.*

*The feast for the well was the size of a village and the volume of a city. Everyone smiled at me, which a traveller from elsewhere must learn to read correctly: in Emarrea a smile is the ordinary weather of a conversation, and wants nothing from you. Everyone teased me, which is how the kitsune tell you that you have been accepted. I was teased about my feathers, my spectacles, my waistcoat and my column, in roughly ascending order of fondness. Halfway through the evening my cup of plum wine became, in my hand, a cup of foxfire that sang the first line of a drinking song and then became plum wine again. The child responsible was not punished. The table applauded, the child bowed, and after a moment, so did I. A good trick, in Emarrea, ends in laughter for everyone, the person it was played on included; anything less is merely a trick.*

*Two things I saw that evening I have seen nowhere else. At the head table a woman rose before the noodles, said only that her sister had called, and left. Nobody asked why, and nobody needed to: a family call is answered, and the feast closes over the empty seat. And near midnight two young kitsune stood up together and announced that their bond had run its course, and the whole table drank to it, to the season they had had and to the season after. Then they sat down side by side and finished the meal. The kitsune say a bond is freely entered, joyfully shared and lovingly released, and I had taken the third part for poetry until I watched a village raise its cups to it.*

*I wrote nothing that night. The column was three days late. My publisher has asked me, not for the first time, why I cannot simply decline an invitation, and I have explained, not for the first time, that I was not invited. I was collected.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2531 MR*

## Column 12 · On the Gods Who Share a Shelf
*(the household shrine · the Kyūbi-no-Den and the Thirteen; quoted on the Emarrea page)*

*Every kitsune house I have slept in keeps a shelf, and every shelf is crowded. My host in Biozuri last spring, a lantern-maker with three partners, two children and an opinion on everything, kept hers above the kitchen door, and it held, by my count, seven gods, two ancestors, a dried plum and a child's drawing of a fox that may or may not have been devotional.*

*The first incense of the morning goes to Hahane, the Mother-Root, who heads the kitsune's own Nine-Tailed Hall and keeps love, family, festival and the bond of many hearts. It goes to her first in every house in the kingdom, without an exception I have found, and when I asked why, my host looked at me as one looks at a man who has asked why the door is opened before one walks through it.*

*After Hahane the order is a matter of the household's taste and the day's business. Honokage, the Soft-Shadow, god of foxfire and kind misdirection, gets a lantern rather than incense, lanterns being what he likes. Yumegatari, the Dream-Teller, keeper of story and memory and the wish-lanterns, is thanked whenever a story is told well at the table, which in a kitsune house is most evenings. Then come the Thirteen, as many as the house finds useful or beloved. Jianna sits on the shelf of anyone in trade. Forseti comes down from the back of the shelf for a contract and goes back up afterwards. Iro, Sarrum and Vesuna each have their devout corners of the kingdom, and Bikiargi's small shrine comes out for the lunar festivals.*

*And there is Fisaya, who is on every shelf, generally at the front. She is the most worshipped of the Thirteen in Emarrea by a wide margin: the goddess of change, among the only people within the Thirteen's reach who change their own shape, and of the wind, among a people whose caravans and letters go everywhere. On my host's shelf she is a kitsune, multicoloured, and plainly enjoying it. On every shelf on Haizetsua she is a Tengu. I am told by those who would know that the kitsune have the likeness right, which leaves my people with some explaining to do, and I have decided not to be the one who does it.*

*The kitsune find it faintly puzzling that anyone keeps only one god, much as they find it faintly puzzling that anyone keeps only one partner. Both, they allow, work for some people; neither is the rule. The Heartcourt has nine pillars and none of them is Faith, which the kingdom regards as obvious and visiting clergy regard as an oversight. The temples are run by neighbourhoods and the shrines by households, and the gods appear to manage.*

*The Nine-Tailed Hall, despite its name, holds three. The other six seats, the kitsune say, are open: kept for ancestral spirits, or for the small gods of particular places, or simply waiting until the world's stories grow long enough to fill them. Theologians argue over whether the empty seats are real or pious poetry. I put the question to my host. She said the seats would be filled when they were filled, that it would be rude to rush them, and that breakfast was ready, in that order of importance.*

*I leave incense, as my readers know, at whatever temple stands nearest the road, a habit of seventy years that I cannot account for theologically. My host watched me light a stick for Hahane before my own breakfast, and said I would make a perfectly good kitsune, if not for the feathers.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2532 MR*

## Column 13 · On the Night of the Fox Lanterns
*(the Festival of the Fox Lanterns at Kawaakari · the Storyweaver · a petition on the riverbank; quoted on the Emarrea page)*

*The Festival of the Fox Lanterns is kept wherever in Emarrea there is water to float a lantern on, which is most of it. Every river town lights its own, Biozuri lights its own, and I have seen a village with nothing but a well float a single lantern in a bucket and call it a festival, correctly. The one everybody goes to, if they can, is Kawaakari's. Kawaakari is a village of hot springs, plum terraces and one river, the Hoshigawa, which carries enough crystal dust down from the mountain to sparkle at noon, and on that one night carries a good share of the kingdom's wishes and most of its Hearts. I have attended it more times than I have managed to describe it, and my publisher has the drafts.*

*The week before, as tradition and my stomach both require, I dined at the House of a Thousand Flavors in Biozuri, whose lantern-season menu is called Dreams Beneath the Lantern Sea. Between the courses Elhara, the Storyweaver of the Cook's Circle, came to each table and told a story, a different one at every table. It is understood, by a courtesy older than the House, that when the Storyweaver speaks, Yumegatari is listening, and possibly speaking too. Mine was about a lantern that would not float. I will not tell it. Readers who would like to hear it are welcome to have been there.*

*At Kawaakari the whole village goes down to the river at dusk, and so do the thousands who came for it, and every one of them carries a paper lantern. On it they write a wish, or a name, or a story that ought to be kept. The lanterns are lit with foxfire, which throws no heat and so burns neither the paper nor the hand that holds it, set on the water, and let go. They are Yumegatari's offering, and the kitsune say that what is written on a lantern is told to her as it passes. Within the hour the Hoshigawa is a road of light from the bathhouse steps to the bend below the terraces, the crystal dust lit gold from above, and both banks stand in a silence no kitsune can otherwise keep for more than a minute.*

*As at every great festival in Emarrea, any citizen may bring a matter straight to a Heart, and since the Hearts come to Kawaakari for the lanterns, the matters come to the riverbank. This year the Emerald Heart sat barefoot on a stool by the water and took petitions until the lanterns were lit. I watched an old brewer from the plum terraces explain, at length and with gestures, that the footbridge to the upper terrace had washed out in the spring and that the village council had been discussing it ever since. Shionori listened to every word. Then they asked how many planks the bridge had wanted. The brewer did not know. Shionori said that was the first thing to find out, and that they would walk up and count them in the morning; and in the morning, I am told, they did.*

*I wrote my wish on my lantern, as I do every year. I will not tell you what it was, which is the one rule of the festival every kitsune keeps, and I watched it go down the river with all the others until I could no longer tell it from the rest.*

– *G., The Travelling Chronicle, Letters from Biozuri, 2531 MR*

## About the chronicler (canon summary)

Quick-reference for readers of these columns who want the chronicler's shape without leaving the file. Full sheet in [`../../glossary.md`](../../glossary.md); the voice-and-registers canon (the four rings, the dial, the tea rule, Vyrenna) in [`../../voices.md`](../../voices.md).

- **Golivander Tessek.** Tengu, ~110, born on Haizetsua (the western tengu island of Vindul); white-brow mature feathering, spectacled, ink-stained claws, a worn travelling satchel.
- **Trained at Thekkavar** (City of Learning, Ezkudon) in natural philosophy and rhetoric, apprentice-and-leave path; modest Occult training picked up alongside. Cites Jeanne Pintos as model.
- **Based at The Lantern Press** in Frae City (Halvar Erikson, publisher; Maren Erikson, his daughter, reads the drafts). Rarely home; three-quarters of the year on the road.
- **Loves to travel and to experience the world.** Intensely curious, tries everything once. The Catjomin tasting at Kawaakari, the Foxfire Stone Massage, the row-C seat at the Onyx Spectacle's third trial: same impulse.
- **Naturalistic and unbound to any church.** Treats the Compact as physics. Drops incense at any temple on the road as travel-habit.
- **Method.** Never prints uncorroborated gossip; protects sources by deliberate elision (the *I shall not name* signature); revises when corrected in print (Saemi's correction is the precedent); refuses payment from subjects.
- **Voice.** Witty and true, warm underneath: the *Guide* the world with him at the edge, the *Chronicle* him in the frame (see `voices.md`). The tea is the one thing he will not joke about.
- **Two products.** ***A Traveller's Guide to Tyrnarra***, the multi-volume reference work, published in parts and bound by region; ***The Travelling Chronicle***, the periodical of his experiences. *Letters from Biozuri* is the Emarrea section of the latter.
