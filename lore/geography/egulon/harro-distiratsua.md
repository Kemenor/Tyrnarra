# Harro Distiratsua (Egulon)

**Position:** Western Egulon. Lautara (Atarialda) across the western lake, the Crossroads tri-point at the northwest corner, Zuzental across the northern range, the Emerald Isles' Southern Isle across the water to the northeast, Argia Esfera east across Vonura, Lua Lasai to the south. Ljosarn stands on the eastern lake-line and belongs to no one. See [`../egulon.md`](../egulon.md) for the domain frame.

**Polity:** The Kingdom of Harro Distiratsua, a Reconstruction-era crown carrying the old geographic name. Royal seat: Ongetorra. The crown rules with the sworn Lamphold houses; the Bright Ring holds its Ring Liberties within the kingdom.

**Peoples:** Sprite-dominant, with the domain's Leshy and Shoony living throughout and the corridor's usual mix in the mainline cities. The craft belongs to the place rather than to any one people.

**Etymology:** Basque *harro* (proud) + *distiratsua* (the radiant). Spelling corrected to the map's reading, 2026-08-13. The glossary carries the full derivation, with Ongetorra, Sidijos, dirdai, and the rest of this build's coinages.

### The fit

Anyone on Talan can sell you light. The lantern-craft, the enchanted glass, the magitech glow-work: much of it is made elsewhere, and a Harro shop will cheerfully sell you a Sumendar-forged housing. The fitting is what you came for. When the light has to be right, the blaze that makes a ballroom, the amber hush of a vinoteque, the night-light a merchant buys his frightened daughter, you buy from a Harro shop, and the price of the fitting commonly exceeds the price of the lamp. The craft's proverb is old enough that the guild-halls argue over which city coined it: *nobody noticed the light, only the evening.* A fitter takes a commission by sitting in the room at dusk, sometimes for the whole evening, sometimes for three, and asking what the room is for.

### Each city carries its light

The trunk line crosses the kingdom west to east, Ongetorra to the lakeshore, and the mainline cities wear their light as their name and their argument. One keeps itself festival-white the whole year and sleeps in shifts. One burns low and amber and closes its shutters outward, so the streets hold the color and the houses keep their own. One lights its streets from below, the paving warm underfoot, and strings no lamps at all above shoulder height. A signature is the city's public register, and inside every city the registers bite: the festival-white core runs three streets deep before the dusk quarters begin, the border between blaze and half-light is argued lamp by lamp at the ward-meetings, and on high nights the amber city's core goes festival-hue while the Sprite wards shutter against it. The last time a mainline city changed its whole register, the debate ran eleven years, two guild-halls stopped speaking, and the change carried by the width of a single ward.

Off the trunk hang the gem-towns, each a branch-line or a cart-road from the main run, and each keeping one thing brought to perfection: a bread, a spring-bath, a cheese-cellar, a view. A gem-town introduces itself by its one thing, the junction platform-boards list temptations beside destinations, and the towns watch the boards the way farmers watch weather.

The evening trunk-run is timed to cross the kingdom at lamplighting, each city's signature coming up in sequence outside the window, and ends at the shore where the horizon carries the god-city's glow; the crews call the run riding the dusk, and the window seats on the northern side sell out first.

The Line numbers its stops outward from Ongetorra by the rail. The crown's tax-rolls number the same towns by the old road, and the two counts part ways where the rail cuts straight through miles the road once looped. Each town wrote itself down under whichever number served it at the time, so the kingdom has two Fifth Stops; freight consigned by number arrives at the wrong one a few times a season, gets carted the difference at the shipper's cost, and both towns keep the five.

### Dusk

For themselves, the Sprites keep dusk, and the reason is optics before it is custom. A Sprite's feelings surface as light. In full brightness that light washes out and the Sprite with it: a Sprite in a festival-white street is masked, anonymous, one silhouette among the rest. In true darkness the same light shows everything, every flicker of temper and want, readable across a square. Dusk sits between. The half-light is tuned so that small feelings stay under the ambient warmth and only what moves a person strongly rises above it; what a street learns about you at dusk is what mattered.

The natives read it. A household dims the lamps before its door when someone dies, and the street learns the grief without a word crossing the threshold. A house lit hard and white is a house keeping something its own, and the neighbors ask nothing. Children learn the courtesy before they learn the stove: you do not carry a blazing lantern into a dusk street, because it blanks every heart it passes. Visitors do, and are forgiven once.

At the half-light the gloamsingers start, small dun animals of the hedgerows and parks, singing at that hour and at no other. The song is quiet and falling, with none of a bird's brightness, and travelers write with some consistency that it made them homesick for a place that was not their home. Harro folk stop noticing it by their tenth year and miss it badly, physically, anywhere else.

The streets hold the half-light all night, so the only true darkness in the kingdom lies above the terraces. On summer nights the young climb to the high meadows to go loud: everything they carry, burned openly at each other in the one dark that shows all of it, for hours, among the few they brought up the mountain. They come home before dawn with their light spent. Magistrates fine it as a fire-risk when they must, and the fines are small.

### The dirdai

Every wine-land of Talan keeps its own kind the way other lands keep a red or a white, and this one keeps the dirdai: luminescent only in motion. In the bottle it sleeps dark. The pour wakes it, it glows on the swirl, glimmers on the sip, and goes dark in a still glass, so a table drinking it flickers all evening with the rhythm of its own conversation. The lowland terraces grow it and every Lamphold town presses some; festival stalls sell stirlight by the cup, young and rough. The vintages come down from the mountains. The Bright Ring's cellars, cut into rock where the temperature holds, raise the tier the trade prices in gold, the prestige casks go east for the lake to finish, and the Ring releases its vintages when it pleases.

### The Bright Ring

The mountain arcs that circle the kingdom hold the old Sprite courts, scattered through the high forests from the northern range around to the southern, and older than the crown, the houses, and the roads. A court is a seasonal gathering that stopped being seasonal: seat-halls in the high woods where the deep craft is kept, the glamour-work the lowland shops sell simplified versions of, the registers of light the lowlands have no names for, and the dirdai cellars. The Ring entered the kingdom by contract, and the contract is renewed at every accession: within its bounds the courts stand free of toll, levy, and Lamphold law. The crown keeps the bargain because the Ring is too strong to suppress and too weak to secede, and both parties have held both facts for three centuries. The courts come down for the great festivals; the lowlands go up for them; a fitter who studies a season in a Ring hall hangs the fact over her shop door for the rest of her working life.

### The crown of the ways

The kingdom was made in the dark. Through the Dark Era, walking to the light meant walking through this country, and a beacon is useless without a road that reaches it. The houses that fortified the waystations kept the wayfires lit through the monster centuries: fed the refugee columns, armed the escorts, oiled the lamps, hosted the Ordained's riders carrying the beacon outward. They came out of the dark owning the roads, and the Reconstruction made it formal. These are the Lamphold houses, a dozen-odd landed lines, each named for its keep, each holding its hereditary stretch of road and rail with the towns on it. First among them stands House Ongetorra, which held the western gate through the worst of the flood; its lake-castle takes the traveler's toll and gives the traveler's welcome in the same courtyard, and is now the royal seat.

The crown holds the trunk road, the customs, and the treaty-pen. The houses hold their stretches as sworn vassals, with their own tolls, courts, and lamps. The towns hold charters; the Ring holds its Liberties. The exchequer is way-tolls spent on ways, published yearly, and read closely. A foreign envoy is received at Ongetorra and a treaty is signed by the crown alone; a new customs-shed is built by the house whose stretch needs it and co-funded from the common toll-share by a formula so old that arguing it is a hobby. The college of house-heads deadlocks freely on precedence, on toll-shares, on the marriages of heirs. It has never deadlocked on a bridge.

### The Lamp Ride

Succession is a journey. The new monarch lights a lamp at Ongetorra's hearth and carries it east along the old refugee road, waystation by waystation, keep by keep. Each house renews its oath as the lamp crosses its stretch, and each shelters the flame through its own miles; a house that let it gutter on its ground would carry that for a generation. At the mountain turnings the crown speaks the Ring Liberties upward into the arcs, and the courts answer with light on the ridgelines. The ride ends at Sidijos, the lone keep above the eastern shore, with the god-city burning on the horizon across the water, and there homage is owed at last, to a monarch whose lamp arrived lit.

### The Everbright Line

The chartered company that runs the trunk rail took its name uninvited from the god-city's byname. It moves more of the corridor in a season than the house roads move in a year; an express that stops makes a town and an express that ceases to stop unmakes one; Line money has already bought out two tired houses' debts, and the HRA's delegates ride every carriage of the argument.

### Faith

The kingdom reads Iro as the warmth that fits, keeps the festival calendar with the lakeshore rites, and sends its healers east. Against the vigil-country's kept flame and Ljosarn's kept promise it holds the third corner of the domain's argument: light as a craft of measure, poured to the drinker. (How Argia Esfera answers is left open here; the vigil-country is unbuilt, and its stance lands with its own build. The domain file's existing line, that a Sprite there learns to dim or to dazzle very carefully, is the only committed canon on the seam.)

### Live tensions

The Line against the houses: timetable against toll-right, new speed against old stone, with a crown whose treaty-pen weighs less each year and a charter that comes due for renewal within the decade. The Ring's standing question, what the mountains are owed for what the lowlands sell, raised at every Lamp Ride and left standing at every Lamp Ride. And the branch-lines: each one the Line prunes moves some gem-town's one thing a cart-road further from the world, and the pruning schedule is the Line's alone.

### ◈ Popular Belief: the honest glass

The folk hold that dirdai tells: brighter in an honest hand, sullen in a liar's glass, and a proposal made over a bottle that stayed dark should be reconsidered in the morning. Merchants swear by it, mothers-in-law rely on it, and every barkeep in the kingdom has watched a negotiation die because one glass sat dark while its holder talked too long. The Ring's masters keep one standing answer to the question of whether it is true: *the wine shows movement, and an honest heart moves.*

### ⚿ GM Secret: what the dusk remembers

The Harro dusk is a copy. The Material Sprites are Talan-born past any memory, and the register their country is tuned to, the exact half-light their streets keep and their hearts rest in, is the standing dusk of the Feyworld: the light of the plane their line left, preserved in temperament long after the memory of the place was lost. The melancholy travelers hear in the gloamsingers is inherited, and the animals keep the hour because their stock crossed when the Sprites crossed. In the Bright Ring's oldest halls hang pieces of fitting-work the masters maintain and can no longer account for, lighting built to answer rooms that exist on no plane a mortal of Talan has stood in. No mortal scholarship connects the register to the plane.
