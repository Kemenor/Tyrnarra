## The flight

Dear Madam Parisa,

You have sent me quite on a journey with your request for gozai and zerai.

As always I had an early start into the day, but much to my dismay none of the airship ports had a passage to Sombral in the morn. The earliest they leave is at noon. A kind man at the counter explained it to me: leaving in the morning means flying over Argia Esfera during noon, which can end deadly, and only the noonships, the Occultech ones, are equipped to handle the heat.

So I booked passage on the earliest ship and sat down in a café and enjoyed a little rare rest.

The views of Lautara made me smile, same as every time. Seeing the holy mountain. We cut through Azkataria, south to Jakinduria. We saw Thekkavar in the distance, tucked behind its mountains the way a scholar holds a good book to the chest. Then east through Lua Lasai's north, a quick pass through Harro, and by Ljosarn with its holy flame to the south-east.

The barman, nice chap, lifted my confusion why we weave through the mountains and do not take the southern way. The heat was the answer once more.

Though I find not a lot to complain about, the views of the lands below were quite unlike any I have seen before.

We held a little north, over the Green, as it is colloquially known I was informed, to the river where the grapes for the gozai are grown.

The river is a beautiful spectacle shimmering with the light of the sun, terraced almost all the way, the vines growing in many colours: one side a green bank with colours of blue, green, and teal, the other side almost like desert, its grapes of red, orange, and brown. All made a little hazy by the still lingering heat.

In the distance the burning mountain was already visible, the fire on top coloured as well, flickering into violet, then blue, and back to violet. Its foot is ringed by a forest in a golden hue, and above it the vines carry the same gold, shimmering in the light with emerald and diamond, colours neither bank produces.

An old Argian told me of the fire-reading the priests turn into weather forecasts, and told me to check the reading for the coming week after landing, or I might be surprised and need to seek shelter with a family or two.

## Sombral

We came down at Sombral with the sun just gone and the heat still standing in the streets like a guest who has not noticed the hour. The mast is on the city's highest roof, and the first thing on it, before customs or porters, is the reading-board: a violet chalk mark and beneath it, in a priest's square hand, *hard sun, seven days; keep in at noon.* Which in my trade, Madam, means buy early, and I have.

The city is white, and it is built for shade the way Merkavar is built for bargaining. Every street runs under arcades or awnings or vines trained overhead, the windows are narrow and deep, and the shutters are painted one blue I have seen nowhere else, save on the houses that keep a cask. Those are painted a deep amber, the colour of old gozai, and an Argian reads a street by them the way we read the signs over our stalls: behind every amber shutter a family cask is lying down. At this hour everything is open and everyone is out; the markets set up after dark, children run the arcades, and a family eating supper on its own doorstep asked me to sit. The people are mostly the dog-folk, warm and direct, and not quick to let a stranger walk off hungry.

The lodges stand along the river where the two waters meet, cut back into the bank so the casks lie cooler than the families above them, and barges come down all night stacked with barrels from both banks. Beyond the last lodge the town simply stops and the dunes begin, orange even by lamplight, and at their edge a pair of knights in pale armour, water-skins at their belts, were doing nothing I could name except watching the sand.

Tomorrow I taste gozai from both banks at the Posovelho lodge, before the heat. The zerai I may taste, I am told, and may not yet buy; that right is earned here, and I intend to learn what the earning costs.

Yours in haste and in the shade,
Rustam
