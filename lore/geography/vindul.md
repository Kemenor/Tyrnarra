# Vindul: Wind · Fisaya

**Borders:** Brauogi, Ehizahar

**Seas:** Hafra (Fellibylur west coast; Haizetsua's coasts).

**Etymology:** Icelandic *vindur* (wind) → shortened and softened → *Vindul*

**Position:** Northwest Talan

**Terrain:** Arctic/icy north coast, mountain range (east), ancient old-growth forest, open grass steppes (south), rivers and lakes. Massive island to the west (Haizetsua).

**God's city:** Haizava, The Shifting City

**Peoples:** Four dominant ancestries are anchored here: **Tengu** (Haizetsua), **Strix** (Fellibylur), **Kashrishi** (the eastern range), and **Jotunborn** (Baerfrost). The domain-level read is a feeling of each people, never a practice; the cultures live with the sub-regions below, and the four feelings are four ways of meeting the same sky.

- **The Tengu answer the wind.** Voice as temperament: expressive, social, song where another people keeps silence, shaped by leaving and shaped again by coming home. The wind speaks, and a Tengu speaks back.
- **The Strix ride it.** Weather in the blood: restless in still air, most alive when the sky is moving, reading a front the way other folk read a face. Flight is the disposition, whatever the trade.
- **The Kashrishi watch it.** The observer's stillness inside the moving air: quiet, precise, inward where others are loud. What a Kashrishi believes is their own; what a Kashrishi has measured is everyone's.
- **The Jotunborn stand in it.** The storm arrives and they remain: stoic, slow, weather met as a fact to be respected and outlasted, anger as rare and as total as a winter front.

**Sub-regions:**
- **Baerfrost**: the fully arctic sub-region of Vindul, the northern coast where the ice never fully retreats. Home to the largest stable Jotunborn population on Talan and, since the Dark Era, to one of the continent's most distinctive small polities: the **Hunt-League**. During the breach centuries the arctic clans survived because they could kill what came for them, and the ones who could not died. By the close of the Dark Era the survivors had condensed their politics into a two-tier ritual institution. Each clan picks its own chieftain by its own meritocratic test (most weigh proven kills; some weigh seasons-led, hard-winters-survived, or feasts-hosted; each clan's reckoning is its own affair), and once a year at midwinter the chieftains travel to a fixed cold-hall and meet in council, voting among themselves for the year's **Hammer-Lord**, the Jotunborn whose Merit over the past year stands tallest. Authority is total within the year and ends at the next midwinter without inheritance. The kills that have won the Hammer are remembered in skald-record: corrupted-spawn stragglers in the early centuries, an ice-kraken whose teeth still hang at the hall's beam, a deep-water serpent in 2487 MR. The institution was Dark-Era survival made permanent and perfect for its era; the era is now over. Corrupted-spawn stragglers have dwindled, the deep beasts are rarer each decade, and trade flows up from Skarvorn-allied coastal traders in the south. Some border clans have begun building permanent winter-halls rather than camping, and the families who hold those halls have begun to look very much like jarldoms in everything but name. The Gathering still meets and the Hammer still passes, but the question of *what Merit is for, when the dark is gone* is the loudest argument the chieftains have ever held. A meritocracy of killing is becoming, under its own feet, a meritocracy of diplomacy: a recent Hammer-Lord won on a successful trade-treaty with the **Air Monastery**'s eastern range rather than a kill, and half the chieftains read this as a betrayal of the institution while the other half read it as the institution finally working. The clans' signature drink is **Isbran**, the freeze-distilled "ice-fire" spirit of the deep winter (see [`../glossary.md`](../glossary.md)), drunk at the Gathering feast and poured as the one libation into the Cold-Hall brazier-pit.
  - **The thing under the ice (the sealed dungeon).** In **2524 MR**, in the continent-wide eruption wave that opened the Nine Dungeons (the Hollow of Ten Thousand Threads among them), a dungeon broke open beneath the north pack-ice: a drowned trench-cavern holding one of the Nine Generals of Corruption, the **Maw Serpent** (see [`../factions.md`](../factions.md), *Remnants of Corruption → The Nine Generals*). The clans fought it through a four-year first-wave campaign (2524–2528) under kill-Hammer-Lords and **sealed** it, with no help from the south; but they could not clear it. It sits under the ice still, the General bound-not-slain behind a clan-set seal, with spawn leaking through under-ice tunnels the seal does not reach. The Cold-Hall beam carries its first *sealed, not slain* entry, and that wound is what the institution's present argument grows from. The peacetime *what is Merit for* question got an answer no one wanted, and a hard one: there is a General to kill again, but it cannot be killed the old way. **Two readings split the chieftains.** One holds that the clans sealed the thing alone, the way they outlived the Dark Era alone, and that the road to clearing it is to return to the true roots, harden, and raise Hammer-Lords who can finish what they sealed. The other holds that sealing was not winning, that a Nine General is past any one Hammer's reach, and that the thing is cleared only with the Air Monastery treaty, the Adventurers' Guild, and allies who bring what the tundra cannot. The seal holds, but seals weaken; the next years decide whether the Hunt-League finishes the dungeon before the dungeon finishes the seal, and whether it does so alone or not at all.
- **Air Monastery**: a fortified enclave in Vindul's eastern mountain range, autonomous territory, and the mother-house of **the Wyndwalken**, the order whose cartography sets the gold standard of Talanese map-making. The order's deepest roots are pre-Imperial: an astronomer enclave founded by Kashrishi observers who climbed the eastern peaks in the late Lost Era to read the night sky from the clearest altitude on the continent. The order's secular-contemplative cast was set in that founding. The Golden Empire conquered them in its seventh-century-MR expansion and gave them in return the imperial brass: precision instruments, mechanical clockwork, geometers' standard-rules, and (the gift that would shape every later century of their work) the technique of mapping what one observes against a calibrated grid. They became the Empire's high-altitude observatories and the Empire's mountain surveyors in the same generation. When the breach centuries came and the Empire fell, the order withdrew behind its mountain walls and held; the fortress thickened, the gates closed, the brass instruments folded inside, and the surrounding refugees who climbed the mountain were taken in. Eight centuries of defensive monasticism turned the astronomer-surveyors into a contemplative order with an astronomer-surveyor's discipline still at its centre, and the fire-watch atop the highest tower has been lit every night since the breach centuries as both a navigation point for travellers and a signal that civilization persists here. When the Adventurer Era opened the world back up, the Wyndwalken left their gates. They are a mendicant order now: most monks spend most of their working lives on the road, walking the ranges and coasts and forests of Talan with rule, compass, and field-book, returning to the mother-house only for retreat, archival, training, and the long winter when the eastern passes close. Their maps are the standard reference at every court that can afford a copy; the most prized are said to show *the way a place wants to be found*, an Occult-touched cartography that has never been satisfactorily explained outside the order. The Wyndwalken take their vows on observation and craft rather than on a god's name, and the Kashrishi philosophical cast has held since the founding: theology kept private, civic work made public, the order itself the public-civic crown of that inclination. Most Wyndwalken are Kashrishi, and the Air Monastery is the closest thing the Kashrishi ancestry has to a home-institution on Talan; the remainder are mostly other-Vindul (Tengu, Jotunborn, the latter bringing Baerfrost's earned-standing instinct to the order's earn-it-by-walking map-craft), with a steady minority of aspirants from outside the domain drawn by the order's reputation. Though the order swears on craft rather than gods, many Wyndwalken and most travellers who pass the gates privately keep **Uthra**, the belief-formed god of wanderlust and the open road (see [`../gods.md`](../gods.md), *Named Non-Bound Gods → Uthra*), who is templed nowhere because you honour the god of going by going.
- **Fellibylur**: the storm-wracked sub-region between Baerfrost in the north (separated by the river that runs east through the lands of Villtur), Azkamour to the southwest, and Brauogi to the south and east, with the **Hafra coast** to the west. Open grass-steppe with scattered foothills and one notable interior forest, river-cut, storm-weather year-round. The name *Fellibylur* (Icelandic *fell* "lone mountain" + *bylur* "blizzard") originally described a literal storm-peak in the deep-old usage; the peak-reading faded over centuries and the name persisted as a description of the *region's character*, where storms gather and roll across open lands. Strix are the kingdom's anchor ancestry, common across both halves and dominant in the interior; the coast mixes them with Tengu sailors and Kashrishi merchant-families. The kingdom is governed under **the Stormpact**: a chartered Council of two seats. **The Skybell Republic** holds the coast and the Hafra ports, named for the consecrated bronze storm-warning bells rung from each port-tower when storms are inbound; it registers chartered merchant-houses, certifies **Stormriders** (the kingdom's signature export, foul-weather Hafra pilots hired across Talan for open-Hafra crossings nothing born outside Fellibylur can read fast enough), maintains the bell-network, and keeps the continent's open-Hafra sea-charts at Brasswatch in its port-archive, **the Saltkeep**, the master record every Stormrider's field-book feeds and draws from (the Wyndwalken map the land; the Stormriders chart the open Hafra). **The Thunderhost** holds the steppe-interior and the clan-circuits, a moot of Strix clan-chieftains who meet at midsummer at a fixed inland gathering to deliberate matters of grazing, horse-breeding, weather-lore, and the joint Stormpact business. The two seats vote together on questions that touch both halves of the kingdom (trade-charters with Baerfrost's Hunt-League, Wyndwalken cartographer commissions for the coast, disputes that cross the coast-interior line) and rule their own halves between sessions. The Stormpact itself was sworn in the early Adventurer Era when the post-Dark-Era trade re-opening brought Baerfrost caravans south and southern merchants north through the same Fellibylur passes for the first time in centuries; the two halves of the region had to agree on a shared law or be ruined by competing for the same traffic, and the Stormpact is the agreement that held. Fisaya is the kingdom's unifying faith: storms are read as the goddess speaking, and reading her is both the Skybell Republic's commercial practice (the bell-network is Fisaya-consecrated infrastructure) and the Thunderhost's spiritual practice (the chieftains take their seats on the strength of demonstrated storm-augury). The most prestigious Stormriders train for years at both sides: a Wyndwalken-quality field-book in one hand and a Thunderhost-trained ear for thunder in the other.
- **Haizetsua**: the great western island of Vindul, Fisaya's domain, home of the Tengu. Mountain-range interior, coastal lowlands to the south and east, sheltered harbour at the capital **Vindboorg**. Governed by the **Skarvorn Republic** (the seated body of the eleven Tengu clans, no single ruler). Famous **singing cities**: dwellings carved so wind through tuned cavities produces sustained tones. Signature export: the **Three Teas** (Skardun · Vintek · Korrek). The **Vornsketta** is the Tengu migration custom that sends every young Tengu off-island for years. Three Pines (Myrkono's outer-island cluster) sits off Haizetsua's southern coast. (*haize* = wind in Basque; the name echoes the domain.) **Full canon: [`vindul/haizetsua.md`](vindul/haizetsua.md)** (Republic, eleven clans, Vornsketta, singing cities, stormsinging, eight winds, Fisaya devotion, lifespan).
- **Haldmark** *(formerly Azkamour)*: the north-western march of **Brauogi**, the **Kholo** keeping-heartland, fronting the Vindul/Fellibylur frontier. Its men's protective order, **the Vaka**, is Baerfrost's Hunt-League read in the mirror: they earn their say by what they keep safe, not by what they kill. The old name Azkamour survives as its principal border trade-town. Full canon: [Brauogi → Haldmark](brauogi/haldmark.md).

#### Haizava, the Shifting City (god's city-state)

Haizava is Fisaya's seat and the one city on Talan that will not hold still: the city the wind rebuilds. Its streets, spans, and whole districts ride sails, vanes, counterweights, and rails, and when the weather turns the wind drives them into a new configuration, so the city is never the same shape twice. You do not learn Haizava, you learn to *read* it, and you keep a wind-reader where another city keeps a map; it is the one place the Wyndwalken's cartographers cannot fix to paper, which leaves Vindul holding both the masters of mapping and the city that defeats them. **Fisaya knows every wind.** She does not move the city by hand; she knows where every breath of air will go, and that is the craft: the engineering tells how the city answers a wind, the augury tells what the wind will do, and a forecast is both worn by one person. **The wind seats the government.** Haizava has no standing ruler. Authority belongs to whichever district the wind carries adjacent to **the Eye**, the one wind-still point at the city's heart, the calm at a storm's core and Fisaya's seat; that district is **the Windward**, and it governs from the Eye until the wind turns and seats another, a rotation called **the Turning** (*the Windward holds till the wind turns*). Each district fields its own **wind-readers** and **trim-wrights** to forecast its path and nudge it centerward, so the contest for the Eye is one of reading and trimming, the wind holding the last say. Fisaya's clergy, **the Windwatchers**, stand sworn out of it: they keep the Eye, read the whole sky, call the Turning, and pilot the gondolas that thread every district alike. The principle that holds it together is *you may ride the wind to power, you may not chain it*: a district that grasps to hold the Eye is fighting the wind rather than riding it, and it loses the wind, storms turning against the grasping ward until it is cast to the rim. **Daily life, when your address depends on the weather.** The day begins with the reading, every district posting its dawn forecast, and *"if the wind holds north"* prefaces every plan; when a reconfiguration is coming the Windwatchers sound the **shift-bell** and the city clears its moving spans, battens down, and rearranges over the next stretch. Homes are built to move and nothing is left loose, so Haizavans travel light and hold things loosely and find a street that never changes faintly sad. Most of the time they simply walk, since the forecast tells them where to be; for a fixed destination across a shift they ride a **windrift** (formally a *Haizola*), the clergy-piloted gondola that drifts the **wind canals** above the reshuffling streets; but the everyday craft is the **svif**, a personal wind-board half surf and half skate that takes a license almost every local holds and no traveller does, so a licensed rider in the air and a visitor stuck on stale directions are sorted at a glance. The license is also a Haizavan's coming-of-age: a child rides pillion behind a parent or is carried by windrift until the day the city trusts them to ride the shifting streets alone, which is the day it trusts them to read the wind, for in a city that rearranges under you a misread is a fall. The test is faced once, a first solo ride through a Turning, the young rider taking the canals during a reconfiguration and reading the sky rather than any map; the everyday trick-crews are a calmer joy thrown in the held air between Turnings, never into a shift, the newly-licensed showing the wind what they can do. Svifs carry almost nothing, so the week's groceries on a day the market has drifted three districts off mean a long walk or a paid windrift, and everyone has a story about the larder ending an hour from the kitchen. Heavy freight moves by **cargo airship**, which Haizava builds, wind-craft hulls flown by wind-reader pilots, a thing apart from the Order of Steam's steam ships. Every home keeps a roof-peak vane that is also its Fisaya-shrine, read each morning for the household's own small wind; a still vane is a quiet dread. **Faith: the wind that carries into every home.** Fisaya is no distant goddess here. She is present as herself, the nine-tailed kitsune of many colours with hair trailing into cloud, and she rides openly: a crew of youths throwing svif-tricks down a canal may look over to find the goddess at their shoulder, nine colours and all, match their best line and better it, and ride on into a gust with a backward grin, and no one takes her for anyone else. Worship is reading the wind; her **Breath** is the augury, her **Travel** blesses every setting-out, and her **Storms** and **Ruin** are the great city-reshaping tempests, theophany and danger together, the one time the Turning halts and the whole city answers as one. Her great holiday is **the Fairwind**, days of svif-races and trick-shows down the canals where the champions earn the right to challenge the goddess herself, who rides as one more competitor and is loved for it. **Peoples.** Haizava is cosmopolitan, the capital where the domain's wind-and-sky folk converge: **Strix** the natural pilots of canals, svifs, and airships; **Jotunborn** standing in what the rest of the city only reads, the crews still out when the shift-bell empties the streets; **Kashrishi** bringing the Air-Monastery's observation-discipline made urban, and the standing irony of the continent's mappers in the one city no map holds; **Tengu** crossing from the island, the voices that answer what the others read. The wind-reading craft is the civic common ground, open to all, and no ancestry rules because the wind does. **Placement.** Haizava stands on the great river that runs the **Baerfrost / Fellibylur border**, with the eastern old-growth forest close by and the **Air-Monastery** range near to the northeast. It is the windiest, most wind-shaped ground in the domain by no accident: warm storm-winds rolling north off the Fellibylur steppe collide here with the cold air spilling south off Baerfrost's ice, and a front of warm-meeting-cold is where the strongest and most *readable* winds are born. The great river is the one thing that does not move, the constant the city reconfigures around and over, with the Eye anchored at its bend; the forest and the range comb and channel the gusts; and the masters of mapping keep their mother-house within sight of the one city no map can hold.

#### ⚿ GM Secret: Who Banishes the Grasping

**The wind does not banish the grasping district. The Windwatchers do.** When a ward over-trims to chain itself to the Eye, the sworn-neutral clergy quietly counter-trim it, setting the great mechanisms and the canal-currents against it until it loses position, and let the city believe the goddess turned the weather on a tyrant. *Fisaya banishes the grasping* is true in its verdict and false in its hand: it is mortal enforcement dressed as divine will, and the Windwatchers keep the belief alive because a check everyone credits to the goddess needs no visible enforcer and draws no faction's blame. Fisaya is genuinely present and genuinely knows every wind, but she rides svifs and carries the breath into every home and leaves the governing of mortals to mortals; the Windwatchers' deepest vow is the secret of whose hand actually keeps Haizava free.
