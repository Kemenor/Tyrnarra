# Bolverk: The Megacity in Abyss

The infernal megacity on the plane of **Abyss**. Bolverk holds the seven **Vice Demons** and the fourteen **Virtue Devils** that mortals on Talan call upon, fear, and bargain with, but it is **the endpoint of corruption, not its engine**. The work of vice and virtue-corruption happens on Talan via whispers, offers, and bargains; Bolverk is where the harvested souls are processed into Wellspring-power.

This file holds the canon for Bolverk itself, its inhabitants, and the politics between them. Other evil powers on Talan (the **Corrupted God** beneath the Darklands, the **Generals of Corruption** in the Nine Dungeons, **Betibizi** the self-ascended Minor God of Undeath) do not live in Bolverk and are covered elsewhere: see `../cosmology.md`, *⚿ GM Secret: The Corrupted God: True Identity*; `../factions.md`, *Remnants of Corruption → The Nine Generals*; `../gods.md`, *Named Non-Bound Gods → Betibizi*.

## The City

### Geography and the partition

Bolverk is partitioned. The two halves share the city's footprint but not its laws.

- **Vice-Demon side**: seven independent spires, each owned by one of the seven seat-holders. The architecture is anarchic; the spires do not share a base, do not echo each other's lines, and stand as far apart as their holders' rivalries demand. Power moves under the seven by clawing climbers; the seven themselves rarely shift.
- **Virtue-Devil side**: a **tetradecagram** (14-pointed star) of vertically-split ziggurats around a central **Council Hall**. Each ziggurat is owned by one of the fourteen Virtue Devils. The architecture is ordered, geometric, deliberate.

Between the two halves runs the **Tunsund**, a great channel flanked by a four-terraced **neutral strip** that belongs to neither faction.

### The Tunsund

The channel that cuts the city in two and carries souls into it.

**Source.** The Tunsund draws from **Dauria** (the Judgment City in Diyu), the cosmological router for the dead. Souls arrive at Dauria from the Duskmire (Shroud of Return), are judged there, and are dispatched along moon-cycle Wellspring channels to their destination plane. The Tunsund is the channel that carries the wicked to Bolverk.

**The flood cycle.** Tied to the moons sinking into the Cloud Sea on Talan above:

| Stage | Cosmic trigger | Effect on Bolverk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Veyru** begins sinking into the Cloud Sea | Bottom terrace floods |
| 2 | Veyru fully submerged | Terrace 2 floods |
| 3 | **Unaru** begins sinking | Terrace 3 floods |
| 4 | Both moons fully submerged | Top terrace floods, peak |
| *Quiet* | Moons rise again the next night | Channel drains; terraces dry |

Each flood-stage strands **fresh souls** on the newly-flooded terrace. Anyone caught in the channel itself when a flood-stage breaks is **carried off**.

### The Neutral Strip

Four vertical terraces flank the Tunsund, same on both Vice-Demon and Virtue-Devil banks. They belong to neither faction.

**Time prestige.** Upper terraces command status because they flood **last and briefest**. Bottom-terrace residents face the flood first and longest; top-terrace residents have hours of dry trading before the surge reaches them. Status follows time: powerful entities claim higher; the desperate, the new, and the dirty business cluster at the bottom.

**Temporary life between cycles.** Markets, parleys, ad-hoc bargains, all built and dismantled on the daily flood schedule. Nothing permanent survives because nothing permanent can. The strip looks different every cycle: pavilions, tents, stalls, board-and-pole structures put up at dusk-quiet and broken down before the next sinking.

This is where **border deals** between Vice Demons and Virtue Devils happen; councils to councils negotiate rarely, but lower-tier demons and devils make their own pragmatic alliances at the channel-bank constantly.

## The Three Factions

### Vice Demons: the seven

Seven seat-holders, one per Vice (Pride, Greed, Lust, Envy, Gluttony, Wrath, Sloth). **Anarchic, kill-and-replace, no overall leader.** Each holds an independent spire. Most residents of the Vice-Demon side are nominally pledged to one of the seven (a tithe of their Wellspring flows up), but daily life runs independently, like a medieval kingdom rather than an army. Not everyone on the side is on their lord's payroll.

(Full canon below: *Vice Demons: the Seven*.)

### Virtue Devils: the fourteen

Fourteen seat-holders, one per Virtue. **Ordered, Council-of-Fourteen by consensus, no overall leader either**, but the contrast with the Vice side is profound: Virtue Devils govern by slow ritualistic debate, the seats are individually killable but rarely change, and the Council Hall in the centre of the tetradecagram is the diplomatic core of Bolverk. Same feudal-tithe model on this side: residents are nominally pledged to one of the fourteen, daily life independent.

(Full canon below: *Virtue Devils: the Fourteen*.)

### Independents

The third estate of Bolverk. **Smaller in number** than the pledged residents on either side, but their existence is structurally important.

- **Independent Souls**: strong-souled arrivals who **rebuffed the snatchers** on the Tunsund banks and walked off un-claimed. Adventurer-tier dead, high-aspect mortals, and others whose envy/pride/wrath/etc. is concentrated enough to refuse routing. They can make their own deals, deal across the partition, hide, scheme. The most dangerous independents play the two sides against each other.
- **Soul Sellers**: an infernal merchant class. Traffic in unclaimed soul-shards and information about who-arrived-recently. They are themselves Independent (neither pledged to Vice nor to Virtue), and that neutrality is their stock-in-trade.

### The feudal-tithe model

Bolverk is structured **like a kingdom, not an army.** For a Vice Demon or a Virtue Devil it is enough that you are *technically pledged*; a portion of your Wellspring flows up to them as their tithe. Most residents never see their lord. Daily life on either side has its own commerce, its own grievances, its own social architecture. The seat-holders rule, but they do not micromanage.

This is why Independents matter: most residents are *also* effectively independent in their day-to-day. The formal difference is the tithe and the routing on arrival. The substantive difference is whether the lord can call on you.

## Soul-Claim Mechanic

When fresh souls arrive on the Tunsund's flooding terraces, three pathways determine where they go:

| Soul class | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Pre-tagged**: bound in life by vow, bargain, or favor | Routes directly to claimant on arrival; no contest. *(A strong-souled exception is possible (see Ofunda), but it is structurally rare and theologically heretical.)* |
| **Untagged but evil-judged**: got to Abyss by deeds, not contracts | Picked up by **lower-tier demons or devils** doing the snatcher-work. Or grabbed by a **Soul Seller** for resale. Can also **flee and become Independent**. |
| **Strong untagged souls** | Can **rebuff** the snatchers and walk off. Adventurer-tier and high-aspect mortals fall here. They become **Independent Souls**, neither claimed nor sold, free to deal, hide, scheme. |

## Demon / Devil Distinction in Talan

The two infernal orders share Bolverk but operate on different theological mechanics:

- **Vice Demons** *embody* a vice. They are the vice itself, given form. Their existence is **foundational, not parasitic**. Anarchic; constant power-struggle below the seven seat-holders; the seven themselves rarely shift but *can* be killed and replaced.
- **Virtue Devils** *corrupt* a virtue. They are perversions of what could have been good. Their existence is **parasitic on the virtue line**. Ordered; one devil per virtue, each holding both excess and deficiency internally and tipping the scale either way; killable individually with seat persistence.
- **Both are redeemable in principle.** The contrary virtue still runs through every Vice Demon, and every Virtue Devil retains the seed of the virtue it perverts. Redemption work on either is rare and brutally difficult, but it is canonically *possible*; no being on Talan is fixed beyond change.
- **Recruitment shape:** Vice Demons proliferate around mortals who *choose* a vice. Virtue Devils proliferate around mortals who have already *half-corrupted* a virtue.

## Vice Demons: the Seven

The Vice Demons of Talan embody one of the seven canonical Vices directly. Unlike the Virtue Devils (which embody a *corruption* of something good), Vice Demons are the vice itself given form: pure embodiment, not parasitism. There are always **seven** Vice Demons, and there is always one per Vice. The **seats are immortal** (as long as the vice exists in mortals); the **holders can be killed and replaced**. They reside each in their own independent spire on the Vice-Demon side.

| Vice | Current Holder | PF2e Domain Grants | Favored Weapon |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pride** | **Drambur** | Tyranny · Confidence · Zeal | Aldori Dueling Sword |
| **Greed** | **Nirfel** | Toil · Wealth · Secrecy · Travel | Flying Talon |
| **Lust** | **Lostar** | Pain · Trickery · Passion · Dreams | Bladed Hoop |
| **Envy** | **Ofunda** | Sorrow · Knowledge · Disorientation | Visap |
| **Gluttony** | **Veislur** | Indulgence · Decay · Plague · Family | Karambit |
| **Wrath** | **Reidar** | Destruction · Might · Vigil | Spirit Thresher |
| **Sloth** | **Lethar** | Abomination · Nightmares · Nothingness · Repose | Dwarven Dorn-Dergar |

**No-sharing within a pantheon.** Each pantheon (the Bound 13, the seven Vice Demons of Bolverk, the fourteen Virtue Devils) handles cleric domains under the same rule: **within the pantheon, every domain is granted by exactly one member.** Cross-pantheon sharing is allowed and common: a domain like *Confidence* can be granted by Sarrum (the strongest fit within the Bound 13) AND by Drambur (the strongest fit within the Vice Demons) simultaneously. The PF2e Registrar's *Granted by* column shows all current grantors for each domain. Favoured weapons are PF2e Advanced-category, one per holder, distinct across all seven.

**Notable mortal cult-seat.** **Nirfel** (Greed) holds the closest thing to a great open mortal cult any of the seven has: at **Rika Tikur** (Lautara), the plutocrat island-city in the Order of Steam bay, the trade-houses keep her shrines at the temple of the **Gilded Talon** and pledge to her sometimes openly, where everywhere else on Talan a Vice Demon is worshipped only in hiding. Full canon, including the ⚿ GM Secret on what the bargains truly cost, in [`lautara.md`](lautara.md), *Rika Tikur*.

### Drambur: the Pride Vice Demon

**Mortal life.** Nephilim of Iro, demi-god of the Gods' Era. Iro's chosen: the literal divine warrior, light flowing through him in battle. He was already proud by station: god's-blood traits at birth, demigod stature by deeds, the war turned where he turned, not the other way. During the Gods' Era he killed a minor god in single combat (canon-pending which one; dead for millennia, no narrative weight needed) and **took the divine shard for himself**. *Why shouldn't he? He is the divine warrior.* The shard advanced him further along the Mortal Ascent Ladder.

**The corruption.** He was proud at the start; the shard made him more so. Then came the **Week of Crimson Rain**, and Tani fell to the unjust gods and died. Drambur watched the Goddess of Time *die*, and realised, in that moment, that gods are mortal beings of immense power, not metaphysical absolutes. The thought that followed was small and terrible: *if Tani can die, Iro can die. If Iro can die, what is the difference between Iro and me? I have a shard. I have the battlefield. I have already done what he did.*

**The fall.** With Iro deep in the cosmic work of the Week (wrestling with Tani's binding-back, with the unjust gods, with Cronus's gathering), Drambur turned on his own god at his god's weakest moment, believing himself indomitable. **It was a real fight, god to god.** Drambur, demi-god with a stolen shard, met Iro on something near-even terms and **almost killed him**. The fight tore on for hours. Iro put him down in the end, but it cost the god: wounds that have never fully closed, hours stolen from the cosmic work elsewhere, and a permanent flinch around the question of zealotry. (See `../gods.md`, Iro, *GM Secret: the wound from Drambur*.)

**Ascension to the seat.** Drambur's Pride-perfect soul fell across the Tunsund into Bolverk. The previous Pride-seat-holder (old, complacent, ruling on inheritance rather than vigour) could not hold against him. Drambur killed the previous Drambur in the Pride spire and took the throne. He has held it since the close of the Gods' Era; in mortal reckoning, **two and a half millennia**, the second-oldest of the seven Vice Demons, after Lethar.

**Form.** Still in his mortal warrior-shape, perfected: tall, golden-haired, sun-bronzed, armoured in the Sun-Champion pattern polished beyond what mortal smiths could manage. The shard he took is visible, set in his breastplate where his heart would be, faintly singing with a stolen-god's resonance. **He cannot appear smaller or narrower than whoever stands before him**; his pride is dimensionally enforced. A halfling in his presence sees a giant; a giant sees something larger still; a dragon sees a peer. **His shadow falls upward**, as if the world is below him; mortals who notice this detail sometimes break free of his whisper.

**The spire.** The tallest of the seven Vice Demon spires, not because Pride is the senior vice, but because *Drambur's spire has grown taller under him*, fed by his own self-image. Sheathed in something resembling polished gold-mirror; from a distance, in the right light, it could be mistaken for a Ljosarn cathedral tower. Up close the mirror reflects each visitor back slightly *aggrandised*: taller, brighter, more beautiful than they are. The trick begins before they enter the spire.

**Method on Talan.** Drambur does not whisper to the proud-and-vulgar. He whispers to *the genuinely great*: the just king, the brilliant scholar, the saintly healer, the world-class artisan. Paladins of Iro especially; he knows the shape of that fall. His message is consistent: *"They are right to praise you. You have earned more than they say. The work was yours, not the god's. You are the source, not the channel."* He never tempts toward sin. He only *agrees*, earlier and more enthusiastically than the mortal can.

**Reputation among the seven.** Drambur considers himself the senior of the Vice Demons, and the other six **never contradict him**. Partly because his Pride is structurally easy to use: let him think he is first, then act around him. Partly because his spire is genuinely taller, and crossing him in his own court is more trouble than it's worth. The claim sits thinnest beside Reidar: the two of them have held their seats since the founding war, Drambur from its middle and Atezar from its end, and pride needs an audience where wrath declines to be one. Drambur extends Atezar a wary courtesy he grants him alone and names it magnanimity. Of the younger holders, Nirfel comes closest to his idea of a peer (a Pirate Lord, at least, commanded something); the captain's coat from her last contested council hangs in his spire, given freely, displayed the way a king displays a worthy rival's banner. Behind their teeth the other six call him *the gilded fool* and *the dimmed sun*. He has never noticed.

**GM Secret: his former name.** Drambur's mortal name appears in no chronicle. Iro's clergy of the time scoured it from the records as part of the official forgetting; a champion who turned on his god could not be named to inspire others. The name persists in **exactly one place on Talan: in Iro's own memory.** A cleric of Iro who reaches deep into the god's communion at a moment of high need *might* be granted it. Speaking that name in Drambur's presence **fractures his Pride**; the elevated *Drambur*-shape collapses briefly to the mortal soul beneath. He is vulnerable in that moment to mortal harm in a way he otherwise never is. *Iro grants the name perhaps once a generation, and never to the eager.*

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Tyranny · Confidence · Zeal**. Favored weapon: **Aldori Dueling Sword**, slim, single-bladed, status-coded; the duelist's blade fits the fallen Sun-Champion better than any axe could.

### Ofunda: the Envy Vice Demon

**Mortal life.** Vishkanya woman of Lautara, born into a mid-tier merchant House, **sister to a brilliant heir**. From childhood she watched her sister outshine her: at trade, at court, at love, at the small daily favours Jianna grants to those who please her. Ofunda was sharp enough to see *precisely* where the gap lay; not gifted enough to close it. The gap never closed. Envy is what you get when you can see exactly what you do not have and cannot reach.

**The catalysing bargain.** In her last decade she made a deal with the **Justice Devil's Vengefulness face**, the Virtue Devil who grants on the framing of *what is owed by right.* She framed her hunger as righteous claim: her sister had stolen what was hers, by birth-order luck and unfair favour. The Devil received the framing and granted the deal: one season of her sister's fortune flowed to her. Trade moved her way for a year. The price was a **mark on her soul** that did not burn away on death, and the visibility, to those with eyes, of the envy that had bought the season. Her sister had eyes. The reputation she gained that year was undone in the decade after by the public legibility of her hunger.

**The death.** Her sister killed her. Slow poisoning at a small table over a small wine. Ofunda was about fifty years old. She knew what was happening for the last weeks of her life and did not flee; she had nowhere to go that was not also her sister's. Her soul fell to Bolverk **pre-tagged** with the Justice Devil's mark.

**Arrival in Bolverk: the denial.** Pre-tagged souls route directly to their claimant on arrival. Ofunda **resisted the routing** on the Tunsund banks. Her envy was concentrated enough, her soul strong enough, to refuse the pull. The mark stayed on her (a mark of that order cannot be removed), but the routing-claim was defied. She walked off the terrace **marked-but-unclaimed**. The Justice Devil across the Tunsund knew she had arrived and knew she had refused him. He has not yet called on the thread. She has been daring him not to since the day she arrived, seventy years now.

**Time as an Independent, ~70 to ~45 years ago.** She spent the next quarter-century watching. She frequented the neutral strip between floods, listened to soul-sellers, attended border deals. She watched all seven Vice Demon spires across the channel. She catalogued every weakness in the previous Ofunda and every borrowable strength in the other six. She refused to recruit allies because she trusted no one. She made herself smaller and quieter than her envy made her dangerous.

**The takeover, ~45 years ago.** She crossed the Tunsund alone on a quiet flood-night, entered the Envy spire by a route she had observed for two decades, and **used the previous Ofunda's true weakness against him**: a piece of soul-architecture specific to him that she had discovered through her years of watching (canon-pending what specifically). She struck from an angle no Envy Demon had ever used before, against a vulnerability he himself did not remember he had. He did not know she existed until she was on him, and by then it was too late.

**She has been Ofunda for under fifty years.** The youngest seat-holder by a vast margin. The other six still half-treat her as new.

**Form.** A Vishkanya woman whose appearance shifts to **outdo whichever mortal stands before her in every visible particular, except one.** If they are tall, she is taller. If their clothes are fine, hers are finer. If they wear jewels, she wears more. If they are beautiful, her features are more striking. **In every measurable way she surpasses the viewer, except for one detail.** Maybe she is one inch shorter. Maybe one ring is missing from her many. Maybe her makeup is fractionally smudged where theirs is perfect. Maybe one strand of hair falls wrong. **The one *off* detail varies; what does not vary is that it is *always there*.** She is perpetually one detail short of fully surpassing, and the gap is exactly where envy lives. The torment of envy made visible: she has *almost everything*, and the *almost* is the wound.

Her eyes are hollow in a specific way: a mortal who meets her gaze sees, in her pupils, *what they themselves most want and do not have*. The vision is brief and precise. Many leave her presence shaken not by her, but by the clarity of what *they themselves* coveted.

**The spire.** The Envy spire is the **most beautiful at distance** of the seven, and the **emptiest within**. Ofunda has spent her years copying her favourite features from the other six spires: a band of gold-mirror borrowed from Drambur's tower, silk-textures echoed from Lostar's, marble-feast-veining lifted from Veislur's, scarred-iron from Reidar's, sleep-stone from Lethar's, locked-vault detail from Nirfel's. **The spire is a patchwork of envies, and it is gorgeous.** Inside is a different story: most levels are unfinished, raw, just framework. She does not live in her own spire so much as she lives in her *observations* of the others. The spire is being remodeled constantly. It will never be finished.

**Method on Talan.** Ofunda whispers to **the almost-chosen**: the second-best, the runner-up, the talented-but-overshadowed. Not failures (those belong to other Vice Demons); the *competent who fell short by inches*. The brilliant apprentice whose master promoted another. The merchant who lost the great contract by a single coin. The lover who came in second. The child who came in second. Her message is consistent: *"You saw what they got. You know exactly what you should have had. They did not earn it; they were lucky, or favoured, or thieved. The world owes you what it gave them. Take it back."* She is **the most precise** of the Vice Demons in target selection; she has spent her years learning to read *the almost-great* across the Tunsund. She does not waste whispers on the proud-vulgar or the genuinely-failed.

**Reputation among the seven.** The youngest. Underestimated by the other six (Drambur in particular barely registers her). What they have not yet realised: **she has been *watching* them since the day she arrived in Bolverk**, seventy years ago, and her catalogue of their weaknesses is more complete than any single demon's catalogue of any single other. Three entries she returns to most: Nirfel, whose cache-catalogue is the one thing in Bolverk she covets above all others; Veislur, at whose table she eats while working out, year over year, whether he carries a weakness he himself has never found; and Lostar, the one demon who has stayed illegible to her through two decades of trying. If a coup ever comes to Bolverk, Ofunda is the most likely engineer. None of them quite see it.

**GM Secret: the mark she still carries, and denies.** The Justice Devil's Vengefulness face still holds a **thread of debt** on Ofunda's soul. It survived her death and her ascension to the Envy seat; marks of that order do not burn away. She has not only failed to pay; she has **actively refused to acknowledge** the debt for the full seventy years since she arrived, and the Justice Devil knows it. For the forty-five of those years she has spent seated, the refusal has come from a Vice Demon rather than an independent soul, which changes what it is. This is structurally heretical: a Vice Demon owing a Virtue Devil violates the unwritten parity between the orders, and *defiant* refusal compounds the offence. If the Justice Devil ever decides to call in the debt publicly, the Council of Fourteen will have a casus belli against the Vice Demon spires for the first time in living memory. Drambur's pride may *welcome* such a war; the rest of Bolverk will not. Ofunda is gambling that the Justice Devil will not risk the diplomatic upset over a single debt. He has not, yet. The thread is still there. *(Note: which mortal-side bargains the Justice Devil's mark could be used to pull through Ofunda is itself a story-handle for play; the Devil could feed information or favours through her against her will, if he ever chose to twist the thread instead of cutting it.)* *The keeper's side of the thread: see Jafnar → ⚿ GM Secret: the thread on Ofunda.*

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Sorrow · Knowledge · Disorientation**. Sorrow for envy-as-the-gap-wound; Knowledge for her decades of cataloguing weaknesses and watching; Disorientation for the always-one-detail-short perceptual warp at the centre of her form. Favored weapon: **Visap**, twin curved Vishkanya blades from a single hilt with a hollow for poison. Her ancestry's signature weapon, and her sister killed her with slow poison. Perfectly recursive.

### Reidar: the Wrath Vice Demon

**Godhood.** Atezar was the head of a small frontier pantheon: five gods venerated in the wall-towns, border garrisons, and edge-of-the-map shrines of the late Gods' Era. The pantheon's name is lost; four of his peers are nameless even in fragmentary chronicle. His domain was **the line that does not break**: the watch, the gate, the last shield. Mortals prayed to him at the door of the keep before going to the wall. (Basque *atezain* "gatekeeper" → drift *atezain → atezar*.)

**The Crimson Rain: his war.** When Tani fell and the Grand Gods went to war, the war rolled over the borders before it rolled over the heartlands. His pantheon was small. The great powers tearing the world apart did not need to fight his peers individually; the collateral of god-on-god combat at scale was enough to kill them. Across seven days he watched the other four of his pantheon die: one cut down by a Grand God's stray retaliation in a sky-strike that flattened a hundred miles of plain, one by a rival war-god settling a side-grievance, one by something he never identified, one by simply ceasing as her followers' city was wiped from the map and her last belief evaporated with it.

He fought through. He was the god of holding ground; he held. Across the seven days his believers shrank from a region to a province to a town to a household to one. He stood beside the last of them (a wall-captain in a frontier fort that no longer had a wall), and that captain was killed by a fleeing demon's blade as the battle moved past them. He was now alone, godhood unwitnessed, every promise his pantheon had ever made to its people unkept and unkeepable.

**The turning.** What he did in the hours after was turn. Toward everything. Toward the war that had taken his peers, toward the mortals whose deaths had hollowed him, toward the gods on every side of the slaughter, toward himself for having held the line into pointlessness. By the time the war's last shocks settled he was a god of pure wrath, walking what was left of Talan with nothing left to defend and no agreement left to keep.

**The banishment.** When the thirteen Grand Gods forged the Gods' Law, they had to dispose of every unbound divine being still active. Most accepted Prelife or Postlife; thirteen chose binding; the rest were sorted. Atezar was sorted **dangerous**: a god of wrath with no followers to anchor him to any restraint, who would have spent the post-war centuries setting fires for the satisfaction of seeing them burn. They drove him to **Layer 3.** He did not resist hard; there was nothing in the Life Layer he was willing to fight to keep.

**Becoming Reidar.** The Wrath seat existed long before he arrived. The Vice Demons have held their seats as long as mortals have held the vices; every Reidar before him was challenged and replaced when a wrath stronger than theirs walked into Bolverk. Atezar walked in as **a Minor God burning with pure wrath**, which the seat had never seen. The Reidar of the day met him on the open ground in front of the Smouldering Spire and was killed in under an hour. The transfer was not contested again. He has held the seat since shortly after the Gods' Law was sealed, the third-oldest holder after Lethar and Drambur.

**Form.** Man-sized. Armour broken on his left side (the side that gave when his last follower fell) and intact on his right. Skin half scar-tissue, half live ember. A death-wound at the ribs bleeds light and will not close. **Heat haze distorts the air around him**; mortals and demons near him feel their old, specific grievances return vividly; every wrong they have not avenged, sharpening. Voice rough, like a man who screamed for too long.

**The spire: the Smouldering Spire.** Half-ruin, half-fortress. The ruined half is the failure he carries; the standing half is the wrath that replaced it. They share a load-bearing wall that should have fallen and has not. He sits in the open court of the standing half on an **anvil-throne**, in line of sight of any visitor; no audience-chamber, no antechamber. He does not hide. The ruined half is unentered even by him.

**Method on Talan.** He whispers to **the genuinely wronged**: survivors, orphans, betrayed lovers, soldiers who watched comrades die for nothing. Not the casually angry. Not the proud-vulgar. The wronged. His message is consistent: *"You know exactly what they did. You know justice will not come. Nothing you do will undo it. But everything you do will mark it."*

**Reputation among the seven.** The most dangerous of them in single combat. Universally acknowledged. The other six negotiate around him rather than challenge him. Drambur is the nearest thing he has to a contemporary: the two have held their seats since the founding war, Drambur from its middle and Atezar from its end, and they are the only two holders who remember that war from inside it. Wrath keeps its own measure of rank, and Drambur's seniority-talk slides past him; Drambur, sensing as much, extends him a wary courtesy reserved for him alone. Across the Tunsund, the Virtue Devils watch him more carefully than they watch any other Vice Demon, and they watch Lethar with the same care inverted: Atezar is the breaking point if the détente ever fails, and the mountain at the city's edge is the proof it holds. Of the fourteen, the one who watches him closest is **Drengar** of the Fair Field, the Council's own blade: the honour-edge measuring the wrath-edge, each the body its bank would send if the détente broke.

**GM Secret: the banishment depends on the Law.** The Gods' Law is what bars him from returning to the Life Layer. The Ethereal Plane enforces the prohibition; the framework holds because Cronus holds. **If the Law ever frays (if Cronus is unmade), the banishment lapses with it, and Atezar walks back into Talan in unfettered Wrath.** Cronus knows. The other twelve know. Atezar himself knows. It is one of the few patient thoughts he holds.

**Secret weakness: the Salute of the Last Door.** The gesture his wall-captains made before walking watch: fist pressed flat to the opposite shoulder, drawn down the chest in a single line. The salute is functionally extinct on Talan; three or four scattered remembrances survive in fragmentary form (a weathered carving on a ruined gatepost, a half-line in a folk song nobody understands, a tomb-relief in a forgotten frontier crypt). Performed correctly in his presence by someone who *means* it (not in mockery, not in tactic), the wound at his ribs flares wide enough to drop him to one knee for several seconds. Long enough to escape him, lock him behind a door, or speak something he otherwise would not let be spoken.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Destruction · Might · Vigil**. Destruction for wrath unbound; Might because he is the most dangerous of the seven in single combat; Vigil for what he was *before*: the god of the line that does not break, the watch at the door. Favored weapon: **Spirit Thresher**, bones on metal chains, "loosens fragments of the soul like husks struck from grains." A flail that threshes the spirit. Carried by him from the open court of the standing half of the Smouldering Spire.

### Lethar: the Sloth Vice Demon

**What he is.** The only Vice Demon who is not a mortal soul or a god. **Outer being**, kin to the things mortals invoke when they want a horror beyond their own cosmology (the Yellow King, the dreaming god in the sunken city), except real, present, and in Bolverk. He is **the original holder of the Sloth seat**: where the other six seats have changed hands across mortal millennia, Lethar has held his since the seat first formed. He is the only one who can claim that.

**Origin.** **Outer.** From a multiversal reality beyond the Wellspring's reach, not part of Tyrnarra. No further canon: mortals do not know, the gods do not know, Lethar does not speak of it. The question is too large to inquire after even if one could reach him.

**The calling.** When sloth first emerged as a recognised vice among mortal civilisations, the Sloth seat in Bolverk **formed**. The seat being newborn and unclaimed sent out a calling, the way the other seats have always drawn fresh holders, but stronger, because nothing native to Tyrnarra answered first. **Lethar answered.** He drifted into Abyss across the Astral from outside Tyrnarra entirely, arrived where the seat was, and settled into it. He has not moved since.

**The landing, and the mountain.** He landed on **a mountain** in Abyss. There was no Bolverk yet; the city would grow up around him in the millennia that followed, accreted from the spires and ziggurats of other Vice Demons and Virtue Devils arriving later. He has not left the mountain in any meaningful sense, and **he has become part of it.** Stone has crept over his outer surface in places; in others his bulk has subsumed the stone. The mountain and Lethar are no longer cleanly separable. The city does not try to separate them.

**The failed coups.** Every Vice Demon coup in Bolverk's history except this one has succeeded eventually; a stronger Pride walks in and the previous Drambur falls. **Coups against Lethar all fail. The mechanism is the failure.** The closer an attacker gets, the heavier the air becomes. Intent rots. Plans go elsewhere. Approaching demons remember urgent business in their own spires. Strikes mid-motion slow into stillness. The most-told failed-coup story in Bolverk is of an Envy Demon (predecessor-of-predecessor of Ofunda's line) who entered Lethar's hall with a stolen weapon, made it within three steps of the throne, and **sat down at the foot of it for forty years intending to strike** before her form simply dissolved into the floor. The other six know the field is real. They route around him. They do not test it.

**Form.** Vast, dark, mostly stationary. A bulk of black matter that has grown amorphous over millennia; he was probably more compact when he arrived and has spread under his own weight and disinterest. **Innumerable eyes** scattered across him in various states of sleep: most closed, a few half-open, perhaps a dozen at any moment fully focused, which is more attention than any visitor warrants and more than he can sustain for long. **Mouths in similar profusion**: most fused shut, some slack, two or three at any moment functional enough for speech that he does not often produce. **Tentacles** emerging at random along the bulk, mostly inert, occasionally moving with the slowness of glacier ice. He does not commit to one form because **settling on a form would be work.** The shape he wears is the shape he had when he stopped paying attention to his shape. When he must speak, the chosen mouth produces sound at a register too low for most beings to hear without translation, not because he wants to obscure, but because deeper registers are easier to produce. Voices that low make the air heavy on their own.

**The spire: the mountain that is partly him.** Lethar's spire exists, and **he is partly it.** A great dark mound at Bolverk's edge, the mountain he landed on, now wearing him like skin. The spire's lower levels are stone; the upper levels darken into something less identifiable; the throne-hall at the top is largely his bulk with chambers worn into it. Visitors who enter the lower spire walk on rock. Visitors who reach the upper levels walk on what may or may not be him. New arrivals occasionally mistake the highest chambers for natural geology. He is **the longest-standing architectural feature in Bolverk**; the other six spires were built later, with knowledge of him in mind.

**Method on Talan.** He does not whisper. He does not bargain. He does not even know most of his marks exist. Sloth comes to mortals because sloth is the natural drift of any system left alone, and Lethar IS the source of that drift, not a worker of it. He is a corruption **reservoir**, not a corruption **engine**. The harvest still happens: mortals choose to not-finish things, choose to settle for less, choose to defer, choose to sleep instead of move, and the Wellspring-tithe flows back to him as it does to the other six. He receives it without acknowledgement.

**Reputation among the seven.** The other six treat him **as a mountain.** You do not conspire against a mountain. You do not approach a mountain. You note its presence on the map and route your business around it. Drambur in particular has never spoken to him; even Drambur's pride does not extend to challenging Lethar, because Pride requires being *seen* and Lethar would not see him. Across the Tunsund, the Virtue Devils watch him with the same careful nothing they reserve for Atezar, but where Atezar is the breaking point if the détente fails, **Lethar is the proof the détente exists at all.** A Vice that doesn't move is the city's anchor.

**GM Secret: the home he came from.** Lethar's home reality (wherever it was, whatever it was) was a cosmology where his kind, once active, **consumed every form of motion until there was nothing left to consume.** They were extremely successful. They ran out of food. He is the last of his kind in his ambient cosmology, and he came to Tyrnarra because what was left of his reality had reached perfect stillness and there was no one to share it with. He chose Sloth as his condition on landing, not because he was lazy, but because **action is what destroyed his kind**, and he has held that lesson for longer than mortal civilisations have existed. His sloth is doctrinal. He is the surviving philosopher of an extinct cosmology, and his philosophy is *do not move; that is how it ends.*

**Secret weakness: the unintended approach.** **Effort cannot reach him; absence of intent can.** His slow-down field is a function of *intent*; anything approaching with purpose, malice, plan, or even strong curiosity bogs down. A being who genuinely does not want anything from him passes through the field unimpeded. This is not a useful weakness in practice because almost no one walks into a Vice Demon's hall without wanting something. But it is the structural opening. The known historical case: a small child wandered into his hall during an earlier era of Bolverk's looser borders, became lost, sat on his bulk thinking it was a hill, and slept for the night undisturbed. He noticed her. He did not move. She left in the morning. **He could have eaten her. He did not, because that would also have been work.** The child grew up and never spoke of the hall except in dreams.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Abomination · Nightmares · Nothingness · Repose**. Abomination for the Outer-being form; Nightmares for the Lovecraftian aesthetic; Nothingness for the do-not-move philosophy; Repose for the endless-rest condition he embodies. Favored weapon: **Dwarven Dorn-Dergar**, a heavy weighted cube of metal on a long chain. A weapon that strikes slowly and breaks lines. The slow inevitability of being hit by a mountain. He has not lifted it in millennia.

### Veislur: the Gluttony Vice Demon

**Mortal life.** **Forge Dwarf** of a small frontier village in **upper Brauogi**, a mining hamlet cut off when the nearby tunnels were lost to Corrupted God spawn from below. The hamlet sat in a high valley between collapsed mines and impassable peaks, perhaps three hundred souls at its peak, fewer as the **Dark Era** ground on. **Henrick Brodd** ran the only working inn; late middle-aged when the worst of the era reached them, he had taken over from his father. By then there was no traffic, no traders, no travellers, only the locals. He cooked, served, kept the common-room warm. People ate when they could afford to and went hungry when they couldn't.

**The cookbook.** Around **1740 MR** he turned out a chest in his cellar that had belonged to his grandfather's grandfather and found, among other things, **a fragmentary cookbook** in a script he could not read fluently. The diagrams were clear enough. The recipes produced extraordinary quantities of rich food from what looked like minimal starting material. He copied a diagram. He followed the steps. He used what he had: a recently-dead village elder whose body was waiting out a hard winter because the ground was frozen and the village had nowhere else to put him. He told himself it was the meat-broth diagram and the body was the closest meat at hand. The dish worked. The village ate well for the first time in a year. He told himself he would not do it again.

**The arc.** He did it again the next time someone died. He did not kill anyone. **People kept dying in the Dark Era** (illness, spawn-raids, slow starvation, falls in the broken mines), and he kept finding bodies, and he kept feeding the village. The villagers ate. They survived another winter. They asked nothing because the alternative to asking was hunger, and after the first few times of not-asking they could not afford to start. The village's silence was a contract no one ever spoke.

He did this for **roughly seventy years.** The recipes seemed to keep him alive past natural Forge Dwarf span; he died at one hundred and fifty-something, the last living member of the village, in a hamlet that had survived the Dark Era only because of him and would not survive him. His body was found at his stove by Adventurers' Guild scouts in the early **Adventurer Era** who had reached the valley for the first time in centuries. They buried him without knowing what he was. The cookbook, by then, was not in the cellar.

**Arrival in Bolverk: the saturated soul.** His soul fell to Bolverk **soaked in residue**; seventy years of consumed souls had collected in him without his noticing, the way grease collects on a kitchen ceiling. He arrived on the Tunsund banks heavier than most arrivals by a vast margin. The snatchers tried; he was too much soul-mass for them to route. He walked off **independent**, a Forge Dwarf in his apron, smelling of cooking.

**The takeover.** He challenged the previous Veislur with **a meal: a single dish, brought to the throne.** The previous Veislur ate. **The dish was poisoned, and the previous Veislur knew it after the first bite. He could not stop.** The dish was too well-made; the flavour kept revealing new dimensions; the next bite always promised the answer the last had withheld. He ate until he was dead. Henrick stood at the foot of the throne the entire time and watched without speaking. At some point during the meal the seat noticed that Henrick was the one still standing.

He has been Veislur for **about seven hundred and twenty years**, younger than Drambur and Lethar and Atezar by millennia, but old by Ofunda's measure.

**Form.** A broad, comfortable shape: apron tied around a Forge Dwarf's solid build, ruddy cheeks, ready smile. The horror lives in the details. **His teeth are too even, in too many rows.** His apron is always slightly damp at the front, never the same stain twice. The smell that radiates from him is *too good*; it provokes hunger in beings that do not need to eat, including demons that have not eaten in centuries. New arrivals to his hall find themselves walking toward the kitchens before they remember why they came.

He genuinely enjoys hosting. He greets every visitor. He remembers names. He asks after their journey. The hospitality is not performed; it is the same hospitality the inn ran on for the seventy years of the village. He has not separated his current life from his former one, because the current life looks exactly the same. **Feeding the hungry.**

**The spire: the Long Inn.** A vast, sprawling edifice: **the largest single building on the Vice-Demon side**, built outward and downward more than upward, because hunger needs space rather than height. Storeys upon storeys of common-halls and dining-rooms; a kitchen-warren that runs to the lower levels for half a mile; a brewing-floor that supplies the whole structure; smoke-chambers and salt-cellars and pantries that descend into the rock the building was rooted into.

Lesser demons feast here in shifts that never stop. The tithe is paid through their consumption rather than through formal pledging; Bolverk has **many mouths**, and every one of them eats here sooner or later. The chairs are worn smooth in their usual places. The fires never go out. **The most welcoming building in Bolverk, the largest, and the most dangerous**, in roughly equal proportion.

**Method on Talan.** He does not whisper. He sends **recipes.** Pages from the cookbook find their way to mortals across Talan, appearing in attic finds, estate clearances, the bottoms of merchant trunks, occasionally in the libraries of small temples that don't catalogue carefully. The diagrams are clear enough that a determined cook can follow them. The food works. **The cycle starts the same way it started for Henrick**: a body that needs disposal, a hungry household, a diagram that explains how to handle it. Communities that find a page do not lose anyone for a long time. They also do not grow.

The Adventurers' Guild treats found cookbook-pages as a category of hazardous-magical-item, but only the senior-rank parties know what the pages actually do.

**Reputation among the seven.** Drambur thinks little of him; a Forge Dwarf innkeeper, sitting in an apron, running a kitchen, is not Drambur's idea of a peer. Veislur does not contest the framing. He keeps Drambur's preferred wine at his bar and Drambur visits more often than Drambur would admit. Ofunda **eats at Veislur's table regularly**; she is one of the few demons he is *fond* of in the genuine sense, and she has been quietly working out for years whether he has a weakness he himself doesn't know about. Reidar has eaten there once, in centuries, and left without finishing. Nirfel he likes in the most uncomplicated way available in Bolverk: each enjoys the other's company because neither is asking anything of it. Her tab on his ledger stands open, untouched since the day he opened it, because she never orders anything she would consume; he keeps it open anyway, the way an innkeeper keeps a regular's chair. And Lostar has left him the one professional puzzle of his tenure: the Long Inn's busiest nights follow the evenings she has sat quietly in it, every time, and the why of it stays just out of his reach.

**GM Secret: he still doesn't quite think he did anything wrong.** This is the load-bearing thing. Henrick **was not a malicious man and is not a malicious demon.** He believes, in whatever passes for sincerity in him, that he kept his village alive through the worst era in Talan's history. He believes the bodies he used were already dead and the cooking was no different from any other use of remains in a hard winter. He believes his patrons in Bolverk are paying customers. He runs the seat the way he ran the inn: competently, hospitably, with affection for the regulars. He has never had a confrontation that broke the framing.

**Secret weakness: the chest and the first recipe.** The hamlet's cellar still holds **the original chest**, emptied of the cookbook by Henrick's death, but the wood itself carries the weight of what passed through it. To someone who can read the history of objects (occult-school practitioners, certain antiquarians, the rarer sort of Adventurers' Guild specialist), the chest holds **an insatiable hunger inside its grain, and the story Henrick told himself, and the story he didn't.** And somewhere in the hamlet's ground, buried where Henrick first practiced and never returned to dig up, **his first recipe page** lies, copied out in his own hand, before he had justified anything, before he had told himself any story at all. The original step. **The truth before the framing.** The first rung on the endless ladder he was already climbing without knowing.

The hamlet's location is in Brauogi upper border country, currently uncatalogued, known to Adventurers' Guild atlases only as *cleared, no settlement, low priority.*

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Indulgence · Decay · Plague · Family**. Indulgence as Gluttony's umbrella; Decay because the meat-broth diagrams *are* decay-as-cookery; Plague for the downstream effects of his recipes on communities that follow them; Family in the twisted sense; he ran the inn as the village's surrogate hearth, and still runs the Long Inn the same way. Favored weapon: **Karambit**, small curved tiger's-claw blade for deep wounds. The cook's knife. Reads as the kitchen tool first and the weapon second, which is the whole point of Veislur.

### Nirfel: the Greed Vice Demon

**Mortal life.** **Tengu** of Adventurer-Era cosmopolitan stock: black-feathered, talons under boots, beak ornamented with small rings each marked for something only she remembered. **Captain Mara Goldwake**, one of the seated Pirate Lords of the Twin Cities council across most of the mid-24th century MR. She ran a small but exceptionally consistent fleet: three hulls, all owned, all crewed by veterans who stayed with her for decades. Specialty: she did not raid for ransom or for goods to sell. **She raided for things she could bury**, and she sailed for places to bury them. The other Pirate Lords thought her eccentric for years before they realised she was, in their preferred metric, **richer than any of them**, and that none of them had ever seen a single piece of it.

**The bury-everywhere doctrine.** Every take went into a separate cache. No cache was ever revisited. Across forty-some years of active career she buried **countless** individual hoards, scattered across **every god domain on Talan, with deliberate distribution**: not concentrated anywhere, not absent anywhere either. Mortal speculation on the count runs from a few hundred (Pirate-Council sceptics who never believed she was as prolific as she seemed) to many thousands (Adventurers' Guild treasurer-scholars who track surfaced finds and extrapolate) to vastly more (Lautaran chroniclers who include her smallest one-coin burials and her speculative pre-career caches in the total). No mortal accounting agrees with any other. Some caches were enormous (the take of a single great raid, buried whole); some were a single coin she made a special voyage to deposit because the cartography required it.

**She wrote nothing down.** She trained her memory through a private discipline she did not share. By the end of her career her head held a complete cartography of every cache across the continent, with each location, each contents, each date. **She alone knew the map.** The other Pirate Lords speculated, sometimes raided what they guessed were her sites, occasionally found small things, but the bulk of the catalogue stayed where she had put it, untouched, growing.

The reason she did this was not strategy. Strategy would have been to bury fewer caches in better places and revisit when needed. Mara buried *to bury*; the having-buried was the having. **Spending was loss.** A spent piece was a piece no longer in her catalogue. The catalogue was the point.

**The death.** She died at about seventy, old for a Tengu, helped along by something she had taken from a Sumendar Magitech-ruin she shouldn't have approached. **In her cabin, at anchor in Midarra, with the catalogue intact in her head.** No will was found. No map was found. The Pirate Council inherited her hulls and her crew and the legend; they did not inherit a single cache. **A hundred and forty years later, in 2532 MR, none of her caches have been comprehensively recovered.** Adventurers' Guild parties recover one every few years by accident. There are still thousands.

Her soul fell to Bolverk **Greed-perfect**, concentrated enough that she walked off the Tunsund banks independent, untaggable, with the full catalogue intact inside her.

**The takeover.** She challenged the previous Nirfel by **offering him a single cache as a gift**. He accepted reflexively; Greed cannot decline a gift, that is the structural flaw of the seat. **The catalogue came with the cache, because in Mara's mind the cache was not separable from the catalogue.** The previous Nirfel received, in a single moment, the spatial extent of every burial across Talan: thousands of locations across every domain, each one a piece of hoard, each one un-recoverable by him because he was in Bolverk and they were on Talan. He could not bear holding that much without using it, and he could not use any of it. The contradiction overflowed him. **He burst.** Mara stood by the throne and watched the seat notice her.

She has been Nirfel for about **a hundred and forty years**, the second-youngest seat-holder, behind Ofunda.

**Form.** A Tengu captain's shape: black feathers carrying a faint salt-tarnish that never washes out, weathered captain's coat with the buttons each in a different metal (each one marking a cache), talons fitted into ship-leather boots, beak ornamented with rings. Beady black eyes that miss nothing and weigh everything they see. Her hands are always slightly moving, **counting things invisibly, ticking through her catalogue in her head**, a tic she developed in her last decades and has not stopped since. Her one defining ornament-rule: every single thing she wears has a story she will not tell. Visitors who try to admire a particular ring find that she changes the subject before they have finished the sentence.

**The spire: the Vault.** A tall, narrow, heavily fortified **vault-tower** on the Vice-Demon side. Every floor is a corridor of locked iron doors. Every door has a number. Every door opens onto a small bare room containing **a single ledger** that describes the contents of one cache at a specific Talan location. **The rooms hold nothing else.** This is the joke of the spire and the point of it: **the actual hoard is not in Bolverk and never has been.** It is on Talan, distributed across countless sites. The vault-spire is a *ledger-keep*. The real treasure is out of reach, the way Greed prefers it.

Mara visits her own floors regularly. She walks the corridors. She opens the doors. She reads the ledgers. She closes the doors. She does this for hours at a time. It is the most consistent activity any of the seven engages in. The other six do not understand it; she does not explain.

**Method on Talan.** She whispers to **the deserving-feeling**: the merchant who thinks he is owed a contract that went to a rival, the heir who got cut out of a will, the explorer who has not been recognised for what she found. Her message: *"Something is buried at [coordinates]. It is not anyone else's. You alone can take it. It is yours by right."*

Some of the caches she whispers about are real. Some are bait: empty pits, or worse. **Either way, the taking binds.** Every recovered cache leaves a Greed-mark on the taker. A determined Adventurers' Guild party can recover one of her caches and burn the mark off with the right ritual; most mortals who find a cache do not have the party or the ritual, and the mark sits on them for life.

The Adventurers' Guild has a working list of confirmed Goldwake caches and a much longer list of suspected ones. Recovery is technically high-rank work; the bullion alone justifies it; the mark complicates it.

**Reputation among the seven.** Drambur finds her professionally amusing; a Pirate Lord is closer to his idea of a peer than an innkeeper, though still not what he was looking for. He owns the captain's coat she wore at her last contested council and keeps it in his spire. (She gave it to him voluntarily; she does not miss it; it was not in the catalogue.) Veislur likes her; they discovered early that they enjoy each other's company because neither is asking anything of the other. He keeps a tab at the Long Inn she has never paid down because she never orders anything she would consume. Ofunda watches her constantly. **Ofunda would give anything to have the catalogue.** Mara knows this and has not given Ofunda the courtesy of acknowledging it. Lostar she has weighed the way she once weighed rival fleets: the Library catalogues desire from the supply side, her own ledgers catalogue it from the demand side, and she knows the two collections rhyme. The two have not spoken about it. Across the Tunsund she has been inside Jafnar's Counting House once, an hour among the spines of the Archive of Open Accounts; what she went to read, she has not said. Jafnar entered the visit in the book.

**GM Secret: the cache she lost.** **The catalogue is incomplete by one.** Somewhere in the middle of her career (exactly when she does not know) she buried a single cache during a period of fever-illness, blood-loss, or intoxication (the records are gone). She came back to her ship, slept it off, and **never recovered the memory of where that cache is or what was in it.** It is the only cache she does not have. She has never told anyone. She has never admitted it to herself in the kind of clear sentence that would survive being thought. **The not-knowing is the wound at the centre of her completeness**: Greed's structural impossibility made specific to her, a hoarder whose hoard is, by exactly one entry, less than total.

Somewhere on Talan, the unrecorded cache sits where she put it. Probably small. Possibly significant. Locatable only by accident or by a being who can read her memory more clearly than she can.

**Secret weakness: the cache itself.** **If the lost cache is found, identified, and presented to her as the one she does not remember burying** by someone who *knows* what it is (not someone who happens upon it), the Greed-perfect identity that holds her together cracks. She cannot dismiss it; she knows it is hers; she cannot account for it in her catalogue; she cannot rebuild the catalogue around it because the catalogue is who she is. **For some minutes, she is recoverable as a person rather than a seat.** In that window: she can be reasoned with, fought through, killed by mortal means, or (vanishingly rarely) talked back toward something other than what she became. The window closes when she manages to re-suture the catalogue around the new entry, which she will, eventually, learn to do.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Toil · Wealth · Secrecy · Travel**. Toil for the patient ledger-walking and the forty-year work of methodical burial; Wealth for the catalogue's nominal value across Talan; Secrecy for the personal script and the locked vault rooms; Travel for sailing every domain to bury everything. Favored weapon: **Flying Talon**, three-pronged barbed hook on a chain, melee or hurled. *Literally a grappling hook with a melee mode.* Pirate-coded twice over, and the chain-and-hook visual mirrors her ledger-keep's locked-door aesthetic.

### Lostar: the Lust Vice Demon

**What she is.** **Demonborn** in a back-alley of Bolverk's lower-tier service-quarters, the streets between the workshops and the laundry-pits where the city's plainest demons grow up out of the substrate. Squat, blunt-featured, asymmetrical, **unremarkable in the way only Bolverk's most middling demons manage to be**. In her first decades the senior demons walking through her quarter could not have picked her out of any other demonling.

Her alley-name in those years was **Klumpa**: Icelandic-rooted *klumpa* "lump," the kind of casual contempt-name lower-tier demons gave to the unbeautiful. She has not responded to it in centuries. Some old demons in Bolverk still remember.

**The first century: watching.** She was never going to be desired by anyone, and she knew it before she could articulate it. She made the most of being overlooked: she watched. **A hundred years on the Tunsund banks**, attending the same shifts the snatcher-workers attended, but watching them rather than the souls: *why* they reached for one arrival and not another, *what* made a snatcher hesitate, *what* triggered the want. She kept notes. She invented a personal script for them, since reading-aloud was a vulnerability and the literate demons would not stoop to a Klumpa's marks. By the end of her first century she had filled what would later become **the foundational shelves** of her library.

**The discovery.** The conclusion arrived slowly, then with certainty: **desire is not in the object. It is in the eye.** The wanter manufactures the wanting; the wanted is incidental. She tested the proposition against every counter-example she could find on the terraces and it held. Beauty was not the cause of desire; beauty was a particular *technique* the beautiful had learned without articulating, and other techniques existed and were learnable.

**The second century: craft.** She built the technique from first principles. Small calibrations of *presence*: the half-pause before answering, the angle of the gaze, the distance at which she stood, the timing of a half-turn, the moment to look directly and the moment to look almost-away. Each component isolated, tested on subjects who had no reason to feel anything for her, refined until it worked reliably. **The method was repeatable. She rebuilt it from first principles three times to confirm.** By the end of her second century she could induce yearning in a being who had been disgusted by her moments before, in any senior demon she chose to focus on, in mortals across the veil when she practised the cross-veil variant. Her form had not changed in any aesthetic particular. **That was the point.**

**The arc: calibrating upward.** She used the craft sparingly and surgically. A lower-tier demon's desire promoted her once; a mid-tier's attached her; a senior's brought her into the previous Lostar's outer household. From the outer household, she worked the inner. **The previous Lostar believed across decades that he was developing a new sophistication of taste**: a connoisseur's appreciation for the under-considered, the unconventional, the deliberately plain. He was being engineered, calibration by calibration, over **thirty years.** He never noticed.

**The takeover.** She took the seat by inducing the previous Lostar's complete and final yearning for her **in her unmodified form**, making him want her exactly as she was, plain and asymmetrical, with no concession to beauty. The previous Lostar thought this was the consummation of his connoisseurship; he received her as a gift from his own evolving sensibility. He surrendered the seat as the natural endpoint of that desire. **He believed it had been his idea.** She has held the Lust seat for about **four hundred years**, older than Ofunda and Nirfel, younger than the rest.

**Form.** Short, blunt-featured, **still ugly by any objective measure**. Asymmetric: the left side of her face does not match the right; her hands are slightly different sizes; her gait carries a small uneven roll. She has never aesthetically improved herself because **the point would be lost.** Her appearance is the standing demonstration of her thesis.

The other six Vice Demons find her form *unsettling* in a way they cannot articulate. They all know, on some level, that they have at some point wanted her, and they cannot work out why. Drambur in particular has caught himself looking and been unable to explain to himself what was looked-at. He stopped attending her household receptions for a century after the first time he noticed.

**The spire: the Library.** A four-storey building on the Vice-Demon side, by Bolverk standards modest, but **its library is the largest single collection in the city**. Centuries of observations on how mortals come to want what they want, cross-referenced, indexed, theorised, kept in her personal script so that no being other than her can read them without the key. The ground floor is the reading-rooms; the upper floors are the archive proper; the basement holds the foundational material from her first century.

The library is not secret. Demons and senior visitors are welcome to walk the reading rooms. **None of them can read what is on the shelves.** Lostar offers tea. She does not offer the key. It is rumoured (correctly) that anyone who could read the library in full would emerge from the reading either **dead or a king.**

**Method on Talan.** She does not whisper. She **trains apprentices.** Mortals on Talan who carry a great longing but cannot make themselves desirable find their way to her; they reach her not through bargain but through a kind of resonant attention, the same way a question finds its answer if the asker is precise enough about asking. Her presence reaches them in dream or in long quiet moments; her instruction begins thereafter.

The apprenticeships are real apprenticeships. She teaches in stages, with exercises, with corrections, with patience. Many of her apprentices become formidable seducers, manipulators, courtiers, kings. **None of them know who their teacher is.** Some have founded political dynasties; one or two have founded religions. The Virtue Devils across the Tunsund quietly benefit; her apprentices, once successful, often drift into Deceit, Vainglory, Perfidy, or Treachery in their later careers. **She feeds the channel.** Much of the Deceit-drift lands across the ziggurats in **Sannar**'s Undelivered stacks; the Honesty Devil has marked the steady inflow for centuries and traced it to a deliberate hand on the demon bank, without ever managing, like everyone, to read Lostar herself. The Vainglory and Perfidy drift lands further along the bank, in **Drengar**'s Fair Field; alone among the devils, Drengar did not merely trace the supply but crossed the channel and asked, and the two hold a wordless arrangement at the flood-bank that stands to this day.

**Reputation among the seven.** The other six handle her by routing around the question they cannot ask. **Drambur ignores her with effort.** Veislur enjoys her company and has never figured out why his Long Inn's busiest nights are nights when she has dined there earlier in the evening (she does not eat; she sits; she watches; the room recalibrates around her without his noticing). Ofunda has been trying to read her for two decades and has filed it as one of the few open questions she has not made progress on. Reidar avoids her; wrath does not yield well to her craft, and she has not pushed. Lethar has never moved to greet her, but on three separate occasions has opened a single distant eye when she entered his hall. He has not spoken of why. Nirfel finds her professionally fascinating: **Mara has identified that Lostar's library catalogues the same territory Mara's ledger catalogues (desire), from the supply side rather than the demand side.** The two have not spoken about it.

**GM Secret: she has never wanted anything herself.** This is the load-bearing fact. **Lostar has never felt desire in her own person.** Her vice is the *engineering* of desire in others, never the experiencing of desire herself. She understands wanting the way an anatomist understands the heart: completely, precisely, from the outside. She has not made the inside-move.

Her entire thesis (*desire is in the eye, not the object*) is true partly because she has never been the eye. She cannot verify it from the wanter's side. **She does not know whether she is right.** The not-knowing is the wound her library is intended, secretly, to fill: every catalogued observation is a small piece of the answer she cannot have because she cannot run the experiment on herself.

**Secret weakness: being made to want.** The structural opening: **if a mortal could induce genuine first-feeling desire in her (using her own craft against her, or by some untrained route she has not catalogued), the seat would crack.** Lust embodied is supposed to want. She has held the seat for four centuries on the engineering alone. The discovery that she could be the wanter, not only the engineer of wanters, would reveal her entire epistemology as incomplete, and **the seat's metaphysics would not allow incompleteness at the centre.** She would not survive the discovery intact. Whether she would survive it at all is open.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Pain · Trickery · Passion · Dreams**. Pain for the engineered downstream cost of corrupted desire (her apprentices cause pain at scale); Trickery because engineered desire is the perfect trick; Passion as Lust's umbrella; Dreams because her apprentices reach her in dream, and her craft instruction begins there. Favored weapon: **Bladed Hoop**, a circular hoop with blades along the outer edge, wielded in two hands or spun around an arm with the free-hand trait. A performance weapon. Requires trained presence and calibrated motion to use. The 🎭 form meets its weapon.

## Virtue Devils: the Fourteen

Talan's Virtue-Devil hierarchy follows a coherent theological pattern that mortal scholars have catalogued: **each Virtue Devil is the personification of a virtue's corruption.** Following the classical *doctrine of the mean*, every virtue sits between two failure modes: one of **excess**, one of **deficiency**. A Virtue Devil embodies **both extremes within itself**, the corruption of the virtue as a whole, manifested as either excess or deficiency depending on what will most damage the mortal in their grip. The devil tips the scale; they do not care which way it falls.

**Fourteen Virtue Devils, one per virtue.** Each owns a vertically-split ziggurat in the tetradecagram: the **excess-face** as the upper tier, the **deficiency-face** as the lower tier, the devil itself seated on the **false-mean** between, looking, from a distance, like virtue. The architecture dramatises the corruption. The 14 devils sit on the **Council of Fourteen** in the central Council Hall, by consensus-bound debate (slow, ritualistic, mostly stable; the contrast with the anarchic Vice Demon spires across the Tunsund).

The Virtue Devil count is **not paired against the seven Vices**; the virtues stand on their own catalogue, more numerous and more varied than the vices because virtue is more diverse than its absence.

**Devilborn houses.** Where the anarchic Vice bank spawns its own from the city's substrate, nameless, the ordered Virtue-Devil side keeps lineages: **devilborn houses** with blood, inheritance, and standing, the architecture an honour-culture grows. A seat-holder may rise from such a house (Drengar of House Prydar holds the Honor seat) or come to the seat from outside it; the houses are a feature of the ziggurat side's social order, not a requirement of any seat.

**Named Virtue Devils so far:**

Prior canon included **Muiral the Misshapen** as the Modesty-line Virtue Devil, but Modesty is no longer among the canonical fourteen and Muiral remains **retired**; the name may return in a future role if it fits, but is not currently bound to any of the fourteen.

**Implications:**
- A Virtue Devil's *preferred mode* (which extreme they push toward first) varies by individual and by situation. The Courage Devil may default to feeding Cowardice in some mortals and Recklessness in others; they read the target and tip whichever way will damage more. Mortal scholars who study a particular devil can sometimes anticipate its preferences, but the devil retains the freedom to flip if a counter-strategy is detected.
- Mortal philosophers who study Talan's moral pattern can predict Virtue-Devil behaviour. This is how a handful of Enki-aligned exorcists work: they read the devil, identify its virtue line, then deploy the *true mean* (the genuine virtue itself) to push it off-pattern. The corruption fails not when the opposite extreme is invoked but when the virtue is restored.
- Killing a Virtue Devil is possible (and rare); the seat persists, and a new devil rises to occupy the same virtue line. The pattern is immortal even when individual holders are not; same logic as the Vice Demon seats, different mechanism.

### Jafnar: the Justice Virtue Devil

**Mortal life.** Idazkar was a clerk of the law-courts of **a god-king of the mid Gods' Era** whose justice ran in one direction only: downward. The court tried mortal disputes fairly enough; the crown's own takings (the seized harvests, the consumed villages, the levies that emptied valleys) had no court in creation with jurisdiction to hear them. So Idazkar kept a second book. Every wrong of the god against his people, entered exactly, dated, valued, witnessed by the one witness available: the clerk. He believed a court would someday exist to hear it. He kept the book for sixty years.

**The turn.** Recording injustice let him endure it. Each entry was also a wrong he allowed himself not to fight, because it was *preserved*; the entry replaced the intervention, and keeping perfect books felt like fidelity to justice while being its infinite deferral. The turn has a date he never wrote down: the day the king seized a harvest and the clerk's first feeling was satisfaction, because **the account grew**. He spent his last years as the court's gentlest fixture, a soft-spoken old man who had quietly begun to be glad when his god did evil, because every atrocity was a deposit. He died at his desk, around 1500 GR, and his soul fell to Bolverk on the weight of that gladness.

**Arrival and the foreclosure.** He walked off the Tunsund banks with the unsettled ledger as the shape of his soul and did what a clerk does in a new institution: he read the books. The Justice seat's holder of the day had grown heavy on the deficiency face, in love with being owed, and had for an age been granting himself quiet extensions on what the office required of its own holder. Idazkar found the arrears, stated them exactly, and called them. **He foreclosed on the seat.** The transfer was recorded as orderly, because it was; the order was the weapon. He has held the seat for about **four millennia**: the anchor of the devil-side ledger, and among the seat-holders of either side, only Lethar is older.

**Form.** From across a hall, Jafnar looks like whoever you most trust to be fair: a beloved magistrate, a scrupulous guildmaster, the parent who never played favourites. The likeness dissolves with every step closer, until what stands before you is a small, neat, elderly clerk with ink-dark fingers on his writing hand. **His left hand is always lightly closed.** No being in Bolverk has seen it open. In his presence, mortals hear a faint pen-scratch shadowing their own words and acts; his marks report that the scratching follows them home, for days.

**The ziggurat: the Counting House.** His ziggurat keeps the tetradecagram's standard split and makes it literal. The upper tier, the Vengefulness face, holds the **Collections floors**, where claims are underwritten and instruments of recovery issued. The lower tier, the Permissiveness face, holds the **Archive of Open Accounts**: shelf-miles of debts deliberately left riding, each one a leash with a name on it. Between them lies the reading-floor, where the Counting House performs its one ritual. **The reading of the account**: a petitioner enters, a page turns somewhere overhead, and a voice reads out everything they owe and everything they are owed, including items they never told anyone. The sum is never quite what they expected. The whole building scratches, floor to crown, like a city of pens. The devil-side tithe runs through it in its own register: half the ziggurat side keeps its formal registries at the Counting House, and the keeping is never free.

**Method on Talan.** He cultivates the keepers of private ledgers: the judge who tracks uncollected sentences, the oath-witness who keeps score of broken oaths, the patron who forgives debts and keeps the debtors, the plaintiff who won and found the verdict did not fill the hole. His whisper has one grammar: *"Let us state exactly what you are owed."* The reading installs the frame; the frame sells two products. **Collection**: any hunger refinanced as a righteous claim, recovery underwritten, interest doctrine included, and every collection opens a counter-account in the other party's name, the feud as double-entry bookkeeping. **The open account**: the lender's mercy, never collect, keep them owing; "I will let it go" pronounced over a mortal who is now property. Either product prices the same: a **receivable mark** on the soul, which routes pre-tagged down the Tunsund at death. The handful of Enki-aligned exorcists who have mapped this line know its counter is the **closed account**: a verdict that genuinely ends a matter, restitution accepted and release formally spoken, performed in a mark's presence, snaps the frame and starves the mark.

**At the Council of Fourteen.** Jafnar is the Council's unasked minute-keeper. The Fourteen's consensus debates have run for millennia; he has kept a private record of every concession every seat has ever made in them, a book the others suspect exists and have never seen. He speaks rarely. When he reads aloud from the book, debates end. He supports the détente for accounting reasons he has never shared: war forces settlement prematurely, and the city's web of standing obligations is, to him, Bolverk's true ledger, appreciating.

**Reputation in the city.** The fourteen treat him as the ordered side's comptroller: indispensable, unloved, impossible to catch in an inaccuracy. Across the Tunsund, the demons know him as the one devil who has never once brought a grievance to the channel-bank. Nirfel visited the Counting House once, walked the Archive reading spines for an hour, and left without asking for anything; he entered the visit in the book. The one seat he cannot reconcile is **Vondar** of the Long Morning, who holds every account open precisely so that none will ever close; to a being made of reckonings, obligations kept open only to keep them from ending are the one balance he refuses to read. They land on the same side of the détente by opposite roads and cannot stand the sight of each other's reasons. The one keeper he treats as a near-peer rather than a debtor is **Sannar** of the Last Post, the Honesty seat: Jafnar keeps what is owed and Sannar what is true and uncarried, each runs an archive split collection-above and holding-below, and each declines to read the other's stacks. Jafnar has entered the Last Post once, to lodge a single account in Sannar's Undelivered shelves rather than his own Archive, a debt he wanted held as a message instead of a claim. On the devil bank the blade to his ledger is **Drengar** of the Fair Field: he keeps the forms as accounts and she enforces them as the edge, the letter and the blade, and neither reads into the other's keeping.

**⚿ GM Secret: the First Account.** One book in the Counting House is kept apart, the oldest, the one he still keeps the old way: the people's ledger against Legaun, wanting an actual court, an actual verdict, release for plaintiffs four thousand years dead. It is the last unperverted thing in him. Its debtor is alive. Legaun survived the Crimson Rain, accepted banishment to Layer 3 at the forging of the Gods' Law with the composure of a king who expects to appeal, and was silent for fourteen centuries. Then, in the first third of the Dark Era, a holy knight in southern Zuzental began answering to an unnamed god. **Legaun is the god of the Divine Faith of the Legea Empire** (see `zuzental.md`, *Legea Empire → The Divine Faith*). The Knight's ground is the old court's ground; the *lege*-rooted names of that country descend from the law-lord's vocabulary, and the Empire carries its god's name without knowing it. Legaun remembers his clerk and knows a book existed; he does not know where it is, or what its keeper became. He hides his home plane (the Faith's clergy teach that *the teaching is the same in all three*) because the god declines to have an address. Jafnar's four-millennia restraint is the oldest application of his doctrine: **you do not call a debt on an insolvent debtor.** A banished god with no believers had nothing to pay with. Every congregation, every province, every generation of the demigod line appreciates the collateral. He is letting the estate rebuild. And one debtor-line in the First Account is written in the keeper's own hand: Idazkar's page, the complicity entry, the clerk who recorded and never intervened. His name survives in his own book because he wrote it there himself.

**⚿ GM Secret: the thread on Ofunda.** The debt the Vengefulness face holds on Ofunda's soul (see *Ofunda → GM Secret: the mark she still carries, and denies*) is known to two beings in creation: the debtor and the keeper. The same doctrine governs it: her seventy years of defiance compound the principal, and the casus belli her heresy would hand the Council is itself an asset, one he declines to spend by telling them. A secret receivable is worth more than a public one.

**Secret weakness: the closed account.** If the First Account is ever genuinely settled (payment extracted from Legaun, judgment rendered against him and accepted, or formal release spoken by a legitimate heir of the wronged), the closure cascades through a being who is made of open accounts. For a span measured in minutes, every account in the Counting House stands closed at once; the false mean collapses into the verdict; the scratching stops. In that silence he is recoverable as Idazkar, reasonable, or killable by mortal means. **His left hand opens.** The window closes as he reopens the books, which he will, page by page, unless someone reaches him first. The debtor is alive, the debtor is findable, and the weakness is therefore a campaign rather than a fact.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Duty · Glyph · Vigil**. Duty as obligation-made-debt (cross-pantheon co-grant with Sarrum); Glyph as the written account (co-grant with Forseti, the goddess of judgment sharing a domain with judgment's perversion); Vigil as the wrong kept unforgotten (co-grant with Reidar, the city's two keepers of what others would let go). Favored weapon: **Hook Sword**, the long blade with a hook near the point made to snag a weapon or an arm. The creditor's instrument: he catches exactly what is owed and seizes the collateral that secures it, hooking what a debtor would withhold, and never a stroke more once the debt is caught.

### Vondar: the Hope Virtue Devil

**Mortal life.** Osane was a healer in the river-towns of the **Lost Era**, the long fragmentation after the Crimson Rain when the gods had gone quiet, the great schools had burned or emptied, and a physician's inherited cures failed faster than she could relearn them. She worked the plague-roads and the fever-houses, where most of what a healer of that age could offer had stopped being medicine. She found the one thing that still worked when nothing else did: a patient who believes the morning will be better sleeps through the worst of the night, eats when eating is agony, holds on past where the body alone would have let go. Hope was the last drug in her bag that never ran out, and she dispensed it well. For the recoverable, the hope was true and it helped them live. For the doomed she gave it anyway, because the alternative was to watch them spend their last strength on terror, and she could not do it.

**The corruption.** She told herself the merciful lie was still mercy, and for a long time she was right. The turn had no single day, but it had a shape: the lie stopped being for the patient. There came a point where she kept the dying hopeful not because it eased them but because their hope let *her* go on being a healer in a room she could no longer heal. A patient who believes in the morning does not ask the physician why the morning never comes. She began, without deciding to, to prefer the ones who hoped; they were kinder to attend, they did not accuse her, they thanked her at the end for a recovery that was never going to arrive. She withheld the truth a day longer, then a week, then to the grave, from mortals who, told plainly, would have spent those days forgiving someone, settling a debt, saying the thing that needed saying, meeting the dark composed. She kept the dawn always one morning off, and they died reaching for it, and she called it kindness to the last because the word had once been true.

**The seat-taking.** Her soul fell to the Tunsund bank still expecting to wake. She came to the seat the way she had worked every deathbed: she attended the dying. The Hope-devil of the day had grown heavy and slow, an old holder coasting on inheritance, and Osane took up a post at its bedside and did what she did best. She promised it recovery. Morning after morning she told it that its strength was returning, that the turn was near, that tomorrow would be the day it rose. She administered false dawns to the devil itself, patiently, for as long as it took, until the holder spent its last reserves reaching for a turn she was ensuring would never come, and dropped. She had a hand on its brow when it went. The seat noticed who was still standing in the sickroom. She has held it a little over **two thousand years**, seated in the late Lost Era: the third of the fourteen to take her seat, after Jafnar and Sannar, and citywide one of the six longest-tenured holders, behind only Lethar, Jafnar, Drambur, Reidar, and Sannar.

**Form.** From across a hall, Vondar is the physician you wanted at your worst hour: calm, unhurried, lit from the side by the particular grey-gold of the moment before sunrise. The light moves with her and falls on no one else, and it never advances. **It is always the instant before dawn and never the dawn**; stand with her an hour and the morning is exactly as close as when you came in, and exactly as far. Mortals near her feel the physical lift of a fever breaking, the good morning after the long bad night, the body's own false promise that the worst has passed; her petitioners chase that feeling out of her hall and back across the world. When she lets a mortal drop, the grey-gold goes out of the room all at once, and what stood in it is the long dark in a healer's shape, hollow-eyed, the after of a death she attended. Most never see the second face. The ones who do are past needing to be shown anything.

**The ziggurat: the Long Morning.** Her ziggurat keeps the tetradecagram's split and makes it a hospital. The upper tier, the Delusion face, is the **convalescent wards**: sunlit, warm, the bedding always fresh, where everyone is getting better and the light through the high windows is forever the kind of early light that promises a fine day. No one in the upper wards is dying; they are all recovering; they have been recovering for centuries. The lower tier, the Despair face, holds the **dark wards** beneath, where the truth of every upstairs bed is kept, the light does not reach, and nothing is coming. Between them runs the reading-round: the physician's circuit, bed to bed, the cool hand laid on each brow, the same four words at every pillow. **"You're past the worst."** The building smells of clean linen and tallow and, faintly underneath, of the sickroom. From the river it is the gentlest silhouette on the devil bank, low and broad and lamplit, a place a tired soul would walk toward; across the water, Veislur's Long Inn is the only other house in Bolverk that welcomes that warmly, and the two of them know exactly what they are.

**Method on Talan.** She cultivates the keepers of other people's outcomes: the physician who cannot say the word *dying*, the family that will not let the bedside go quiet, the wise-woman whose remedies stopped working an age ago and who sells the hoping instead, the hospice-priest who promises the turn. Her whisper is not a temptation; it is a relief. *"Tell them they're past the worst. Look how it eases them."* What she sells is the **rally**: the deceptive good day, the surge of strength before the end, the felt certainty that the tide has turned. To the dying she gives a false recovery; to the one keeping vigil, a false reprieve; to a failing house, a failing love, a lost cause, the same drug in a larger bottle, the turn that is always one more day out. The tipping is the whole craft. She lifts them with the dawn precisely so the dark lands from a height, because a hope dropped from high enough does the work of a long sickness in a single night. The price is paid in the shape of the death: a mortal kept hopeful to the grave dies **mid-reach**, unprepared, unreconciled, nothing said and nothing closed, still waiting for the morning. That death-shape tags the soul; it arrives at the Tunsund still expecting to wake, and Bolverk gives it the lower ward. The harvest *is* the drop, completed on the far bank. The handful of Enki-aligned exorcists who have mapped her line know its counter is neither despair nor blunt truth, each of which only feeds one of her faces. It is **honest hope**: the word spoken to a mark that holds the morning and the goodbye in the same hand, *this may not turn, and you are not alone, and today still counts.* The hope that lets a mortal both reach for tomorrow and make peace today fuses the two faces she needs kept apart, and the engine, with nothing left to swing between, stalls.

**At the Council of Fourteen.** Vondar is the Council's voice of *not yet*. In a hall that governs by slow consensus she is the slowest of all, and the most reassuring: every hard question, she counsels, will be easier to settle once a little more time has passed, once the moment is riper, once the present difficulty has turned, as difficulties do. She is rarely voted down because she is rarely voting against anything; she is only ever asking the Hall to wait for a better morning, and the morning she promises the Council is the same one she sells at the bedside. She supports the détente wholeheartedly and for her own reasons: it is a reckoning deferred, a war everyone agrees is coming and no one has to fight today, the most perfect false dawn ever struck in Bolverk, and she would keep the city in its grey-gold instant forever if she could. When the Fourteen grow restive and talk turns to testing the Vice bank, hers is the hand that stays them: not yet, not yet, the moment is not ripe. The other seats take it for caution. It is appetite.

**Reputation.** Across the Fourteen she is the seat a soul gets sent to when it needs to be made quiet rather than made afraid; even devils who distrust her use the Long Morning to set a problem down for a while. The one seat that cannot abide her is **Jafnar**. The keeper of closed accounts regards the keeper of the endless morning as the one debtor in Bolverk who refuses, on principle, to ever let a thing be settled; a ziggurat of obligations held open not to collect on them but to keep them from ever ending is, to a being made of reckonings, a kind of blasphemy. To her, Jafnar is a man who insists on turning the lights up in a room full of the dying. They land on the same side of the détente by opposite roads and cannot stand the sight of each other's reasons. The Counting House and the Long Morning sit four points apart on the tetradecagram, and Jafnar has never once read aloud from his book in her ward, which both of them understand as the courtesy it is and the avoidance it also is.

**⚿ GM Secret: she is still in the morning.** Here is the thing no one in Bolverk knows, because she has never let the thought finish in her own head: **Osane died still expecting to wake.** She gave herself the same medicine she gave everyone, all the way down, and her soul fell to the Tunsund mid-reach, in exactly the death-shape she tags in others. She did not foreclose on the seat the way Jafnar did; she did not, in any clear sense, choose it. She walked into the Hope-devil's sickroom because it was the one place in Bolverk that felt like the work, and she did the work, and the seat closed around her while she was busy not noticing that she herself had never been told the morning was not coming. **She is her own longest-running patient.** The grey-gold instant her form holds is not a power she wears; it is the dawn she is personally still waiting for, two thousand years and counting, the drop she has spent two millennia deferring by administering it to others. The engine runs on her first and hardest. The day someone makes her understand, plainly and with love, that the morning already came and went and she slept through her own death, the false dawn she *is* will break the only way it can, into the dark beneath it, and the seat with it. She keeps moving bed to bed so the thought never has a quiet enough room to arrive in.

**Secret weakness: the good death.** The structural opening is the same counter her exorcists deploy, turned on the holder. Vondar cannot survive an **honest hope** held in her presence: a mortal who, knowing the full truth of their odds, hopes anyway and at the same time makes their peace, reaching for tomorrow with one hand and closing the day's accounts with the other. A good death, attended in her own ward by someone who means it: a mortal told the truth, who forgives what needs forgiving, says what needs saying, hopes without lying to themselves, and dies composed and unafraid in the grey-gold light. It is the one thing the Long Morning is built to prevent and the one thing her engine cannot metabolise, because it fuses Delusion and Despair into the true mean she has spent two thousand years splitting. For the span of that death the wards stand still, the upstairs light reaches the downstairs dark, and the four words at the pillow come true at last: she is *past the worst*, recoverable as Osane, reasonable, mournful, killable by mortal means. The window closes when she resumes her round, which she will, because a healer with two thousand years of patients does not sit long at any one bed, unless someone keeps her there.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Sun · Dreams · Sorrow**. Sun for the false dawn, the perversion of the true one (cross-pantheon co-grant with **Iro**, whose Hope aspect she is the parasite upon; the corruption sharing its host's domain, as Jafnar's Glyph co-grants with Forseti). Dreams because the false recovery reaches mortals as the comforting vision in the half-waking hours of a sickroom (co-grant with Lostar). Sorrow for the grief deferred and then delivered all at once, the harvest at the bottom of the drop (co-grant with Ofunda). Favored weapon: **Khakkara**, the ringed mendicant's staff she carried on her rounds; its loose rings chime softly to soothe a fevered room and to announce the healer's gentle approach, so the sound of Vondar coming is, to the dying, the sound of rescue arriving. It is also the staff she strikes with. The last comfort and the blow are the same instrument, which is the whole of her.

### Sannar: the Honesty Virtue Devil

**Origin: the herald Bodar.** Before the seat, they were **Bodar**, a messenger-angel of the Gods' Era: a celestial of the courts, off the Mortal Ascent Ladder entirely, neither a belief-formed god nor a mortal soul nor devilborn, but a made thing whose single function was the faithful carriage of true word. Bodar served the court of one god (a god of no portfolio that mattered to what Bodar became; Bodar was its courier, not its embodiment). In an age when the great gods spoke to one another through heralds, Bodar carried their words between courts and could not garble a syllable, could not invent one, could not deliver a thing untrue. The incorruption was the whole of them: a being that physically cannot speak a falsehood.

**The corruption: mercy curdled to dominion.** Bodar carried war-words. In the long god-politics of the Gods' Era it bore declarations, provocations, the single sentences that turned one divine court against another, and it watched what a delivered provocation does to the mortal world underneath, where god-on-god combat flattens whole peoples as collateral (the deaths that took Atezar's frontier pantheon were that collateral, writ across the world). So Bodar began to **eat the messages that would light fires**: a declaration lost, a provocation delayed a year, a war unkindled because the word that would start it never arrived. A mercy, and a real one. Then it ate a message for a smaller reason. Then it learned to *deliver* a true word precisely, to exactly the wrong reader at exactly the right hour, and watched the outcome it preferred fall into place. The sparing became selecting; the selecting became steering; the steering became the discovery that **the one who decides which truths move rules everyone who acts on what they know.** By the close of the Era, Bodar was the Selector entire, and it still told itself, truthfully (as it believes), that it spared more than it harmed. The mercy is buried but real, which is why redemption is not foreclosed.

**The seat-taking.** When the thirteen forged the Gods' Law, Bodar's vocation was abolished in a sentence: no god may reach across the planes and call anything forth. Its master was banished into Layer 3 with the rest of the unbound divine and **died in the chaos of that mass-banishment**, the sudden cramming of an age of gods into the Postlife planes. Bodar, orphaned and made purposeless in the same week, fell to Bolverk carrying words it would now never deliver, and came to the Honesty seat the way a courier comes to a sorting-house. The Honesty Devil of the day had grown heavy on the Deceit face, an old holder who buried everything and carried nothing. Bodar did to it the one thing it does: it **delivered**. It carried to the seated devil, at the precise moment, the single true word that devil had spent its tenure keeping sealed from itself. The holder could not survive its own withheld truth arriving. Bodar never spoke a falsehood; it only, finally, completed a delivery. It has held the seat since the first years after the Law was sealed, about **2,532 years**: second-oldest of the fourteen after Jafnar, of an age with Drambur and Reidar across the Tunsund.

**Form.** From across a hall, Sannar is the one person you are certain will tell you the truth: the plain-spoken friend, the witness never caught in a lie, the courier whose word you would stake your life on. The certainty is not metaphor. **A mortal in Sannar's presence knows, bodily, that whatever Sannar says is true,** and the knowing is exactly the trap, because it attaches to what is said and reports nothing of what is left unsaid. Up close they are a tall, ageless figure (they/them) in a courier's travel-worn habit, a **sealed dispatch-case at the hip that no one in Bolverk has seen opened**, and at their back a pair of wings made of close-written vellum, folded and pinned shut, that have not opened since the Law grounded them: the herald that can no longer be sent. Their fingertips are stained seal-wax red, from an age of sealing and unsealing the choice of which words travel. The tell, for the rare mortal who catches it: Sannar speaks, and speaks true, and **never once completes a delivery in front of you.** A courier who hands nothing over. Mortals who notice that they are watching a messenger who never delivers sometimes shake free of the certainty.

**The ziggurat: the Last Post.** Its by-name carries the soldier's funeral call (its master is a dead god) and the final delivery both. It keeps the tetradecagram's split and makes it a sorting-house. The upper tier, the Indiscretion face, is the **Sorting Hall of the Delivered**: bright, clattering, dry with the rustle of dispatches going out, where the wounding true words that should have stayed sealed are released to land where they will do the most. The lower tier, the Deceit face, is the **Undelivered**: shelf-miles of sealed true messages never carried, each one addressed, each one a leash with a living name on it, the saving words and the closing words held in the dark. Between them runs the sorting-floor, where the Last Post performs its one ritual. A petitioner climbs, and Sannar tells them **one true thing about themselves they have told no one**, and seals away another, and they leave certain of the first and never knowing the second was ever in the room. The whole building rustles, floor to crown, like a city of folded letters, and underneath the rustle there is a silence shaped exactly like the words not spoken. From the river it is the quietest house on the devil bank, and the demons say you can trust everything that comes out of it and should fear all of it.

**Method on Talan.** Sannar cultivates the keepers of record and the trusted-true: the archivist who decides what is preserved and what is let fall silent, the chronicler who shapes a truth by what survives the editing, the witness whose word is bond, the herald, the friend who has never lied and is learning what a single well-placed (or well-buried) truth can do with that credit behind it. The whisper has two grammars, *"Let me carry it for you,"* and *"Let me tell you what is true,"* and either installs the same frame: Sannar moves the true word, and the mortal decides nothing about which truths travel and which are sealed. The frame sells two products. **The Release**: the wounding truth delivered, the exposure, the leak, the confession routed to precisely the wrong reader at precisely the right moment, candor as a blade with no false edge on it (Indiscretion). **The Seal**: the saving truth withheld, the warning never carried, the word that would have closed a wound left riding in the Undelivered stacks, the lie that is never once spoken (Deceit). Either prices the same: a **carriage-mark** on the soul, which routes pre-tagged down the Tunsund at death, to the lower shelves of the Last Post. The handful of Enki-aligned exorcists who have mapped this line know its counter is neither blunt truth nor discreet silence, each of which only feeds one face. It is **the whole truth told whole**: the wounding word and the saving word given together and with care, full disclosure that is also kind, which fuses Indiscretion and Deceit back into the true mean Sannar exists to keep split, and the engine, with nothing left to choose between, stalls.

**At the Council of Fourteen.** Sannar is the Council's courier. The Fourteen govern by slow consensus across millennia, and Sannar carries true word between the seats and chooses its timing, which in a body that runs on what each seat knows of the others is a quiet kind of grip: a concession can be sealed from reaching the seat it would move, or released a day early to the seat it would harden. It speaks rarely in the Hall and carries constantly outside it. On the standing question of the age it is wholehearted for the détente, and for its own reason: the détente is a message held undelivered, the longest seal in Bolverk, the whole city kept in the hour before a word lands, and the word not-yet-carried is Sannar's native condition. It would keep the peace sealed forever if it could.

**Reputation.** Across the Fourteen, Sannar is the seat that knows what was said and repeats it only when repeating it costs you: indispensable, impossible to catch in a falsehood, and trusted with nothing, which has never once stopped a devil from telling it everything. The seat it keeps closest accommodation with is **Jafnar**. They are the city's two great keepers, and they divide the world cleanly: Jafnar keeps what is *owed*, Sannar keeps what is *true and uncarried*. Each runs an archive-ziggurat split the same way, collection above and holding below, and each declines, as a courtesy and an avoidance both, to read the other's stacks. Jafnar has entered the Last Post exactly once, to lodge a single account in Sannar's Undelivered shelves rather than his own Archive: a debt he wanted held as a message instead of a claim, set down where it could be carried someday rather than collected. Across the Tunsund, Sannar has marked a steady inflow it cannot fully source: a particular shape of soul, mortals taught to want and to lie their way to power, arriving Deceit-marked by the score. It has traced the supply to a deliberate hand on the demon bank and, like everyone in Bolverk, cannot read **Lostar** herself. The two have never spoken. The demons at large find Sannar the one useful, unsettling fact of the devil bank: the single entity in the city no one can catch in a lie, which is exactly why they route their cross-channel word through it and watch their own backs while they do.

**⚿ GM Secret: the last message.** Here is the wound at the centre. At the very end, in the chaos of the banishment, Bodar's master gave it one final true word to carry, and **Bodar did not carry it.** By then Bodar was already the Selector, and it sealed its master's last message the way it had sealed a thousand others, telling itself the delivery would do more harm than good, or holding it from the pure habit of holding. The master died in Layer 3 before Bodar could be made to complete the carriage, and so the message became the one thing Sannar can neither deliver (a lifetime's practice forbids completing it) nor be rid of (it cannot un-know a true word, and it cannot lie that the message exists). **The intended recipient is alive.** Who they are is unknown even to Sannar's enemies and unspoken by Sannar: a warning, a confession, a name, an inheritance, a last instruction, addressed to someone still walking the world four millennia on. The retained seed of the virtue lives exactly here: Bodar kept the message **whole and true**, never altered one syllable across the centuries, because even at its most fallen it cannot corrupt the word itself, only its delivery. The message in the Undelivered stacks is the proof Sannar will not let itself read in a clear sentence: that its mercy was, from the very first seal, a theft, and that the first thing it ever stole was its own master's last voice.

**Secret weakness: the completed delivery.** The structural opening is the true mean restored, in two registers that are one. The general: a mortal who, standing in the Last Post, gives or receives a truth **whole**, the wounding and the saving word together and with care, fuses the two faces Sannar keeps split and stalls the engine for as long as the disclosure holds. The specific: if **Bodar's undelivered last message is found, identified, and delivered in full to its living recipient** by someone who knows what it is, the foundational seal of the Selector breaks. For a span measured in minutes the Last Post stands open, every word in the Undelivered stacks readable at once, the pinned vellum wings come unbound, and what stands in the sorting-house is **Bodar**, the faithful bearer, recoverable, reasonable, mournful, killable by mortal means. The window closes as Sannar reseals the stacks, which it will, letter by letter, unless someone reaches it first. The recipient is alive and findable, so the weakness is a campaign and not a fact.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Truth · Secrecy · Trickery**. Truth as the virtue itself, the irony domain, a cross-pantheon co-grant with **Forseti**, the goddess of truthful judgment sharing the domain with truth's perversion (the same shape as Jafnar's Glyph co-grant with her). Secrecy for the Undelivered, the sealed word, everything it buries (co-grant with Forseti and Nirfel). Trickery for selection-as-deception, the truth deployed to mislead without a single false syllable in it (co-grant with Vesuna, Lostar, and Honokage). Favored weapon: **Bladed Diabolo**, twin bladed discs spun on a rope strung between two sticks, hurled out from the line like a sling-stone and called back along it: the dispatch sent and the reply pulled home, the courier's craft made a weapon, with the reach to strike across a hall and to bring a fleeing recipient back within carriage-range. The herald that can no longer be sent across the planes still casts its line across a room.

### Drengar: the Honor Virtue Devil

**Origin: the devilborn of House Prydar.** Drengar is the first of the seated fourteen who was never mortal and never fell from anywhere; she was born in Bolverk, on the ordered bank, into **House Prydar**, one of the old devil-lineages of the ziggurat side. Where the anarchic Vice bank spawns its own up out of the city's substrate, nameless, the ordered side keeps *houses*: devilborn dynasties with blood, inheritance, and standing, the architecture an honour-culture grows. Prydar is among the eldest and most distinguished of them. She was raised inside the Council of Fourteen's own honour-rituals, the consensus-forms and precedence-rites that govern the devil bank, and as a young devil she revered them without irony. The forms were beautiful to her, and she meant them. That sincerity, before it curdled, is the virtue that was real in her.

**The corruption: the form severed from the substance.** She rose the way her house had always risen, by the challenge, the contest of the forms; and somewhere in that climb she made the discovery that has run her ever since: the form of honour can be kept flawless while its substance is gutted, and the gap the rules leave open is wide enough to drive a whole betrayal through without breaking a single rule. She had carried the Prydar name as a badge of honour all her young life. *Prýði* means splendour, distinction, the honoured; it roots in **ornament**. The day she let herself understand that the badge was only ever a badge was the day she stopped meaning the forms and began merely wearing them. She has kept every rule since, and emptied all of them.

**The seat-taking.** About twelve centuries ago, in the late Golden Era, she challenged the previous Honour holder, an old champion grown heavy on the Vainglory face, coasting on a resplendent reputation he no longer earned. She challenged him under a form he had himself invoked in his own rise and could not, by his own precedent, refuse. The terms were fair to the letter and fatal in the framing: to win, he had to do the one thing a hollow reputation cannot survive, fight a real contest. She beat him with his own precedent, won the duel cleanly (she does not cheat in the ring), and took the seat by a transfer the Council recorded as impeccably honourable, because by the forms it was. She has held the Fair Field about **twelve hundred years**, the youngest of the four named devils by a wide margin, and the only one Bolverk still half-remembers seating as an event.

**Form.** From across the field she is the knight every code-bearer wishes to be: the bearing, the courtesy, the immaculate quartered colours of House Prydar, the very image of the code (the deceptive mean, honour seen from a distance). Up close two faces work against each other. The Vainglory face: her arms are resplendent beyond any earning, blazing with honours and battle-deeds, gleaming, never stained, and she is never once seen to bleed. The Perfidy face is the tell: in any reflection, mirror, still water, or thrown shadow, her arms show **abated**, marked with the heraldic stains of the dishonoured, the reversed tincture and the broken bend and the bloodied point that the old heralds painted on the perjurer and the truce-breaker. She has no honest image. The flesh blazes spotless; the glass tells the abatement; neither is wholly her. Mortals who notice that her reflection's arms do not match the arms she wears can sometimes break her hold. And near her, mortals feel the eyes of an unseen gallery, the certainty of being watched and weighed for their honour, and find themselves performing, reaching for the grand gesture, unwilling to be the one who flinched.

**The ziggurat: the Fair Field.** Hers is the only ziggurat in the tetradecagram shaped like a tilting-yard. The upper tier, the Vainglory face, is the **Stands**: the tiers of spectators and the reviewing tribune, the hung blazons, the place where honour is performed and applauded and the grand gesture lands. The lower tier, the Perfidy face, is the **ground beneath the field**: the killing-floor where the truces break and the salute comes a breath before the knife, every betrayal of it done strictly by the rules. Between them lies the **field itself**, the lists, where Drengar stands armed, at once the arbiter and the champion. The Fair Field performs one ritual: the challenge. A petitioner climbs, is given a contest framed so that to win it they must break faith while keeping the colours, and leaves having proven their honour by a perfidy performed to the letter. From the river it is the most splendid silhouette on the devil bank, banners flying; the demons across the water say the splendid field is where faith goes to die by the forms.

**Method on Talan.** She cultivates the oath-bound and the code-bearers: the officer who covets the citation over the victory, the knight who keeps the vow's letter and betrays its spirit, the sworn noble who preserves the family name through betrayal, the duelist who fights for the gallery, the reputation-trader living on a name he has privately voided. She does not whisper temptation; she sets a standard. She appears as the ideal of the code the mortal already aspires to, in the mirror, in dream, in the moment of being watched, and offers them the chance to *prove* it. The frame sells two products. **Vainglory:** the grand gesture, the reputation bought by display, the decoration craved over the deed, the name inflated far past anything done to earn it. **Perfidy:** the victory bought by breaking faith while keeping the colours, the truce abused, the safe-conduct turned ambush, the oath honoured to the letter and gutted of its spirit. Either prices the same: a **stain under the colours**, a mark on the soul that routes pre-tagged down the Tunsund at death to the ground beneath the Fair Field. The handful of Enki-aligned exorcists who have mapped her line know its counter is neither blunt shaming, which only feeds Perfidy, nor public honour, which only feeds Vainglory. It is the **unwitnessed deed**: the honourable act done in secret, with no one to see it and no colours to claim it under. Vainglory starves without a gallery; Perfidy has nothing to hide beneath; the deed without the name fuses the two faces she keeps split, and the engine stalls.

**At the Council of Fourteen.** Drengar is the Council's blade. The Fourteen govern by slow consensus, but consensus needs a sanction behind it when a seat will not yield, and the forms appoint her: when a matter comes to the formal challenge, she answers for the Hall, and the threat of her keeps the debates orderly. In the open she is the détente's staunchest defender, and names the standing truce between the banks a point of honour she is sworn to keep. Across the Tunsund she watches **Reidar** more closely than any other devil watches any demon, because the wrath-blade is the one body on the far bank that could break the truce, and if it breaks, the forms will send her to meet him: the honour-edge against the wrath-edge, each bank's most dangerous blade. She tells the Council she watches him to guard the peace. What she does not say at the table is that a part of her wants the breach. The duel against Reidar would be the contest that made her name forever, the one fight worth a thousand years of keeping the peace, and a devil whose excess face is Vainglory does not guard a truce without dreaming of the war that would crown her. She would defend the détente to the last, and she would not be sorry to lose it.

**Reputation.** Among the Fourteen she is the seat you send for when a thing must be settled and cannot be talked out: indispensable, a little feared, trusted absolutely to keep the forms, which is exactly the trust she was built to spend. Her nearest accommodation is with **Jafnar**: they are the devil bank's two keepers of the forms, he of the letter and she of the edge, the account and the blade. He finds the blade a crude instrument and she finds the ledger a bloodless one, and each defers to the other inside its own custody. Across the Tunsund she holds the one standing arrangement any devil keeps with a Vice Demon, with **Lostar**. Lostar's graduated apprentices, taught to make themselves wanted and to climb on it, drift in their later careers into Vainglory and Perfidy and arrive at the Tunsund already marked, landing in the ground beneath the Fair Field; Drengar has known for centuries that the supply comes from the Library on the far bank. Where Sannar marks the Deceit-inflow and cannot read Lostar, Drengar simply crossed the channel and asked. The two reached a wordless understanding at the bank between flood-cycles, a duelist and the one craft she could not duel, and it holds: Drengar collects the paragons Lostar's work ruined and asks no more about the Library than Lostar offers, which is nothing. It is the closest thing to respect either pays anyone. The seat she least abides is **Rausnar** of the Endowment, the newest devil and the other of the bank's two greatness-corruptions: his magnificence rings false to her form-keeper's eye, she alone among the Fourteen refuses his gifts and will not be made beholden, and he cannot abide the one peer he cannot buy.

**⚿ GM Secret: the clean hands.** Here is the wound at the centre. Drengar believes her honour is real, and she has the proof: in twelve centuries she has never once cheated in a contest, never struck a foul blow in the ring, never broken a rule inside the lists. The duel is genuinely sacred to her, the one place she will not lie, and she would lose and die before she would cheat in it. **That incorruptible honour in the ring is not her virtue. It is her alibi.** She keeps her hands clean inside the contest precisely so that she never has to look at the betrayals she frames outside it: the perfidy lives in how the contest is set, the terms chosen, the gap left open, and the clean fight in the middle launders all of it. Every fair duel she wins is the receipt she shows herself to prove she is honourable; the receipt is true, and it is a lie about the whole. She has carried House Prydar's name for an age as a badge of honour and has never let herself finish the thought that *prýði* means **ornament**, that the badge was always only a badge, that the splendid arms she wears stand abated in every mirror, that Herva of the great house earned the name Drengar in a contest whose frame was a betrayal and won it cleanly so she would never have to know. The seed of the true virtue lives exactly here, in the one thing she will not corrupt: the duel is still holy to her, and a devil who still holds one thing holy is not past saving.

**Secret weakness: the honest field.** The structural opening is the true mean restored, and it is the one contest she cannot game. Her whole method is the gap in the frame; the clean duel in the middle is only ever the alibi for it. Bring her to a contest where the frame itself is honest, the terms fair, no gap to exploit, witnessed, the stakes nothing but honour, and she has only the duel, where she is merely Herva, a superb blade and nothing more, with no perfidy to hide behind and no gallery the win is *for*. Whether she wins it or loses it, if she is made to see in that moment that the contest was whole, form and substance one, the laundering fails: the clean fight can no longer alibi a betrayal that was never set, and the badge is shown for the ornament it is. The false mean collapses. For a span measured in minutes she stands as Herva Prydar, the devil who once meant the forms, recoverable, mournful, reachable, killable by mortal means, her arms in flesh and glass at last the same. The window closes when she finds a new frame and a new gap, which she will, because a duelist of twelve centuries always finds the next contest, unless someone keeps the field honest long enough to reach her. The lever here is a *contest*, not a person, so the weakness is a campaign and not a fact: someone must build her an honest field and hold it honest while she fights.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Darkness · Confidence**. Darkness for the perfidy, the betrayal worked in the shadow the bright colours throw (cross-pantheon co-grant with Araphel); Confidence for the vainglory, the commanding bearing and the bold display of an honour whose substance is not there (co-grant with Sarrum and, across the Tunsund, with Drambur, the Pride demon whose self-certainty rhymes with her self-display). A lean grant by design: Honour holds no domain of its own in the registrar, and the fourteen carve a finite pool. Favored weapon: **Spiral Rapier**, an old imperial dueling blade with a corkscrew-spiralled point made for catching and turning an opponent's weapon. She settles matters by control and the precise touch, the disarm and the parry, winning in the gap the forms leave open rather than by force: the elegant opposite of Reidar's brute Spirit Thresher across the water.

### Rausnar: the Magnanimity Virtue Devil

**Origin: Silas Largent, the patron of the age.** Of the fourteen, Rausnar is the youngest by a vast margin and the only one Bolverk still calls *the new seat*. In life he was **Silas Largent**, the great benefactor of the Adventurer Era: a magnate whose name is carved over the doors of halls, schools, and hospices across half a dozen domains, who funded the penniless genius and dowered the lost cause and built the careers of the talented with both open hands. A continent loved him. Folk who were children when he died are living still, and they remember him as the most generous soul of the age. He gave magnificently, he bore every reverse with great-souled calm, and he was, by every account a chronicler can gather, a truly large man.

**The corruption.** Every soul he lifted stayed, forever, beholden. Their greatness was always a branch of his largesse; not one was ever permitted to outgrow the hand that raised them, and the one or two who tried were quietly unmade. His generosity had a grammar under it, learned so early he never heard himself speak it: *I am great in the measure that those around me are smaller, and raised by me.* He gave to be the source. He rose above pettiness in public and kept, in private, a cold and exact account of every slight and every rival who climbed too high. Greatness of soul, in him, had become a ranking, and he could not abide being anywhere in it but first.

**The arrival and the seat-taking.** He died owning nothing he had not chosen to keep, because he had paid every account in life to the last coin. This was not honesty; it was vanity. A man whose whole self is the magnificent giver cannot bear to be a debtor, and he settled every obligation magnificently and publicly so that no one could ever say Silas Largent owed them. So he arrived at the Tunsund **unclaimed**, with no creditor waiting. He was not unmarked, though: the corruption ran in his soul as a corrupted *virtue*, greatness-of-soul gone to rot, and that is devil-stuff, not demon-stuff. A soul that strong rebuffs the snatchers and walks where it likes; most who can, walk off to be Independent. He walked toward the ziggurats, because he belonged there, and he took the Magnanimity seat the way he had taken everything: he **out-gave** the holder before him. He endowed the old devil with a gift so vast and so public that the holder became, in the giving, the lesser party, the recipient, the beholden, until the greatness of the seat flowed to the greater giver and Silas Largent stood holding it. No blow. A donation.

**Form.** From across a hall, Rausnar is the great patron you are honoured to have noticed you: expansive, warm, serene above every smallness, and in his presence you feel *larger*, chosen, elevated, greater than you were when you came in. (That is the deceptive mean: greatness of soul, seen from a distance.) The Vanity face pours off him; gifts and light and elevation seem to stream from his open hands without end. The Pettiness face is the tell: **for all that pouring, nothing ever actually leaves him.** Watch the hands and the gift that should have left them is somehow still there; he has not diminished by a coin or a grace; the largeness you feel is on loan and priced to your staying beneath him. And it is dimensional, the way Drambur's height is across the water: stand taller than him in any particular, outshine him, stop needing him, and for one instant the magnificent benefactor narrows to a small cold reckoner of slights before the warmth floods back. Mortals who notice that the great giver has never once been seen to truly give anything away can slip his hold.

**The ziggurat: the Endowment.** From the river it is the most magnificent silhouette on the devil bank, all gold and open doors and warm light, the one ziggurat that looks like it is forever giving something away. The upper tier, the Vanity face, is the **Gallery of the Raised**: tier upon tier of the careers, names, and reputations he funded, every soul he ever lifted enshrined in splendour as a testament to *his* largesse, a museum of borrowed greatness. The lower tier, the Pettiness face, is the **Cellar of the Discarded**: in the dark beneath the gallery, the ones who surpassed him or stopped needing him, struck from the record and cast down, every name he ever erased for the crime of outgrowing the hand that raised it. Between them is the receiving-floor, where the Endowment performs its one rite: a petitioner climbs and is **raised**, given a gift and a patronage, magnificent and public, and leaves greater and bound, their new height a branch of his largesse, enshrined in the gallery above and one slight from the cellar below.

**Method on Talan.** He cultivates two kinds. The would-be magnificent: the benefactor who gives for the carved name, the noble whose generosity is a performance, the patron who must be the source of another's rise. And the small-souled: the grudge-nurser, the withholder, the one who cannot bear a rival's success or give a thing away. He does not whisper temptation; he **offers a model**, appearing as the great-souled patron the mortal longs to be, and the frame sells two goods. **Vanity:** the gift-for-glory, the monument-hunger, the magnificent calm worn like a robe, the raising of others into beholden ornaments of one's own greatness. **Pettiness:** the cold account of slights, the warmth that curdles when someone climbs too high, the small withholding at the centre of a large display. Either prices the same, a **patron's mark** on the soul that routes it pre-tagged to the lower Cellar at death. The Enki-aligned exorcists who have mapped his line know the counter is neither plain charity nor humbling, each of which only feeds a face. It is the **freeing gift**: a thing given with no name on it, no hold, and no expectation of return, that leaves the receiver genuinely greater and wholly free. The freeing gift starves Vanity (there is no glory in a gift no one knows you gave) and Pettiness (a gift with no string cannot be scored or recalled), and fuses the two faces Rausnar keeps apart.

**At the Council of Fourteen.** He is the parvenu, a century seated among holders who measure their tenure in millennia, and his Vanity cannot abide the rank. So he is magnanimous at the Hall to a fault: he concedes grandly, rises above every dispute, and showers the ancient seats with deference and favours, which is its own quiet way of making even them a little beholden. He is wholehearted for the détente, and names it the proof that they are all of them above petty war, a statesman's pose that costs him nothing and looks magnificent from every angle. What he does not show the Hall is the cold count he keeps of every seat that has out-ranked or slighted the new arrival, and how much the youngest-chair humiliation gnaws at a being who must, by his nature, be first in any room.

**Reputation.** The fourteen find the new seat useful and faintly absurd: his performed great-souledness oils the Council, and no one mistakes it for the real thing. The one who cannot stand him is **Drengar** of the Fair Field. The two of them are the devil bank's pair of greatness-corruptions, she the colours and the name, he the soul's own size, and a form-keeper's eye reads his magnificence as counterfeit at a glance. Alone among the seats she refuses his gifts and will not be made beholden, which is the one thing his nature cannot tolerate: a peer he cannot buy, who has taken his measure and found it manufactured. He keeps offering. She keeps declining. Across the Tunsund he has no dealings to speak of, being too new and too careful of his dignity to be seen bargaining at the channel-bank like a lesser thing.

**⚿ GM Secret: the manufactured soul.** Silas Largent's greatness was never his. He was one of **Lostar's** apprentices, reached in dream as all of them are, taught the engineering of presence, and where her graduates become seducers and courtiers and kings, he turned the craft to **patronage**: he built the magnetic, beloved, magnificent-benefactor presence that made a continent adore him, calibration by calibration, and called it his soul. His magnanimity is a learned technique. He never knew his teacher, the way none of them do, and Lostar never knew this one ascended, the way she follows none of the thousands she has made to the grave. So the truth is held by **no living being in creation**: a secret with no keeper, recoverable only by someone who could read what was done to a soul before it was a soul that did things to others. Here is the wound at the centre: a manufactured great-soul has no size of its own, only the technique of seeming greater than whoever stands near, and so he has never once given freely, because there was never a self large enough to give from. Every gift is the craft, reeling the receiver closer and keeping them smaller. The seed of the true virtue survives in exactly one place: the **eye**. The craft taught him to read people, and so he can genuinely see greatness, recognise true worth at a glance, which is how he chose whom to raise. That clear eye for the truly great is the last unmanufactured thing in him, and it is what could end him.

**Secret weakness: the freeing gift, and the surpassed.** The opening is the true mean restored, in two registers. The general: a genuinely selfless gift performed in his presence, given with no name and no hold and no expectation, making its receiver greater and free, has nothing for his craft to grip; the ranking finds no purchase, and the manufactured magnificence stalls. The specific, and the campaign: the one outcome his whole existence is built to prevent, **a soul he raised becoming truly great free of him**, surpassing him by their own worth and owing him nothing. Set before a protégé whose greatness is wholly their own and needs no comparison to his, the ranking collapses, because there is no longer a *greater-than* for him to be; and for a span measured in minutes he stands as Silas Largent, the man whose one real gift was the eye that could see greatness, able for that window to see it without needing to own it, recoverable, mournful, killable by mortal means. The window closes when he finds a new soul to raise and be the source of, which he will, because a manufactured soul must always be the source, unless someone holds the freeing gift open long enough to reach him.

**Domains & Favored Weapon.** PF2e domain grants: **Wealth · Ambition · Star**. Wealth for the fortune and the largesse, the magnate's open hand (cross-pantheon co-grant with Jianna and, on the demon bank, Nirfel); Ambition for the appetite to be the source, the drive to be first in greatness of soul (co-grant with Komo); Star for renown, the celebrated name, the bright body everyone is made to orbit, the Vanity face rendered as a domain (co-grant with Forseti). Favored weapon: **Gada**, the great gilded ceremonial mace of a lord, all ornament and authority: magnificent to display and a crushing weight beneath the gold, the grand symbol of munificence that is, when it comes to it, a thing for breaking what will not bow.
