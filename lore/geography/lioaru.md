# Lioaru: Time · Tani

**Borders:** Sumendar, Lautara, Ezkudon

**Seas:** Hafra (southern coast; Lost Isle offshore). **No Midarra access.**

**Etymology:** Icelandic *líð* (passage of time, waning, slipping away) → drifted to *Lioaru*. A land where time doesn't move cleanly; things don't fully pass.

**Position:** Southwest Talan, south of Sumendar, west of Brauogi and Ezkudon, coastal on Hafra to the south

**Terrain:** Desert dominant with orange sand dunes throughout. A green river valley cuts through as an oasis of life. The dark, shadowy **Lost Kingdom** occupies the southern coastal area, a kingdom lost during or after the Week of Crimson Rain, directly tied to Tani's death. **Lost Isle** sits offshore to the west. Temporal anomalies throughout: moments that repeat, ruins newer than they should be.

**God's city:** Valreka, The Whale-Borne City (a nomad city built on titanic sand-whales that roam the desert; never in the same place, it roams to recover Oroiri, Tani's buried holy city). Detailed below.

**Peoples:** Three dominant ancestries are anchored here: **Ghoran** (Galdua Jendea, the heart of Valreka), **Azarketi** (the southern coast and the isles offshore of the Lost Kingdom), and **Nagaji** (Hareaveldi). The domain-level read is a feeling of each people, never a practice; the cultures live with the sub-regions, and the three feelings stand in the three tenses of the goddess.

- **The Ghoran hold what was.** Memory as temperament: unhurried, archival, every Ghoran a living keeper of what it has witnessed, for whom the past is not gone, merely earlier. The Elf carries the long view forward; the Ghoran carries the long memory back. Patience is their texture; memory is their tense.
- **The Azarketi answer it now.** Every Azarketi is born carrying a story bigger than their own life, and what an Azarketi is, is the answer given today: pride worn daily, penance worked daily, the inheritance fixed and the response a present-tense act renewed every morning. A people defined by what they do with the weight before sundown.
- **The Nagaji become what's next.** Shed, and whole again: a self is a finished thing a Nagaji can lay down, and a Nagaji is always already growing the next one. What they were is done; what they are becoming is the real skin. No people on Talan carries less of its own past on its back, and Tani's death-and-return is something they recognise rather than merely revere, for her mystery is written in their own bodies.

The Ghoran hold the past, the Azarketi answer in the present, the Nagaji become the future: three mortal peoples standing in the three tenses of one goddess, and Decay over all of it, for the sand that buries is also the goddess.

**Sub-regions:**
- **Galdua Jendea**: the sub-region that houses Valreka. The sand whales that carry the city roam within this territory. Name is Basque: meaning disputed, possibly "Place of Many Waters" (the hidden seeps and sand-springs the Azarketi water-bearers tend) or "Place of the Lost People." Its northern corner touches Emarrea (Lautara); Valreka periodically drifts close enough for Heartcourt envoys to meet Tani's clergy. Specific cultural detail of these meetings flesh out during Lioaru work.
- **Lost Kingdom**: the ruined / cursed western-coast territory. Specifically: the **Blackened Lands**, the site where Tani was killed by the Storveldi Denbora's ascended ruling class (full Storveldi Denbora account in [`../timeline.md`](../timeline.md), *⚿ GM Secret: The Storveldi Denbora: The Real History*; full Tani-rebirth account in [`../gods.md`](../gods.md), *Tani → ⚿ GM Secret: Tani's death and rebirth*). Their capital (built atop earlier Elden ruins) was annihilated by the divine retaliation that followed her death, and the surrounding land has never healed. Modern features: blackened soil, time-warped strata (moments repeat, ruins read newer than they should be), ambient necromantic resonance, periodic fleshwarp incidents in survivors and trespassers, and concentrations of restless dead. **Origin of the Fleshwarp and Skeleton ancestries**: both arise from the cursed ground itself, not from any current faction's work. **Folk name:** *the black spot on the map.* Considered among the most hazardous regions on Talan; no kingdom claims it; the Adventurers' Guild posts a perpetual standing warning. Tani-mourning pilgrims, necromancers, and ascension-cultists are the most common visitors. **Surviving folk-detail of pre-annihilation Storveldi Denbora life:** they ate human flesh as everyday food, *meat on a stick*, sold from street vendors. The detail survives only as a horror-trope in mortal folk-history, but it lines up with the ruling class's broader contempt for non-ascended life and reads as canonical truth.
- **Lost Isle**: small island offshore to the west, associated with the Lost Kingdom. Shares some of the cursed-ground character of the mainland Blackened Lands but at lower intensity.
- **River Duchies**: the green river valley cutting through the desert as an oasis. A loose confederation of noble houses whose wealth comes from controlling river trade. The valley's headwaters reach up into Emarrea (Lautara) via the river that flows down from the kitsune kingdom's terraced orchards; the Duchies handle the downstream distribution of Emarrea's sake, orchard goods, and theatrical-illusion exports as a secondary trade channel separate from the main Lautaran merchant-house network.
- **Hareaveldi**: fully within Lioaru. Partially independent cultural identity; name predates Tani's influence in the region. Now borders Emarrea (Lautara) on its northern edge. **The Nagaji heartland: the serpent-kingdom of the desert** (seed). An old kingdom whose name and identity are older than the cult it now carries; its people found in the returned goddess what their own skins had always taught, for the desert is the great stripper-away, the land that scours the old skin off everything, and Hareaveldi is the kingdom of becoming. The wider character of the kingdom (its work, its craft, its Emarrea-side cultural interface) is its own later pass.

**Note:** The Lost Kingdom is the single most cosmologically loaded location in Lioaru: it is where a Grand God was killed and where the Week of Crimson Rain began. Its identification with the **Storveldi Denbora capital** (the *Old Race who killed Tani* of mortal myth) is canon truth that most mortal histories do not preserve correctly; mortal folk-myth more commonly conflates the Storveldi Denbora with the Elden, exactly as the Storveldi Denbora themselves did.

**Spiritual root of the ambient necromancy:** the natural-born Skeleton ancestry and the restless-dead phenomenon both trace spiritually to **Betibizi**, the Storveldi Denbora ruling-class survivor who ascended to Minor God of Undeath in the aftermath of the city's annihilation (see `../gods.md`, *Named Non-Bound Gods*). The proximate cause of the cursing is still the divine retaliation that wounded the land; Betibizi is its spiritual focus. Necromantic pilgrims occasionally come to the Blackened Lands specifically to attune to his domain; most of them don't return.

## Valreka, the Whale-Borne City (Galdua Jendea)

Valreka is the city that will not stay still and will not stay buried. It rides the backs of titanic sand-whales across the dune-sea of Galdua Jendea, never in the same place two seasons running, and it spends its days doing in small what its goddess did in whole: refusing to let the buried past stay buried. When Tani was killed in this domain and the Crimson Rain scoured Lioaru, her holy city of **Oroiri** was swallowed and scattered under the shifting sand. Then the goddess came back. Her faithful had no ground to return to, for the sand that took Oroiri would swallow anything built on it, so they took to the whales instead, and they have roamed ever since, recovering Oroiri piece by piece as the wind gives it back. The city of the goddess who died and returned is itself a city that was buried and is being recovered. Valreka calls itself a city of three tenses, after Tani's three faces: it recovers the past, rides the present, and builds the future, and holds all three at once the way the goddess holds her three ages.

Valreka was refounded at the rebirth itself, near the turn into the first year of the Mortal Reckoning, and the **mother whale** has carried it ever since: she has been alive since Tani came back, the oldest living thing in the city and a sacred relic in her own right.

**The herd is the realm.** A noble house is a lineage that can guide a sand-whale, and each house guides one whale, which carries that house's district on its back. The royal line guides the mother whale, greatest and oldest of the herd, who bears the palace, Tani's sanctum, and the old noble quarter; because the whole herd follows the mother, to guide her is to lead the city, and that is the crown. The guiding is a bonded art, a house and its whale across generations, the craft taught only within the bloodline and the beast answering only that bloodline's hand. The whales are near-ageless and seldom die of years; a whale dies from its rider's mistake, an accident, or a catastrophe, and when one dies its house falls, losing district and seat while its people scatter among the other whales, and there is shame in the fall, because a fallen house is almost always a house that failed its beast. The one road up is as hard as the fall is bitter: a commoner who can ride out into Galdua Jendea, find and bond a *wild* whale, and bring it home founds a new house, and most who try die in the sand. The mother whale's death would be the unthinkable, and the city does not speak of it. Yet if that day ever came, Valreka would meet it as the last test of its own creed: a city built on the faith that the buried returns and the dead rise would have to prove it could do what its goddess did, and rise again from the ashes of its own heart.

**The herd, counted.** Nineteen great whales carry Valreka in 2532 MR, about seventy thousand people between them. **Lenama**, the mother whale, carries twelve thousand of those; Ardats five thousand, Anzar four thousand, the other house-whales two to three and a half thousand each, and Tizir the least at twelve hundred. That makes Valreka the smallest of the thirteen god-cities, and the only one that cannot grow except by bonding a new whale. Two whales carry no noble house: Lenama, who carries the crown, and Anzar, who carries the Guild. The herd stood at twenty-six whales at its largest, in the last Golden-Era century, and at eleven in the Dark Era, when five houses fell inside forty years. The herd calves seldom. A calf runs with its dam's house until it is grown, and then a younger child of that house takes its bond and founds a cadet house; that is the noble-born's road up, and the commoner's road is still the wild whale.

**A house is its whale.** A house is called by its whale's name, so that *the Ardats* is the whale, the bloodline that guides her, and the district on her back in one word. Two houses break the rule. The royal line is **Eramun**, a line before it was a house: the Eramun led the faithful onto the whales at the refounding and have guided Lenama since, and they keep their own name where other houses take their whale's. The monarch's title is the **Eskua**, the hand, because a whale answers her bloodline's hand and the herd answers Lenama. **Itri ult Eramun** is Eskua now, a Ghoran who took Lenama's bond at thirty-one and has held it one hundred and forty years. The other exception is the memory-keepers, below, who outlived their whale. The whales' names carry the herd's history on their surface: a house bonded in the Lost Era wears a name from the deep desert, a house bonded under the Golden Empire wears an Imperial one, and a house bonded since the Dark Era wears a name in the city's own tongue, so any Valrekan can hear how old a house is before anyone says.

**The named houses.**
- **Ardats** (Lost Era). The market rides on her back, and when the herd draws up she rides at Lenama's side, so most bridges in the city land on Ardats. Her guiding-line is the best in the herd after the crown's, and holds a moving bridge for two hours when the Eramun work with them.
- **Errok** (Lost Era). The dig-crews' quarter. The Errok hold that their line stood among Oroiri's nobility while the Eramun were still priests, and that the recovery of the buried city belongs by right to them; the crown gave it to the Greymantel instead, four hundred years ago, and the Errok have contested the gift at every Eskua's accession since. The argument stands open.
- **Caldwel** (Golden Era). The cisterns. Most of the water-bearers live on Caldwel, and every halt-argument in the city runs through her house, because a dig goes one more day only as long as Caldwel says the water holds. A plain house nobody courts and nobody crosses.
- **Tiwiz** (Dark Era). The wrights' yard, where recovered Oroiri stone is dressed and the first hall for a newly bonded whale is framed before it is carried across. The Nagaji's whale in all but bloodline.
- **Tizir** (2461 MR). The youngest house. **Amnay u Tizir** was a Stray off Ardats who rode out at nineteen and came back at twenty-two with a wild whale; the house is three generations old, its district half-built, and its bridging poor enough that Tizir is a guest on someone's deck more often than any other house. The old houses say *the Tizir* with a pause in front of it.

Twelve more house-whales ride in the herd unnamed.

**The draw-up.** When the herd rests it draws up in two rings around Lenama, Ardats at her side, the old houses in the inner ring and the young in the outer, and a house's ring is the plainest public measure of its standing. Anzar rides apart, at the herd's leading edge, with her own Stokkul shoal: the first whale a stranger reaches and the last one the departing see.

**What a Valrekan is called.** A Valrekan carries a given name, then the whale they were born to, and then, if they choose it, the whale they live on. The third name is taken by choice and read as a declaration, so *Amnay Ardats Tizir* has told you he was born on the market-whale and belongs now to the upstart. The guiding blood alone put *u* (a son) or *ult* (a daughter) before their whale, claiming the beast as a parent; the Eramun use their line-name where the whale would stand. A child carries the prefix *Mez-* on the given name until the coming-of-age, when it drops and the name may change, though most keep the one they had. The elder suffix *-ghar* has no ritual and no date: one day someone younger uses it for you, and some years after that you begin writing it yourself. Taking the Guild oath adds Anzar to a name, always, and strikes the particle; dropping the birth-whale is the member's own choice.

> *"I was Mez-Tafsut on Caldwel, I am Tafsut Caldwel Anzar on the Roll, and my daughter's crew call me Tafsutghar when they think I cannot hear. I can hear. Now: your names, your rank, and which whale I send the rider to."*
> Tafsut Caldwel Anzar, clerk of the Whalehall desk, entering a new party on the Standing Roll.

The **memory-keepers** are the exception to the fall: the **Greymantel**, a Golden-Era house whose whale died in 2131 MR, in the last years of the Dark Era, and who turned to the past instead of scattering. The crown took them onto the mother whale and gave them the old noble quarter, and they became the city's foremost archaeologists, the ones who lead the recovery of Oroiri. A fallen house's name goes to the sand; theirs stayed because the crown took them aboard, and the Greymantel are the only people in Valreka who carry a dead whale's name. A house with no whale, and so no present, became the keepers of what is gone. The city's oldest argument runs between them and the whale-wardens: the memory-keepers would halt the whole herd to dig one more day for one more piece of Oroiri, and the wardens must keep the whales moving to feed and to live. It is Tani's own nature made civic, Memory against Decay, and it never resolves, because the goddess is both.

**Whether you can get home depends on the herd.** When the whales draw close, at rest or over a dig, the contraption-bridges go up and the whole city becomes one, houses mingling and markets and courtships running along the spans. When the herd spreads to travel, each whale becomes its own island, and you are home or you are not; misjudge the bridges and you are a guest on another house's whale for two days until the herd draws close again, and being a *good* stranded guest is a real Valrekan art. A house that guides its whale well can hold a bridge for an hour while the herd moves, and two skilled houses together can hold one for two, so how connected you stay is a plain measure of how good your guiding is. The city travels in the cool and beds down through the killing noon, and when it can it rests for days, near the River Duchies, or close enough to Emarrea for the Heartcourt envoys to come aboard, or parked over a fresh dig, until a sandstorm rises on the horizon and drives the herd on.

Every whale carries its own shoal of **Stokkul**, the small leaping kin of the great beasts, who ride the whale's wake through the sand, clean its hide, and feed on what it stirs up. From that shoal the city breaks its mounts. The **Outrider Corps** and the city guard's own outrider unit ride them across the open sand between the moving whales, carrying word and healers and the people who cannot wait, and an outrider who loses the herd dies out there. The young ride them for the joy of it: the **Strays**, a crew with a chapter on every whale, who break their own Stokkul and race and breach the dunes and leap deck to deck, free for an hour against the structure of the grown city. To break a Stokkul is the small shadow of the great dream, for the youngster who can ride the dunes is the one who might one day ride out and bond a wild whale and raise a house.

Valreka is **Ghoran** before all, Tani's Guardians of Time, plant-rooted and long-lived, each elder a living archive of centuries the rest of the city holds only in fragments, which suits a people whose whole work is memory and patience. Among them lives a community of **Azarketi**, who have made their atonement a daily labour rather than a rite. They set down the Elden-blood pride most of their people still carry and took up the one work their water-born nature fits a desert city to do: they find and give water. They are Valreka's **water-bearers**, drawing it from deep wells sunk at every resting-place and, where the sand allows, calling it up out of the dune itself by the old water-craft in their blood, so that a city which cannot stay beside a river does not die of thirst between the green places. And they keep the desert's water-map, the hidden seeps and sand-springs that earn Galdua Jendea its old name, the Place of Many Waters: they water the Stokkul shoals, and they guide the Strays to safe water and home again, so that the youngest and wildest lives in the city ride out underwritten by the quietest and most penitent. Their forebears were among those who took the goddess's life; the life of her city, and of its children, now runs through their hands. They carry all three of Tani's tenses in one people: the wrong behind them, the water they give today, the young they keep alive for tomorrow.

And the future tense works with its hands: the **Nagaji**, aboard since the faithful first took to the whales, are Valreka's **wrights**. Theirs are the crews that raise a first hall on the bare back of a newly bonded whale, refit the districts as the herd grows and shifts, and rig the contraption-bridges the city's whole life runs across. Above all, theirs is the work that completes the city's creed: when the memory-keepers have read a recovered fragment of Oroiri to the end, the Nagaji build it into the living city, old stone fitted into new halls, so the buried city is not merely remembered but *becomes* Valreka piece by piece, the way a Nagaji becomes her next self out of the last one. The keepers release a fragment only once it has been read to the end, and *read to the end* is the most argued phrase in the city; the wrights claim the other half of that argument too, for what the city sheds is theirs to call, the wall torn out in a refit, the fragment judged done, the camp left to the sand, and they relish the deciding in a way the keepers find faintly indecent. The same crews close the digs when the storm drives the herd on, and by custom a wright lays a piece of shed skin in the sand before it is covered: what we were stays with what was, and we go on. The keepers would halt the city, the wardens must move it, and the Nagaji are why the moving does not tear it in half. The rebirth-rites carry their cadence, but their temple is the workbench, and their liturgy is the next thing built.

**What the roaming city trades.** Valreka makes almost nothing it can hold and needs nearly everything, so what it offers the continent is the past, and what it must have in return is water. Beyond their own Oroiri-work the Ghoran are a roaming archive for hire: houses, clans, and kingdoms that fear their keeping-houses will fall entrust their records, genealogies, and histories to the living-archives, who carry them in memory across centuries and outlast the settled institutions that crumble, so that to be remembered by Valreka is to be remembered past the fall of whoever wrote you down. What the digs lift that is not Oroiri's own, the Tani-era relics and lost-world finds and buried history of the dune-sea, the city trades to the scholars and collectors the herd drifts near, and dates and authenticates besides, for the Ghoran knew the old eras when they were the present. And the roaming is itself a trade, for Valreka is the one thing that crosses Galdua Jendea entire, carrying word and goods and people between the scattered desert settlements and the River Duchies' edge, the moving market that reaches where nothing fixed can. Against all of it stands the one need that never eases, water, the Azarketi water-bearers' endless labour; food and the settled world's goods the city pays for in remembrance and the recovered past.

**The Godshall rides its own whale.** Every divine city-state carries a Godshall, the Adventurers' Guild's monumental rank, and Valreka's is the only one of the thirteen that travels. It rides **Anzar**, at the herd's leading edge, and the city says **Whalehall** where the paperwork says Valreka's Godshall. Anzar was bonded wild by an outrider in the last decades of the Dark Era, and when the Guild came to Valreka in the reconstruction it had no whale and so no ground, while the rising house of Anzar had a half-empty back. The Guild took rooms as a paying tenant. It grew until the hall was the district: the counter and the Standing Roll in the entry hall at the whale's shoulder, the Bank's vault sunk into the hide-cradle amidships, the taproom astern, and the guides' quarters at the head, where the guiding is done.

**The nine years.** In 2287 MR a bad crossing and a sand-fever took the Anzar guides inside a year, and the last of the blood was a child of six. The wardens' custom for an unguided whale is to take the people off and give her to the sand, since she drifts from the herd within a season. The Guild refused. For nine years its adventurers kept an unbonded whale with the herd by hand, riding her hide, turning her with Stokkul, feeding her at every halt, and forty-one of them died doing it. The child, **Tasa ult Anzar**, took the bond at fifteen and gave up her seat among the houses in the same week, declaring herself and her heirs the Guild's. Her blood still guides Anzar, as a whale requires, but as sworn Guild members on a Guild wage, the **bond-line**, holding no seat and sitting no council. The whale went on. The house stopped.

**A whale with no house.** Valreka says every whale has a house, and Anzar has none, and the city holds both facts at once without strain. The Sovereign sits in no council of the houses, and no house crosses the whale that carries the Bank, the Post, the Blackened Lands warning, and the only people who ever kept an unguided whale alive. A house's child who takes the Guild oath forswears their claim, and the roll on Anzar's deck lists four such children today, among them the Eskua's younger brother, entered in 2470 MR as **Massin Eramun Anzar**. The crown has offered the Sovereign a seat among the houses three times, in 2301, 2398 and 2503 MR, and three times the seat was refused on the same grounds: a house owes the crown, and a Godshall bound to Valreka could hold relations with no Duchy and no one. The city treats the offer as a courtesy and the refusal as one. Outsiders find the arrangement odd. Valrekans find it odd that anyone asks.

> *"Noble? No. Ask any house and they will tell you the Guild is no house. Then watch which whale they send their younger ones to."*
> Ilel Errok, bridge-warden, to a River Duchies factor who had asked whether the Sovereign outranked her.

**The Sovereign.** A **Guild Sovereign** holds the seat, by long custom a Ghoran elder, on the reasoning that an office whose whole weight is precedent belongs to someone who was present for the precedent. **Amestan Anzar** holds it now, born on Caldwel, Sovereign since 2489 MR, and the one who refused the third offer; the Roll's clerks call him Amestanghar to his face. His authority covers Galdua Jendea, Hareaveldi, the River Duchies and the Lost Isle, and the perpetual standing warning posted over the Blackened Lands is signed from his desk.

**The roaming costs the Bank nothing and the Post half a shoal.** The Guild Bank never notices the whales: the enchanted ledgers synchronise across distance, so coin banked at the Whalehall counter comes back out over a counter in Myrria or Frae City, and Valreka's traders use the Guild's vault the way a settled city uses a treasury. The Guild Post is the harder half. Within the herd the Guild runs its own riders off Anzar's shoal. Beyond it, a letter addressed to Valreka goes to wherever the herd happens to be, which is a thing nobody outside Galdua Jendea knows, so the Whalehall pays the Outrider Corps to run its sacks out to the River Duchies' edge and the scattered desert settlements and meet the settled routes there. A Guild courier who loses the herd dies out on the sand like any other outrider.

**The board runs on readiness.** A settled Godshall's board fills from the street. Valreka's fills in bursts, whenever the herd draws near enough to a settlement for a season's work to come aboard at once, and then goes quiet for weeks while the city crosses empty dune. What the desk keeps between the bursts is the **Standing Roll**, its register of parties who have declared themselves available on short notice: names, ranks, what they will take, and where on the herd to find them. A party on the Roll waits nowhere; it is somewhere out on the whales living its life, and an outrider will come. The clerks have read the phrase off the register so often that it is simply what such a party is called, and a crew that keeps its entry current can be raised, briefed and put on the sand inside a day.

**The work comes from everywhere.** The roaming city passes near most of the southwest eventually, and the ledgers and the Post reach the rest of Talan whatever the herd is doing, so the contracts that land on Valreka's board are seldom local twice running. A Roll party guards a dig in Galdua Jendea one week and walks into a River Duchies toll-quarrel the next, and when a posting comes down the ledger from a Kingshall four domains off with a note that it will not keep, the Whalehall sends whoever is ready ahead of whoever is near. It is the one Godshall where distance to the work is a question the desk answers second.

**Worship is recovery and remembrance.** Every fragment of Oroiri lifted from the sand is a rite, the faithful doing in small what Tani did in whole. Patience is the civic virtue, the slow whale and the slow dig; and Decay is revered rather than only resisted, for the sand that buries is also the goddess, which is why the halt-against-move argument is a holy one. Tani's clergy keep the sanctum on the mother whale, and the memory-keepers are as much clergy as scholars. Folk theology holds that Tani is the youngest of the Thirteen, since she alone was born twice and her second birth was the newest of all, and the city loves her the more for it.

**◈ Popular Belief: the old woman at the dig.** Tani walks her city openly, in two of her three faces. In her elder face she comes to the digs: an old woman at the trench-edge whom every crew knows on sight, telling the diggers where to set their hands, or naming the thing they are about to uncover before it is clean of sand, come to help her city remember, and gone again before the find is lifted. In her woman's face she stands at the public functions, the homecomings and the great reburials and the naming of recovered streets, the goddess attending her city's life as its first citizen. Her child face the city has never seen, and the faithful do not remark on it; a goddess shows the faces she shows. The dig-custom stands regardless: any old stranger at a dig is treated gently and offered water first of anyone, out of love for the one who is sometimes no stranger at all.

**⚿ GM Secret: Amona of the Strays.** The elder at the digs and the woman at the rites are the faces the city is allowed to see. The face it is not: among the mother whale's Strays rides a youngster the crew calls **Amona**, for the way she always somehow knows things and frets over the others like a grandmother, which they take for nothing but a funny name for a kid. Amona is Tani. She slips out from under her own clergy to ride the dunes with the crew, and the whole point of her is what she is not: not the best rider, never breaching cleaner than the rest, never vanishing the moment she is noticed. She is just one of the Strays, a little reckless, having fun. The youngest of the Thirteen died in her first youth into a war and the forging of a law and never had a childhood, so she steals one an hour at a time, on a borrowed Stokkul among friends who will go to their graves never knowing they rode the dunes with their god. The grandmother of the dig-tales and the grandmother of the Strays are the same goddess, and no one has ever set the two beside each other.
