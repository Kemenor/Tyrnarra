# Ezkudon: Knowledge · Enki

**Borders:** Lioaru, Lautara, Egulon

**Seas:** Hafra (south-eastern Golden Coast). **No Midarra access.**

**Etymology:** Basque *ezkutu* (hidden, concealed) → drifted to *Ezkudon*. Names the controlling, secretive character of the domain: knowledge here is collected, preserved, and controlled. Access is negotiated, not assumed.

**Position:** South-central Talan, below Brauogi, west of Egulon, north of Hafra

**Terrain:** Distinctive circular ring of forest and hills with mountains at the center, like a natural fortress grown around the great archives. Green forest in the north and west, sandy desert in the south, coastal access southeast, lakes to the west.

**God's city:** Thekkavar, The City of Learning (the formal-institutional pole of Talanese learning; it teaches and keeps. Detailed below.)

**Peoples:** Three dominant ancestries are anchored here: **Catfolk**, **Shisk**, and **Ratfolk**, and the three carry Enki's three faces between them, with the fourth left unclaimed. The domain-level read is a feeling of each people, never a practice; the cultures live with the sub-regions below.

- **The Catfolk are Wisdom: curiosity that keeps.** Not just the urge to know, the urge to know *properly*: completed, ordered, cared for, put where it belongs. A Catfolk anywhere is the one who finishes the question, checks the second source, labels the jar, remembers where the thing was put, and quietly resents an answer left half-done. The patience to not know *yet* is their virtue worn as temperament: knowledge, to a Catfolk, is something you become worthy of. Enki wears their face, and it is the face of restraint: the keeping eye, for whom every closed door is a kept promise rather than a refusal.
- **The Shisk are Mystery: the ones the hidden calls.** The Shisk hunger is specific: not knowledge in general but the *withheld*, the locked drawer, the unmarked path, the subject the village will not discuss. Show a Shisk an open library and a sealed cellar, and you have already lost them to the cellar. It is neither defiance nor greed; the hidden simply itches, and a Shisk scratches. And the calling runs both ways: a Shisk who follows the pull far enough tends to end as a keeper of the very kind of secret that drew them, the knower who cannot tell. The people of Mystery, becoming mysteries.
- **The Ratfolk are Discovery: every question has an answer.** The Ratfolk conviction is that nothing is truly closed: every wall has a seam, every system has a gap, every question has an answer, every stuck problem a route nobody has tried yet. Where the Catfolk ask whether one *should* and the Shisk feel the pull of the withheld, the Ratfolk simply assume the way exists and start looking: try it, fail cheap, try it differently, unsentimental about how a thing is "supposed" to be done. Discovery as motion rather than longing: the answer is always somewhere, and the only real failure is concluding it isn't. The same temperament that threads a tunnel cracks a cipher, a market, a recipe, a negotiation that everyone else has given up on.

Wisdom keeps, Mystery calls, Discovery finds; and **Arrogance**, Enki's fourth face, is the one no people claims: grasping the answer you have not earned, threatening each of the three from its own side, the keeper who hoards, the called who breaks the seal, the finder who enters what should have stayed shut.

**Sub-regions:**
- **The Wildreach**: a sub-region of Ezkudon. The wild people live here, and the area carries a strong wild-magic / wild-fey character. Internal landmark: **Iratxobaso** (the fey-forest), a cluster of multicoloured trees at the sub-region's centre whose presence reads as the source or focus of the surrounding wild-magic. Whether the trees *produce* the wild-magic or merely *cluster where it is densest* is a question the wild people answer differently.
- **Jakinduria**: the sub-region housing Thekkavar, Enki's city-state. The great archives sit at the center of this protected interior territory. *(Basque jakinduria = wisdom, knowledge.)* The central mountains, the natural fortress the whole domain shelters behind, are threaded with **Ratfolk warrens** older than any writ: the people of Discovery live inside the walls the rest of the domain lives behind, and the ways through the mountain are theirs.
- **The Golden Coast**: coastal sub-region of Ezkudon. Despite the domain's secretive reputation, this stretch of southeastern coast sees more trade traffic than most would expect.
- **Lua Lasai**: the guaranteed country on the Egulon border, fronting Jakinduria and the Golden Coast; kept unarmed under the Double Surety, the twin guarantee the powers of both sides countersign. Egulon-primary. Full canon: [`egulon/lua-lasai.md`](egulon/lua-lasai.md).

## Thekkavar, the City of Learning (Jakinduria)

Thekkavar holds more places of learning than anywhere on Talan, and it will teach you anything. The equation and the arcane, yes, but also the turn of a line on a stage, the rise of bread, the carry of a song, the lulling of a child to sleep, because Enki's Occult spark lives in the recipe and the lullaby as fully as in the dissertation, and the city keeps more of those spark-bearing things than any library elsewhere dreams of holding. Its institutes, which the city calls its **Lanterns**, each light one face of the world: the Institute of the Stage, of the Table, of Song, of Natural Philosophy, of the Arcane, of the Lullaby, and a hundred more. To live here is to be always learning something, and the one thing a Thekkavari never does is nothing.

What sets it apart from its knowledge-cousins is the form. **Azkataria** argues knowledge into being on its Open Floor; **Atarialda** remembers it in song and recipe with no library at all; **Thekkavar teaches it and keeps it**, the formal pole of the three, the academy and the stacks. Its own scholars make the journey to argue at Azkataria as a rite they call **the Helgaft**, a season at the foot of Helgafjall and on the Open Floor at Solune, so the three cities are one long conversation and Thekkavar is where it gets written down. But *Ezkudon* means the hidden, and knowledge here is kept as much as given: access is negotiated, never assumed.

A life in Thekkavar is a **descent**. You arrive at the surface, young, in the open stacks and the open lectures, and over a lifetime you earn your way down, shelf by sealed shelf, deeper into your discipline and the city both, and standing here is depth: a scholar carries the **writs of descent** they have earned, and you read a person's life by which doors open to them. The civic virtue is the patience to not know yet, the exact counter to Enki's named sin of **Arrogance**, which is grasping the answer you have not earned. A Thekkavari will carry an open question for a decade without flinching, a master who hands knowledge down too early has erred as gravely as a student who steals it, and the honest answer to most questions here is *not yet*. The city holds that its closed doors are a **mercy**, because the alternative waits at the bottom of the descent: the Infinite Library, where nothing is withheld at all, where you may know everything at once and lose your way in it forever. The slow earned descent is the only way to go deep without drowning.

The pace of Thekkavar is the pace of thought. The city walks, and walks slowly, for to hurry is a small vulgarity here, like rushing a question; the senior and the Convocation ride in coaches and covered chairs as a mark of rank, but the real traffic is vertical and unhurried, the **page-runners** carrying books up from the stacks and down again on request. Famous beyond the domain are its **tea-houses**, the contemplative heart of the city's day, supplied by leaf carried in from every corner of Talan and well beyond (the Golden Coast's surprising trade traffic is mostly this, tea and paper and ink coming in, finished thought going out). Where Azkataria argues knowledge aloud on its Open Floor, Thekkavar **murmurs** it over a long cup, the slow patient trade of half-formed ideas that a scholar no more rushes than they rush a thought, and a Thekkavari can tell you which house keeps the deepest cellar of rare leaf the way another city boasts of its wine. And once each day the bells ring **the Quiet Hour**, when the Lanterns still their lectures, the page-runners rest, and the whole city falls to reading, the tea-houses full and near-silent, an hour of inquiry kept like a prayer. A visitor who talks through the Quiet Hour marks themselves as plainly as one who asks above their access.

No god-king rules the city and no single scholar. Each Lantern is sovereign in its own discipline, setting its curriculum, its depths, and the masters who decide who descends within it; the city itself is governed by **the Convocation**, the assembled heads of every Institute, who settle what crosses between them and speak as one through an elected, term-limited **Provost**. Thekkavar keeps its own law the way it keeps everything else, as a living archive of rulings: a dispute is pleaded, not decreed, both sides arguing by citation before a panel of masters, and the better-reasoned case prevails and is filed as new precedent, so the city writes its law by living it. This is no Forseti court of oaths; Thekkavar has only Knowledge, and its justice is a thesis defended.

The city is three peoples, and they carry the god's three faces. The **Catfolk** are his **Wisdom**: curious and precise, the scholar-caste and archive-keepers in the god's own image, masters of the Lanterns and the degree-and-stay lifers who built the discipline of restraint and hold that the closed doors are love. The **Shisk** are his **Mystery**: the ones the hidden calls, drawn down past their access into the sealed shelves and the Leize itself, the city's only true reach into the deep and its standing temptation both. The **Ratfolk** are his **Discovery**: the city's circulation, the page-runners whose vertical traffic carries the stacks up and down, the warren-crews who open new shelf-ways through the under-city, the people for whom every question has an answer and every architecture a seam. The Catfolk keep; the Shisk are called; the Ratfolk find; and the city is the long conversation among the three. Two other figures cross every story here: the **apprentice-and-leave** who come young, take a discipline, and carry it back into the world (the chronicler [Golivander](../glossary.md)'s path, and **Jeanne Pintos** the natural-philosopher's before him), and the **degree-and-stay** who commit to the descent and never surface. The transients give Thekkavar its reach; the lifers give it its depth.

Apart from all of it stand Enki's clergy, **the Sealed**, who keep the Infinite Library, tend its threshold, and take no part in ruling the open city. They are drawn from all three peoples, and what they hold, they may never tell.

**The city is young and the vault is ancient.** The deepest archives were here before the Crimson Rain, their origin lost; the **Leize** beneath them is Enki's own and older than reckoning. In the **Golden Era** the dwarven Golden Empire, which made its tongue the scholarly standard of the age, built the great Lanterns around those archives and gathered the largest scholarship Talan had ever held, and to that gathering Enki, who had been a wandering scholar before, wandered and sat down, and he has kept his seat ever since. As the knowledge piled deeper, the **sanctum was raised over the most dangerous depth**, and through it the mortal stacks were joined to Enki's Infinite Library. The Empire fell in the Dark Era; the modern city, its Convocation and its writs, is the Adventurer-Era heir, young as a polity and raised once more around a vault far older than itself. Worship here is inquiry: every earned shelf a small rite, every honest question a prayer, and to teach or to keep is devotion.

**◈ Popular Belief: Enki teaches.** They say that now and then a guest lecturer no one quite knows will take a hall and teach, often on ancient history, with the impossible certainty of someone who was *there*, and it will be the finest lecture any of them ever hear, and it will stop short of the one thing the subject was building toward, the withheld part, set down gently with a smile. When the hall empties the master is already gone, and no Institute has record of inviting them. They say it was Enki, who never truly left the lecture hall he sat down in an age ago, and that he teaches his city still, choosing what to give and what to keep exactly as he always has. And it is true. So in Thekkavar you attend every guest lecture and never heckle a visiting master, because it might be Him, and because what such a teacher chooses to withhold is as much a lesson as what they teach. And he is met openly besides: a reader in the stacks now and again finds Enki himself at the shelf, the god browsing the mortal collection with evident pleasure, who will explain, if greeted, that he wandered in by accident while browsing the Infinite one. No door joins the two libraries except through his own sanctum, and everyone knows it, and the explanation is offered and received with complete satisfaction every time: the god's small joke about his own house, and the one fib in Thekkavar nobody has ever wanted corrected.

**⚿ GM Secret: the price in the deep.** The Infinite Library is navigable, to a point; the deeper you go the likelier you lose your way, and once truly lost only Enki can lead you out, lantern of shifting light in the dark. He offers a bargain, and by the equivalent exchange that runs through the Gods' Law, the price for a life is a life's weight. Two doors. **Forget and go:** forget what you found and pay in years off your span, proportional to how deep you wandered, so a scholar lost in the shallows loses a triumph and a little time, and one who plunged near the bottom comes up emptied of the answer and decades older, a youth returned grey in an afternoon. **Remember and serve:** keep what you found, but never speak it and never write it, and give Enki the rest of your days. Those who take the second door are the **Sealed**, each a living sealed Mystery, which is why the deepest knowledge never rises into the open stacks though the clergy hold it: the ones who know it are bound to silence, and they are the gate. The Keeper of the Infinite Library is the eldest of them, holding the most. And there is a depth past which no bargain can balance, where the price would be a whole remaining life, and there Enki does not come; those are the never-led-out, lost for good.

**⚿ GM Secret: what waits in the Leize.** The Infinite Library is not entirely on the Material Plane, and the deeper one walks the further out of the world one goes, which is why the deep holds more than knowledge. Its sheer mass, the forbidden and the merely lost, *draws* things: predators that feed on knowing or are lured by it, gathering in the low stacks the way scavengers gather at a kill. The never-led-out do not fade; they **turn**, centuries in a place half-off the world unmaking a scholar into one of the deep's own dangers. And because the Leize bleeds off the world, Enki is not the only power who can reach a lost soul there: non-bound gods, the demons and devils of Bolverk, and things from further out than any of them can find you first and offer their own deal, and theirs is no equivalent exchange but a trap dressed as rescue, sometimes one that lets you keep the knowledge *and* speak it, at a price paid by more than you. Every closed door in the city above exists so its people stay shallow enough that *Enki* is the one who comes when they slip, and not something with a worse offer. What becomes of the years he collects, and of the knowledge forgotten at the door, and whether the **integration procedure** that the chronicles say persists in the veils has surfaced down there for the wrong buyer, are the deepest Mysteries, and not even the Sealed who walk the threshold hold them.
