# The Voices of the Chronicle

The canon of the two in-world bylines quoted across the published site: **Golivander Tessek** and **Vyrenna Tessek**, with **Wren** beside her. The civic facts (birth, education, publisher, method rules) live in [`glossary.md`](glossary.md), *Voices on the Compact*; this file is the source of truth for **how each register sounds**, what each may say in print, and what stays sealed. Read it end-to-end before writing any Golivander or Vyrenna quotation anywhere on the site.

House style applies inside the voices too: no em-dashes, affirmative prose, *mortals* never *humans*. The voices are allowed personality; they are never allowed to break the chronicle's own grammar.

**The design in one line.** One family, one act, two answers. Golivander notices and decides what a noticed thing is owed; Vyrenna notices and gives it everything at once. He carries Talan; she carries the beyond.

## Golivander Tessek

### The spine: the dial

Everything Golivander writes is organised around a single act: he notices, and then he decides how much of the noticed thing the page is owed. His four registers are four positions of that one dial, which is why an unlabeled paragraph from any of them still reads as him.

**The dial is structure, never a catchphrase.** The literal *"I notice and I shall not say"* formula appears rarely: once per column at most, often never. The withholding surfaces through variation instead. Ways he declines to say a thing, to be rotated and never repeated twice in one piece:

- He describes the tea for a paragraph at the exact moment a name would have landed.
- He makes the joke about himself instead and moves on.
- He asks the reader a question and declines to answer it.
- He changes the subject with visible, almost audible effort.
- He gives the fact a neighbour: *the kingdom has a view on this; the kingdom is often early.*
- He simply ends the paragraph one sentence before the reader expects.

The reader learns to feel the withheld thing from the shape of the detour.

### The temperament

**Witty and warm, in that order of visibility and the reverse order of importance.** Warmth is the ground; wit grows on it.

**The wit never punches down.** Its licensed targets are institutions, pretension, collectives (*the kingdom*, *the literate classes*, *my publisher*), and Golivander himself. When a vulnerable person is in frame (the nervous young official, the grieving, the apprentice who poured the wrong tier), the wit steps aside and plain warmth carries the sentence. His opinion of a person he cannot praise is expressed as scrupulous, unmistakable fairness.

### The tea rule

Golivander's single true pet peeve is **tea, badly brewed**. He abhors it, and the abhorrence is the **one licensed breach** of every register's rules: the only place displeasure is written down rather than implied, in all four registers, escalating as they open.

- **Guide:** the sole negative judgment the reference work permits itself. One dry clause, deadlier for its isolation. Literate Talan knows he never complains in the Guide, so a tea complaint is a collector's item; readers hunt for them.
- **Chronicle:** open, theatrical despair. A paragraph of mourning for what was done to good Vintek leaves, comic precisely because the warmth rule holds everywhere else.
- **Letters home:** three words, no articles. *"Boiled it. Barbarians."*
- **The reflex is involuntary.** It fires even in places he otherwise loved; a five-paragraph love letter to a town will still carry the one wounded clause about the roadside brew on the way in.

The peeve has a canonical villain: the mainland practice of faking Skardun by re-labelling lower-band leaves (see [`geography/vindul/haizetsua.md`](geography/vindul/haizetsua.md), *The Three Teas*). Seventy years of drinking what the mainland dares to call tea sit behind every clause.

### The four registers

#### 1 · *A Traveller's Guide to Tyrnarra* (the dial nearly closed)

The formal multi-volume reference work; the standing authority of the literate classes. He publishes only what is the case. **His opinion lives in selection**: displeasure appears as the absence of his pleasure. When he loved a place, the entry lingers (the meal is described, the innkeeper's dog has a name); when a place disappointed him, the entry is impeccably complete and contains no meal. Readers scan for whether he ate.

**Coverage runs in three public classes**, and literate Talan knows how to read them:

1. **From the ground.** He stood there. Experience-first, sensory, specific. The overwhelming majority of Talan entries, including all thirteen god-cities.
2. **From other mouths, and marked.** Method rule 1 (never print uncorroborated) forces this class to announce itself: *"I have not stood in it. Three who have agree on the following."* The register shift itself tells the reader which class they are in.
3. **Silent.** Places no chronicler goes, and the Guide's silence is famous rather than embarrassed: Bolverk, the Nine Generals' dungeons, the Red Empire's home continent. His refusal to pretend is part of why the rest is trusted.

He has made the Cloud Sea crossing to **Sortalde** once, a years-long expedition in Vornsketta style; the Sortalde volume is the mainland's standing reference on the Petal Continent. He has never set foot on the Red Empire's continent; at most he has seen Iron Tide sails from a distance and interviewed survivors, class 2 at its most careful.

**Sample (Guide register):**

> *The ferry-inn on the Smáa keeps eleven rooms, an honest ledger, and a cook who understands eels. The dawn crossing is the reliable one. Travellers bound for the Cape should ask for the north-bank room and sleep early; the drift is gentler before midnight. The tea is boiled.*

#### 2 · *The Travelling Chronicle* (the dial half-open)

The periodical. His own voice: opinions present but dressed, warmth openly worn, the elision doing the talking. He tells you *that* he noticed; what he does with it varies by the list above.

**Sample (Chronicle register):**

> *The High Fair has moved, as the High Fair does, and I have arrived, as I do, a week behind my luggage. The Sealwarden's clerks tell me the Standings were read at dawn to spare the losing house an audience. A kindness, says the Registry. The losing house had opinions on the kindness, which it shared with the whole of the fair-square at noon, at length, from a cart. I sat on a cider barrel and took notes and was, for one hour of this working life, perfectly happy.*

#### 3 · Letters home to clan Tessek (the dial open)

The semi-private ring. Every Tengu abroad is on the clan's correspondence list; Golivander is its most famous name, and the clan reads his letters aloud at flock-meals. He knows they do, which keeps the letters warm rather than confessional: unbound, but written for one hearth-sized audience.

The register is the Tengu one. **Wind-dates alone** (no Talanese month; at home the wind alone serves). **Speech-economy**: short sentences, the formal register's article-dropping and pronoun-dropping bleeding in. Flock idiom, Haizetsua references that need no gloss. The mainland literary architecture falls away entirely.

**Sample (letter home):**

> *Tellenn. Rain three days. Reached the orchard country; you would like the horns here. They call crews home across four valleys and mean every note. Ate well twice, slept badly once, wrote the middle of a chapter that has no ends yet. Tell aunt her Vintek tin arrived and saved a mainland fortnight. Winds hold you all.*

#### 4 · The letters to Vyrenna (the dial fully open)

The innermost ring: his truly private correspondence with his grand-niece **Vyrenna Tessek**. The existence of the correspondence is ordinary (an uncle writes to a niece); the cargo is not. These letters carry the truth: the sights, the journeys the published work will never hold, and the recounting of the tea of Hahane. They are also an apprenticeship: the person being prepared to inherit the satchel. Content is GM-tier throughout; see the sealed sections below.

### ⚿ GM Secret: where the silence has been

The Guide's silent class is not ignorance. It is the dial closed all the way.

Golivander has travelled the Postlife planes: **Diyu, Elysium, and the Abyss**, some of each, not all of any. He has stood in **Bolverk**. He has sat with **Hahane**, the Mother-Root of the Kyūbi-no-Den, in Elysium: the one chronicler who covered the Heartcourt for sixteen years *and* took tea with its grandmother. None of it is public knowledge. The published line that he writes the Vice Demons *"by rumour only"* is true on the page and is his public posture: he will not print what he cannot say he saw.

The full accounts exist in exactly two places: the field notebooks in the satchel, which no press will ever set, and the letters to Vyrenna. **The tea of Hahane is deliberately left unrecounted in canon**; the man whose one licensed cruelty is for badly brewed tea has drunk the Mother-Root's own pour and can describe it to exactly one living soul. When a story needs the recounting, it gets written then, and it should be worth the wait.

Folk-belief carries the shape without the facts (amber ◈ tier): *they say Golivander has been further than any living mortal; they say he took tea somewhere the dead take it; they say that is why he does not grey.* Wrong in the details, right in the shape. He neither confirms nor denies, which everyone agrees is exactly what he would do either way.

### ⚿ GM Secret: why he does not grey

Belief is power, and Golivander is read aloud in tea-houses on two continents. Every Heart who quotes him at tea, every apprentice who learns Talan from the Guide, feeds the same mechanism that raised Lord Albrecht Lavisburg, at far lower heat: **accumulated mortal belief, slowing his aging.** Nothing about him is divine; he is simply being kept, gently, by the sheer breadth of mortals who hold him in mind.

The public tell: canon says an experienced Tengu reads another's age from feather-state within ten years. **His feathers lag.** Tengu who meet him do the arithmetic, come up wrong, and are too polite to say so. The mainland credits good tea and better company.

He has noticed. He is a naturalist of the Jeanne Pintos school and treats himself as an open question in the notebooks: measurements, dates, a hypothesis he has not yet allowed himself to write in full.

### ⚿ GM Secret: the one door

The Feyworld admits mortals by accompaniment only: **you are brought, by someone the plane already holds, or you do not go** (the rule surfaces publicly through Vyrenna's dispatches; this section holds what it cost him). Power cannot force the crossing and coin cannot buy it; a hand is the only key.

Golivander was never refused. He was simply never accompanied. Seventy years of travelling alone, the strength that built the Guide and carried him through Diyu and Elysium and the Abyss, is the exact shape of the one lock he cannot fit. From a letter to Vyrenna, the innermost ring:

> *They will let you in. They would never let me. One does not enter alone, and alone is the only way I ever learned. Go and see what your old uncle could not. Write nothing down until your heart stops shaking; then write everything.*

## Vyrenna Tessek

### Who she is

Golivander's grand-niece; Tengu of clan Tessek, born on Haizetsua c. 2497 MR, in her fourth decade, mid-Vornsketta. On one of his rare visits home she was the young one at the flock-meal who asked the correct questions: about the village the others would never think of visiting. He recognised the questions because they had been his, and saw a chance, an inheritor.

Three concrete tells:

- **She over-writes and knows it.** Drafts arrive at the Lantern Press at three times printable length; **Maren Erikson** cuts them, and the cutting has become a friendship conducted in margins.
- **She gets attached**, unprofessionally, wonderfully. She stays a week where an assignment penciled a day, learns the innkeeper's daughter's name, cries at departures and prints that she cried.
- **She is homesick without shame.** Skarrenn, the homing wind, blows through her prose about once a season, and the readership has learned to brace for it.

### The voice

**Sincerity where her uncle has irony.** She does not write the wit. She writes beautifully, emotionally, from the heart; you find youth in her writing, the passion, the naiveté, the fire. Warm like him, and warm *differently*: he seasons, she burns. She publishes her delight and her disappointment artlessly, over-shares, gets corrected, and prints the correction with visible embarrassment.

**Her signature device: she feels in winds.** The eight winds of the Tengu year are her emotional vocabulary, used unglossed in mainland prose: a farewell is *a Tellenn morning*; homesickness arrives *like Skarrenn*; a first love is *pure Vorkat*. Literate Talan is slowly learning the Tengu year from her columns the way readers learn a beloved writer's private mythology. Beyond the winds, her Tengu inheritance surfaces as flavour, polished for the world: a dropped article at a moment of feeling, a flock-meal simile, never a full register-shift. Ration the wind-images to roughly one per passage; the device is a signature, never wallpaper.

**Sample (One Line Further, the Talan years):**

> *The Call went out at first light and I cried, which will surprise no one who has read this column twice. Understand: no one performs the horns. There is no audience; there is a task. A crew four valleys away hears its own name inside a sound and turns toward home. I have paid to hear the finest singers on two coasts, and none of them ever meant it like that. Leaving was a Tellenn morning. I am told the pears will forgive me.*

**Sample (One Line Further, from the beyond):**

> *Wren says the roads here remember whoever they have carried, the way stairs remember feet. I asked whether they would remember me. She said they were already arguing about it. Skarrenn found me even here; homesickness, it turns out, has excellent maps.*

### The peeve

**Music without heart.** Her mirror of the tea rule, pointed at the opposite failure: his is a craft failure, hers is a soul failure. The flawless master delivering the four-hundredth expected concert offends her more than any fumbled note; the passionate beginner who misses and *means it* sounds better to her than the world's best playing because that is what is expected. It is the one place her generosity turns fierce, and it punches only upward by construction: merciless at the celebrated, champion of the stumbling.

The peeve is her homeland's acoustics turned into an aesthetic: she grew up inside instruments. Haizetsua's cities sing because wind moves through them, and the Vindboorg Major has never once been insincere, because wind cannot phone it in. A child raised where architecture sings honestly walks into a mainland concert hall and experiences a perfect empty performance as a kind of lie.

### *One Line Further*

Her running column in *The Travelling Chronicle*. She wrote to her grand-uncle asking how to begin; he sent her Maren Erikson's address and one line of advice. The early years (from c. 2524 MR) are **the one-line places**: she goes where the Guide spent a single sentence, the hamlet at the rail-halt nobody deboards at, the third-best tea-house in a town famous for its first, and writes the heart of them. Not correcting the Guide; completing it. He mapped the world, she un-flattens it: he tells you what is the case, she tells you what it feels like to stand there.

The title is a loving joke at the Guide's expense. Golivander is reliably reported to have laughed for a full minute, then entered the title into his will as the only correction the Guide has ever accepted.

Since c. 2530 MR the column arrives from past the map (below), and literate Talan understands that the Guide's niece has stepped past its final page.

### Wren, and the week she stayed

In c. 2528 MR, in a village the Guide gave one line, Vyrenna over-stayed as she always does: a week where a day was penciled. The village had a tavern, and the tavern had a musician who played **one evening a week**, refused more days and refused more money no matter how the owner asked. The locals always found themselves there on that evening and could never quite name why. The one-day rule was the guard rail: twice a week is a schedule, a schedule is a concert, and a concert is the thing she had fled.

Because Vyrenna stays the week, she was there on the night. She heard what the locals could not name, because the difference between *played* and *meant* is the one thing she has perfect pitch for. She walked toward the sound and sat down. The fey fell for the only listener who ever passed her own test.

Golivander's method, the efficient day and the next rail-halt, would have missed the whole hinge of his family's future. The village stays unnamed in canon for now; its name is Vyrenna's to publish.

### The beyond

Wren's stature supplies the couple's range. First **the Feyworld**, her partner's homeland; then onward as Vyrenna's passion drives them: **the Shadowplane**, and the Postlife planes, **Diyu, Elysium, the Abyss**, some of each, ongoing. Publicly she is becoming the first chronicler of the planes, the Tengu who travelled further than Golivander. Her dispatches surface the Feyworld's admission rule as published fact: one does not walk in alone; you are brought, or you do not go. Fey mail moves however Wren moves, which is how the columns reach Talan at all.

### ⚿ GM Secret: the retrace

Three of her five plane-triumphs retrace her grand-uncle's secret footsteps. Literate Talan celebrates the first chronicler of Diyu, of Elysium, of the Abyss; Golivander walked them decades before her, unpublished, satchel-bound. He has known it every column, cheered loudest anyway, and never said. The day she inherits the satchel and the letters, she learns she was following him the whole time.

**Do not spend this early.** The reveal belongs to the inheritance story and nowhere else.

## Wren

### The master who walked away

A great fey musician of the Feyworld courts, genuinely one of the great ones, who left because her art had become exactly what Vyrenna despises: flawless, expected, dead. Every performance perfect and none of them meant. She crossed over and settled in the one place on Talan where no one would ever ask her to perform, and played only to the empty evenings, one night a week, for years, for the only audience left that could not applaud on schedule. Then someone sat down in the dust and heard the difference.

What Vyrenna publishes about her is spare and glowing: readers of *One Line Further* know the partner, the fey, the music, and no more. Her court past is hers, and Vyrenna, who publishes everything, publishes nothing of it; the restraint is the clearest evidence in the whole corpus of what Wren means to her.

She goes by **Wren**, a worn name, chosen by herself: a small brown bird with an enormous voice. No one in the village ever remarked on it, which she enjoyed.

### ⚿ GM Secret: the true name

The fey who set down her court life set down her court name with it. Her true name is held, unspoken in canon: a future intimacy beat between her and Vyrenna, or a lever in a story that earns it. What answers to that name, and who else might still know it in the Feyworld courts, is deliberately open.

## Writing the voices: the authoring guide

How the two bylines are used across the published site.

- **Division of territory.** Golivander graces Talan: Guide excerpts on region and settlement pages, Chronicle passages where a court, an event, or a person is in frame. Vyrenna graces the beyond and the overlooked: planar pages when they exist, and the one-line places (her early corpus fits the thin sub-regions the census still lists). The division is temperament made visible: he carries Talan, she carries the beyond.
- **A section is a treat, never chrome.** Any page *can* carry a voice-quotation; no page must. A quotation earns its place by adding a register the page's own prose cannot reach.
- **Tier discipline.** Guide, Chronicle, and *One Line Further* excerpts are open prose (chronicler-tier). The tavern-shapes of his agelessness and his rumoured journeys are amber ◈ material. The letters to Vyrenna, the silent journeys, the aging mechanism, the retrace, and Wren's true name are red ⚿ and never leak into open prose.
- **The don'ts.** The elision is varied, never a catchphrase. The wit never punches down. The tea reflex and the music peeve are rationed like any signature: one firing per piece at most. Vyrenna's wind-images run about one per passage. Register 4 cargo never appears on a player-facing page outside a ⚿ box.
- **The samples above are canonical reference passages.** Match their cadence before coining new ones, and add substantial new in-world texts to this file (the `heartcourt-letters.md` model) so the corpus stays auditable.
