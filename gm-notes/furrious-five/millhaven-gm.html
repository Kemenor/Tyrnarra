The Town

Millhaven grew the way most good things do, by accident. A mill was built where the Smáa narrowed before its run to the Midarra coast. A waystation followed. Then a market. Then, when boatmen discovered the river-mouth gave them shelter, a dock. Then another dock. Then a second market to serve the first dock's crews, and then someone built a tavern on the bridge between the two sides and realised they were printing coin.

The town sits precisely on the border between the Dreaming Cape and Itsasalda, and precisely on neither. The Cape side smells of salt, woodsmoke, and something faintly strange the locals call the drift, a mild blurring that shows up mostly as déjà vu, the occasional misremembered morning, and fishermen who swear they have hauled the same net twice. The Stillside smells of grain, horses, and fresh bread. The bridge houses smell of opportunity.

Millhaven is warm, hardworking, and deeply resistant to being told what to do by anyone not from here, which includes the three factions on its own council. It has survived storms, a blockade, two merchant wars, and one very confused stretch of days that is never discussed. It will survive whatever the party brings to it, and probably profit from it.

Strategic Position
Why Millhaven Matters
The Smáa carries goods down to the Midarra coast and the central sea lanes, and the town sits where the dream-port culture of the Dreaming Cape meets the rail-and-harbour network of Itsasalda. Moonwatch and the Cape's Wakehelm pilots lie up the coast to the west; Bellhalt and the Halfling rail-lines run east; the grain of the interior moves through the Stillside wharves toward Merkavar. Millhaven sits at the seam of all of it, which is exactly why the Adventurers' Guild keeps an office here.
Magic & Services

As a settlement of its size, Millhaven supports two dedicated practitioners: Oswin Thetch, a wizard who runs a shop called the Empirical Eye on the Cape side, dealing in scrolls, components, and minor enchantments; and Sister Margot of Shuun, a water cleric whose healing house doubles as the Cape's de-facto temple. The Stillside keeps a Jianna held-deal shrine tended by a lay priest, where Stillside bargains are sealed and witnessed, and a travelling cleric of Jianna comes through on market days.

Uncommon items can be sourced with a few days' notice. Rare items require sending up the coast or waiting for the right ship. Nothing legendary is available locally, but locals know someone who knows someone.

The Three Faces of Millhaven

Ask someone where they are from in Millhaven and they will say "the Cape," "the Stillside," or "the bridges," never just "Millhaven." The three districts are distinct enough that they feel like separate towns that agreed, grudgingly, to share a name.

The Cape Side · Dreaming Cape ground
The older half of Millhaven, on ground that belongs to the Dreaming Cape. Weathered wood and river stone, narrow salt-crusted streets. A faint drift runs through it, strongest in the oldest lanes; locals set their clocks twice a day and do not make firm plans past noon. The fishing fleet docks here, and so do the boats that would rather their cargo went unlisted.
  • The Sodden Mast · roughest tavern in town, best fish stew, free-traders welcome if they pay.
  • The Empirical Eye · Oswin Thetch's magic shop. Cluttered, reliable, oddly competitive on price.
  • Sister Margot's House · healing house and temple of Shuun; also the Cape's unofficial dispute resolution.
  • The Drift Quarter · oldest streets, strongest drift. Locals avoid it after dark, not for danger, just for the disorientation.
The Bridges · neither bank · both
Three bridges span the Smáa, and over two centuries the buildings on them have merged, stacked, and sprawled until they are effectively a floating district. No bank's tax applies: the Cape claims the far footings, the Stillside claims theirs, and the bridges are famously unresolved. Lately the sprawl has driven pilings into the riverbed between the spans, carrying sheds, storerooms, a respectable tea house, and two private docks reachable only by skiff or by climbing down from the Low Span. The Watch has no jurisdiction here. Nobody who uses those docks wants it to.
  • The Wayward Compass · Adventurers' Guild Office, on the central bridge. See dossier ↗
  • Clearwater Exchange · the bridge merchants' trading house. Legitimate front, excellent wine, poor memory.
  • The Low Span · southernmost bridge, lowest over the water; market stalls by morning, a fighting pit by night. See dossier ↗
  • The Toll House · technically collects bridge toll, actually collects information. Run by Vara Mink.
  • The Pilings & the Quiet Docks · the river sprawl between the spans; two private berths, no manifest required, someone always seems to be expecting you.
The Stillside · Itsasalda ground
Wider streets, taller granaries, a proper market square. The Stillside is where the money is counted and the imported grain is stored, and it keeps the steadfast tide-watching temper of Itsasalda: the dock that does not move while the world passes through. The drift dies at the river, and the clocks here keep true. The Merchant Council's formal meeting house is here, and so is the Stillside Watch, who have jurisdiction over exactly this bank and are quietly furious about the bridges.
  • The Held Stone · the Jianna held-deal shrine where Stillside bargains are sealed and witnessed. Ancient, well-kept, genuinely beloved.
  • The Council Hall · formal seat of the Merchant Council. Arguments echo in the rafters.
  • The Grain Exchange · where the imported grain is weighed, priced, and entered on the held-deal registry.
  • The Golden Plough · best inn in town. Clean, expensive, frequented by merchants and guild representatives.
The Merchant Council of Millhaven

Nineteen seats. Three factions. No permanent majority. The Council governs through consensus, economic leverage, and occasional spectacular shouting matches. Decisions require ten votes. The Cape holds eight, the Stillside holds eight, one short each, always. The Bridge merchants hold three, and every vote they cast is a political event. To pass anything, a major faction needs at least two of the three bridge seats, so the bridge bloc negotiates internally before it negotiates externally, and nothing is ever simple.

The Current Crisis
A ship carrying a combined grain-and-cargo shipment, backed by both Cape and Stillside council money, went missing three weeks ago on the Midarra run. The Stillside blames Cape negligence. The Cape blames a rival out of the Muino-saila ports. The bridge merchants have called for an independent investigation, and everyone suspects they already know what happened.

The Stillside
8 seats · Speaker: Bram Aldover, grain-master
Tap ▾
The oldest money in Millhaven. The Stillside weighs the grain that feeds the town, the river trade, and half the coast's shipping; it knows it and expects appropriate gratitude.
Bram Aldover speaks with the patience of a man who has watched every faction think it can outmanoeuvre him and then watched it fail. He is not clever in a flashy way. He is relentless. The Stillside wants two things: control of the Grain Exchange pricing and a Watch that actually patrols the river docks on their side. They will trade almost anything for the second. Eight seats is the largest bloc, and still one short of a majority; that one vote has cost Bram more sleep than he admits.
What They Want
  • Cheaper river-toll rates for grain shipments
  • A Watch commander loyal to Stillside law, not bridge custom
  • The missing ship found; they had 40% of the cargo
  • Bridge merchant tax declarations, which they suspect are fabricated
⚓
The Cape Side
8 seats · Speaker: Dara Wynn, former captain
Tap ▾
The Cape faction controls the docks, the fleet, and the river mouth, the actual throat of Millhaven's trade. Without their ships the grain sits in warehouses and the market dies.
Dara Wynn gave up her own vessel to take a council seat, which she describes as "the same job with worse weather and no horizon." She is sharp, direct, and quietly furious about the missing ship, not for the money but because she personally vouched for the captain. The Cape faction keeps a flexible relationship with the Midarra free-traders and does not officially acknowledge the boats that use the Cape docks at midnight. Unofficially, those boats pay excellent mooring fees. Eight seats matches the Stillside exactly, and neither side can pass anything alone; Dara finds this less frustrating than most, because she is used to tides.
What They Want
  • The missing ship investigated without Stillside interference
  • Right to extend dock jurisdiction to the river mouth
  • A contract with the Guild to escort high-value Cape shipments
  • Formal recognition of the Midarra free-traders as legitimate partners
⚖️
The Bridge Merchants
3 seats · the swing vote
Tap ▾
Three seats, three very different agendas, and the casting vote on every contested decision. The Bridge faction is less a faction than a negotiation permanently in progress.
The bridge holds three council seats by tradition, one for each of the major bridge institutions. In practice that means the Guildmaster of the Wayward Compass, a theatrical Kitsune who is definitely not a pirate lord, and the most uncomfortable legitimate businessman in Millhaven all vote together. They agree on almost nothing except that the bridge district should stay ungoverned by either bank. With Cape and Stillside locked at eight apiece, any motion needs ten votes, so any motion needs at least two bridge seats. Two, not three. One bridge member can always defect, and all three know it, and they spend considerable energy making sure neither of the others does so first.
What They Want
  • Bridge district formally recognised as self-governing
  • Continued ambiguity about toll-collection jurisdiction
  • The Guild to stay neutral in inter-faction disputes (Aldric's line)
  • Open sea lanes and light oversight on Midarra traffic (Sable Rei's line)
  • Predictable, enforceable contracts and actual law (Tomas's line)

Aldric Fenn
Guildmaster · Wayward Compass · Bridge Seat

Runs the Wayward Compass with a veteran's pragmatism and a quiet moral core. On the council he votes the Guild's chartered neutrality, and turns both banks down with equal warmth.
Both factions court him constantly; he refuses them with a warmth that infuriates everyone equally. He is one of the most powerful people in Millhaven and works very hard to make sure the town does not fully realise it. The work he brings the party himself is the quiet, off-the-books kind: not illegal, just not for the council's ears.

Sable Rei
Merchant Lord · Bridge Seat · the Clearwater docks
Tap ▾
A Kitsune of indeterminate age who introduces themselves differently on different days. Almost certainly a pirate lord with an impeccable paper trail and a fleet registered in six different ports.
Charming, theatrical, and genuinely fun to be around, right up until they are not. Their fox form has silver-black fur and they have been known to shift mid-sentence for emphasis. On the council they vote for open water and light oversight, and they always have a reasonable-sounding justification ready.

Tomas Hewer
Owner · Clearwater Exchange · Bridge Seat
Tap ▾
Built the Clearwater Exchange from a single Low Span stall into the bridge district's primary legitimate trading house over thirty years of relentless, lawful work. Deeply proud of this.
He is acutely aware that his two council colleagues are, respectively, the Guildmaster of a world-spanning power and a shapeshifting probable pirate lord, and that his vote is the one that makes the bridge bloc function. He drinks more than he used to. He is not corrupt. He would like everyone to stop testing that.

Read Aloud to Players · Arrival
Read Aloud

You smell Millhaven before you see it. Salt and woodsmoke from the west, bread and horses from the east, and under both of them river water, cold and constant, carrying everything the town touches down to the sea.

The town sits on both banks of the Smáa, where it narrows before its final run to the coast. The western bank is the Cape side: older, saltier, streets worn smooth by generations of fisherfolk who never quite made it back to wherever they came from, the buildings leaning slightly toward the water as if thinking about it. The eastern bank is the Stillside, wider-streeted and better-kept, smelling of the grain warehouses around the market square and the held-deal shrine that has stood there since before anyone's grandfather was born.

Between them, straddling the river on three bridges that were never meant to hold this many buildings, is everything else: shops and stalls and offices and taverns, stacked and cantilevered over the water, connected by staircases that don't quite meet and walkways that technically belong to no one. The Smáa runs dark beneath it all, unbothered.

On the widest of the three bridges, above a sign that swings in the river wind, is the office you are looking for: a compass rose, painted gold, slightly faded. The Wayward Compass. The door is open. It usually is.

Millhaven is a town that has survived everything it has been asked to survive and profited from most of it. It is warm, and loud, and has opinions about everything. It will probably have opinions about you, before long. Welcome.

GM Tone Notes

Millhaven is cheerful about its own chaos. The three-faction council, the unlit boats at the Cape docks, the drift in the old lanes: locals treat all of it as normal. They have built a working town out of contradictions and they are quietly proud of it. The party should feel like they have landed somewhere alive, not somewhere waiting to be rescued.

The river is the spine. Every district has a relationship with the Smáa: the Cape works it, the Stillside sells across it, the bridges live on it. When in doubt, put a scene near the water; it gives you ambient noise, movement, and the reminder that the coast and the open Midarra are right there at the end of it.

The council conflict is background radiation, not a main plot. It should be felt, in overheard arguments, in which merchants are friendly and which are not, in the Watch's visible frustration; the party should not be asked to solve it in session one. Let it simmer. Let them notice it themselves.

The drift is subtle. Do not lead with it. Let a player notice that a fisherman seems confused about what day it is, or that the clocks on the Cape side do not match the ones on the Stillside. The Drift Quarter is there when they go looking. They do not need to go looking yet. (For what the drift actually is, see the player page's GM Secret: it is the Dreaming Cape's Wellspring Stillpool-river reaching its eastern edge.)
