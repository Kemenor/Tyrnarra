The Narrows Job
The line goes dead at the Arrol Cut. The doors bang open at both ends. A calm voice calls a signal you don't know.

The Contract
Read Aloud · Posted at the Wayward Compass
GUARD WANTED — RAIL CONSIGNMENT, MERKAVAR LINE. Chartered escort sought for a sealed shipment leaving Merkavar by rail. Two prior runs through the hill country were stopped and stripped; the line wants blades aboard who can hold a car. Travel to Merkavar at the Guild's coin, board the consignment, ride it through, deliver it whole. Pay on delivery. The Compass vouches the contract; it does not vouch the cargo. — See Aldric Fenn. Neutral charter applies.
GM Note · Fenn's Position
Aldric Fenn votes the Guild's chartered neutrality and asks nothing about the manifest, which is exactly why the job is here and not with the Stillside Watch. The real client is House Eisenhart, through a Merkavar broker; Fenn knows only that the coin is good and the route is cursed. He briefs the party flat: ride it, hold it, deliver it. He mentions that two crews already tried and failed, and lets that sit.
GM Note · Why the Job Came to Millhaven
Merkavar is the densest information-market on the continent: kitsune brokers read every freight-yard, the smile-watching crowd clocks every stranger. Hire escorts in Merkavar and the cargo's existence is market-gossip before the ink dries; the buyer hears, the robbers hear. The first two runs were leaked exactly that way. So Eisenhart's broker reached past the city to the Wayward Compass at Millhaven: a neutral-chartered office under a man who asks nothing, in a bridge-town where unlisted cargo is a way of life. Outsider blades nobody in Merkavar is watching. Stahl wants people who do not ask, because in Merkavar everyone who asks, tells.
The Beats

1 · Take the job at the Wayward Compass in Millhaven. Sister Wenna and the factor Edrik Stahl are travelling the same run.

2 · The river to Merkavar — two days by barge up the Itsasalda water-route, into the lake and the grand arrival.

3 · The rail station — meet Stahl, see the consignment loaded, board the outbound train.

4 · The ride out — Wenna slips rear; the line dies at the Arrol Cut; the ambush.

5 · The running fight — ambush in Steerage → rearward under fire across the Gun Flat → free Wenna in the Brakeman's Van → the lull, heal up → forward, fresh, into the Strongbox and Brannek Vael.

6 · Aftermath — the crate, the Wright's claim, who walks away holding it.

The Throughline
The Wright's footprint is everywhere if you look: a brass three-spoke maker's mark stamped on a coupling at the yard, on the jammed brake, on the over-pressured furnace-tap. Players who clock the mark early piece together that it is an inside job before the reveal. The planted accomplice in Steerage is the same hand at work.
The Approach · By Boat
Read Aloud · Into the Lake
For two days the river holds you close: brown water, overhanging green, the bargeman's pole finding the bottom. Then the banks fall away on both sides at once, the current goes still and wide and impossibly clear beneath the hull, and you understand you are no longer on a river. The lake of Merkavar opens like a held breath let go. At its centre, rising straight from the water with no shore to stand on, is Helgafjall: a peak crowned in snow that no summer has ever taken, mirrored perfectly in water that no winter has ever frozen. The bargeman touches two fingers to his brow without being asked. And on the far shore, the city. It does not resolve so much as accumulate — wharves, then market-tiers stacked on the wharves, then whole quarters stacked on the tiers, then the great pale domes of the Assembly over all of it, and the nearer you draw the larger it refuses to stop being, until the masts in the harbour are a burned forest and the sound carries to you across the water: one vast murmur, the tide-roar of a hundred thousand bargains struck at once in more tongues than you can sort, threaded through with bells, and every bell a deal sealed. You have seen cities. You have not seen this. A god lives here, and the city has grown to the size of the saying so. Your boat slips in among a thousand others, and over the harbour-mouth she stands to receive you: Jianna, three storeys of lake-pale stone, a veiled woman with one hand open and offered to every hull that comes in, and one hand closed at her side. Beneath her bare feet, worked into the plinth so you pass over it as you dock, a thing is trodden into the stone: a taloned, gilded shape, a demon of hoarded gold, her clawed reach broken under the goddess's heel. The bargeman spits neatly into the water as you pass it. "Merkavar," he says, as if that explains it. "Where the deal is honest." He does not say tries to be. He doesn't have to. You're already close enough to see the beggars under the open hand.

The city treads her in marble and haggles her in its heart every hour. What Merkavar stands for. Or tries to.

Failure (<17): The surface read only — a greed-demon under the goddess of fair trade. No name.
Critical Failure (≤7): A confident misread (wrong vice, or "Merkavar banned greed outright"). A local within earshot winces.
GM Note · The Statue
Merkavar's civic boast: honest commerce mastering predatory greed. The irony runs two ways and stays GM/folk-tier — far out in the Order of Steam bay, Rika Tikur keeps Nirfel's shrines openly and prospers on her closed fist, and Greed is one of Jianna's own four faces, weighed in every Merkavar deal. A Lore (Merkavar / Mercantile) reaches the Success tier but not the Critical irony; Society gets only the civic-boast read.

Meeting the Factor
Read Aloud · The Platform
He finds you before you find him: a short, damp Dwarf in a too-good coat, a strongbox chained to his wrist and a ledger clamped under the other arm like a man holding his own ribs in. "You're the Compass blades. Good. You're late, the schedule is not mine to move, and the cargo does not get discussed." Edrik Stahl says his own name as though it costs him. He counts you twice, glances down the platform at nothing, and lowers his voice. "Whatever you were told the run is worth, it is worth more to the wrong people. Get it to the bay. Ask me nothing." Behind him, a broad Halfling woman with a coin already in her fingers tips it into the gutter-stream and murmurs something to the water.
◈ Asking Edrik What's Inside — Diplomacy
DC 23 · Edrik L3 (DC 18) + Very Hard 5 (under orders to say nothing) · soften to DC 20 with real leverage · Deception or Intimidation substitute
Critical Success (≥33): A half-truth — "Order work. Reclaimed. Going somewhere it'll be locked away and forgotten, which is where it belongs. That's the most you get."
Success (≥23): He grumbles something useful despite himself — "It's worth robbing, that's why you're aboard. Two crews already tried. Now shut yer trap."
Failure (<23): Grumpy stonewall — "The cargo is not discussed. Watch the doors, not my ledger."
Critical Failure (≤13, realistically a fumble): He decides you ask far too many questions to trust and moves to cancel the contract. (Fenn can salvage it, or the party rides on uncontracted — the train still gets robbed.)
GM Note · Boarding
Stahl rides the Strongbox car with the sealed crate; the party and Sister Wenna take Steerage. The consignment is brokered Eisenhart steel and a single chained "almost," outbound to a buyer. Let the players settle and meet a civilian or two before Wenna excuses herself rearward (the toilet) and the train rolls.
The Line Dies
Read Aloud · The Arrol Cut
The clatter of the rail is the kind of sound you stop hearing. Which is why you feel it before you understand it: the song under the wheels goes wrong, a long iron groan, and the car lurches as the brakes bite hard enough to throw the standing. Through the window the canyon walls of the Arrol Cut close in on both sides, and ahead, where the track should run on, a fresh fall of rock sits across the rails like it grew there. Then the doors at both ends of the car bang open at once, and the first voice you hear is calm, almost kind, calling a signal you don't know: "Forward car, hold. Rear car, take them."
GM Note · The Trigger
That calm voice is Brannek Vael, forward at the Strongbox, already cracking the crate. The crew boards from both ends; the planted accomplice (the Hryngar Taskmaster seeded in Steerage) drops their disguise and calls the boarders in. The party is between the rear press and the forward objective, exactly as Vael wants them.

Run-of-Play

The party starts in Steerage (4). The rear is Gun Flat (5) then Brakeman's Van (6); the Strongbox (3) is forward. The intended shape is three real fights plus a rescue:

Steerage ambush → rearward through the Gun Flat's fire → free Wenna in the Van → the lull, heal to full → turn forward, fresh, into the Strongbox climax. The party doubles back because you don't leave a captured ally and a flanking crew at your back. The boiler clock in the Cab and the Coal Tender stay forward and optional.

Pacing · ~540 XP across the train
This is a running set-piece, not six rest-separated fights. Run it as escalating waves: the boarders press over rounds, the party fights forward, and only the Strongbox needs them fresh — which the rescued healer guarantees. Scale how many board to the party's attrition. The freed Sister Wenna resets the party after the lead-up fights, so the severe boss math (150 XP) holds.

Vael rigged the cab's mana-furnace to over-pressure: a rising-pressure complex hazard with the Wright's brass maker's-mark on the tap. Brannek Vael · The Signal-Wright
Read Aloud · First Words
He does not look like a bandit-lord. He looks like a foreman near the end of a long shift: square, grey-bearded, an exo-frame of fine brass-and-leather bracing one shoulder, ember-patient eyes that have read a hundred gauges. When he speaks it is the unhurried cadence of a man calling signals down a line he built. "Twenty years I set the gear that keeps this stretch from killing fools. They broke me over the one thing that was mine. Tonight I take it back. You can stand aside, or you can be in the way of a man who is only here for one box."

GM Note · Running Vael
He announces his moves before he makes them, the way he called signals. He fights to finish and leave, not to wipe the party: he wants the crate, not a massacre. He opens with thrown charges from behind his two casters, uses Foul Concoction to screen the vault, and will cut and run with the crate the moment he can — a fleeing Vael with the prize is a better story than a dead one. He kills Edrik only if the factor does something stupid.

A Cape-side Halfling of Millhaven, bargewoman's daughter, cleric of Shuun of the working water. Broad, unhurried, river-calloused; tips a coin to any current she crosses and means it.

Edrik Stahl
Eisenhart factor · non-combatant (L3)
Tap ▾
A fussy, sweating Eisenhart house-clerk shepherding the consignment and a locked manifest he will not let out of his sight. He knows what is in the crate and dreads anyone learning it.
Useless in a brawl, invaluable after: he can name every car, every seal, and — under pressure (see the Diplomacy check) — what the Wright is really after. What Happens After

The chained "almost" is the Wright's own work: a signal-relay device that can seize a Magitrain's safety-gear from outside the cab — a master-key to the southern line's signals. The Order confiscated it when they broke him and quietly sold it onward; tonight it was riding to a buyer who should never hold it. Brannek robs the train to take it back, and he is not wrong that it is his.

The open question the party inherits: it cannot stay with Vael, it cannot reach the buyer, and the Order does not want it found. Whoever carries it off the Arrol Cut decides who owns the rail's blind spots. The boiler Vael over-pressured in the cab is the same lesson in miniature — he wanted the party to see that the line answers to him.

If the Party Holds the Crate
Stahl pays in full and wants it locked away and forgotten. The Guild takes no position. But the device is worth a kingdom's ransom to a Pirate Lord, an Order rival, or the original buyer, and someone will come asking. A thread for later: who told the buyer it was on this run, and is that the same someone who leaked the first two?
If Vael Escapes with It
A fleeing Wright with the master-key is the better hook. He vanishes back into the Order of Steam's volcanic belt, and within a season the southern line starts having "accidents" only he can explain. The party may be hired to bring him in — or to decide he was right.
The Wright's Claim
Vael is sympathetic on purpose. If the party talked to him, they may not want to hand the device to the Order any more than to the buyer. Leave it unresolved. Let them argue about who deserves to hold the rail's blind spots.
