# Defining Ancestries: the conventions

How to write a Tyrnarra ancestry. Distilled from the 2026 ancestry-rebalance pass; applies to every
future people, per-tribe pick, heritage, and god-city reconciliation. Companion to the standing rules
in [`CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md). The rebalance tracker (`ancestry-rebalance-pass.md`) closed with the
pass and is deleted; its full decision log lives in git history, and the still-open items were
harvested into [`open-threads.md`](open-threads.md) and [`god-city-seeds.md`](god-city-seeds.md).

## The core rule

**An ancestry is a feeling: the temperament a traveller meets, anywhere on Talan.** Cultures, customs,
institutions, naming schemes, rites, and dress belong to *places*; a sub-region's culture is kept by
everyone who lives there, and the same people lives differently in every land that holds it.

The test for every sentence in a feeling: **does it still describe a member of this people far from
their heartland?** If it only describes them in one region, it is culture; move it to that region's text.

## The ten rules

1. **Feeling, not culture.** No practices in the ancestry text. A naming scheme, a rite, a council, a
   token, a reckoning custom: all of it belongs to a sub-region. (The trap that keeps recurring:
   "Hobgoblin elders introduce by their oldest oath" was a culture; "the given word is load-bearing"
   is the feeling.)

2. **Feeling, not job.** No occupational definitions. "Diggers," "soldiery," "herders," "clerks" are
   what a people *does somewhere*, not what they *are*. Occupations may appear at domain level as
   economy (the grain/herd/ore triangle), but the feeling must generalize: Ratfolk are not tunnel-crews,
   they are *every question has an answer*; the same temperament cracks a cipher, a market, a negotiation.

3. **They are not the god's races.** A god's portfolio is *inspiration, never mapping*. Sometimes the
   faces genuinely sort one-per-people (Enki: Wisdom keeps, Mystery calls, Discovery finds); sometimes
   the frame is the god's tenses (Tani), or stances toward the domain's weather (Vesuna), or the bare
   element with no god-language at all (fire, wind). Choose the frame the domain offers and drop it the
   moment it forces anything. **Never write aspect-catechisms** ("Flame is X; Passion is Y; …" per
   people): that turns peoples into sermons.

4. **Individually shaped, not templated.** A shared frame (a closing coda, a one-verb summary) is fine;
   the *bodies* of the entries must each have their own shape, length, and texture. If every entry is
   one verb plus an element-relation, the template is doing the defining. Find the one word that IS the
   people (Dwarf = *methodical*) and watch for neighbour-words that belong to someone else
   (steadfast = Minotaur; the long view = Elf; place-memory = Leshy; time-memory = Ghoran).

5. **The dark face has three moves.** A god's dark aspect is never just assigned: either **no people
   claims it** (Arrogance, the shared sin threatening each people from its own side), or **nature
   itself claims it** (Cruelty: the cruelty of survival, not machination; no one needs to be cruel for
   life to have cruelty), or **one people knows it from the inside** (Hobgoblins and Tyranny, Conrasu
   and Madness): knowing it from the inside means having the standing to name it, never embodying it.

6. **No migration framing.** When a heartland moves in the meta, the people *always were there* in
   the world. No "gone south," no "kept herds here instead," no origin-journey invented to explain a
   rebalance. (Genuine in-world historical migrations already in canon, like the Eizhalun crossing,
   stay; they are story, not bookkeeping.)

7. **The peoples of a domain form a structure.** Phases of one hunt, three tenses, four stances, four
   answers to one fire: the set should interlock so that each people believes its part is the essential
   one. The tension is generative and cross-people (the watch and the rail; the two lights and the
   ballast; keeper, called, finder), never a ranking.

8. **Placement is separate from feeling, and optional.** Feelings come first; heartlands second;
   culture last. Where the map offers it, one sub-region holds a people's fullest expression, but not
   every sub-region needs a people (faith-defined and floor-defined regions stay unsorted), god-cities
   belong to everyone, and an unassigned heartland is fine: assigning it or not does not change the
   feel of the ancestry.

9. **Check the flanks.** A new feeling must not collide with an existing people's defining word, in its
   own domain or any other. When two peoples share a verb (Vanara ride change; Strix ride weather),
   the elements must differ enough that meeting the two peoples feels different. And **an ancestry
   describes itself, never itself-through-others**: cross-people contrasts belong in the flank-check
   and the domain-structure coda, not inside a people's own feeling text.

10. **One concrete proof per feeling.** Each entry earns one image or gesture that makes the
    temperament unmistakable: the sorrow is in the skin I left; reads wreckage the way a Dwarf reads a
    schematic; the one still there when everyone else has gone home. Abstract adjectives without the
    proof are a draft, not a definition.

## Method (per region)

Feelings first → placement second → culture last. Read the domain file and the god's
subtitle/domains before proposing (ask for the portfolio if unrecorded). Surface proposed file-prose in
chat **before** writing anything; corrections produce new text and are **not** a yes; on the explicit
yes, write the files and commit in the same motion.
