## (A) `lore/geography/lioaru/river-duchies.md`

# The River Duchies (Lioaru)

*Written under the [`region-prose.md`](../../../docs/region-prose.md) contract (2026-10-07). Parent domain: [`../lioaru.md`](../lioaru.md). The column quoted throughout is [`river-duchies-chronicle.md`](river-duchies-chronicle.md).*

**Etymology:** plain Talanese, the Golden Empire's name for the duchies of its granary province, kept when the valley was cut back out of the thicket. Adjective: **Duchy**; its people, **the Duchy folk**. The valley's own words are in **Ameslay**: **Lemersa**, **the Mida**, **the Hemmal**, the slow isles **Tamegra**, **Tamtunt** and **Azay**, and **Agu**. (Derivations in the glossary.)

**Position:** the south of Lioaru, on the Hafra. North and west, Galdua Jendea, its pale band running along the western fields toward the Lost Kingdom; east, Hareaveldi across the river line; north-east, Emarrea, upstream; south, the sea, with the slow isles off the river mouths.

**Terrain:** a green valley of about 50,000 square miles between the dune seas. One great river comes down from Emarrea's highlands and spreads across the plain into a fan of slow channels; the main stream reaches the sea at the head of a deep bay. Off the river mouths of the south-east, a chain of three islands built from the flood's silt, tidal flats between them, and beyond them, in the fog, Agu.

**Character:** the slowest rivers on Talan run through its fastest ground: everything grows in a week and rots in the next, and the Duchies have made the rot their trade.

**Peoples:** about 1.8 million, Lioaru's most populous country. Nagaji and Azarketi the most numerous, the Azarketi on the channels and the isles; kitsune in the upriver towns; few Ghoran; the rest the southern mix. Every family's grandparents came from somewhere else.

**Tongue:** Ameslay; Talanese on the barges and the quays.

**Faith:** Tani, and above all her Decay, which most of Lioaru only resists and this valley reveres.

**Rule:** eleven **dukes** under **the cut law**, each in a duchy; **the Mida**, the dukes sitting together at Lemersa, for what concerns them all; in the flood, **the flood-warden**. **Tassadit Uttis Ath Akli** is the Mida's eldest.

**Capital:** **Lemersa**, at the head of the bay where the river meets the sea, about 40,000. It belongs to no duke.

**Founded:** the duchies under the Golden Empire; refounded at the return, at the end of the Dark Era.

## The quick valley

> *At dawn the market is heaped with fruit that ripened in the night. By noon it is soft, and the carts come round.*
> Golivander Tessek, *The Travelling Chronicle*, 2509 MR

In the River Duchies everything that grows and everything that rots does it quickly. A field gives three harvests between floods. A fig that sets in one week is ripe in the next and gone over in the week after. Bread left out overnight is furred by morning, and the dead, laid in the black earth, are part of the next year's field within a season. People and animals live at the ordinary pace; the quickening is for the crops, the fruit, the ferments and the rot. The Ghoran, being of the green, feel it: the few who live in the valley flower out of season and grow restless, and most move on.

The quickening ends at the valley's edge. Grain carried up to Galdua's rocks or onto Valreka's whales keeps as well as anyone's, and the slow isles off the coast keep everything.

**The turning.** At dawn the stalls are heaped with fruit that ripened in the night. By noon it is soft, and the vinegar-makers', the date-wine brewers' and the fish-sauce yards' carts go round the market buying it by the basket, at a price the morning's seller had counted on. By evening it is in the vats. Decay is the side of Tani most of Lioaru only resists; here it is revered, and it is also the trade: date wine reckoned in floods, river vinegar, fish sauce, a cheese in every house, and black earth from the compost-yards at the edge of every village. What the valley cannot eat today it turns, and what it cannot turn it sells to someone leaving.

**Water.** The river brings more than the valley can drink. Channels are left running all night, fields are flooded on purpose, and a householder waters the road to keep the dust off the neighbours' fruit. Galduans, whose springs are given and never sold, find it obscene.

#### ◈ Popular Belief: sleep in a field at noon

Sleep in a field at noon, mothers say, and you will wake a year older. They say it to keep their children out of the fields in the heat. The quickening leaves people alone.

## The river and the Hemmal

> *Once a year it comes down out of Emarrea's highlands, climbs out of its channels and lays a hand of new silt over everything.*
> Golivander Tessek, *The Travelling Chronicle*, 2509 MR

Once a year **the Hemmal** comes down from Emarrea's highlands. The river climbs out of its channels, the whole valley goes under, and when the water goes down it leaves a new layer of silt over every field. The valley counts its years from the flood, and its ages and vintages in floods: *a nine-flood wine*, *she is forty floods*.

**The flood feast.** Before the water comes, everything that would rot under it is eaten. The whole valley sits at long tables on the levees for the last fresh meal of the year. On the flood's eve the seed-boats come home across the bay from the island stores, every village's seed for the new silt.

**The flood-warden.** For the weeks of the flood one warden, chosen by the Mida each year, commands every duchy's levees, crews and boats, and the warden's word is law until the water goes down. Then the dukes take their duchies back, and the warden names the flood for what it did: **Aghiwel**, a flood that came early and fast; **Aludh**, one that left the silt deep; **Asemmam**, one that soured the low fields. **Aghilas Aludh Ath Arezki** is this year's warden.

## The cut law and the Mida

> *"Thicket is what a lazy man leaves behind. My people did not leave those fields. They were driven off them."*
> Lounis Aghiwel Ath Sekkur, duke of Westfold, at the Mida, 2532 MR

Under the Golden Empire the valley was the granary province, divided into duchies. In the Dark Era it could not be held. The open valley had no rock and no wall, the Corrupted God's forces came through the flat country, and the people fled up to Galdua's rocks, onto Valreka's herd and upriver into Emarrea. Untended quick land went to thicket in a single year. Within a generation the duchies were a jungle of rotting fruit around a few walled river-towns that held on.

At the end of the Dark Era the families came back, most of them down from the rocks and off the herd, with Emarrean, Hareaveldi and Azarketi families beside them, and cut the valley back out of the thicket. Each party that cut a duchy free founded its house, and its heirs are the dukes.

**The cut law.** Land in the River Duchies is held by keeping it cut. A field left untended for a year goes to thicket, and thicket belongs to whoever cuts it back. When a farmer falls sick, the neighbours cut the field for him, so that it stays his.

**The dukes.** There are eleven duchies, and each duke rules one: holds court, keeps the levees and the crews, and takes the tolls on the duchy's stretch of the river. A duke receives an envoy whose road comes through his duchy, and only what concerns them all goes to **the Mida**. The dukes rarely cut any more. Their tenants do.

**The Mida.** The eleven sit together at **Lemersa**, which belongs to no duke, at a low table. The Mida decides by majority and signs the valley's treaties; its eldest, the duke who has sat longest, speaks and signs for it. It builds the river works and the quays of Lemersa with the tolls. **Tassadit Uttis Ath Akli** has sat longest.

**The Westfold fields.** Since 2524 the Rot-Tyrant's legions have ranged across Galdua's pale band, and the band runs along Westfold's western fields. The farmers there have pulled back, and their fields have gone to thicket. The neighbouring dukes now send crews, with hired guards and adventurers, to cut the abandoned ground and keep it, as the cut law allows. **Lounis Aghiwel Ath Sekkur**, duke of Westfold, holds that land taken by the legions is not land neglected. The cut law does not say who judges whether a field has gone to thicket, the duke or the flood-warden, and the Mida has to decide it.

## The slow isles and Agu

The quickening stops at the shore. Off the river mouths of the south-east lie **the slow isles**, three islands the flood's silt built, with tidal flats between them that boats cross at high water and carts at low. On the isles everything keeps. **Tamegra**, the largest, holds the valley's seed stores, and the seed-boats sail from it on the flood's eve. **Tamtunt** holds the cellars, where wine brewed in a week in the valley ages for years. **Azay** holds the oldest trees in the south: pale, smooth trunks under crowns grown so wide and flat over the centuries that a grove seen from the water looks like a second, higher ground.

Beyond Azay lies **Agu**, a small island in a fog that does not lift. On the year's lowest tides, a few days in all, the flats run dry from Azay to Agu, and it can be reached on foot. I have found no account of Agu by anyone who stayed the night.

## Daily life

The day starts at the dawn market and the morning's work. At noon the carts go round, and then the valley sleeps, long. The evening is for the vats and the tables. The land does most of the work, and the day is built around letting it.

The towns smell sour-sweet, of vinegar and lees and fruit going over. Everything is eaten the day it is picked, fish sauce goes on everything, and the tea is fermented. *It'll keep* is the valley's joke, and *let it turn* means *let it be; it will become something*.

**Sloth.** The valley's vice is sleeping through a harvest it grows anyway: admired in a farmer, despised in a duke who leaves his crews to cut.

**Getting about.** On foot along the levees, by skiff on the channels, by boat to the isles. The drift-barges come down from Emarrea at the river's own pace, a week with nobody hurrying, and go back up towed by buffalo along the levee towpaths.

**Visitors.** A visitor keeps a peach for later and finds it juice by supper, leaves bread out and finds it furred by morning, and calls the water wasted. A native eats it today, turns it, or sells it to someone leaving.

**The young.** A child keeps a garden that grows in days and rots in days. At about fifteen comes **the first cut**: a corner of thicket to cut and keep cut for a whole year, and then it is theirs to work. The young brew in secret and badly; their elders find the jars, say nothing, and drink the **first brew** at the flood feast with straight faces.

**Faith.** The valley keeps Tani as Decay, the side of her most of Lioaru only resists, and a small shrine stands at every compost-yard. The dead go into the black earth, and *she is in the barley now* is said kindly. At the landings the river-traders keep a word for Shuun.

## The Duchy folk

Nagaji and Azarketi are the most numerous, the Azarketi on the channels and the isles; kitsune hold the upriver towns, Emarrea's barges coming and going past them; and every people of the south-west is somewhere in the valley. The valley is no people's home: the families who came back at the return came from the rocks, the herd, Emarrea and Hareaveldi, and the valley has refilled in four centuries on land that gives three harvests between floods.

## The tongue

The Duchy folk speak **Ameslay**, a sister of the Awal of the rocks and the herd; the returners brought it down with them. Talanese is spoken on the barges and the quays.

## What the Duchy folk are called

A Duchy name is a given name, a birth-flood and a house: **Tassadit Uttis Ath Akli**. The birth-flood is the name the flood-warden gave the flood of the year one was born, so that a name tells an age, and everyone born under one flood is of it together: *we are both Aludh*. The vintages carry the same names, and *an Aludh vinegar* and *an Aludh child* are the same year. The house is **Ath**, *the people of*, and its founder's name, carried by all the house's children.

**Sample names.** Given: Tassadit, Lounis, Aghilas, Akli, Sekkura, Arezki, Fadhma, Taous, Mohand. Birth-floods: Aghiwel, Aludh, Asemmam, Uttis. Houses: Ath Akli, Ath Arezki, Ath Sekkur, Ath Mohand. Whole: *Tassadit Uttis Ath Akli* · *Lounis Aghiwel Ath Sekkur* · *Sekkura Aludh Ath Mohand*.

> *The River Duchies are reached three ways: by drift-barge down the river from Emarrea, a week at the river's pace, which is the only pace it has; by the salt landings on the northern channels, below the southern rocks of Galdua Jendea; and by sea into the bay at Lemersa, where the river meets the Hafra.
