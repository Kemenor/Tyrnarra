## Phase 1: the GM's ideas

**Thought dump (GM, 2026-10-22):**
- **The centre of the Golden Empire was here**, close to the Red Dominion and No Man's Land.
- **The breach of the Corrupted God was worst here:** here is where they dug too deep and the flood started; it broke out elsewhere afterward, but it started here.
- **The land became genuinely unliveable for a while;** people fled wherever, also onto boats.
- **At the beginning of the Adventurer Era they came back:** people of the sea claiming homelands they could no longer call home.

**Answers to the four questions (GM, 2026-10-22):**
1. **Exile:** harbours along the Hafra, borrowed or bought, and their own ships. The Hafra already has specialists (Three Pines builds and escorts, Fellibylur's Stormriders chart), so the Dominion's people are the **carriers and crews**, known in every port; **each port's Dominion crews keep a piece of the culture alive in their own way**.
2. **The return:** no full retreat. **The capital on the southern point stood** through the Dark Era. The rest was overrun; on their return they had to **earn it back, from monsters and mortals alike**.
3. **The breach:** **the sealed city**, the old imperial capital in the **north-east**, **the scar of the world**; a place of **dark tourism**. The country is empty on the map; map additions to site at Phase 4.
4. **Strangers in their own land:** some are and some are not.

**Approved summary (GM, 2026-10-22):** the Golden Empire's old heartland on Sumendar's long western coast. In its north-eastern mountains, the sealed city: the imperial capital where the miners dug too deep in 1321 and the Dark Era began, now the scar of the world that visitors pay to look into. Through the Dark Era the city on the southern point held as the fleet's anchor while the rest was overrun; the people who fled became a people of the sea, the Hafra's carriers, known in every port, each port's crews keeping their own piece of the culture. At the start of the Adventurer Era the fleet came home, beached its hulls and broke them into towns, and earned the country back from monsters and mortals alike under the command that had held it together at sea. The contradiction: a sea people claiming a homeland it could no longer live in; some have become people of the land again, and some never will. Neighbours: No Man's Land's cinders north-east; Haraour, which took in breach refugees through Jaurei, east; Burdineyja's islands west; Auran's cargo south-west.

## Phase 5: government (closed 2026-10-23)

**Starting point (GM):** no emperor; the navy holds it. The 2026-10-06 earmark: a **naval stratocracy** (the navy is the state, its ranks the offices; a junta seizes a state, a stratocracy is one). The form is fixed, so the candidates are variants of its mechanics.

- **G1 · The Admiralty: rank is office.** A captain governs a ship and the town it came ashore as; a commodore a coast; the Admiralty at the point the whole, under a head (named in Phase 8). Captains commissioned from examined officers with sea-time. *Census:* nearest Haldmark's Vaka (service earns the franchise; the army does not rule there).
- **G2 · The ship's articles: captain and quartermaster.** The captain absolute *in action*; a crew-chosen quartermaster over shares, disputes, provisions; the scar country permanently in action. *Precedent:* 1700s pirate articles. *Sameness risk:* Tahu Tangata's two chairs (ground and fire) in the same domain.
- **G3 · The muster: belonging by ship, not by ground.** Every Dominion house is entered on a ship's muster roll and goes by its ship's name (sketch 4). The ships are the state's units. A beached ship-town is still a ship on the roll (its harbour-master, sketch 3, its officer; its bell, sketch 5, the ship's bell); a quarter in a northern port is the shore-berth of a ship that still sails. *Census:* the unusual list's "allegiance by person, not by ground"; set apart from Valreka's chosen whale by being inherited and tied to service.

**★ Claude's recommendation: G1 + G3, G2's quartermaster optional.** The only form that fits a people 60% abroad: belonging rests on the roll, not the ground; Roshanak's sketches become the state's machinery.
- **Four answers (ordinary):** day to day, each captain over its muster, commodores over coasts and quarters abroad, the Admiralty over the whole; envoys, the Admiralty's port-officer abroad (a consul), the head at the point; treaties, the head; the bridge, the Admiralty's yard, labour mustered from that coast's ships as service.
- **In action (special state):** on the frontier and in a fight at sea, a captain's orders are absolute; the Admiralty's reach waits until the action ends.
- **Offices filled:** captains commissioned by the Admiralty from examined officers with sea-time; the flag officers sit as the Admiralty; the head chosen by the flag officers from among themselves, serving until she strikes her flag or the board removes her.
- **Fit to rule:** a navy provisions, plans, keeps records (the roll, the logs), keeps law at sea and consuls in foreign ports; it kept a scattered people fed, supplied and in touch for eight centuries.
- **Accepted:** founding, the fleet carried them out of the breach and home; standing, the Admiralty's berths and convoys make a Dominion crew welcome in every Hafra port, the house's name is on the roll, and the frontier needs a standing force.
- **Honest cost:** every office is a rank, so to govern you must have served; a farmer in the held south has a muster but no say; the land is run by sailors for the fleet's trade; commissions go by patronage ("interest") as much as by examination.
- **Incentive check:** promotion by command record rewards captains who push into the scar for land and glory, pressing on the No Man's Land border (a live-tension candidate that falls out of the rule). No seniority criterion, so rank does not reduce to age.
- **Three tests:** unity, the quarters abroad are held by the carrier network (berths, convoys, cargo under one command; a quarter that breaks away loses all three); external agency, the head signs, port-officers make local deals (berths, tolls); internal provision, the yard builds, funded by a share of every cargo carried under the Dominion's flag.
- **Muddy lines (tension candidates):** reclaimed land, the ship whose crew cleared it or the Admiralty?; the quarters abroad, does the Admiralty's word bind a house four generations in a foreign port, under that port's law?

**Questions to the GM (open):**
1. G1 + G3, or otherwise?
2. Keep G2's quartermaster as a crew-chosen office on each ship (shares and disputes), without the full split?
3. The head chosen by the flag officers from among themselves, serving until she strikes her flag?
4. The mortals of reclaimed land who stayed (squatters, holdfasts): did they join by being entered on a muster, making "being entered" the one way anyone becomes Dominion?

**C · The muster's voice (GM, 2026-10-23, in place of G2's quartermaster).** The GM asked how ships really ran: in navies every office was appointed and the crew-chosen quartermaster was a pirate institution; medieval sea law (the Rolls of Oléron) had the master consult the crew before sailing in doubtful weather. The GM wanted that: a voice for the people without an office or a vote. Options put: C1, the asking; C2, the bell (the right to be heard); C3, the muster's consent on what is the muster's.

**Phase 5: GM's answers (2026-10-23)**
1. **G1 + G3: yes.** A naval stratocracy whose units are ships: rank is office; belonging by the muster roll, not by ground.
2. **C1, the asking: yes** (in place of G2's quartermaster). Before a decision that puts the whole muster at hazard (at sea, doubtful weather; ashore, the scar is the weather: pushing into the scar, moving the town, taking new ground) the captain must put it to the muster and hear its answer. The captain may overrule; the asking and the answer go into the log, and a captain who overruled and lost answers for it before the board.
3. **The head: yes.** Chosen by the flag officers from among themselves; serves until she strikes her flag or the board removes her.
4. **Entry by muster: yes.** The mortals of reclaimed land who stayed (squatters, holdfasts) joined by being entered on a muster. Entering a muster is joining the Dominion; leaving the muster is ceasing to be Dominion (GM).

**Phase 5 checklist (closed, GM 2026-10-23):** the form (G1 + G3); the four answers; in action; the muster's voice (C1); offices filled (captains commissioned, flag officers as the Admiralty, the head chosen from among them); why fit, why accepted; the honest cost; the incentive check; the three tests; the muddy lines (reclaimed land; the quarters abroad); entry by muster, leaving the muster is leaving the Dominion; G2, C2 and C3 dropped.

## Phase 6: economy (proposed 2026-10-23; not approved)

**Canon checked:** Auran (the carriers handle most of its cargo, two days out to Siyabask: drawn essence in sealed jars, cloudship silver); Balatur Erui (every cargo between the seas changes hulls at Gesalkai; "the salt hulls that carry the Hafra side of the trade are mostly Three Pines work"); the River Duchies (the Golden Empire's granary province, capital Lemersa at the head of the bay, across from the Dominion); Haraour (no rail, its carts go east to Eldara's line; a line on to the Dominion is its open question); `transport.md` (no Dominion entries yet; Hafra saltkeels 120–180 mi a day).

1. **Subsistence.** The home feeds itself in an ordinary year: fish all along the coast, the farms of the held south, and the reclaimed fields behind the ship-towns. In a lean year grain comes across the bay from the River Duchies at Lemersa, the Empire's old granary feeding the Empire's old heartland, carried in Dominion hulls. The coast never goes hungry; the frontier towns inland go short first. The quarters abroad live on their wages like any port folk.
2. **The draw.** Few outsiders come to trade: the Dominion goes to them. Those who come, come for the scar: scholars (Roshanak), the curious who pay for the guided look, and parties of four hired to clear nests. **Proposed:** the Admiralty pays a bounty per nest cleared, through the landing town.
3. **What the home makes (choice).** E1 timber: clearing the overgrowth is the reclaiming, and the cleared trees are ship timber. E2 iron: the imperial workings on the frontier (the red lakes), reopened where the ground is won. E3 both: timber the everyday trade, iron the prize. **★ Recommended: E3.**
4. **The carrying is the economy.** The Dominion's wealth is freight: its carriers take a share of every cargo under its flag (the Phase 5 share that funds the Admiralty's yard). Partners: Auran (silver and essence to the mainland; existing canon); Gesalkai on Balatur Erui (salt cargo to the Weighhouse); the River Duchies (grain, in lean years and in trade); the Hafra ports with Dominion quarters (named later). It buys grain in lean years, finished goods, and hulls.
