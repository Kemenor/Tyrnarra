Two seas: a mountain range along the Midarra (fresh) north coast; the long west coast and the point on the Hafra (salt). A mountain cluster in the north-east by No Man's Land; forest on the north-west and south-west coasts; a small dune field in the south; the forested river valley on Haraour's border down to the bay; two rivers crossing the strip west to the Hafra. ## Phase 1: the GM's ideas

**Thought dump (GM, 2026-10-22):**
- **The centre of the Golden Empire was here**, close to the Red Dominion and No Man's Land.
- **The breach of the Corrupted God was worst here:** here is where they dug too deep and the flood started; it broke out elsewhere afterward, but it started here.
- **The land became genuinely unliveable for a while;** people fled wherever, also onto boats.
- **At the beginning of the Adventurer Era they came back:** people of the sea claiming homelands they could no longer call home.

**Answers to the four questions (GM, 2026-10-22):**
1. **Exile:** harbours along the Hafra, borrowed or bought, and their own ships. The Hafra already has specialists (Three Pines builds and escorts, Fellibylur's Stormriders chart), so the Dominion's people are the **carriers and crews**, known in every port; **each port's Dominion crews keep a piece of the culture alive in their own way**.
2. **The return:** no full retreat. **The capital on the southern point stood** through the Dark Era. The rest was overrun; on their return they had to **earn it back, from monsters and mortals alike**.
3. **The breach:** **the sealed city**, the old imperial capital in the **north-east**, **the scar of the world**; a place of **dark tourism**. The country is empty on the map; map additions to site at Phase 4.
4. **Strangers in their own land:** some are and some are not.

**Approved summary (GM, 2026-10-22):** the Golden Empire's old heartland on Sumendar's long western coast. In its north-eastern mountains, the sealed city: the imperial capital where the miners dug too deep in 1321 and the Dark Era began, now the scar of the world that visitors pay to look into. Through the Dark Era the city on the southern point held as the fleet's anchor while the rest was overrun; the people who fled became a people of the sea, the Hafra's carriers, known in every port, each port's crews keeping their own piece of the culture. At the start of the Adventurer Era the fleet came home, beached its hulls and broke them into towns, and earned the country back from monsters and mortals alike under the command that had held it together at sea. The contradiction: a sea people claiming a homeland it could no longer live in; some have become people of the land again, and some never will. Neighbours: No Man's Land's cinders north-east; Haraour, which took in breach refugees through Jaurei, east; Burdineyja's islands west; Auran's cargo south-west.

**Canon notes from Phase 1:** canon never places the breach ("deep beneath the earth", under "Imperial mining country"; the Wardstones in Myrkono anchor the binding, not the dig); the Empire's throne sat in Sumendar's dwarven mountains and its capital is "the Dark-Era ruin in Sumendar's outer reaches", so the sealed city places it. Place the capital and the mine in the Dominion's **north-eastern mountain cluster**, not in No Man's Land, so Eldara's "ground no kingdom would claim" stands. Haraour's "those who sought refuge went in through Jaurei" takes the breach refugees without a change. The throne in Sumendar and the breach site may be open history; the Emperor's deliberate dig stays ⚿. Sameness to keep: the San Francisco 1849 ship-buildings as the real model for the beached hulls, against Dragon's Reach (one alien hull a city grew inside); the River Duchies' return (they went back to farming; these came back changed); Floteyn's Nation Afloat (afloat by choice and for good; this is an exile that ended); Haraour's guests next door (a meal and home rested; here a guided look into the hole).

**Held idea, not hard lore (GM, 2026-10-22): nests.** The land they claim is not as safe as they would like: where the Corrupted God got its first foothold, nests of spawn may survive to this day. Kept separate from the Nine Dungeons. For Phases 4, 7 and 9.

## Phase 2: seeds

**Seed round (2026-10-22):** S1, the fleet ashore (every town a broken-up ship keeping its name, ranks and captain; held ship by ship); **S2, the homeland held for a people at sea** (the Dominion the home port of a people largely still out on the Hafra; the people is the fleet, so the state is the fleet's command; the land/sea split through every family; a muster of the whole people at the point); S3, the scar's keepers (the Dominion exists to keep the sealed city).

**Chosen (GM, 2026-10-22): S2, with S1's beached ship-towns as the visuals.** **S3 dropped as a seed (GM):** the weakest; how the scar is kept and governed is an aside inside the sealed city, not a seed.

**The seed (approved, GM 2026-10-22):** *A homeland won back for a people who mostly live at sea.* **How many live at sea is kept open (GM):** it has never been defined; Phase 4 sets it, and the sentence's "mostly" is checked then.

**Phase 2 checklist:** three seeds offered as shapes of the GM's idea, each with what follows, a canon hook and the specificity test; sameness checked across the setting; S2 recommended and chosen with S1's ship-towns as the visuals; S3 dropped; the seed sentence approved.

**Sameness notes (authoring only):** S1 beside Dragon's Reach (one alien hull a city grew in; here a fleet broken into towns by choice); S2 beside Floteyn's Nation Afloat (afloat for good with no homeland; here a diaspora with a home port) and Valreka (a people that moves together; here a people spread apart and called home); S3 beside the Lost Kingdom and the Basogur's sealed Stitchery.

## Phase 3: traveller's image

**Candidates (2026-10-22):** 1, a town roofed with ships (upturned hulls, keels as ridge-beams, figureheads over doors, a ship's bell for a clock); 2, the home ships at the point, every crew with its own version of the song (close to Floteyn's Raft); 3, the question in every port. **1 chosen (GM, 2026-10-22).**

**The speaker (GM, 2026-10-22):** **Roshanak Abri Kuzegar** (Hareaveldi register: given + self-name + family), a scholar of Thekkavar's Lantern of Natural Philosophy, bound for the sealed city to study what came up out of the breach; she takes the Hafra route on a Dominion carrier round the point and up the west coast. 2532. **Traits:** she draws everything (in Hareaveldi an image is how a thing is known); she came to read the Empire in its ruins and keeps sketching the roofs. **Form: a sketchbook**, her notes under numbered sketches (two Sumendar letters to masters already exist: Joana's, Natsume's). **Placement:** the head of the ship-towns section.

**The sketches (GM, 2026-10-22):** render them, both locally (FLUX.2 via ComfyUI) and in Midjourney, and keep the better. No lettering in the images; her notes stay as text under each sketch. A sketch card on the page is a new Style B pattern (approved in principle). One test sketch after the notes are accepted; the final set at the publish, once Phases 4–7 have fixed the ship-towns.

**The sketchbook (written by Claude; accepted by the GM, 2026-10-22, with every [new] addition kept). Placeholders [the capital], [the ship's name], [a northern port], [the landing town] wait for Phase 8:**

> **1. The point, from the deck.** [The capital] on its rock at the end of the country: walls down to the water, a harbour inside them, and the town above in stone. The captain says it is the one town on this coast that stood. I drew it for the walls. I meant to come back to the harbour, and by then we had sailed.
>
> **2. The first ship-town, from the deck.** Forty-odd roofs along the shore, and every one of them a boat turned over. The small ones are fishing boats; the hall in the middle is a whole carrier, keel to the sky. From the water it looks like a fleet that sank the wrong way up.
>
> **3. The harbour-master's house.** The keel is the roof-tree and the ribs are the rafters. The planks still lie over one another the way they did at sea, so the rain runs off them the way the water did. He has cut his door where the hull was once patched, and kept the patch. I came here to draw the Empire.
>
> **4. A figurehead over a baker's door.** A woman with her arms full of fish, salt-white, painted fresh this spring. The house goes by her ship's name, and so does the bread: in the queue they ask for a loaf from the [ship's name]. I was meant to spend this morning at the milestone on the coast road.
>
> **5. The bell.** Where a town at home would keep a clock, this one has a ship's bell on a frame in the square, the ship's name cut round its rim. It rang twice while I drew it, for a boat coming in and for the tide.
>
> **6. Two crews.** Ours, and a crew at the next berth whose people have kept a quarter in [a northern port] for four generations. They sing the same song in two versions, and stop at the third line to argue it. Both crews are the Dominion's.
>
> **7. The mountains.** At evening, far inland to the north-east, a darker line under the cloud. We land at [the landing town] tomorrow and I go inland. The guides there take parties up in fours.
>
> *From the sketchbook of Roshanak Abri Kuzegar, scholar of Thekkavar, on the Hafra coast of the Red Dominion, 2532 MR*

**Additions to flag [new]:** the point city built in stone (it stood, so the ship-towns are the returners' towns, not the capital); about forty roofs in a town, the hall a whole carrier; a house going by its ship's name, and the bread with it; the bell rung for boats and the tide; a Dominion crew whose people keep a quarter in a northern port for four generations (the port unnamed); a landing town on the west coast for the road inland; guides who take parties to the sealed city in fours.
