## The shared frame (both options)

- **A republic that takes a dictator by oath**, Roman-style. In a crisis the Republic swears one citizen total power "until the danger is past", and swears its own obedience in return; the dictator is re-sworn by the Republic periodically and replaced when too old.
- **Why it is Hobgoblin at full strength.** The feeling has two faces: *slow to promise, immovable once sworn* (the office used and laid down, as sworn) and *the word kept past its right life* (the office held because the word holds). A country of the given word should show both.

## Option A: Legea's door (century-long dictatorship, no dungeon)

**The danger is Legea's reception, not Legea's army.**

1. **Namur is a republic of sworn members.** A Namurese becomes a citizen by swearing the citizen's oath to the Republic at coming of age; marriages, debts, militia service, and office all rest on sworn words.
2. **Legea's reception closes every oath a convert swore before** (canon, `legea-empire.md`, *Oaths*). The Faith runs reading-houses abroad and missionaries in every border village (canon, *Reach*).
3. **So each reception removes one sworn citizen from everything the Republic holds together**, and leaves a household with a spouse unbound, a creditor unpaid, a militia short. Legea never has to cross the river with an army.
4. **The hot war, around 2400 MR.** Legea pressed south; the Republic swore a dictator "until the danger is past"; the war ended in a real peace, sworn by Namur and entered on the page by Legea, and both have kept it.
5. **Why the dictator was never laid down.** Within a decade of the peace the Republic had lost more citizens to reception than it lost to Legea's army in the war, and the dictator said so in the assembly with the rolls in hand.
6. **Why it needs a dictator, not a law.** The Republic's own founding oath guarantees every citizen free worship, so the ordinary assemblies cannot lawfully close a ford to a reader or forbid a citizen to cross and be received. Only the emergency office stands above ordinary law. That is why the danger keeps the dictator: the Republic cannot defend itself against the door without suspending itself.
7. **The cold war since.** Fords patrolled, readers turned back at the river, the far-bank reading-houses watched, families split across the water; Legea's sea-trade still passes under the capital's walls at the river-mouth.
8. **The live tension.** Legea will not stop receiving, because receiving is what it is; "until the danger is past" therefore means never, while both countries stay themselves. The reformers' case: a republic whose citizens stay only because the door is guarded is not a republic of the word; the word has to be kept freely or it is a chain. The dictators' case: a people of the word bleeding its oaths across a river is exactly the danger the office exists for. Legea's case: a tyranny over conscience, with its readers as martyrs. Each is partly right; Tyranny beside Justice.
9. **What it costs canon:** nothing. Zuzental keeps its clean ground.

## Option B: the Cincinnatus Republic (worked out in the build)

Dictators sworn and laid down as needed, a Dark-Era precedent of a dictatorship re-sworn for generations, one who tried to stay and was taken down, and a dictator sworn when a dungeon of the Nine broke open in 2524, still re-sworn after eight years of a gate sealed and not cleared. This is the direction the GM chose to work out on 2026-09-30; the design lives in the build, not here.
