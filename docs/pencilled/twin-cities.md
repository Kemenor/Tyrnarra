## Phase 1: the GM's vision (2026-10-27)

**The image: a stacked flotilla city.**
- **Growth.** A few pirates moored together to enjoy spoils and trade. More ships found the spot, for trade, gambling, revelry, talk and strategy. The inner ships stayed, were rebuilt and taken apart and turned into buildings: a tavern, a gambling hall, a brothel, whatever their owners pleased.
- **Upward.** The tavern needed room, so a ship was hoisted onto it and modified: a second storey, then a third. The tavern's top deck was later joined to the gambling hall next door, both grown tall, both with VIP decks up there. Three more ships were stacked just for housing. The tavern owner bought another ship and set it on top as his mansion. Old gangways became hallways and wide promenades, with doors on both sides: quarters, shops, by now even bakers.
- **Inside.** Entering the city is entering a different world, with its own rules and its own time. The stacked ships keep the sun and moon out, which suits a city that has decided sleep is for someone else.
- **Downward.** As it grew up it grew down: hulls pushed under by sheer weight, some flooded, some dry. The bilges. Used all the same, waste not want not: what the air-breathers stop using, the water-dwellers take over.
- **The spires.** The residences of the Lords of the sea, each a legendary, feared pirate with crews and captains under them. Most Lords will not sit in their spires; when their flags show on the horizon, crowd on all sail.

**The feeling.**
- From outside, what many regions call it: a den of villainy, a float of scum and salvage, pirates lawless in everything.
- Stay a while and it is a city that ticks a little differently. The baker still bakes, the barber still cuts hair (and knows how to cut a throat).
- It calls itself **the truly free city, free by choice, not by designation**. The good and the bad are both here, and everyone is welcome.
- Life runs on **pirate law**: the crew votes, and no captain is untouchable, nor any Lord, by vote or by gun and blade.

**Naming hint (for Phase 8):** nautical terms for the city's parts (the bilges, hulls, decks and so on).

## Phase 1: the GM's answers (2026-10-27)

1. **The twin is the air-pirate city.** The vision above describes only the sea city. The twin is the sky city: four islets, home of the airship pirates. Sea-ship pirates below, airship pirates above, two sides of the same coin. They can join and move together, and they do not have to. (The Lords' spires belong to the sea city.)
2. **Movement.** The outer ring is working ships that still sail; the inner city holds machines that drive it forward, since every kind of ship has been built into it. **The Lord of the city calls a move**: a voted pirate Lord who holds the seat until voted out, killed, or leaving it. Pirates still always vote their own captains.
3. **Who votes: everyone who lives there.** Nobody signs onto a ship; the districts are run as ships because they were ships, so they still technically are, and living in one makes you its crew.
4. **"The truly free city" is a deliberate jab at Frae City**, because it is what the pirates would say.
5. **The Lord of the city and the Pirate Lords.** The Pirate Lords are the top brass. One person is voted the city's Lord; that can be a Pirate Lord, and does not have to be.

**Earlier sea-city summary (approved 2026-10-27, superseded by the combined summary below).** The Twin Cities are two pirate cities: a stacked flotilla of sea-pirate hulls on the Midarra, and above it the four islets of the airship pirates' sky city; they can join and travel as one, or go their own ways. The sea city grew from a mooring into a dark, sleepless world: taverns, gambling halls and housing on the upper decks, drowned bilges given over to the water-dwellers, and the Lords' spires on top. It moves on the machines of every kind of ship ever built into its core and the still-working ships of its outer ring, whenever the city's elected Lord sets a course. Its contradiction is a den of villainy that turns out to be a working town under pirate law: every district is still a ship, everyone aboard is crew with a vote, and no captain or Lord is safe from a vote or a blade. It shares the crossing with Frae City and calls itself the truly free city, by choice and not by designation, a deliberate jab at Frae City's freedom in its god's name; that rivalry is its sharpest neighbour contrast, with the Sweetwater League's Raft (which unlashes after each sitting) beside it. Myrria and Eldara never sleep either; here the reason is the hulls that keep the sky out.

## Phase 1: the sky city (GM, 2026-10-27)

**Same basis, expressed differently.** Each city has its own Lords. Most pirates are pirates of one only, sea or sky.
- **Sea:** the waves under them, every step shaky, every step an effort; open seas, but always water.
- **Sky:** nothingness around them, and they love it. They do not see the water they sail over; they see the clouds above and dive into them. Every sky pirate knows the cold droplets on the skin.

1. **The image.** Open and bright: hardly a gaze in it that cannot find the sun, or the stars at night. Glass, windows, or just a hole where the air sings through. Wide plazas and gathering places, tables outside with chairs in front of every locale, hammocks swinging free or hung off the edge. It does not sleep either; it twinkles under the stars. Laughter and voices carry on the wind.
2. **The lift.** Old artifacts of the Gods' Era, Elden or divine. As the city grew too large, the airships took up part of the weight. A humble beginning; the end product is the image above.
3. **The prey.** An airship hunts more than airships. The sky pirates are neither richer nor choosier than the sea pirates, and they do not look down on them.
4. **Together and apart.** The Lords of sea and sky sit together, for kinship and strength in numbers: each gains from the other, and together they may be strong enough to withstand the powers that want to drag them down. When they can, they build a single city: the markets combine, the people mingle. They part when they must: when hunted, or when the work is somewhere else.

## Phase 1: approved summary (GM, 2026-10-27)

The Twin Cities are one pirate nation in two cities on the Midarra. Each has its own Lords, and the Lords of both sit together as the top brass, for kinship and strength in numbers against the powers that would drag them down. Most pirates belong to one city only: the sea pirates live on water that fights them at every step, and the sky pirates live in an emptiness they love, diving into clouds and knowing the cold of the droplets. The sea city is a dark, stacked flotilla where the hulls shut out the sky. Its upper decks hold taverns, gambling halls and homes, the water-dwellers have its drowned bilges, and the Lords' spires stand on top. It moves on the machines of every ship built into it whenever its elected Lord sets a course. The sky city is four bright islets on Gods'-era artifacts, with airships now carrying part of the weight: plazas, outdoor tables, hammocks over the edge and wind singing through the windows, with sun or stars in sight from everywhere. When they can, the two cities join into one, and their markets and people mix; when they are hunted or the work is elsewhere, they split. Neither city sleeps, both live by pirate law (every district is a ship, everyone aboard has a vote, and no captain or Lord is safe), and both call themselves the truly free city, by choice and not by designation, aimed straight at Frae City on the same crossing.

## Phase 5: government (GM, closed 2026-10-29)

**Approved (GM liked all recommendations, 2026-10-29):** the city's Lord elected ship by ship (each district's show of hands, carried by its captain); captain and quartermaster in every district (captain commands in action and under way; quartermaster divides shares, settles quarrels, speaks for the crew); the vote over the body (a killed Lord or captain: the crew votes *deposition*, killer free and may stand, or *murder*, killer marooned); deals on word and flag, nothing signed; districts build their own by crew vote, the city's Lord the city-wide works from the city's cut; in action the city's Lord commands outright; joined, each city keeps its Lord and joint matters go to the Lords sitting together.

**The two kinds of Lord (GM, 2026-10-29):**
- **The city's Lord** is an office: elected, and the one who actually decides over the city.
- **A Pirate Lord** is a standing, the classical legend (a Blackbeard): the top dog, feared, renowned, rich and powerful all at once. It ties to *belief is power*: a legend believed across the Midarra gives real weight. The Pirate Lords hold no office; they hold weight, the power to sway people, and they sit with the city because leaving them out would be a costly mistake. (This replaces the *by flag* proposal as the definition; captains sailing under a Lord's flag is one expression of the renown.)

**The Phase 5 output (approved 2026-10-29):**

- **Offices.** Every district (a ship) elects a **captain** and a **quartermaster** by show of hands; everyone who lives aboard votes. Each city elects its **Lord** ship by ship: each district votes, its captain carries the result. The city's Lord holds the seat until voted out, killed, or leaving it. Pirate captains are elected by their crews, always.
- **The Pirate Lords** hold no office. A Lord is a legend: feared, renowned, rich and powerful at once, whose name told across the Midarra carries real weight (*belief is power*, `cosmology.md`). Nobody appoints one; you are a Lord when the other Lords and the city cannot afford to leave you out, so the list is always argued at its edges (about seven now). A legend must be fed: a Lord who stops raiding stops being told, and stops being a Lord. A **spire** (a ship stood on end atop the stack) is the city's acknowledgement; the skyline is the current list. Captains sail under a Lord's flag for the legend, and a Lord's word binds every captain under that flag. The city's Lord may also be a Pirate Lord: rare, and the most powerful person in the city; a city's Lord who is not spends the office managing the Lords.
- **The four answers.** Day to day: the district captains and quartermasters. An envoy: the city's Lord, or a Pirate Lord for that Lord's own flag. A treaty: nobody signs; deals are a Lord's word and flag. The bridge: a district builds its own by crew vote, from its shares; the city's Lord builds city-wide (the engines, the lifts, the tow lines) from the city's cut of every prize (Phase 6).
- **The special states.** *In action* (attacked, or under way): the city's Lord commands outright, the one time anyone does. *Joined*: each city keeps its Lord; what touches both goes to the Lords and both city Lords sitting together, who also settle disputes between crews and share out the waters.
- **Unity:** any crew can sail away; it stays because a lone ship is hunted and the city is the one market that buys a prize without asking where it came from. **External agency:** a deal is a Lord's word, backed by every captain under the flag; buyers learn which Lord they are dealing with. **Internal provision:** the district for itself, the city's Lord for the whole.
- **Powers.** The city's Lord may set the course, command in action, and spend the city's cut; may not command a Lord's fleet or overrule a district's vote. The Pirate Lords command their own fleets and sway everyone else; they rule no district.
- **The vote over the body.** When a Lord or captain is killed, the crew votes over the body: *deposition* (the killer goes free and may stand) or *murder* (the killer is marooned). The quickest way to become a legend is to kill one, so Lords are dangerous to each other and to every ambitious captain.
- **Muddy edges:** who commands a Lord's fleet defending the city; how far *in action* stretches; who counts as a Lord.
- **Incentive check:** captains, Lords and city Lords all win by bringing in the biggest shares, so the city rewards its best raiders and cannot vote itself out of piracy without voting out the people who feed it. This powers the tension.
- **The honest cost:** violence is part of the constitution; shares buy votes; the loud and the feared win; there is no peaceful Twin Cities under these articles.
- **Census (off the page):** nearest are Auran (Tolwaja by household vote), Baerfrost's Hunt-League (chieftains seated for proven kills; the deed-route was rejected for that reason), the Raft (one island one voice; here a ship is a street with a vote), and the Red Dominion's absolute command in action (there the captain overrules the muster at other times; here never). The Pirate Lords as believed legends lean on the unusual form *a ruler believed into being* without being rulers.
- **Mara Goldwake check:** *seated* reads as sitting with the Lords; her legend was the hoard nobody ever saw.

## Phase 6: economy (GM, closed 2026-10-29)

- **Subsistence:** they feed themselves on water and fish and buy their grain. The Midarra is fresh, so drinking water is free; fish from the outer ring's boats and from fish-pens and weed beds in the shadow under the hulls, kept by the Merfolk of the bilges (their trade with the decks above); the sky city's islets carry gardens and orchards in the sun, so the sky grows the fresh food (fruit and greens up, fish and goods down: the joining is also a market); grain bought on the coasts and from the Brauogi grain ships on the crossing, sometimes taken as prize; a lean year means more raiding.
- **The draw:** the market that asks no questions (the Midarra's fence); the night that never ends (taverns, gambling halls, the brothel); crews for hire, and a place for the hunted to vanish; the pass.
- **The city grows from its prizes:** every taken hull sails on under a pirate captain or is broken, hoisted and built in; a district can be dated by the prize it was.
- **Sells:** prize goods at a discount, repairs and salvage, passes; the sky city fresh food and fast carriage. **Buys:** grain; guns (Saegate pattern, in through Gesalkai's Weighhouse); engines and airship parts from the Order of Steam (light, pending its rework).
- **Partners:** **Rika Tikur**, the main buyer of prize goods (canon: answers only to capital; its houses keep shrines to Nirfel, once Mara Goldwake; reach for Phase 9); Gesalkai (the guns); Legea after the armada (buys passes for its wax ships, on the terms of the Legedi's second line); the League (pays nothing in public; the accommodation, GM-tier).
- **Routes:** no rail; the city keeps to the crossing because the prey and the buyers are there; the sky pirates fly at about 60 mph to anywhere a hull cannot reach (inland caravans, river barges, coast towns, other airships).
- **The pass:** canon's *the Pirate Lords take their share*, made concrete. The Lords share out the waters, so a shipper buys a pass (a pennant or a token) from the Lord whose water it crosses, from that Lord's factor in Solkai or Rika Tikur, or pays when stopped; the captains under that Lord's flag honour it. No canon move (it fills an existing line). It covers the League's secret: in public the unharried mail reads as luck or an unadmitted pass.
- **The city's cut:** one share in ten of every prize sold in the city, to the city's Lord, for the engines, the lifts and the tow lines.
- **The stake in the tension:** every coast pays the pirates and profits from them (passes paid and resented, prize goods bought cheap and their sellers denounced, Legea's terms in its book); inside, the vote rewards the biggest shares, and a pass is worth buying only while the unprotected keep being taken.
- **Table note:** none; the pass as a prop is homebrew.

## Phase 7: daily life (GM, closed 2026-10-29)

- **The day:** the sea city keeps no common hour; each district keeps its own watches, and *what time is it?* is answered *which watch?* (unlike Myrria, where the clock kept is a signal; here the hour is whatever your crew says). A sea-city baker bakes when the flour comes in, buys fish through a bilge hatch from the Merfolk, sells on the promenade, and raises a hand when the deck votes on the roof. The sky city lives by the sun, the one place in the Twin Cities that has it: work in the morning, long afternoons at the outside tables, lamps along the edges at night.
- **The shared ritual: the joining** (the drip, the faces turned up, the markets pouring together). The daily civic act: **the hands**, the show of hands that settles everything from a leaking roof to a captain.
- **Senses and habits:** the drink is whatever came in last (a tavern's list changes with the prizes); clothes from every coast at once, prize-taken (a merchant's fine coat over bare feet; its quality says which crew); below, tar, lamp oil, wet wood and bread; above, wind, fruit and cold droplets; an apple from the sky city is a gift below.
- **The vice: the tellers.** Tavern storytellers tell the Lords' deeds for coin, and an ambitious captain pays to have their own told: where belief is power, this is how a legend is fed, and buying stories is how one tries to become a Lord. (Gambling halls stay ordinary; the Red Dominion's vice is the odds at Tuta.)
- **Speech:** *put it to the hands* (let's vote on it); *deposition or murder?* (the standing joke over any quarrel); asked where they are from, a resident names their **deck** (the district), keeping clear of the Dominion's *which ship?*.
- **Movement:** the sea city on foot along the promenades, up ladders that were masts, by rope-lifts between decks, by boat round the outer ring; the sky city by small airship skiffs between the four islets (Haizava has the rope-bridges). **Signature movement: the baskets** dropped from the islets at the joining.
- **Visitor against native:** the visitor sees no law and takes a show of hands for a riot; the native knows whose water this is, whose pass holds in it, and which deck's articles run on which stretch of promenade. The visitor keeps the time; the native asks which watch.
- **Youth:** a slope, the **first share**: a first run on a prize crew earns a half share, and the half share brings the right to raise a hand; the full share comes later. Sanctioned transgressions, both tolerated because Lords are made of them: sea-city youth climb the stack to touch a Lord's spire (the Lord's crew chases them off; a child who touched one boasts of it for years); sky-city youth jump the gap between islets when they swing close at the joining.
- **Faith as lived: many things.** Sea crews keep **Shuun**; sky crews **Fisaya** (the Tengu among them most); navigators and the night watch **Bikiargi**; and **Cronus is prayed to as well** (GM, 2026-10-29): he is freedom still, however the pirates sneer at Frae City. Clergy: whichever priest came aboard; no state temple. The Lords' legends are believed rather than worshipped; a toast to a dead Lord whose spire still stands is an ordinary evening's. (For Phase 9: whether anyone honours Goldwake knowing what she became; the broken-chain man said to drink in pirate taverns.)

## Phase 8: naming (GM, closed 2026-10-29)

- **Family: Talanese, native** (where strangers gather from everywhere, the shared tongue becomes the native one: as Frae City, Rika Tikur, No Man's Land). Adventurer Era, Talanese returning. **Local tongue: deck-talk**, Talanese under a sailors' cant.
- **Personal-name structure (GM, 2026-10-29):** most keep the names they came with and carry on their own naming traditions, so a child may carry a mix of two cultures' names. Then the **told name** comes: the name the tavern tellers tell of you, built on a deed, never chosen, replaceable by a bigger one. With the told name, the bearer **chooses which of the inherited names to keep** beside it. A Lord is known by the told name alone (*Goldwake*). Until a told name comes, the city calls you by your given name *out of* where you came from (*Jory out of Bellhalt*; the city-born *out of* a deck, *Pell out of the Spar*): an address, not a name part. A **bought name** (paid at the tellers before the deed) is an insult. Fingerprint: inherited parts from several traditions, pruned by choice, with a Talanese deed-name. Distinct from the Red Dominion's sea name (crew-given once, kept for life, beside a fixed rating and ship).
- **Names:** the sea city **Freeboard** (nautical: the height of the deck above the waterline; *free aboard*, the jab in the name; outsiders say *the Stack*); the sky city **Skysail** (the highest sail, above the royal; the lowest deck and the highest sail of one ship); the pair stays **the Twin Cities** abroad, *the Board and the Sail* aboard; the four islets **the Fore, the Main, the Mizzen, the Jigger** (the masts of a four-master; Skysail is one ship in law; the first rock moored to is the Main); demonyms **Freeboarder**, **Skysailer**; the pass **colours** (*buy a Lord's colours*, *sail under Goldwake's colours*; a forgery is *false colours*); the joining **the Mooring** (*the sky's come to moor*; *rafting up* avoided for the League's Raft); the vote over the body **the Last Hands**; the city's cut **the Tenth**; a half-share youngster a **halfer**; the offices **the Lord of Freeboard**, **the Lord of Skysail**; promenades named for rigging (**the Lower Spar** now canon), districts for the prize ship they were.
- **Named figures:** **Goldwake** (Mara Goldwake, sky Lord c. 2350–2390, canon); **Odile Brasshand**, Lord of Freeboard, a quartermaster risen, no Pirate Lord, her family out of Legea (she kept her day-name); **Kesti Downdraft**, Lord of Skysail and a Pirate Lord, Tengu; **Ninehulls**, a sea Lord who took nine ships in one night, the given name forgotten; **Sunfall**, a sky Lord who strikes out of the sun; **Pell out of the Spar**, a teller of the Lower Spar.
- **Checks:** freeboard, skysail, topgallant, jigger, colours, mooring are standard sailing terms; every coined name searched across lore, docs and published, no collisions.
