## Phase 1: the GM's answers

Received and confirmed 2026-10-02.

1. **The inner forest:** "somewhere familiar people visit and return from."
2. **Goblin appetite:** initially "Both", then clarified as **"all three actually"**: delight in everything life offers; wanting what lies beyond reach; the tension between enjoying something and using it up.
3. **Outsiders:** "I think more the first", referring to welcoming strangers into a sheltered country.
4. **The central building:** "It doesn't need to be a church." The building's purpose is open; the cathedral icon and the *eliza* root do not require a religious institution. This relaxes the Phase 0 church framing without deciding a replacement or changing the name.

**Approved summary:** Haraour Eliza is a sheltered Goblin heartland, welcoming to strangers, with a forest inside its mountain ring that locals visit as a familiar part of their country. Its people delight in what life offers, hunger for what they have yet to experience, and live with the tension between enjoying something and exhausting it. That appetite gives room for generosity, curiosity and excess, expressed differently across the country. The central building's purpose remains open. No Man's Land's frontier, the Order's industry and Tahu Tangata's renewing fire remain the neighbouring context.

**Phase 1 checklist:** the four seed questions answered; prompts offered; the GM's clarification and open building purpose incorporated; detailed government, economy, daily life and traveller's image left to their phases; summary confirmed.

## Phase 2: seeds

**Seed round (2026-10-17).** Three shapes of the GM's seed-bank idea were offered:
- **A, Brown on the plate:** the bright things kill, the cooks at the gate know each one's fire, and a dish is safe once its colour has cooked out; the cook-houses rule. Left the palace with no part.
- **B, the garden without the gardener's notes:** the paradise is the planted garden of a forgotten court; the Goblins recover its uses one bite at a time. **Chosen (GM, 2026-10-17).** A's colour-cooks-out image was never adopted, and the GM rejected it at Phase 3: it is wasteful and adds nothing (why would the garden's food turn brown when ordinary vegetables do not).
- **C, the season inside:** what kills in spring feeds in autumn, and families go up into the ring by season. Rejected as the weakest: close to Tahu Tangata's field clocks, the River Duchies' flood calendar and the Basogur's guided road.

**The GM's amendments to B (2026-10-17):**
- **The palace and the Eliza are two buildings.**
- **The palace stands in the capital and is the gate:** the one easy way into the ring is a tunnel through the mountains, dug by the palace's builders, entered through the palace. The mountains can also be climbed, the hard way.
- **Both builders are forgotten.**
- **The Eliza**, the building at the ring's heart (the map's cathedral icon), is not yet defined. The GM's sketch: a great building with winter gardens, a great kitchen climbing floor over floor, perhaps a spiral tower for drying, perhaps a great smokehouse.

**Follow-up answers (GM, 2026-10-17):**
- **One court** built both the palace and the Eliza, and dug the tunnel between; who they were waits for Phase 9.
- **The tunnel is a day's walk**, and by tradition **you walk it in**: the walk earns the hunger for paradise.
- **A river runs through the tunnel**, flowing naturally out of the ring past the palace (the map's eastern stream). By tradition **you float out**: having eaten, you rest in a boat and the river carries you from paradise back through the tunnel to the capital.
- **Government:** the GM leans toward a monarchy; for Phase 5.
- **The tunnel is dark**, lit only by what travellers bring (GM, 2026-10-17): the contrast with the garden is the point, and a glow-worm ceiling would echo Burdineyja's stars below.
- **Empty boats are hauled back upstream** along the towpath the walkers use (GM, 2026-10-17).
- **Tone to carry:** a sleeping traveller carried out of paradise by boat through the dark reads like a funeral barge; the page leans on that knowingly.

**The seed (closed):** *A garden planted by a court no one remembers, and a people who have spent the centuries since learning to eat it.*

**Phase 2 checklist:** three seeds offered as shapes of the GM's idea, each with pitch, what follows, canon hook and specificity test; sameness checked across the setting; B recommended and chosen; the GM's amendments and follow-ups recorded; the seed sentence approved.

**Sameness notes (authoring only, never on the page):** the Basogur (a deadly forest entered with guides: here the danger is in the eating, and the forest is a pantry, not a crossing); the Wildreach and the Golden Coast (colour: here bright is the warning, as in poison frogs and fungi); the River Duchies (turning food: they rot and ferment, here the poison is taken out); Tahu Tangata (heat opens the puahi to renew the ground, not to eat); Baratalda (every fruit grows there, unexplained: here the garden was planted); the Lost Kingdom and the Storveldi (dead civilisations: here the builders are known only by what they grew).

## Phase 4: place, peoples and history

**Proposal round (2026-10-17) and the GM's answers (2026-10-19).** Approved:
- **The tunnel:** the ring's river drains out underground through the mountain; the court followed it, cut the road beside it and widened the channel. About 25 miles, a long day's walk in and about 12 hours afloat out; a bore of about 40 ft (water for two boats to pass, a 10 ft towpath); dark.
- **The garden:** planted around the Eliza and gone wild across the whole valley floor (about 100 miles across) since.
- **The garden was a plant bank (GM's idea):** the original owners kept it as a bank of plants, which is why it holds so much variety, including plants found nowhere else.
- **A preserve, farmed and cooked from (GM):** the garden is kept like a preserve, and its people also farm it and cook from it.
- **Inside the ring:** people live there for working years, loosely, not by strict rule; the refuge is why.
- **The Empire:** Haraour an Imperial province (560–1325 MR); the garden's dishes an imperial luxury; the capital's Talanese name from then.
- **The refuge:** the outer country went in through the tunnel and sealed it through the Dark Era, lived off the garden, and learned most of what is known; the return and the crown at its end.
- **Numbers:** about 3 million; the capital about 75,000.
- **The Goblin feeling-only fix** in `sumendar.md` (GM: "definitely keep the fix").
- **Opened by the GM:** the poisons. A people who can cook the poison out also hold the poison, and would do more with it, though perhaps less publicly. For Phases 6 and 9.

**Second round (GM, 2026-10-19):**
- **The god (GM's idea):** the plant bank dates from the **Gods' Era**, and **the Eliza was a church after all**, of a **nature god who truly lived there**. The bank was his and his people's. The palace, the tunnel and the Eliza are the work of that god's people. Tier: proposed as ⚿ (the open record knows only the name and argues over it); the god's fate, name and his people's fate go to Phase 9. Canon to respect: a dead god who is forgotten is gone permanently (`gods.md`, *Death & Regeneration*).
- **Canon move 1 approved** (`sumendar.md`, the Haraour entry), now worded around the Eliza as the garden's house whose name says church; the open record disputes it.
- **Canon move 3 approved for now** (`transport.md`: a road from a halt on Eldara's line, about 150 miles, no rail). **TBD:** a line may later sweep through to the Red Dominion; settle at that build.
- **The other Phase 4 points approved**, with two changes:
  - **The Ash-Binder closure dropped.**
  - **The refuge revised:** the country did not flee and the capital was never abandoned. Those who sought refuge went in through the palace and found a home in the garden, because there was room; many stayed, and their descendants live inside today beside those who come in for working years. The house that held the palace kept the gate through the Dark Era and took the crown at its end. (This also removes the River Duchies' "return" overlap.)

**Closed with (GM, 2026-10-19):** about **100,000 living inside the ring** (refuge families' descendants and people on working years), within the 3 million.

## Phase 5: government

**Seed round (2026-10-19):** G1, the gate crown (the crown holds the palace, the tunnel, the river, the boats and the haulers; the toll; reeves outside, a warden at the inner mouth; the households inside run their own); G2, the warrant crown (the crown stamps every cook and dish sold); G3, the keeper-king (the crown holds the record and the knowledge). **G1 chosen (GM, 2026-10-19).**

**The GM's combination:** **the keepers of the record certify cooks themselves**, G2's warrant moved from the crown to the keepers. Two powers that pull, the crown and the keepers, in a changed form of merchant against nobility. The GM asked whether this is a cliché. Answer given: it holds if it stays **two monopolies checking each other**, each with a fair case and a fault (the crown sells faster than is safe; the keepers' caution is real and also keeps their certified cooks a closed shop), neither able to win outright.

**Approved (GM, 2026-10-20):**
- **Certification plant by plant** (after the fugu licence); a cook's standing is how many of the garden's plants she may serve. The keepers choose their own from certified cooks who have done tryings. The crown cannot certify; the keepers cannot toll or shut the gate.
- **The heir lives working years inside the ring** before the crown passes.
- **The poisons are the muddy line and the likely live tension.** Certification covers food; a poison sold as a poison falls under neither power's rule, and the gate lets it out. **The trade is not all in the dark (GM):** the Adventurers' Guild has genuine, lawful uses (poisons against monsters; the Guild follows local law, `factions.md`), and the Mercenary Guild buys for every purpose (deniable, informal, no buildings). The muddy part is where else it is sold.
- **The numbers (approved, GM 2026-10-20):** the garden holds more kinds than anyone has counted (tens of thousands); the record names about 9,000; about 600 are cleared for the table, roughly one every four years over 2,500 years, most in the Dark-Era refuge; about 50 are safe raw and need no certificate; about 550 need preparation and are certifiable, about a hundred of them the staples. An ordinary garden cook holds 10–20 certificates (a full table), a master 80–150, the keepers at the top a few hundred; no one holds all. **A certificate is needed only to serve or sell to others**; at home anyone cooks what they dare, and most poisonings happen at home.

**Closed with:** the gate crown (hereditary in the house that held the palace; the heir's working years inside; the palace, tunnel, river, boats, haulers and toll; royal reeves outside, a crown warden at the inner mouth; the households inside run their own); the keepers of the record at the Eliza (the record, plant-by-plant certification, self-chosen from certified tryers); the four answers; the special state of the gate shut; the powers (the crown may not tax climbers, certify, or take inside land; the keepers may not toll or shut the gate); the two pulls; the poison line; the honest cost of two monopolies.

**Phase 5 checklist:** census held against Appendix B, nearest polities named and kept off the page; three government seeds with the four answers, fitness, acceptance and cost; the incentive check (the toll capped by free climbing; the keepers' closed shop); the three tests on G1; the special state answered twice; powers and the muddy line settled; succession settled; G1 chosen with the keepers' certification combined in.

**Census and sameness notes (authoring only):** nearest gate monopoly, Auran's one-quay law (a commonwealth there); hereditary crowns, the Thousand Kingdom; two pulling powers in one Sumendar sibling, Tahu Tangata's diarchy (here the keepers are an institution, not a co-ruler); a controlled necessity, Argia Esfera's Aguarda at the wells.
