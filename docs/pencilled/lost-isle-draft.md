# Auran (Lioaru)

*Written under the [`region-prose.md`](../../../docs/region-prose.md) contract (2026-10-06). Parent domain: [`../lioaru.md`](../lioaru.md). The column quoted throughout is [`auran-column.md`](auran-column.md).*

**Etymology:** the islanders' own name, in Hauri: Balochi *hawr* (rain) with the plural *-ān*, "the rains", drifted to *Auran*. Demonym and adjective: **Auran**. The three craters keep names in the Silent Tongue: **Gulgir**, **Storngir**, **Silfgir**. The island's own words are in **Hauri**: **Siyabask**, **the Bask**, **the Tolwaja**, **the Daptar**, **the Durbra**. (Derivations in the glossary.)

**Position:** the outermost land of the south-western Hafra, two days' sail from any coast. North-east, the Red Dominion's point, about 285 miles off; east, Galdua Jendea's west shore, about 330; east-south-east, the Lost Kingdom, about 310. About 240 miles west lies the edge of the Cloud Sea.

**Terrain:** about 11,900 square miles, near round and some 130 miles across. Three old volcanoes stand inland, each with a lake in its crater and a cap of cloud of its own: Gulgir in the east, a day's road from the coast; Storngir in the south; Silfgir in the west, facing the Cloud Sea. Below them lies green farmland under ordinary rain. The coast is cliff and black lava shelf almost all the way round, with coves for fishing boats; on the east coast an old lava flow runs out into the sea as a natural breakwater, the Bask, with deep water behind it.

**Character:** an island whose rain runs gold, silver and starlight in the streets, and whose people draw it out before sundown, every rain, or lose it.

**Peoples:** about 300,000. Azarketi three in five, at their fullest on Talan; the rest the southern mix, Ghoran, Nagaji and kitsune; in Siyabask, everyone the ships bring.

**Tongue:** Hauri; Talanese on the quay and in the trade.

**Faith:** Tani, whose hour is sundown.

**Rule:** **the Tolwaja**, who keeps the quay and the island's chest, chosen by the island's households for seven years, one household, one voice. **Hani Gipt Siyasang** has held the seat since 2525.

**Capital:** **Siyabask**, on the Bask, about 9,000.

**Founded:** the craters' names in the Elden Era; the rain-share in the Dark Era.

## The rains

> *On my third afternoon a bell rang from the rim and the street emptied upward. I mean that: everyone went onto the roofs.*
> Vyrenna Tessek, *One Line Further*, at Gulgir, 2526 MR

Each of Auran's three craters rains its own rain. The cloud that caps a crater holds over it, and when it breaks, the rain falls inside the rim, onto the town that lines the bowl and into the lake at the bottom. It looks like ordinary rain until it lands. Then the runnels colour: at Gulgir the colour of late honey; at Storngir plain wet stone, with a cold point of light in every drop; at Silfgir a sheen on the channels, thin as breath on a mirror.

It rains about as often as rain does anywhere. Gulgir rains every few days, Storngir about once a week, and Silfgir every week or two, the least and the thinnest of the three. By sundown the colour has gone out of the water, and whatever has not been drawn out by then is only water.

**The drawing-out.** When the cap darkens, a bell rings from the rim and the town goes up onto its roofs. Every roof in a crater town slopes to one gutter, the gutters slope to the street, and the streets are cut with channels that run down to the settling tanks and the pans in the lakeside square. The whole town works the rain with boards and brooms, turning the flow at every corner, until the light goes. At the pans the rain is drawn out of the water and sealed in jars. At Silfgir the drawn silver goes on to the stills, and the stills are worked through the night.

**Three rains.** Gold is the commonest, and its trade is ink: writing in gold-rain ink stands up off the page in poor light, though it gives no light of its own, and the writer still needs a lamp. Aurans keep it as medicine too, a drop in the tea for a fever or on a wound, and swear it cures; it helps a little. Starlight is a reagent prized by mages and enchanters of every school. Silver is the rarest: a rain's whole catch at Silfgir, distilled, fills a vial no longer than a finger, and every cloudship built on Talan carries it.

#### ⚿ GM Secret: the Elden's taps

The three craters are Elden works, built in the Elden Era and still running. Each is a tap on the Wellspring's outflow: it draws a thread of source-fluid up through the mountain and lets it fall as rain, shaped by the light it rises through, the sun's at Gulgir, the stars' at Storngir, and at Silfgir the glow of the Cloud Sea. Raw Wellspring will not hold a form, which is why the rain fades by sundown unless it is drawn out. The engine in each mountain mediates the outflow the way the gods' presence once did, so the craters cause no Stillpool births. The taps are small working pieces of the craft the Elden reached for at their ritual site.

That is why starlight answers every school of magic: it is a sliver of the source of all of it. It is why a cloudship's binding needs silver: the Cloud Sea is the veil, and Wellspring shaped by its light is what lets a hull ride it. The craters' Silent-Tongue names are the machines' own. And the Azarketi, many of whom claim Elden blood they do not have, live off a real Elden machine.

## Siyabask and the one harbour

> *The quay at Siyabask is like every quay I have stood on, loud and crowded and smelling of tar, until you notice that nobody on it looks at the sea.*
> Vyrenna Tessek, *One Line Further*, 2526 MR

Auran's coast is cliff and black lava shelf almost all the way round. Fishing boats work out of the coves, and one place on the island can take a deep-keeled Hafra hull: the bay behind the Bask, an old lava flow run out into the sea and set as a long breakwater, with deep water behind it. Siyabask stands on the flow itself.

Everything the craters catch goes out through Siyabask. The saltkeels come in from the north-east, most of them the Red Dominion's carriers, two days out, and others straight from further ports; Fellibylur's Stormriders chart the lane. On the quay the buyers watch the three cloud-caps inland, and when one darkens, the chalk prices on the warehouse boards change. The Guild keeps a post-house on the quay, within the reach of Valreka's Sovereign, and the Post goes out on the carrying ships.

Three roads run inland from Siyabask, one to each crater: a day to Gulgir, two or three to Storngir, four to Silfgir on the far side. A coast track joins the fishing coves. The roads carry the down-carts: after each rain the drawn essence goes down to the port in sealed jars, and the silver goes down under guard.

## The rain-share

The rain on Auran belongs to the island, and every rain's sale is divided three ways. A third goes to the town that caught it, shared among everyone who worked the rain: **the catchers' third**. A tenth goes to the island's chest, for the roads, the walls, the Bask and the ships. The rest, a little over half, is **the rain-share**, paid once a year to every household on the island, in the farms and the coves as in the crater towns. Every household is entered in **the Daptar**, the share-roll, and draws one share. Shared among some 60,000 households, it buys a household's salt, its iron and a good coat; the island still farms and fishes for its living.

**The bargain.** The rain-share is a Dark-Era law. When the Golden Empire fell, Auran was left alone at the edge of the Hafra, and raiders came for the craters. The whole island's levies held the roads, and its boats held the Bask. The crater towns gave the rain to the island in return for its spears, its bread and its one quay. The bargain still runs both ways: a crater town is a bowl of roofs and channels with little farmland inside the rim, and it eats what the farms send up the road. The rain is sold at the quay at Siyabask and nowhere else, so the world buys it at one price.

**The Tolwaja.** The Tolwaja keeps the quay, the island's chest and Siyabask. She sets the prices, signs the island's trade and its treaties, receives envoys at her table on the quay, keeps the walls and the ships, judges at the quay, and decides the roads and the works the chest pays for. She sells nowhere but the quay and rules nothing inside a crater town: each crater town governs itself under its warden and builds its own roofs and channels. The households choose her for seven years, one household, one voice. Every village and town counts its households' voices and sends the tally to the quay, and the highest tally wins. She may stand again, and if the seat falls empty a tally is called at once. Those who keep no household of their own have no voice: the young until they found one, lodgers, sailors with no house on the island, and foreigners. **Hani Gipt Siyasang** has held the seat since 2525, and the tally falls this year.

**The sundown account.** Every evening the Tolwaja comes down to the quay steps, closes the day's books in public and answers any question put to her before dark. The account is held in Talanese, for the buyers on the quay. Answers the island disliked are remembered at the tally.

#### ◈ Popular Belief: sell a rain before it falls

Sell a rain before it falls, the island says, and the crater stops raining. The quay sells only what has been drawn.

## The silver

> *"The law says the rain is sold at the quay. Show me where it says how many quays."*
> Chakar Dur Kohbrah, warden of Silfgir, to the Tolwaja at the sundown account, 2532 MR

For more than a thousand years silver was the poor rain. It is the thinnest of the three and the hardest to draw, and the Empire's buyers paid little for it. Silfgir caught it all the same, because a rain is answered, and the share carried the silver town out of gold and starlight.

Around 2300 MR a Vindul yard built the first cloudship, and its binding took Auran's silver. Silfgir learned to distil, and within two centuries the poorest of the three craters became the one the world presses hardest. Every cloudship built on Talan since carries Auran's silver in its binding, and a hull takes the silver of several rains. The yards buy at the quay like everyone else, at the Tolwaja's price.

**The west harbour.** Silfgir's rain now carries the share, and Silfgir keeps the same third as Gulgir. Its warden, **Chakar Dur Kohbrah**, asks for a half. The farming households answer that they carried Silfgir when its rain was worthless, and Gulgir and Storngir watch, because if Silfgir gets a half, they will ask for one next. A Vindul yard has offered to build Silfgir a harbour of its own under the rim, an Arcanotech breakwater on the west lava shelf, at the yard's cost, and Chakar has not refused it. With its own quay Silfgir could sell its silver directly, and the yard would buy it without the Tolwaja's price. The one-quay law says the rain is sold at the quay; whether it forbids building a harbour, or only selling from one, is a question the law leaves open. Hani Gipt Siyasang stands for the tally this year, and the question comes up on the quay steps every evening.

## Daily life

In a crater town the day runs on standby. The workshops, the still-houses, the roof-menders and the channel-clearers keep one eye on the cap, and when the bell rings everything stops and the town goes up. Every household keeps a rain-cloak and a wide hat on the peg by the door. In the countryside the farms and the coves work under plain rain and watch the caps from far off. In Siyabask the day runs on the quay, the weighing and the warehouses, and ends with the sundown account.

The crater towns greet each other with *which cap?* On Auran *by dusk* is a promise and *tomorrow* a polite no. A quarrel is settled before dark: a neighbour comes to the door at dusk to have it out or have it done, and stays for supper. The bread is barley, eaten with fish and figs, and a drop of gold goes in the tea when someone is ill. The quay's vice is betting on the caps, which crater rains next, and the buyers bet too, with their prices.

**The share-day.** Once a year the rain-share is paid in every village and town on the same day, and every debt on the island falls due that day. Auran settles up by sundown.

**Getting about.** On foot, by mule and by cart on the three roads; by fishing boat round the coast.

**Visitors.** A visitor expects the gold to glow. A visitor tries to keep a puddle past sundown, takes *tomorrow* for a promise, and tries to buy at a crater town and is sent down the road to the quay. A native knows which crater will rain before the bell rings.

**The young.** A child's first rains are worked with a bowl: the child runs after the runnels that jump the channels, and what a child catches in a bowl outside the channels is the child's own, unshared. The grandmothers and the channel-wardens keep the bowl-right from turning into theft, and the young stretch it as far as it goes, standing just outside the channels with bigger and bigger bowls until the wardens chase them off. In the teens comes the first rain worked with a board, and a catcher's portion in one's own name. A household of one's own brings the share and a voice at the tally.

**Faith.** Tani is kept plainly. Sundown is her hour on Auran, and closing the day before dark is said to be hers. Each crater square has a small shrine of her three faces and one priest, and the first bowl of every rain is poured back into the lake.

## Peoples and history

Auran is the Azarketi's fullest home on Talan. They came to the island in the Lost Era, among the peoples the Crimson Rain had scattered, drawn by its water. The rest of the island is the southern mix, Ghoran, Nagaji and kitsune, and Siyabask holds everyone the ships bring.

The rains are older than any record, and the craters carry names in the Silent Tongue. Under the Golden Empire Auran was a far province, and gold ink and starlight were its trade. When the Empire fell, the island held alone through the Dark Era, and the rain-share was made. The first cloudship, around 2300 MR, turned Silfgir from the poorest crater into the most pressed.

## The tongue

Auran speaks **Hauri**, one of the tongues of the south-western sands, a cousin of Hareaveldi's Sokhan. Talanese is spoken on the quay and in the trade, and the sundown account is held in it.

## What an Auran is called

An Auran carries a given name, a bowl-name and a household name: **Hani Gipt Siyasang**. The bowl-name is given by the grandmothers at a child's first catch, from that moment, and kept for life; it is usually a single word. The household name is chosen by a household's founders when they found it and entered in the Daptar, which never takes a name twice. Children carry their parents' household name until they found their own, so a family name lasts one generation, and an Auran's last name says whose house they keep. A child before its first catch carries a given name and a household name.

**Sample names.** Given: Hani, Chakar, Mayro, Gwahram, Hammal, Doda, Balach, Bibarg, Sammi, Banari, Lali, Raji. Bowl-names: Gipt, Burt, Dur, Brah, Passaw, Banda, Istar. Households: Siyasang, Kohbrah, Zirkirr, Tiyab, Nodband, Jambar. Whole: *Hani Gipt Siyasang* · *Chakar Dur Kohbrah* · *Doda Tiyab* (a child before its first catch).

## The five checks

- **Endings.** Every paragraph and section ends on a fact. Fixed in drafting: the ◈ first closed on "Nobody on Auran has tried" (an untested claim and a soft epigram), now "The quay sells only what has been drawn"; the rain-share opening dropped a closing "and nobody owns the crater it falls in" (explained an implication).
- **Leaks.** Open prose names the Elden only as the craters' Silent-Tongue naming stratum; the taps, the Wellspring, the ritual site and the Corrupted God stay in the ⚿. The Azarketi's origin is told as "among the peoples the Crimson Rain had scattered", with no Storveldi; their Elden-blood claim appears only in the ⚿ and in `ancestries.md`'s existing folklore paragraph. The tension carries no campaign prompt.
- **Consistency.** Names and spellings agree across the draft and the column (Gulgir, Storngir, Silfgir, Siyabask, the Durbra); dates agree (the column 2526; Hani 2525, the tally 2532; the first cloudship c. 2300; the Empire's fall 1321). The column says "Silver rains least" and the draft "every week or two": consistent. The Guide line reads "the nearest crater town lies a day's road inland", matching a day to Gulgir.
- **Arithmetic.** 300,000 on ~11,900 sq mi is ~25 per sq mi (held heartland, 20–40). Siyabask 9,000 is 3% (1–3%). Gulgir 6,000, Storngir 4,000, Silfgir 3,000: the four towns hold 22,000, about 7%, the rest farms and coves. 300,000 at five to a household is 60,000 households. The split: a third + a tenth + the rest; 1/3 + 1/10 = 13/30, so the rain-share is 17/30, "a little over half". Distances from the sketch: Gulgir ~26 mi (a day), Storngir ~64 (two or three days), Silfgir ~103 (four days).
