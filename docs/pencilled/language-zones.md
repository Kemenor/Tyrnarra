## The rule (Option B, at the branch level)

- **The real-world family is the Tyrnarran kinship.** Regions whose word-bases share a real-world branch (Germanic, Romance, Celtic, Semitic, Berber, ...) speak related tongues, and a closer real-world relation means a closer Tyrnarran one (Italian and Portuguese are near cousins).
- **Kinship counts at the branch, never the superfamily.** Indo-European and Afroasiatic are the real world's deep time; in Tyrnarra that depth belongs to the deep stratum, older than any record. So Germanic, Romance and Celtic are three separate families, and Semitic and Berber are unrelated.
- **Three layers stand outside the rule.** The deep stratum (Basque/Icelandic with drift), Talanese (English with drift, the Imperial braid), and the Wildreach's Feyworld-touched speech (see Zone 2).
- **Zones follow physical location, not god domain.** A region joins the family its ground and neighbours give it; a domain border is no language line.
- **A tongue with no defined neighbour is a seed, not an isolate.** Its family is open until the regions around it are set.
- **Language follows culture, never ancestry** (GM, 2026-10-01). A tongue belongs to a place and the culture raised there: a kitsune raised in Merkavar speaks Merkavar's tongue, and anyone raised in Emarrea speaks Emarrea's. An ancestry shares a tongue only as far as its people live together in one culture.
- **Every base is rendered speakable for a German- and English-speaking table** (GM, 2026-10-01). The real-world language is inspiration for the sound, never a transcription: names come out westernized and pronounceable, in plain letters, the way the Order of Law's Tibetan names are already rendered. A hard sound (an ejective, a click, a tone, a pharyngeal) is suggested by spelling the table can read (*kh*, a doubled consonant) or dropped.
- **Existing names do not constrain the zones.** Most were coined before the strata and the registers existed, and the naming-scheme pass can rename them. Older lines written to justify a tongue under the old "Talanese and the old tongue only" frame (Kotokoe as an "ancient parallel tradition", the kitsune populations "mutually unrecognisable") are context, and may change.

## Zone 1: the East (pencilled 2026-10-01)

**The shape.** The Basogur Jungle walls the east off from the north; the outer sea closes it on the east; the inner sea opens it to the west, and the land runs on south into Egulon. Inside it, three families and one braid.

- **Celtic, the oldest in the east.** Vernua (Irish) and Nahaskel (Welsh), cousin branches (Goidelic and Brythonic), pressed between the jungle, the inner sea and their neighbours: a family once wider, pushed back to the jungle's edge by the two that came after.
- **Germanic, the north-east core.** Kaosadaemi (old Germanic, canon already "the Thousand's pre-Dark-Era layer") and the Thousand's spine. Walled by the jungle and the sea, it stays an eastern family. *Settled in Q3: it came from the Emerald Isles, the Empire's cradle; the Thousand was its first conquest, Kaosadaemi its second.*
- **Romance, the inner-sea and southern family.** Sister tongues from **an old Romance root older than the Crimson Rain** (GM: sisters, not Legea as the source). The chain runs Legea (French) → Namur (Italian) → Harro Distiratsua and Lua Lasai (already earmarked Iberian) → Argia Esfera (Portuguese); Namur and Harro touch at the corner. (Atarialda was pencilled here by position first, then moved to the South with Lautara's plain.)
- **The Thousand Kingdom, the braid.** Where the Germanic core from the north meets the Romance (French) core from the west: one people whose speech is built from both (canon word-base: Germanic / French).
- **The Order of Law, a monastic pocket.** Tibetan, carried in by the founding and kept apart on purpose; it explains itself as a monastery.
- **The Emerald Isles, native Talanese.** Canon already: the kingdom names itself in plain Talanese (Oathmoore, the Watchstone, the Lyteward Bough, the Bridgelands). Kept (GM, 2026-10-01). The Isles face Germanic in the north and Romance in the south (the founding isle); a trading kingdom hosting Sortalde's embassies speaks the tongue everyone understands. *Why it speaks Talanese natively: see Q3, the Isles were the Empire's cradle.*
- **The Basogur Jungle, the barrier with its own tongues.** Anadi (Swahili), Vanara (Sanskrit), and the druid tribes, who speak **the deep tongue as a living language** (their Basque tree-words): the only living speakers of what named the domains.

**The canon move this costs: Legea.** Its word-base is unset from Ge'ez/Amharic and becomes French (Romance). Its published names (Debreqal, Amdeqal, Tesfa Worash, the Orratzak and the rest) re-render into French; its personal-name structure (day-name + Deia + reading-house) stays, and its holy words stay deep-stratum *lege-* Basque. Lore, glossary, register table and the published page all follow when this commits.

## Zone 2: the South (pencilled 2026-10-01)

**The shape.** An East-Asian trade country to the north (Lautara, one family across the domain) and a Greek scholars' country to the south (Ezkudon), meeting on Azkataria's coffee-house floor, the corridor Ezkudon's scholars have walked for centuries.

- **Japonic across Lautara.** Emarrea's Japanese is the **old highland core** (highlands keep the older form). The plain (**Itsasalda, Merkavar, Azkataria, Atarialda**) speaks **sister tongues** on a Ryukyuan starting sound (Okinawan, Amami, Miyako, Yaeyama: Japanese shifted, *e* → *i*, *o* → *u*). Merkavar's kitsune speak the plain's tongue like their neighbours.
- **Variety inside the family (GM: we are not bound by base Ryukyuan).** Lautara is the continent's busiest country and must not sound like one place. Each region gets rules on top of the family base, worked at its naming pass. Three levers:
  1. **A starting dialect**: a different Ryukyuan tongue, or Old or Classical Japanese, per region.
  2. **Sound rules of its own**: a vowel shift, a dropped final vowel, a consonant change (*k* → *h*, *t* → *ch*), a favoured syllable shape; one or two per region, applied consistently.
  3. **A contact layer**: what each region's neighbours and trade lend it. Itsasalda's harbour takes from every port that docks there; Azkataria takes scholarly Greek from the corridor; Atarialda takes a word from everywhere its wanderers went; Merkavar keeps the trade-speech of the whole plain.
  The personal-name structures stay unique per region, as always, and carry the rest of the distinction.
- **Rika Tikur: native Talanese.** A company city of strangers that sorts by holdings speaks the one tongue everyone shares.
- **The Dreaming Cape: the plain's tongue at home, Talanese for its young institutions** (the Adventurer-Era Theocracy, Moonwatch, the Twin Lantern, the Wakehelm).
- **Greek across Ezkudon**, one tongue with deep dialects: **Attic** for Jakinduria and Thekkavar (the scholarly voice; the god-city keeps its own naming scheme over it, and Imperial Dwarvish stays its old scholarly register), a **Doric or Ionic** drift for the Golden Coast and the ring country.
- **The Wildreach: its own thing, outside the family rule** (GM, 2026-10-01). The region will be fey-based and connected to the **Feyworld** (canon: the Life-layer plane of Chaos, Zurzar's wood-stratum, entered by mortals only when brought), so its speech can be more magical and strange than any mortal family. It joins the deep stratum and Talanese as a third layer standing outside the rule. Designed at the Wildreach's build; two directions to start from: a tongue that **will not hold still** (words and names that shift with season, mood or speaker, the plane of Chaos in the grammar), or a tongue **only partly spoken** (sung, coloured, gestured, with the spoken part rendered in our voice by rule rather than by a real-world base).
- **Hareaveldi moves to Zone 3** (Lioaru's desert ground, beside Valreka's Berber; the Nagaji heartland). Persian stays unspent and goes to the south-west as a candidate; Tamil (Dravidian) is the other carried-over candidate.
- **Existing names** (Lautara's Basque market, coffee and rite names; Itsasalda's Icelandic river and range names) go to the naming pass; they do not constrain the zone.

## Zone 3: the South-west (pencilled 2026-10-01)

**The shape.** Two families meet in the desert south (Berber and Iranian, unrelated), a family of Sumendar's own holds the mountain country, and the forge and frontier ground speak Talanese. Two arrivals stand apart: the Order of Steam's Germanic and the Dragons' Georgian.

**Lioaru**
- **Galdua Jendea (and Valreka): Berber, kept.** Valreka's whale names already carry the strata (Lost-Era deep names, Golden-Era Talanese, Tamazight since the Dark Era); Valreka keeps its god-city scheme over the region's tongue.
- **River Duchies: a Berber sister** (a Kabyle or Tashelhit sound). The herd trades and marries into the Duchies through Asif, whose house "speaks with a valley accent": kinship makes that literal.
- **Hareaveldi: Persian.** The living Iranian tongue of the Nagaji heartland's desert.
- **The Lost Kingdom: the old Iranian root, the oldest sister** (GM). The Blackened Lands are **not empty**: Fleshwarps, Skeletons and perhaps more live in the cursed land, the remnants of a dead civilisation, and their speech should **feel old**: an Avestan or Old Persian sound, the archaic form beside Hareaveldi's living one. **The Persian family came from here.** *Canon to settle at the Lioaru build:* (1) this answers the open thread *The Storveldi tongue* (open-threads, *Languages*): it survives, in the Blackened Lands and as the root of the Iranian family, which ties the Iranian family to the Storveldi Denbora, a pre-Rain people (and so to Q2); (2) the descent sits on both tiers (GM, 2026-10-01): **open prose**, scholars argue over a likeness between fragments from the Blackened Lands and the speech of Hareaveldi; **⚿ GM Secret**, the Nagaji of Hareaveldi speak the daughter of the tongue of Tani's killers; (3) the lore's "no kingdom claims it" and its visitor list stand, and the build adds who lives there.
- **Lost Isle: open, a Persian or a Berber sister** (GM: either). It sits off the Lost Kingdom's coast and Galdua Jendea's west shore; decided at its build.

**Sumendar**
- **A family of Sumendar's own, on a Māori base** (GM). It ties together **Tahu Tangata, Haraour Eliza and the Red Dominion**, and maybe **Burdineyja**. **Not seafaring**: an inland and mountain family; Tahu Tangata touches the Midarra only by a sliver of the Order of Steam bay beside the Dreaming Cape. **The Red Dominion alone is seafaring** (the west coast). Tahu Tangata's name is not read as Polynesian; the Māori base is chosen for the sound, and the sisters can draw on the wider Polynesian family (Hawaiian, Samoan, Tongan, Tahitian) as starting sounds, with the same variety levers as Lautara (a starting dialect, sound rules of its own, a contact layer).
- **No Man's Land: frontier Talanese** (decided at its build).
- **Eldara: Talanese and the old tongue.** The god-city's own naming scheme draws on both (the city's own name is deep-stratum; Emberwardens, the Fog District and the Throat are Talanese).
- **Dragon's Reach: Georgian (Kartvelian).** The Dragons came from beyond the world, so their tongue has no kin on Talan; Georgian sounds unlike anything else on the map and its family has no relatives anywhere.
- **Order of Steam: Germanic (German proper)** (GM, confirmed), from canon's Old Dwarvish (*Stahlglanz*, Eisenhart). See Q3 below: Germanic may be the Golden Empire's family.
- **Burdineyja: in the Sumendar family** (GM). Its Hafra group lies off the Red Dominion's coast and takes the tongue of the shore it faces; through the Elden gate the same tongue is spoken on the Midarra group beside Moonwatch, a Sumendar tongue on an island in Lautara's waters.

## Zone 4: the West (pencilled 2026-10-01)

**The shape.** A Norse sea-world on the western inner sea; Myrkono's land-country open, decided after the North, once the pressure from both sides is known.

- **Myrkono: Quechua** (GM, 2026-10-01). Myrkono held through the Dark Era (Ilun Tasun has farmed its valleys "for more generations than they trouble to count"; the Wardstones were kept), so by the Brauogi rule it kept a tongue of its own, and that tongue is none of its neighbours' (Uralic north, the deep tongue north-east, Talanese east, Norse across the water south). Chosen on its merits and for balance: Slavic was set aside because it would have made Talan's west European as well, and Myrkono was the last large open zone to rebalance. **The fit:** the Andean peoples read constellations in the *dark* clouds of the Milky Way, shapes made of darkness, which is Izarelai's faith (the god is the dark between the lights); a mountain-walled basin, high herb-gardens, a grass-sea; and a sound that westernizes easily (Inti, Killa, Wayra, Pacha). **Sister sounds:** a Cusco sound, a Central sound and a Kichwa sound, worked per region at the naming pass (Ilun Tasun, Itzasoa, Izarelai, Three Pines), with the variety levers. **Three Pines** takes a Finnish contact layer from the Tengu at Grannholm. **Myrria** keeps its own scheme over Ilun Tasun's tongue.
- **The western inner sea: Norse (North Germanic)** (GM): the Floating Isles, Balaena, Balatur Erui, and Uravel (own scheme over it). **Icelandic stays deep stratum only** (GM): too much is already named in it. So the living isles draw on the **mainland Scandinavian end** (Danish, Swedish, Norwegian), which keeps the strata audible: a deep name sounds Icelandic and worn, a living one sounds mainland and plain. **Why it fits an archipelago of born and dying islands:** the names date the islands. The oldest isles carry deep names (Breidey, GM-Written, keeps its Icelandic name as one of the oldest), and the young ones carry names in the living tongue; the archipelago does visibly what the strata rule does everywhere, and a sailor can hear an island's age. The Scandinavian dialect continuum is canon's "every docking a new dialect"; the Nation Afloat's crew-tongue has the Hanseatic model canon already names (a Low German-like common tongue of the boats). Norse is Germanic, so the isles are kin to the Order of Steam and the Thousand (Q3).
- **Tvisol (Twin Suns and Bikitsa): decided with the North.** One people across the Brauogi/Myrkono line, facing only the Midarra; leans Norse with the sea-world.

## Zone 5: the North, worked from Ehizahar westward (in progress)

**Ehizahar (pencilled 2026-10-01).** The Golden Empire never held Ehizahar, so its speech is the north-east's own, untouched by the Germanic spread: Talan's largest non-imperial family.

- **Lands of Villtur: Mongolic, a dialect continuum by ground** (GM). Some clans speak the same, others differ; the **jungle** clans sound different from the **plains**, and both from the **ice**; at this size even two plains clans may share a base with their own differences. Sister sounds to start from: Khalkha for the grass, a Buryat sound for the northern snow and forest, an Oirat or Kalmyk sound toward the Baratalda border, and the jungle-edge clans' own, with the variety levers (a starting sound, a rule or two of its own, a contact layer: the Basogur for the jungle clans, the arctic coast for the ice).
- **Veidrath: the common Mongolic** (GM): the gathering's shared speech, the one every clan can use at the market and the ring, plus the god-city's own naming scheme.
- **Ardo Beroa: the island dialect** (GM), as canon already has it (the Villtur register "with one word changed and two dropped"), with a contact layer from Baerfrost and the arc ships.
- **Fenurra: four Mongolic dialects, one per tribe** (GM: Northeast Caucasian or Mongolic dialects, either works; Mongolic chosen). Canon already calls Fenurra "a Villtur country that finished becoming itself" (`villtur.md`); kin to Villtur in speech, apart from it in politics, which is the realistic pattern. The four tribes' dialects share **a harder crater sound of their own** (a sound rule: hard *k* and *kh*, doubled consonants, the hyphenated compound names canon already uses, as in Rhukk-Khaz and Zai-Vith), so Fenurra keeps its edge. This retires the old "Latinate / Germanic" base, which nothing on the map could explain, and keeps Fenurra clear of Dragon's Reach's Georgian. The existing names go to the naming pass.
- **The Basogur fringe:** as Zone 1 (Anadi, Vanara, the druids' deep tongue).

**Vindul and Haldmark (pencilled 2026-10-01; GM: "for now okay, we can always adjust later").** One **Uralic** family across the north-west, four sisters fitted to their ground. The far north was never held by the Empire.
- **Baerfrost: a Sami sound**, the arctic north coast; Finnic rune-song is a ready model for the skald-record.
- **Haizetsua: a Finnish sound**, the island; its Tengu names (Tessek, Karrok, Skennok, Vellok) already sit well in a westernized Finnish sound.
- **Haldmark: an Estonian or Karelian sound**, the cold march (settled here though it is Brauogi's; GM). The Kholo are an old people, Lost-Era deep.
- **Fellibylur: a Hungarian sound**, the steppe (the real family's steppe-riding branch). The **Thunderhost** interior keeps the old tongue; the **Skybell** coast ports lean toward Talanese through the Hafra trade. (Turkic was proposed and dropped: Fellibylur borders no Mongolic ground, and a Turkic pocket between Uralic and Talanese made no sense.)
- **Haizava: Uralic, plus its own scheme** (both banks of its river are Uralic).
- **Air Monastery: Talanese, with the old imperial craft vocabulary** from the brass the Empire gave it; a monastic pocket of a mendicant order that walks all of Talan.

**Brauogi (pencilled 2026-10-01).** The rule: **what never fell kept its tongue; what was emptied and refilled speaks Talanese.** (The Empire rose in the south, so Brauogi was never its longest-held ground; the reason is the Dark Era's.) The blight emptied Greenward's basin (the Four That Held; Babesarri "took in the people who had lost their fields") and Baratalda's valleys (rebuilt from the books of the mixed migratory crews), and land resettled by refugees of many peoples takes the tongue they share as its native one, the real-world creole pattern.
- **Greenward: Talanese.** The basin that died and was reclaimed.
- **Baratalda: Talanese**, with a Mongolic contact layer from Villtur's hunters at the Sagarri fairs.
- **Soul Tree: Talanese.** The dying of every land come to it.
- **Hirubaso: Talanese at home, and the Elkaride's own Grove Tongue** (GM, 2026-10-01): a druidic tongue on an Ainu sound (a real-world isolate, so kin to nothing on Talan), taught only within the order and spoken by no one else, distinct from the Basogur druids' Court Tongue. Its own name and sound rules land at Hirubaso's naming pass.
- **Sugeiturri: the deep tongue, Basque, still living** (GM). One of the oldest kingdoms on Talan, unbroken continuity: the River Houses never stopped speaking the tongue that named the world. Every old name in Brauogi is already Basque (Sugeiturri, Iturmen, Hirubaso, Garaztegi, Babesarri, Harrate, Zutarri): Brauogi spoke the deep tongue as a living language until the blight, and Sugeiturri still does.
- **Lurrath: Talanese** (the god-city speaks its host's tongue, plus its own scheme), with **the old tongue kept in the Naming** and in its builders' craft-words, as a liturgy keeps an older language.
- **Haldmark:** Uralic (see Vindul above).
- **Tvisol: a Quechua sister with a Norse flavour rule on top** (GM, 2026-10-01): the languages mix slightly across the years of ferry trade over the Midarra, so a Norse sound rule rides over the Quechua base (*Erfivin* a loanword from it). By the Brauogi rule it kept its tongue: the corruption took a generation, but its own surviving children rebuilt the kingdom. Half of it is Myrkono's Bikitsa, and it is "fiercely one people".

## Q2: the deep tongue (in progress, 2026-10-01)

*Naming note (GM, 2026-10-01): in canon the Elden tongue is named **the Silent Tongue** ("the Elder Tongue" sat too close to *Elden*), beside **the Court Tongue**.*

**The deep stratum is two tongues, not one** (GM). This answers the open thread *The Old Tongue* (open-threads, *Languages*): two separate pre-Rain tongues, never one ancestor.

- **Basque is the gods' tongue on Talan, not the true divine language** (GM, refined 2026-10-01). **The gods speak every tongue**: a prayer reaches its god in whatever language it is prayed. Basque is the tongue the gods **used on Talan**, the speech of their courts and households in the Gods' Era (2944–1 GR), when they ruled as kings, landlords and law; the true divine or angelic speech is a cosmology question and lies elsewhere (see *Raised* below). As the speech of divine rule it was **the naming tongue** (Reading 2, GM): old and holy things were named in it across every family's ground, the way medieval Europe named in Latin whatever it spoke at home, while mortals spoke their own families' tongues (the Romance root, the old Iranian root, Celtic) day to day. It lived as a spoken tongue only among those closest to the gods' order, which is exactly who still speaks it: **Sugeiturri**, whose River Houses kept the gods' rivers before mortals took them at the Rain, and the **Basogur druids**. Brauogi spoke it until the blight.
- **Icelandic is the Elden tongue** (GM): the tongue of the Elden, who dominated Talan from 6000 to 2945 GR and held their own against the gods. Their ground-names outlived them; comprehension did not ("little of their culture survives in comprehensible form"). It has **no living speakers** (Icelandic is deep-stratum only, Zone 4).

**What it explains (draft reading, to confirm):**
- **The deep stratum has an order inside it.** Divine names reach back to Creation (Ehizahar, "the oldest name on Talan"); Elden names come from the Elden's three millennia of dominance; Gods'-Era names are divine again. An Icelandic domain name (Vindul, Floteyn, Lioaru) is Elden ground the god kept; a Basque one was named by the gods; a **compound** is the gods renaming Elden ground and half-keeping it (Brauogi, *brauð* + *ogi*, "the bread named twice over in two ancient tongues"; Myrkono, *myrkur* + *kono*).
- **The Storveldi lie is in their name.** The Storveldi Denbora claimed Elden descent; their name pairs an Elden word with a divine one (*stór-veldi* + *denbora*), a people who spoke the old Iranian root at home dressing themselves in both deep tongues.
- **Each age of dominion left its ruling tongue on the map.** The Elden's (Icelandic), the gods' (Basque), the Golden Empire's (Imperial Dwarvish, now the spine of Talanese). Three masters of Talan, three strata of names.
- **The magical-tongues thread:** the bound thirteen speak every tongue, Talanese included; Basque is their court tongue on Talan, not their true speech; the Elden tongue is the "named-as-lost" Elden tongue of the elden.html TBDs, lost in comprehension and alive in names.
- **⚿ GM-tier consequence:** the Elden became the Corrupted God, so the Elden tongue on the map is the tongue of the thing bound beneath the world. Chronicler tier: scholars can tell two old strata apart and credit the elder to the Elden from ruin-inscriptions; the Corrupted God's identity stays sealed.

**The Elden tongue fades, it does not stop** (GM). The Elden vanished in a single day in 2945 GR, so their words and roots persisted for some time after them: the peoples who had lived under them went on using Elden names and words into the early Gods' Era, fading as the gods' court tongue took over. An Icelandic name can therefore date from the Elden Era or the first stretch of the Gods' Era.

**Icelandic names on things named after that** (Kyrrskog c. 1330 MR, Odain, Hverhofn, Fiskhofn, Haldmark, Fellibylur, Tvisol, Helgafjall, Hringseyja, Rika Tikur, GM-Written Breidey, and the rest) (GM): **re-rendered at the naming pass by default**, into the region's family or the gods' tongue; **kept where the place plausibly was Elden ground**, named then and settled later (case by case; Breidey only on the GM's word, since it is GM-Written); a **learned coinage** (a scholar or founder reaching for Elden words the way scholars reach for Latin and Greek) only where the name has a real scholarly reason.

**The gods' tongue carries the liturgy** (GM): the rites of the bound thirteen's clergy keep Basque as a holy tongue, the way Latin carried the Church. The sanctum names already show it (Igarbe, Zutarri, Urbarren, Urdeia), and so do Legea's *lege-* holy words.

**Q2 is pencilled.** Still owed elsewhere: the true divine language (cosmology, parked below).

## Q3: the Golden Empire (pencilled 2026-10-01)

**Two dwarven cultures, two Germanic roots** (GM). Imperial Dwarvish is Germanic; "Dwarvish" names two cultures' tongues, never an ancestry's.

- **The southern root: Old Dwarvish.** The mountain dwarves of Sumendar, there since the Elden or Gods' Era; the Order of Steam keeps their old speech on purpose (German sound, *Stahlglanz*). Its daughters: **the Norse isles** across the water (Floteyn borders Sumendar).
- **The imperial root: Imperial Dwarvish.** The Isle dwarves of the **Emerald Isles**, who arrived there in the Elden or Gods' Era. Its daughters: **the Thousand's Germanic spine** and **Kaosadaemi**; and **Talanese**, the braid that grew from it everywhere the Empire reached.

**⚿ The cradle (GM tier), reconciled with the published record** (`published/setting/talan/historical/golden-empire.html`: origin "the southern heartlands", capital "a Dark-Era ruin somewhere in modern Sumendar's outer reaches", and outside the Empire at its peak "the still-fractious kingdoms of what is now Zuzental's far east"; `timeline.md`, *The Golden Era*). Every published line stays true as the chronicler's record.

1. **The cradle.** The Empire was founded on the Emerald Isles. The Emperor was born near a Stillpool "on the southern foothills of what would become the imperial heartland" (`timeline.md`, *⚿ The Golden Empire: The Real History*): **the Southern Isle**, which canon already calls the Emerald Isles' "founding bough", whose name "preserves the founding memory". The Isle dwarves took **the Thousand first and fully**, its Germanic spine laid over the native French (Romance) speech, which is why the Thousand is a braid and why Zuzental's legal Talanese is "the closest of the modern dialects to the original Imperial Dwarvish" (`_continent.md`). **Kaosadaemi** came second ("the Thousand's pre-Dark-Era layer").
2. **The throne moved south.** As the Empire grew, the dynasty moved its capital into Sumendar's dwarven mountains, to the mines and the southern dwarves; the Empire ruled from there for centuries, so the record remembers it "rising from the southern heartlands". The archives that would correct it "did not survive the Dark Era's first decade" (`timeline.md`).
3. **A vassal empire.** Beyond its first conquests the Empire spread by keeping local rule largely intact (the published page's "provincial nobility"), which is why local tongues survived everywhere it ruled, beside the Talanese braid.
4. **The cradle's mainland broke away early.** By the Empire's peak the Thousand had shattered into the "still-fractious kingdoms of Zuzental's far east"; those are the thousand polities **Renauld Fyrstmond, the Old King**, later hammered together. The Isles stayed crown land under direct rule.
5. **After 1321 the Isles fell hardest.** Ruled directly, with no vassal to hold them, they collapsed with the Emperor and emptied. The modern Emerald Isles kingdom was refounded later on the Southern Isle, the founding bough, without knowing whose cradle it was. Its native **Talanese** is the imperial tongue's direct daughter at the cradle (this replaces the earlier refugee explanation in Zone 1).

**The imperial loan layer** (GM, 2026-10-01): every once-imperial region carries Talanese and old Imperial words in its names and trades; never-held ground (Ehizahar, the Basogur, Baerfrost, the Thousand after it broke away) carries the least, so a place's old imperial past is audible.

## Raised for the later questions

- **Q2, the deep tongue.** Three families were already spoken before the Crimson Rain, so the deep stratum cannot simply be "what everyone spoke then". It is either the families' **ancestor** (they grew out of it before the Rain, and only the oldest things kept its names), a **naming tongue** (people spoke their families' tongues and named old and holy things in the deep one, as medieval Europe named in Latin), or **an older people's tongue** (the land's first speakers, whose names outlived them, as Basque place-names lie under Spain). Also: is the deep stratum one tongue in two renderings (Basque and Icelandic), or two deep tongues, one east and one west? *Settled in part (GM, Zone 4): Icelandic stays deep stratum only; the living Norse of the isles draws on the mainland Scandinavian end.*
- **Q3, the Golden Empire:** pencilled above (the Emerald Isles cradle, two dwarven Germanic roots).
- **Q2, settled in part by Brauogi:** the deep tongue (Basque) was a **living language** in Brauogi until the Dark Era's blight and still lives in **Sugeiturri**; with the **Basogur druids** (Zone 1) that makes two living pockets. Q2 still owes what the deep tongue was before the Rain and how it sits beside the families already spoken then.
- **Q2 also takes:** the Iranian family has a known pre-Rain origin (the Storveldi homeland), so whatever Q2 decides about the deep tongue must sit beside a family that was already spoken before the Rain, and beside the deep-stratum name *Storveldi Denbora* itself.
- **The true divine language (cosmology, to park).** Proposed: the Wellspring's own speech, the tongue in which a word makes what it names. Canon already glimpses it in the Lost-Era leak, when "certain valleys spoke languages that produced what they named" and "infants were born already speaking forgotten tongues" (`timeline.md`, *⚿ The Lost Era: The Real History*). Whether the planar layers keep tongues of their own (the Feyworld's speech behind the Wildreach; Bolverk's scripts) belongs with it. A `cosmology.md` question, not a zone.
