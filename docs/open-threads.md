# Open Threads: Tyrnarra

Status-based punch list for canon work. Each gate represents how far along a thread is.

**How to use this file:**
- *Gate* tells you what kind of work is needed next.
- *Topic* (in the entry header) shows which canon domain it belongs to.
- *Decided* prevents future sessions from re-litigating settled bits.
- *Open* is the specific work to do.
- *Where* points to the file(s) where the thread is currently anchored.

This file holds **genuinely pending work**: unanswered canon questions, blocked or queued builds, reworks, and publishing debts. Story-paced enrichment of canon-complete material (named figures, festivals, rites, rolling texture) lives in [`deepening-ideas.md`](deepening-ideas.md). Pending label work on the map art lives in [`map-todo.md`](map-todo.md).

When a thread closes, delete the entry (git history preserves the resolution context); if only chronicler-tier follow-ups remain, move the remainder to [`deepening-ideas.md`](deepening-ideas.md). If closing it publishes new HTML, also update [`site-inventory.md`](site-inventory.md); if it renames or adds anything on the map, add a line to [`map-todo.md`](map-todo.md).

### [Cultures] Naming scheme and local tongue for every built region *(rolling)*
**Decided.** A regional register has two parts: a **word-base** (the real-world language that names the region's places and things, shareable with neighbours) and a **personal-name structure** (unique to the region; a name tells you the bearer's culture). Canon in [lore/geography/_continent.md](../lore/geography/_continent.md), *Naming strata across the eras*, with the register table; each structure is recorded in [lore/glossary.md](../lore/glossary.md) under its region's block, and the region's own file carries a *What a … is called* section (Villtur, Vernua, Kaosadaemi, Argia Esfera are the pattern). The table's rule is that a region defines its register at its build; everything built before the practice began (the Valreka deepening, 2026-09-17) carries none.
**Open (GM, 2026-09-29).** Go through every built region and give each two things:
- **A naming scheme.** Word-base plus personal-name structure, recorded in the register table, the glossary block, and a *What a … is called* section in the region's lore file. The word-base is chosen against the neighbours so the region sounds like itself; the structure grows from the region's own culture (Valreka's whales, Nahaskel's coin, the Anadi's woven story) and is never reused.
- **A local language definition.** The tongue itself: its name (coined per the naming rule, etymology in the glossary); who speaks it and where (the whole region, the household, the rite, one trade or class); how it sits beside Talanese (a bilingual norm like Kotokoe, a Talanese dialect with a local lexicon, a ritual survival, a lost tongue kept only in names); and what of it surfaces in the region's names. A region that speaks only Talanese is a valid answer when its history earns it (a Golden-Era foundation, a rail town); then name its dialect.
- **Names already in canon.** Per region, decide whether the existing Dark-Era institution and place names are re-rendered into the new word-base or kept as Talanese-era survivals; the naming-strata ladder gives either reading a history. Page mirrors follow on the publish signal.

**The frame (2026-10-01).** Its core is canon: the family each region speaks is in [lore/geography/_continent.md](../lore/geography/_continent.md), *The families of Talan*, and the two deep tongues in *Naming strata across the eras*. Define a region's tongue and word-base inside the family given there. The region-level moves still owed (Legea re-rendered into French, Fenurra into Mongolic dialects, the Lost Kingdom's inhabitants, Askamira's people, the Emerald Isles and Thousand reconciles with the cradle, the Silent-Tongue names given after the Elden) are listed in the working record [docs/pencilled/language-zones.md](pencilled/language-zones.md) and land at each region's naming pass.

**God-cities (GM, 2026-09-29).** A god-city gets **its own naming scheme** and **speaks its host region's tongue**. So its word-base follows the host's language, and what is its own is the personal-name structure (and any naming habits of the city's). One reconcile follows: **Valreka and Galdua Jendea** share one row today (Tamazight, the whale names). The whale structure is Valreka's, so Galdua Jendea needs a personal-name structure of its own over the shared Tamazight base, plus the local-tongue definition Valreka then speaks.
